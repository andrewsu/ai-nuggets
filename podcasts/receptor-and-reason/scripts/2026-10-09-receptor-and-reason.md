## Script

Welcome to Receptor and Reason for Friday, October ninth. Eleven items.

There is a thread worth naming before we start, because it will change how you read almost every paper today. In each of them, the load-bearing step is not a measurement. It is a choice the authors made — where to put a cutoff, how many clusters to ask for, which control to call a control, whether to describe two additive effects as opposition. The data are usually fine. The sentence on top of the data is where the work is being done, and today that sentence is doing more work than it earned.

We open with the paper that will travel furthest outside our field. A group at UC San Diego exposed human stem-cell-derived cortical organoids to acetaminophen and found, essentially, nothing. Twenty-five, fifty and one hundred micromolar, from day twenty-one to day twenty-six, against vehicle. No change in growth, in rosette area, in proliferation markers, in apoptosis, in firing rate or burst frequency or network synchrony on multi-electrode arrays, in synapsin puncta. Bulk RNA sequencing at one month found zero differentially expressed genes at the low dose, ten at the middle, sixteen at the high. Single-nucleus sequencing at three months found zero in any cell type.

The dose choice is the best thing in the paper and deserves credit. One hundred micromolar is about fifteen micrograms per milliliter, which is squarely the peak plasma concentration after a one-gram oral dose. Twenty-five micromolar is the modeled mean fetal arterial concentration. This is not the supra-physiological rodent dosing that makes most of that literature uninterpretable.

But read the limitation the authors put in their own supplement, because it reframes the whole thing. Fatty acid amide hydrolase is low in these organoids, and the cytochromes — two E one, one A two, three A four — are low or undetectable. Fatty acid amide hydrolase is what you need to make AM404, the metabolite that carries the endocannabinoid and TRPV1 mechanism, which is the leading proposed route for any central developmental effect. The cytochromes are what you need for the reactive metabolite. The authors present this as a feature, isolating the parent compound. As a scientific statement, fine. But the introduction and discussion then offer this null as experimental context for the familial-control epidemiology, and you cannot use a null from a tissue that provably cannot generate the proposed active metabolite, with no placenta, no liver, no maternal immune compartment and no fever, to contextualize an association whose hypothesized mechanism runs through exactly those compartments.

Two more things. There is no power analysis anywhere, and the three-month arm, the one supporting the persistence claim, is two cell lines per condition — fifty-five thousand nuclei is doing rhetorical work that eight samples cannot support. The cell-proportion numbers are also not cell counts: the methods state that nuclear segmentation was not feasible, so colocalization was quantified with Manders' coefficients, and then the results report those coefficients as percentages of cells. And the one positive finding — that the handful of differentially expressed genes enrich for neurodevelopmental pathways — comes from running sixteen genes against the entire human genome as background rather than against the organoid-expressed gene set, which returns neurodevelopmental terms by construction. The irony is that the only place this paper overclaims, it overclaims toward harm.

My read: a careful, genuinely well-replicated null about unmetabolized acetaminophen on isolated cortical tissue. That is a real contribution and it is narrower than it will be reported as.

Now to the cutoff problem in its purest form. A preprint out of Cincinnati Children's, funded entirely by Spinogenix with three of seven authors employed there, claims to define a subgroup within idiopathic autism that could serve as enrichment criteria for therapeutic development. The biomarker is resting relative gamma power — thirty to one hundred hertz over one to one hundred hertz. They take the median of a fragile X reference cohort, subtract one standard deviation, get point one eight five, and apply it to a decade of archived autism electroencephalograms. Twenty-two percent clear the bar.

Here is the number that undoes it. The autism cohort's own interquartile range tops out at point one eight. The imported, genetically anchored threshold lands on this cohort's seventy-fifth percentile. Every selection claim in the paper is reproducible without ever recording a single fragile X participant — it is "take my top quartile," relabeled.

It gets worse in three directions. There is no typically developing control group anywhere, so twenty-two percent is uninterpretable; and a sentence in the limitations refers to "the fragile X and control groups," while the epoch counts cover two hundred seventy-five recordings for two hundred participants, which suggests a control arm existed and was removed. Second, the age gradient sold as the headline — eight percent under thirteen, eighteen percent in adolescence, thirty-two percent in adults — is what relative band power does arithmetically, because the delta and theta denominator shrinks across development. There is no aperiodic separation; they say so. Third, thirty to one hundred hertz at the scalp is the electromyographic band, and the only defense offered is component analysis and manual review. The figure caption concedes the game: the reference cohort was, in their words, conditioned on elevated relative gamma. And the one prospective claim — an autism cohort screened on gamma in their registered phase two-b trial — is not in the registry as posted, which lists fragile X only.

Stay with imposed partitions, because the next one is the same error with better data. A large consortium analysis in Molecular Psychiatry derives three psychosis imaging neurosubtypes from multiscale functional network connectivity — seventeen hundred fifty-three scans, with a held-out replication set of three sixty-two. They reduce five thousand four hundred sixty connectivity edges to three latent components, then run k-means on the probands.

Two things to know. The replication does not re-cluster; it assigns held-out subjects to the discovery centroids by nearest distance, which guarantees every point gets a label and tests nothing about whether clusters exist. And the number of clusters was chosen by sweeping Calinski-Harabasz and silhouette from k equals two upward — indices that cannot return one, and that will nominate some k on a single multivariate Gaussian.

The decisive table is the clinical one. In the replication set, every clinical analysis of variance is null: Young Mania, Montgomery-Åsberg, all three PANSS subscales, total, schizo-bipolar scale, age of onset. Eight for eight. The discovery hits do not replicate — and the table then adds a pooled row combining both sets, which recovers significance and is not an independent test, since discovery is seventy-nine percent of it. The subtype-by-diagnosis association also fails in replication. Meanwhile cognition orders cleanly and monotonically across subtypes in both sets, which is what a severity continuum looks like. And buried in the supplement: applying a standard head-motion criterion would remove thirty-five percent of scans, twenty-six percent of controls against forty percent of probands, and the primary clustering was run on the uncensored data. The paper's own limitations section says it cannot distinguish discrete entities from structured partitions of a continuum. Believe the limitations section.

A third version of the same problem, now about a verb. Also in Molecular Psychiatry, a group at Ohio State reports that gestational restraint stress reprograms placental signaling and opposes maternal immune activation in the fetal brain. The data are careful: a real two-by-two for cytokines and bulk sequencing, one animal per litter, cluster-robust standard errors on fetal weights.

But "opposes" is not what they measured. Three fetal brain gene modules move in opposite directions for the two exposures, each at a false discovery rate around three percent — and the stress-by-poly-I-C interaction terms come in at point eight seven, point six four and point four five. The text itself says "additive." If the effects are additive and nearly equal in magnitude, the doubly exposed animals should sit at control baseline, which is a genuinely interesting prediction and not an antagonism. The supporting numbers are thinner than the modules suggest: the interaction term yields four genes in fetal brain and seven in placenta. The single-cell arm that carries the placental mechanism is three dams versus three, with four placentas pooled per library, and in the cell types named as showing coordinated interferon suppression — B cells, T cells, decidual stroma — the gene-level counts are zero, one and two. The one real signal is endothelial. And no corticosterone was measured anywhere; restraint suppresses food intake, and reduced trophoblast oxidative phosphorylation with suppressed interferon tone is also the signature of gestational undernutrition.

Palate cleanser, and the best mechanism paper of the day. A group at POSTECH, in Neuropsychopharmacology, asks what makes a fear memory precise rather than general, and lands on the inhibitory half of the amygdala engram. The claim is that inducible cyclic-AMP early repressor — a dominant-negative CREM-family transcriptional brake — gates GABA-A alpha-one receptor expression on lateral amygdala engram cells, and that losing that inhibition is what turns a discriminator into a generalizer.

What makes it worth your time is the control architecture. Overexpression is restricted to engram cells using an intersectional cross; a non-restricted, amygdala-wide version of the same manipulation does nothing to discrimination; and co-overexpressing the alpha-one subunit rescues both the generalized freezing and the discrimination index. There is a lovely input-specific result not in the abstract: terminals tagged by the conditioned tone evoke proportionally larger excitation and inhibition onto engram cells, leaving the ratio unchanged, whereas terminals tagged by a novel tone evoke larger inhibition with equal excitation. The inhibitory bias is specific to off-target inputs. That is a real circuit-level account of precision.

Two caveats. The claim that learning enhances inhibition rests on comparing engram to neighboring non-engram cells in conditioned animals, with no naive comparison for most of it — which leaves the allocation account wide open, since CREB-high cells are preferentially recruited into engrams and CREB drives the alpha-one subunit. And the bidirectional claim uses two different assays: the overexpression arm tests a two-kilohertz novel tone, where baseline freezing is at floor, while the depletion arm had to switch to eight kilohertz to have room to improve. The repressor pulse peaks around two hours after learning, which if it holds up is a well-defined therapeutic window.

Receptor attribution next, from bioRxiv. A Cardiff and Catanzaro group gave lisuride to the Strasbourg genetic absence epilepsy rat. Lisuride matters because it is the standard counterexample — a serotonin two-A ligand that is not psychedelic — and recent work recast it as a low-efficacy partial agonist rather than a biased one. At point five milligrams per kilogram the effect is dramatic and biphasic: time in seizure down ninety-nine percent at twenty minutes, still down eighty-two percent at forty, then up a hundred eighteen percent at two hours. Nine animals per group.

The pharmacology is the point. They pretreated with the selective two-A antagonist MDL-eleven-nine-thirty-nine, and the early suppression survived almost intact. The delayed pro-absence rebound was blocked. So the anti-absence effect — the one you would want to develop — is not a serotonin two-A effect at all, which separates lisuride cleanly from the two-A agonist TCB-2 and points at the dopaminergic side of its profile. Dissociating initiation from maintenance is a nice secondary result: the early phase reduces both seizure number and duration, the late phase only prolongs them.

One caveat the authors raise in a single sentence and do not test. Absence seizures here are scored as spike-wave discharges accompanied by behavioral arrest, and lisuride produced early agitation and locomotor activation. The ninety-nine percent suppression lands exactly in the agitation window. There is no vigilance-state analysis and no sleep scoring, so some unknown fraction of the most impressive number in the paper could be an animal that was too busy moving to be scored as arrested.

Staying with controls that are not quite controls. Also in Neuropsychopharmacology, a Ghent group drove the locus coeruleus with transcranial ultrasound in awake mice, reading out calcium in noradrenergic cells and extracellular norepinephrine in hippocampus with a genetically encoded sensor. They did the hydrophone work most ultrasound papers skip, scanning through an implanted skull. Lateral half-width is point six millimeters, which is fine. Axial half-width is four millimeters, so the full-width beam spans essentially the entire dorsoventral extent of a mouse brain.

And then the number that reframes the paper, which they report themselves: at the intended target, the off-target control condition delivered sixty-one percent of the on-target acoustic intensity. The active control is a thirty-nine percent dose reduction at the same place. Everything attributed to target specificity is equally consistent with a monotonic intensity-response. The auditory confound is explicitly unresolved — no deafened animals, no masking — and the pulse envelope, eighty milliseconds on and three hundred twenty off at two and a half hertz, is close to worst case for the known ultrasound auditory artifact in the most startle-responsive nucleus you could pick.

What is genuinely novel survives all that: calcium in locus coeruleus cells goes up and stays up, while hippocampal norepinephrine goes net down. Their explanation is alpha-two autoinhibition uncoupling release from sustained calcium, which is the most interesting idea here and is entirely untested — no clonidine or yohimbine challenge. Treat the imaging arm with more caution still: whole-brain glucose uptake down thirty-five percent and brown fat uptake up three-point-seven fold, in a pre-post design with no sham and no reference-region normalization, so the brain decrease may be partly a mass-balance artifact of the brown fat sink.

Now three papers about language models, and the best of them is the one that measures the thing nobody measures. A group at NYU Langone pulled a hundred twenty-eight thousand conversations from a secure institutional assistant used by sixty-three hundred clinicians over eight months, and compared the task mix to fifty-eight published medical benchmarks. Documentation and administration is thirty-six percent of real queries. Knowledge retrieval is twenty-nine. Diagnosis and differential is three-point-seven percent. The median benchmark covers about thirty-one percent of the real task mix, and the median benchmark contains zero documentation items.

Two numbers from deeper in. Out of a hundred twenty-eight thousand queries, the recommended safest response was escalate for thirty-two and abstain for fifty. Eighty-two total. For a field that has spent years tuning refusal behavior, that is the most useful fact in the paper. And the harm ratings that drive the headline are the least reliable field in it: for the binary high-risk flag, inter-rater alpha is point one three with a confidence interval including zero, all three clinicians agreed on the harm grade for twelve of a hundred queries, and the model's twenty-four percent becomes six percent by clinician majority — the paper reports a calibrated three-point-seven percent in methods while the abstract runs on thirteen.

Be careful with the conclusion, though. They measure a distributional mismatch and conclude that benchmark scores say little about deployed performance — but they never scored a model on a single real query, and an unrepresentative benchmark can still predict deployed quality if performance correlates across task types. Measured premise, unmeasured conclusion. The mismatch is also mostly one category: for a medical question-answering benchmark, sixty percent of the gap is literally documentation, and the newest clinician-sourced benchmark already largely fixed it.

Two shorter ones that rhyme, both posted yesterday. The first is an evidence-constrained agentic framework for interpreting regulatory variants: a bank of DNA-language-model binding classifiers feeds a planner that queries allele-specific binding data, motif scores and candidate regulatory elements, and a deterministic decision tree assigns a verdict — supported, contradicted, rescued, abstained. The architecture is right. Typed evidence provenance, an explicit flag for whether an experimental claim is permitted, and a refusal-capable stopping rule are exactly what variant interpretation needs. But the paper's own table shows the language-model planner reaching identical verdicts to a hardcoded priority list in five of five cases, saving four tool calls by declining to query a database that by construction cannot change a verdict. The frontier-model baseline section contains no numbers and says so. And the only component that can still hallucinate — the narrative reporter — is explicitly deferred to future work.

The second is a spin-detection study from Ljubljana, framed as finding outcome switching in published trials. It reports accuracy of point nine zero; the majority class is point seven nine, so the margin over a constant predictor is eleven points. The decision threshold was tuned on a hundred-instance set containing thirty-one positives, the prompt was iterated against one model's development errors and then broke the largest model in the ensemble, and the comparison that matters — a fine-tuned BERT roughly ten points better — was quoted from a 2019 paper rather than rerun on their split. The explanations are generated after the label and about one in ten contains a hallucination. Both papers build a language model into a loop where the model turns out not to be the load-bearing part.

Which brings us to a psychiatric interviewing agent, out of Tehran. Two agents: one conducts a free-form interview with a private notepad and a skills catalogue, the other reads only the finished transcript and emits up to three ranked diagnostic codes, or none. They ran the right baseline — plain prompting of the same backbone with the same turn budget — and on held-out synthetic cases they get sixty-one percent rank-one accuracy against fifty-two.

The appendix tells you what actually happened, and to their credit it is right there. On transcripts where the human experts did propose a diagnosis, the agent and the baseline both issue one every single time; there is no difference at all. The entire advantage comes from the cases where the experts withheld — nineteen correct withholdings out of twenty-nine against eleven. Family-level accuracy does not improve: sixty-one of eighty-one against sixty. The gain is finer code selection inside the right family, plus abstention, at thirteen times the interview tokens. In the human study the agent scored lower than plain prompting on expert-rated diagnostic inquiry and on medical rule-outs. One number I did like: in simulation the agent reserved high confidence for five reports and all five were correct, against fifteen errors in the baseline's high-confidence bucket — but with real participants it issued zero high-confidence hypotheses, so the calibration that looks useful never fired.

We end light, with a methods paper you may actually use. A Tsinghua and Fudan group built a spike-inference model for two-photon calcium traces around bidirectional infinite-impulse-response state-space scans whose decay rates are initialized across a physiological range, explicitly mirroring first-order calcium kinetics, with a conditioning encoder that embeds indicator identity and sampling rate. Zero-shot, holding out one indicator at a time, mean correlation is point six five against point four six and point four eight for the two standard methods — about thirty-five percent relative.

The appendix is unusually honest and quietly deflates it. Scale the baselines to comparable capacity and they come up to point five eight and point five five, so roughly half the headline gap is parameter count. The build-up ablation puts the dynamics-informed state-space idea that names the paper at about a third of the remaining gain, with loss and head engineering doing more. And the win is correlation-only: on their own tables the model is frequently the worst method on normalized error and bias while winning on correlation, which contradicts the text's claim of consistently lower error. There is no spike-timing metric anywhere — no F-one at a tolerance, no van Rossum distance. If you care about rates, this is probably worth adopting. If you care about timing, nobody has shown you anything.

That is the day. The common failure is not fraud and it is not sloppiness — most of these are careful papers. It is that the sentence at the top is one notch broader than the design supports, and the one notch is usually a cutoff, a cluster count, a control that was not controlled, or a verb. Read the limitations sections; today they are consistently more honest than the conclusions. See you tomorrow.
