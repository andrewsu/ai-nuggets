## Script

Welcome to Receptor and Reason for Wednesday, October twenty-first. Eleven items today.

The thread is the reference standard — not the effect, the ruler. In most of these papers the headline is only as good as the thing it is measured against, and in several the reference turns out to be built, partly or wholly, out of the thing being tested. That is a different failure from a weak effect. A weak effect you can detect. A circular reference agrees with itself by construction.

We start with the most consequential result of the week for anyone who thinks structural imaging is close to clinical use in psychosis risk. The PRONIA consortium has taken its own published grey-matter classifier for transition to psychosis — the twenty twenty-one model — frozen it, and run it against four external cohorts with no refitting, no site harmonisation, no recalibration. A thousand and fourteen participants, a hundred and thirty-two transitions.

In discovery, the out-of-training area under the curve was zero point seven four two. Externally it is zero point five six three — and split by cohort the pattern indicts itself. The two cohorts previously used to evaluate this model come in at zero point six five and zero point six nine; the two seeing it for the first time, at zero point five three and zero point five six. Prior exposure, not biology, explains the spread. Calibration is worse: the slope falls from zero point seven four to zero point one seven.

Then the comparators, which are the reason this is the lead. The model's Brier score externally was zero point two five, against zero point one three for assigning every participant the pooled external transition rate. It loses to the base rate. And a clinical-only logistic regression on symptom ratings, no imaging, leave-one-cohort-out, scored zero point five six eight — identical. Adding the frozen imaging score to it changed discrimination by fourteen thousandths, interval spanning zero. Imaging adds nothing over symptoms; symptoms add nothing over the base rate. That whole chain is reported by the group whose model it is.

The kicker is their brain-age moderator. Trained on eleven hundred healthy controls with an in-sample R-squared of zero point six nine, in the clinical cohorts its predictive R-squared is negative in every one — worse than guessing everyone is the cohort mean age — and the observed gap spans about seventy-eight years inside a sample aged twelve to forty-two. The paper's own pitch is a sliding-window method for mapping where discrimination fails; I would not lean on it, since nothing survives multiplicity correction and the good windows sit on seven to nine events. The authors say all of this themselves.

Second, machine assistance for reading neurologic autoantibody cell-based assays, from two Chinese centres across fourteen targets — aquaporin-4, NMDA receptor, LGI1, GAD65, the usual panel. The model is deliberately unglamorous: classical segmentation of transfected cells, red-channel intensity inside the mask against a pericellular ring, then a randomised-trees classifier calling negative, weak-positive-or-equivocal, or positive. Three-class agreement on the held-out four hundred and ninety-nine was eighty-five percent. But recall on the weak-positive class — the only class a tool like this exists for — is fifty point seven percent, and twenty-eight of the thirty-six it missed were overcalled as positive.

Then the reader study. Four readers, two experienced and two not, read all cases unassisted, waited two weeks, and reread them with the model's call displayed. Seventy-three of nearly two thousand ratings changed; Fleiss kappa rose from zero point seven seven to zero point eight one, unanimity from seventy-nine to eighty-three percent. Stop there and you would call it a win. Now the geometry of those seventy-three changes: ninety-three percent moved directly onto the model's category, ninety-seven percent moved closer to it, and not one moved away. The most common revision among the experienced readers, forty-three of their fifty-six, was weak-positive to positive — precisely the direction in which this model is wrong half the time. Assistance moved readers toward the model exactly where the model is worst, and the kappa gain is indistinguishable from anchoring. And the reference standard is the session-one majority vote of those same four readers. So eighty-five percent is agreement with four technologists, not accuracy against disease; there is no orthogonal assay and no algorithmic comparator, not even a plain intensity threshold. Five of forty-five aquaporin-4 negatives were called positive, and a false aquaporin-4 result is a neuromyelitis optica misdiagnosis. Their own discussion says reader-model agreement should not be equated with accuracy, which is the correct conclusion.

Third, an audit of what is actually in a depression-assessment transcript, from East China Normal and Fudan. They took three hundred and eighty-two dialogues — ninety-nine Chinese psychiatric interviews scored on the Hamilton, two hundred and seventy-one English distress interviews scored on the patient health questionnaire — and asked, for each symptom dimension in each transcript, whether the words spoken actually support a judgement on that symptom. Across twenty-seven hundred cells, eighty-three percent do not: forty-three percent have nothing, forty percent partial. The split by corpus is the finding. Seventy-four percent of the psychiatric-interview cells carry sufficient evidence, against one point nine percent of the distress-interview cells — and those supply seventy-nine percent of the total. The benchmark the field leans on hardest is the one where the transcript mostly does not contain the answer.

Their model audit is deliberately small — eighty-seven cells, three cheap models, one run each, so ignore any ranking — but the shape matters. All three got exactly fifty-two right, with different correct sets; of the forty-two they jointly got right, eight had aligned sufficient evidence and six had evidence pointing the other way. Demanding a decisive quote in the prompt did not help. The honest negative is better than the headline. Their evidence standard is itself three language models fused by a latent-class fit, so they ran perturbation checks where the correct state is known, and no condition passed their own gate — humans recover the intended state eighty to ninety-two percent of the time, the judges four to twenty-eight. Meanwhile the panel looks excellent by convention, Fleiss kappa zero point seven nine, seventy-three percent unanimous, while its eigenvalue participation ratio is one point four: five judges carrying the information of one and a half independent raters. Agreement without independence, and that generalises to every judge panel I have read this year.

Fourth, and read directly against that, a multimodal system for depression assessment from counselling audio, out of a Hong Kong and Shenzhen collaboration: a fine-tuned seven-billion-parameter omni model, three-stage curriculum, synthetic hundred-hour speech corpus. It reports a Matthews correlation of zero point seven zero against zero point six five for zero-shot Gemini two point five Pro, on a test set of thirty-three conversations with twelve depressed and identical recall. Back out the confusion matrix and the entire pipeline buys one fewer false positive out of thirty-three. There is no text-only ablation, so the acoustic channel is never shown to do anything, and the trivial baselines sit unlabelled in the results table — one row is the always-depressed predictor, another always-healthy, both reported as model failures rather than as the floor. The good negative is that bolting their reasoning scaffold onto frontier models makes them worse, collapsing Gemini to all-positive: structure without reliable perception just launders a prior.

Fifth, the cleanest statement of today's thread, from a benchmark of genetic-perturbation prediction. Twenty model configurations across six architecture classes — graph networks, variational models, GEARS, language-model retrieval, and plain ridge and kernel ridge — on CRISPR interference screens: about a thousand shared single-gene perturbations across four essential-gene screens, and three thousand across three genome-wide screens. Their out-of-distribution split is the real contribution: instead of holding out perturbations at random, they cluster perturbations by ground-truth response profile and assign whole clusters to train or test, and they show that random splitting leaves essentially no pathway-enrichment structure to generalise across — which is why random-split benchmarks look solvable.

Here is why the item belongs here. The predict-the-training-mean baseline — output the average perturbation response regardless of which gene you knocked down — is not a comparator in this paper. It is the origin of the axis. Every score is scaled so zero is exactly that baseline and one is perfect. On the essential-gene screens, under the hard split on unseen perturbations, the twenty models average zero point one four and the best reaches zero point two four. On the genome-wide screens the best of twenty sits between zero point zero seven and zero point zero nine — one to nine percent of the distance from mode collapse to correct, before the hard split. And under those conditions models differ from each other less than one model differs from itself across seeds, with between-seed rank correlation falling to around zero point three. The leaderboard stops existing exactly where it would matter. Caveats: three seeds, no confidence intervals, compute not matched, and GEARS was run with its gene-ontology graph replaced, which its authors would fairly object to.

Sixth, a foundation model for brain-organoid network dynamics, out of Santa Cruz. Thirty-two hundred high-density multi-electrode recordings, nine hundred and fourteen hours of spiking, six Mamba-2 layers and about twelve million parameters compressing history into a five-hundred-and-twelve-dimensional state whose size does not grow with electrode count. The architecture is sensible and generalisation to held-out lines is real. But the only forecasting baseline is a causal exponentially weighted moving average of each neuron's own recent firing — no transformer, no population autoregression, no feature classifier. And the paper's best honest negative undercuts its own headline: directions separating randomly relabelled episodes were at least as aligned with spontaneous activity as the real balance-versus-failure direction, so the structure is not a dedicated axis, it is one the network was already using. The genotype work has the mirror problem — the sixteen-p-eleven-point-two contrast crosses donor, sex and differentiation protocol, while their one isogenic comparison, a SHANK3 heterozygote against its parent line, is null on every test. The permutation nulls are done by exact enumeration, which makes that null credible rather than underpowered.

For a pharmacology audience there is one detail I cannot get past. The methods describe treating organoids with gabazine at one micromolar, NBQX at twenty, and AP5 at a hundred — GABA-A, AMPA and NMDA blockade. Each compound appears exactly once in the manuscript, in that paragraph. There is not one pharmacological result anywhere. A model of network dynamics built on the one preparation where receptor-level perturbation is cheap, with the perturbations run and unreported.

Seventh, from organoid dynamics to primate cortical dynamics under a drug. A Pittsburgh and Columbia group infused ketamine intravenously in two rhesus macaques performing a memory-guided saccade with a variable delay, with reward scaled inversely to reaction time so anticipation pays. Ketamine flattened the reaction-time-versus-delay function: unchanged at short delays, slower at long ones, as if less time had elapsed than really had. The interaction carrying that claim is significant in both animals and survives adding saccade covariates. In two hundred and ten lateral intraparietal units the ramping slope flattens — crucially for both up-ramping and down-ramping neurons, so this is not rate suppression — with the delay subspace rotating about four degrees per hundredth of a milligram per litre of modelled plasma while the response shape is conserved.

Three things to hold. The control is the pre-infusion saline block of the same session, always first, never counterbalanced. Plasma was never assayed — the pharmacokinetic constants are human, because macaque data do not exist, so every dose-dependent claim is dependence on a modelled quantity. And the one analysis actually anchored to behaviour, a population axis built to carry elapsed time and reaction time, shows a null effect of ketamine at p equals zero point three, which the authors state plainly. Clock rate versus clock noise is never adjudicated, and a widened Weber fraction flattens that anticipation function identically — which is the standard human finding. Read it as a direction-of-effect result with a real reversible manipulation, not an established neural correlate.

Eighth, chemistry that is measured rather than scored: a novel deorphanisation route for GPR84, from Oxford. The claim is that the endogenous agonists are alkylpyrones, and that they are not products of a pathway but mistakes made by one. Block the ketoreductase domain of fatty acid synthase and the beta-ketoacyl intermediate goes unreduced; the ketosynthase runs one extra Claisen extension on it, and the resulting tricarbonyl cyclises off the carrier arm as a four-hydroxy-pyranone. Chain length is just how many reductive cycles finished before the derailment. This was described in purified pigeon liver synthase in the nineteen sixties and dismissed; the new claim is that mammalian cells do it and that the products signal.

What persuades me is the receptor side, the one genuinely non-circular test in the paper: CRISPR knockout of GPR84 in THP-1 macrophages abolishes the response to the whole series, with a complement response preserved as a receptor-independent control. The pocket rationale is designable too — the pyrone's carbonyl and hydroxyl can engage both the arginine and the tryptophan where a medium-chain fatty acid carboxylate reaches only one. Now the problem. There is not a single numeric potency in the text — no half-maximal concentration, no maximal effect, no p-value, all of it in figure panels or an unsupplied supplement. The only quantitative sentence is that the smallest fragment is roughly tenfold more potent than a literature value for capric acid, inferred rather than stated, against a number from a different plate on a different day. The known synthetic agonists were not run, there is no antagonist to pair with the knockout, and the other free-fatty-acid receptors were not counter-screened. The concentration argument, the crux of any deorphanisation, runs the wrong way: the most potent congeners are detectable only at picomolar levels while the abundant one is weakest, and the intracellular pool is unmeasured because these compounds degrade in extracts. Nothing pro-inflammatory was measured despite the title. And the inducing condition is an inhibitor the authors concede has no known counterpart in unmodified biology. Run it as a concept — the agonist is a mistake the enzyme makes — not as a result.

Ninth, a cytokine target in arthritis pain from King's College London, where the comparator is the story. In human stem-cell-derived sensory neurons, leukaemia inhibitory factor at ten nanograms per millilitre moves fourteen hundred and forty-one genes, against two hundred and forty-six for interleukin-6 with its soluble receptor and a hundred and forty-five for interleukin-11 with its soluble receptor, both at a hundred nanograms — a tenth of the dose and roughly six times the reach, with the interleukin-6 gene set nested almost entirely inside it. TRPV1, SCN10A, NTRK1 and the histamine-1 receptor all move, in a dish with no glia and no immune cells. Tofacitinib reverses every sensitisation gene except TRPV1 and a sodium-channel beta subunit. Patient synovial fluid drives STAT3 phosphorylation in those neurons, and blocking this cytokine suppresses it better than tocilizumab does.

The title says pro-algesic, and that part is not there yet. The human correlation between synovial cytokine and reported pain was run and was not significant — twenty-two patients, mixed diagnoses, a single eleven-point rating on the aspirated knee, unadjusted for inflammatory markers, with the coefficient and p not reported anywhere. Everyone scored at least three, so the pain axis is range-restricted. The animal arm is one half-microgram bolus into a healthy knee, read by pressure application with no threshold in grams given, a day-by-treatment interaction at p equals zero point zero four, and a stated post hoc that compares the treated group to its own baseline rather than to vehicle. And there is no neutralisation in any animal — no antibody, no receptor antagonist, no gp130 blockade, no sensory-neuron receptor deletion. Everything causal is in a dish. Take the comparison against interleukin-6 trans-signalling, which is clean and genuinely surprising, and wait for the blockade experiment.

Tenth, a small masterpiece of the thing this episode is about, from Hamburg. Eighty-two healthy volunteers, between-subject, contact heat on the forearm. They were told sham nerve stimulation at three frequencies gave low, medium or high analgesia. No stimulation was ever delivered — the experimenters covertly lowered the thermode instead, calibrated per person. One group started with the strong treatment and worked down, the other started weak and worked up; identical temperatures, identical totals, only the order differed. Then both were tested on the same medium treatment. Starting strong wins by twelve points on a hundred-point scale, Cohen's d of zero point eight three, and expectancy ratings did not differ between groups at all, which kills the obvious mechanism.

The exploratory analysis is the keeper. The untreated control stimulus — the same calibrated temperature, delivered before every treatment block — was rated as more painful by the descending group as the session went on: no difference in block one, three point seven points in block two, four point two in block three. Relief here is a difference score. So about a third of that twelve-point effect is the baseline drifting upward rather than the treated condition coming down, and the authors name it themselves as a perceptual contrast effect. With only two order arms and no no-history control they are explicit that they cannot say whether one group had a placebo effect, the other a nocebo effect, or both. No drug, no physiology, no preregistration. Worth carrying into any analgesic trial with a within-session baseline.

Eleventh and last, oxytocin and opioid demand, from Florida. Rats self-administering intravenous oxycodone with price raised across sessions until they quit, fitted to the exponential demand equation. Daily systemic oxytocin at fifteen international units per kilogram increased demand elasticity in males and did nothing in females. Photometry with a dopamine sensor in accumbens core gives the awkward result, and I mean that as a compliment: acute oxytocin increased oxycodone-evoked dopamine in both sexes. Dopamine amplification tracks neither the behaviour nor the sex difference. Three things cap this at a minute. No receptor pharmacology at all — no oxytocin receptor antagonist, no vasopressin-1a control, at a systemic dose where cross-reactivity is not excluded. No natural-reward comparator, so general behavioural suppression is not ruled out. And the demand intercept is computed in the methods and never reported for the treatment effect, so we cannot tell whether motivation changed or just intake — while the sex dissociation rests on separate within-sex tests with no interaction term, in a design where dosing began at a different price point for each sex because the male effect was found first.

That is eleven. The lead was a grey-matter psychosis-risk classifier that lands at zero point five six three externally, loses to the base rate on Brier score, and adds fourteen thousandths of discrimination to a symptom-only model. Four readers anchoring ninety-seven percent of their revisions onto a model, concentrated where it is wrong half the time, against a reference made of their own earlier votes. Eighty-three percent of depression-assessment cells with no sufficient evidence in the transcript, judged by a panel with the independence of one and a half raters, and a multimodal pipeline that buys one conversation out of thirty-three. A perturbation leaderboard where the train-mean baseline is the origin of the axis. An organoid foundation model with three receptor antagonists in its methods and no pharmacology in its results. Ketamine dilating a monkey's sense of elapsed time while the one measure tied to behaviour comes back null. A GPCR agonist that is a mistake the enzyme makes, validated by knockout and quantified nowhere. A cytokine with six times the reach of interleukin-6 trans-signalling at a tenth the dose and no blockade in any animal. A twelve-point analgesic order effect, a third of it the untreated baseline drifting up. And oxytocin raising demand elasticity in male rats while raising evoked dopamine in both sexes.
