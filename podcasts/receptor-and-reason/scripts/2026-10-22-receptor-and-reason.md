## Script

Welcome to Receptor and Reason for Thursday, October twenty-second. Eleven items.

The thread today is the label on a number versus the recipe behind it. Yesterday was reference standards; today is one level further in — what is actually inside the variable you just named. A polygenic score called GABAergic, a receptor map called histaminergic, a fidelity score called competence, a citation called evidence. In every case the name makes a promise, and in every case you have to read the construction to find out whether the promise is in there. Several of today's papers check their own recipe and publish what they find, which is why they are worth your time.

We open with the one most directly in your lane. A group at Adelaide, with the ISPC and PGRN-AMPS consortia, asks whether polygenic scores restricted to neurotransmitter gene sets predict SSRI outcome better than a genome-wide score. Thirteen hundred and ninety-four patients across two pooled pharmacogenomic cohorts — seven sites spanning the United States, Germany, Thailand, Taiwan and Japan on citalopram or escitalopram, plus a Mayo cohort on fluoxetine, citalopram, escitalopram or paroxetine. Response is a fifty percent drop at four weeks; six hundred and twenty-two responders, three hundred and fifty-four remitters.

The headline is that the GABAergic score associates with response at an odds ratio of one point four five per standard deviation, interval one point one four to one point eight five, against nulls for serotonin, dopamine and glutamate. Top versus bottom decile, two point five nine.

Now the recipe. The weights do not come from any GWAS of GABAergic biology. They come from a five-fold cross-validated GWAS of SSRI response in these same fourteen hundred people, then masked down to the seventy-six GABA genes. So the construct is not a GABA score. It is an SSRI-response score wearing a GABA mask, and the direction of effect — higher score, better response — is true by construction. It cannot tell you whether more or less GABAergic tone helps, which means the discussion's whole arc through magnetic resonance spectroscopy GABA deficits, allopregnanolone, brexanolone and zuranolone is decoration. No GABA was measured anywhere in this study.

Two more things. The pathway score buys one and a quarter percent of Nagelkerke pseudo-R-squared against zero point eight four for the genome-wide score — four tenths of a point, no formal comparison, and the authors say plainly that this does not establish greater predictive value. And in the ancestry sensitivity analysis the effect lives entirely in the Asian-ancestry participants, in a pooled mixed-continental sample controlled by five principal components with a European linkage-disequilibrium reference panel. That is the signature of a score tagging cohort, not neurotransmitter.

Second, a paper that takes a variable the field treats as pure methodological nuisance and asks whether it is a disease variable. You know rs6971 as the polymorphism that sorts people into high-, mixed- and low-affinity TSPO binders, and you know it as the thing you covary out, or screen on, before a TSPO PET study. A group at Alabama Sanger-sequenced eighty-six women enrolled in a fluorine-eighteen DPA-714 study — thirty fibromyalgia, twenty-one ME/CFS, eleven multiple sclerosis, twenty-four healthy controls — and report that controls were seventy-nine percent high-affinity binders against roughly forty-three to forty-eight percent in each patient group. Omnibus Fisher p equals zero point zero four nine; per-group p values of zero point zero three, zero point zero four five and zero point zero two seven.

If that held, it would be a design problem rather than a covariate problem for the entire TSPO neuroinflammation literature in these conditions — excluding low-affinity binders would preferentially delete patients, and adjusting for genotype would regress out variance that tracks case status, including in these authors' own parent study.

I do not think it holds as stated, and the reason is the direction of the anomaly. Published high-affinity rates run about forty-six percent European, sixty-seven percent African, ninety-seven percent East Asian. The patients here, at forty-seven percent, sit on the European rate. The controls, at seventy-nine, sit above every population listed. It is the control arm that is strange, in a sample where ancestry is self-reported race and no genome-wide data exists, so no principal components are computable. Bonferroni over three conditions would fail all three. And the fragility is two — flip two of twenty-four controls and the pooled result dissolves.

But invert it and there is a real finding. These controls were screened to daily pain at or below one and fatigue at or below two. If that screen enriches high-affinity binders, then the problem TSPO PET has is control selection, and that claim is both defensible and testable.

Third, staying on receptor maps, a reanalysis asking whether the structural brain signature of dyslexia liability aligns with any neuromodulator system. No new imaging: released voxel-wise morphometry maps regressed against a dyslexia polygenic score in up to thirty-five thousand UK Biobank adults, correlated across two hundred and eighteen regions against the Hansen PET atlas — nineteen tracers, real binding maps, not gene-expression proxies.

Three systems survive: histamine H3 at minus zero point six two to minus zero point four eight, vesicular acetylcholine transporter, and a putative NMDA signal, all at q below zero point zero five across three spatial null models.

Then the authors run the test almost nobody runs. Receptor maps are heavily collinear — the first principal component carries thirty-eight percent of receptor variance and itself correlates with liability. Remove each map's leave-one-out common axis and two of fifty-seven tests survive. Remove the common axis and nine geometric position terms together and zero receptors are significant in any cell. A Williams test comparing H3 against the cholinergic map gives t of minus one point eight nine at n of two eighteen — and minus zero point seven seven at the effective n of thirty-nine that their own surrogate nulls imply. So the three systems are not distinct from each other or from the shared neuromodulatory gradient, and replication in children and in ABCD fails outright.

Worth adding that H3 is the thinnest tracer in the atlas: eight participants. And the most interesting result is one they could have buried. In the per-child analysis, deviation along the H3 axis predicted reading at minus three point four one points per standard deviation — significant in the direction opposite to the one they prespecified, and they report the failure rather than reframing it. Their own Discussion says the data support no pharmacological inference. That line belongs in the title.

Fourth, deep brain stimulation for OCD, and here the number you need first is the sample size: three patients. Each had a twelve-day inpatient stereo-EEG mapping stage across capsule, nucleus accumbens, ventral pallidum, subthalamic and cingulate targets, then explantation, then a four-lead system placed at personalized targets chosen from that map. Two responded — fifty-five percent Y-BOCS reduction at sixty and thirty-two weeks — and one did not.

The predictor is not a normative connectome. It is invasive effective connectivity: single-pulse evoked potentials measured during the mapping stage, from the sites that actually relieved symptoms to the prefrontal regions carrying a gamma symptom-state biomarker. Accumbens-to-orbitofrontal connectivity correlates with acute response at R-squared of zero point nine zero.

Read the recipe. The subnetwork, the connection and the outcome variable were all chosen from the same six hemispheres the cross-validation then refits — the methods call these influence diagnostics rather than validation, which is the honest name. The exact permutation p of zero point zero zero one four is one over seven hundred and twenty, the floor. The long-term result has no statistic at all, just a rank order. And the leads were implanted in the hemisphere with the strongest connectivity, so chronic outcome was obtained under a treatment allocated by the predictor under test.

What survives all that is an internal negative control, and it is genuinely sharp. In the same six hemispheres, conventional patient-specific probabilistic tractography did not track response, while an axonal-activation model over a curated capsule connectome did, at R-squared zero point seven one with a permutation p of zero point zero zero seven. That is a falsifiable claim about why capsule connectomics has been inconsistent — it is which fibers the pulse recruits, not how much structural connectivity is there — and it is testable in the cohorts this paper does not have.

Fifth, something wet, and clinically load-bearing for anyone who prescribes lithium. Lithium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus is the constraint that ends lithium courses, and the standard account is loss of aquaporin-2 in collecting duct principal cells. A Paris group fed mice lithium chloride for twenty-eight days and ran spatial transcriptomics across the kidney. GDF15 — the stress cytokine you will know from cachexia and from plasma proteomics of aging — comes out as the third-most-upregulated gene in principal cells, and both GDF15 and the stress transcription factor ATF3 trace a corticopapillary gradient that mirrors measured tissue lithium. It is vasopressin-receptor independent: neither tolvaptan nor a selective peptide V2 antagonist prevented induction, and tolvaptan modestly increased it.

The causal arm is a global GDF15 knockout, and it is prevention-only. Those mice have less polyuria, higher urine osmolality, and a cleaner structural endpoint than most of this literature offers — microcysts in eleven of twenty-three lithium-treated wild-types and zero of eleven knockouts.

The finding I would hold onto is a dissociation. Knockouts concentrate urine better while aquaporin-2 fluorescence per principal cell stays equally low in both genotypes. The benefit comes from a shift in collecting-duct cell composition toward principal cells, not from restoring the water channel. That is a real challenge to the aquaporin-centric account.

Two cautions. The paper never prints a serum lithium concentration anywhere in the text — therapeutic range is asserted against a supplementary panel and a citation, and for a prescribing-relevance story that is the one number you most want. And a constitutive knockout of a systemic anorexigenic hormone, with non-littermate controls from a separate colony, in a model where the drug is delivered in food, is a confound you have to say out loud. The near-term pitch here is biomarker, not drug.

Sixth, the one unambiguous potency win of the week, and it comes from formulation rather than medicinal chemistry. A VCU and UT Austin collaboration takes KT109 — the covalent DAGL-beta inhibitor that has carried most of the antinociceptive literature for this enzyme — and does nothing to the molecule. They rebuild the liposome — distearoyl phosphatidylcholine, PEGylated lipid and cholesterol, with cholesterol replacing the DOPE of their first-generation formulation. Encapsulation efficiency goes from zero point four percent to roughly forty, and stability to a hundred and twenty days.

In paclitaxel-induced neuropathy, free drug has an ED-fifty of thirty-one milligrams per kilogram. The liposome is at zero point two two six — the paper calls it a hundred and sixty-four fold, though the quotient of the two reported values is a hundred and thirty-seven. Full reversal at four tenths of a milligram per kilogram, lasting twenty-four hours from one injection, undiminished after seven daily doses. Competitive activity-based protein profiling in macrophages shows the formulation spares two known KT109 off-targets and reduces FAAH engagement while keeping DAGL-beta.

Now the recipe problem. The paper demonstrates target engagement in no in vivo tissue at all — brain negative, spleen negative, dorsal root ganglion never assayed, peritoneal macrophages never assayed despite intraperitoneal dosing. No pharmacokinetics, no biodistribution, no lipidomics: two-AG, the product of the enzyme being inhibited, is never measured in any tissue, by a lab that can measure it. And no phospho-AMPK anywhere, so the title's "mediated by AMPK" rests on a single dose of dorsomorphin, the least selective AMPK tool there is, with no activator rescue and no genetic arm in this paper. The authors' own Results heading says AMPK plays a role, which is the defensible version.

Everything between the dose and the paw withdrawal threshold is inference. But the dose-response is real, and a formulation that turns a tool compound into something with drug-product stability earns the follow-up experiments.

Seventh, and back to construct labels. A Dartmouth-led group scored psychotherapy fidelity with open-weight language models on something the field has mostly lacked: three hundred and eighteen real, full-length, in-person CBT-for-psychosis sessions from forty-five participants with serious mental illness, transcribed locally so no protected health information leaves the building. The instrument is the sixty-item Cognitive Therapy Scale for Psychosis, collapsed to binary adherence.

The best configuration — a nine-billion-parameter model with evidence extraction before judgment and the rubric split into ten passes — reaches Cohen's kappa of zero point four six against a human-human ceiling of zero point seven one. The lever is inference structure, not scale: that decomposition takes the same model from balanced accuracy zero point six one to zero point seven two, and does nothing for a different model.

The item table is the beat. Social skills training, balanced accuracy zero point eight seven. Homework, zero point seven one. Then collaboration at zero point five three, feedback at zero point five four, guided discovery at zero point five five — six of ten category intervals cross zero. The model is strong on items you could nearly regular-expression and at chance on the competence constructs that actually predict outcome. Note the rubric contains an item about whether the therapist's tone of voice was empathetic, scored from a transcript with prosody discarded.

So the thing being measured is protocol adherence, not therapeutic skill, and the external validation shows it: the CBT-versus-non-CBT separation is carried by whether a worksheet got assigned. Anyone planning to use an automated fidelity score as a trial quality gate or a supervision signal is buying a checklist auditor and should not price it as a rater. One caveat on the headline — the thirty expert-rated sessions were used both to calibrate across forty-five configurations and to report performance, so that kappa is a best-of-forty-five on n equals thirty.

Eighth, who should judge clinical generative AI. An Israeli group at Clalit benchmarks a closed commercial scoring model against twelve language-model judges across four tasks — a hundred and eighteen Hebrew pediatric decision-support answers rated by five physicians, HealthBench, MedHELM, and HealthBench Professional.

The result everyone will quote is cost: twenty-two to eighty-nine dollars per million judgments against four to twenty-one thousand for frontier judges, with median latency of about half a second against seven to fifty-seven times that. The result I would actually carry is different. Frontier models were the worst judges in both tasks that have physician labels. GPT-six scored agreement of zero point three eight against physicians while Llama four, GPT-four-o and GLM-five-point-three reached zero point six zero to zero point eight one. The mechanism the authors surface is that frontier models judged far fewer criteria met than physicians did — forty-six and forty-one percent against seventy-seven. So pick an evaluation judge by its measured agreement with clinicians, not by its position on a capability leaderboard.

Two corrections to the framing. The cost-matched open-weight baseline, at one point one times the price, agrees better with physicians than the commercial product does — the seventy-to-two-hundred-fold savings are measured only against frontier judges, so this is a real-time-gating result, not an economics one. And the product detected thirty-one percent of the answers physicians failed on safety; routing that one rubric to a frontier model raised sensitivity to about half. A judge that misses two thirds of unsafe answers is not a patient-safety monitor at any price. Note also that the vendor's architecture and training data are undisclosed while three of four benchmarks are public files whose labels sit on GitHub.

Ninth, a five-page pilot with one number in it that you should remember. Ask a model to verify a biomedical claim closed-book and cite PubMed IDs. Across two hundred claims, it emitted three hundred and thirty-five identifiers; three hundred and twenty-seven of them resolve to a real PubMed record. A cross-family judge rated three of those three hundred and twenty-seven as relevant to the claim. The examples are the kind you will quote at a lab meeting: a claim about tumour necrosis factor and interleukin regulation cited a paper titled "The proper eyeglasses for children."

The mechanism matters. An identifier drawn from parametric memory lands in a dense, sequentially assigned namespace, so it dereferences to something. An existence check is not a grounding check — and PMID existence is exactly the check most citation validators implement.

Give the model search and citation relevance goes from three in three hundred and twenty-seven to about two thirds, essentially for free, because citations are constrained to retrieved context. But be careful with the rest: none of the accuracy differences between realistic harnesses reach significance at a hundred claims, and the retrieval-only baseline is a strawman with six percent gold recall. The second-order finding is the better one anyway — grounding degraded abstention. Agents correctly said "not enough information" on thirty-three to forty-two percent of claims where the single-shot system managed seventy-two to eighty-one. Finding something makes the model commit.

Tenth, the same lesson in structural biology. ProteinGeoBench puts four frontier agents on a hundred and thirty-one tasks and separates retrieval from computation. The best model averages zero point nine zero seven on lookup and zero point seven one six on numeric geometry; another is at minus zero point two one nine. Fluent at lookup, lost in geometry — except the fourth model inverts it, so this is three of four, not a law about agents.

The part worth your attention is the grading. The verifier pays zero point one for any non-empty answer, and ten point two percent of four thousand two hundred and forty-nine rollouts scored exactly that — on two tasks, every model on every run. And of the trials earning no accuracy credit on tasks where the submitted number could be recovered, eight of fifty-five submitted the exact negative of the reference value — on one dihedral task, six wrote plus forty-two point nine where the reference is minus forty-two point nine, across four models from three providers. The atom order is named explicitly in the prompt, so that is a handedness convention error, shared. The concept-task judge panel agreed at a Krippendorff alpha of zero point one zero five. There is no non-LLM baseline anywhere, and that absence is not in the limitations. The authors do withdraw four effects that vanished once both arms were graded in the same pass, which is more than most benchmark papers manage. And at one run per task, twenty-four percent of bootstrap replicates name a different top model.

Last, the lightest item, and the one that names today's thread explicitly. A single author in Athens proposes LOGOS, which is not an algorithm or a model but a comparison protocol for dosage regimen design. You define nested regimen classes under one fixed objective and one resource rule — a reference schedule, then adding amount choices, then timing, then infusion structure — and the grammar on top says a feasible candidate in the larger class establishes only existence, while a strict class gain requires beating a valid upper bound on the smaller one. The target is a familiar failure: a paper reports that adaptive dosing beat a comparator, but the comparator had a different budget or more monitoring, so the gain is unattributable.

Be clear about what this is. No clinical validation, no real pharmacokinetic data, zero drugs, zero patients — the author says so repeatedly. Everything is dimensionless analytical examples, and the headline demonstration shows all that extra machinery is worth about one percent.

But one result cuts against a pharmacometric reflex. Jensen's inequality says concave benefit favors smoothing, so flat exposure wins — proved here for the periodic case. Over a finite course with a terminal horizon, the certified optimum is load, maintain, then coast with no input at all. Flat is not optimal when the clock stops. And the companion example is the honest one: after stopping the drug, injury peaked about twenty-five times later than the post-stop check. Stopping is not the same as safe.

Treat it as a reviewer's checklist for model-informed precision dosing claims, and do not let the word framework imply it has ever touched a drug.

That is the hour. Read the recipe, not the label.
