# EvoTrace — an agent that withholds its own results until the evidence chain checks out

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.25.769078v1

## Script

Most agentic science papers ask whether the agent got the right answer. Today's preprint, posted to bioRxiv on Wednesday from the Suzhou Institute of Systems Medicine at the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, asks a different question: can anyone else check? The system is called EvoTrace, and it does end-to-end viral molecular evolution — quality control, phylogenetics, molecular clock dating, hypothesis testing. The framing is that trust in an automated analysis should rest not on how capable the generating model is, and emphatically not on the model's own self-evaluation, but on whether every claim can be traced back to primary evidence by someone who wasn't involved in producing it.

That turns into four design commitments. Ground the agent's judgment in real domain knowledge. Bound every computation to an explicit applicability domain. Bind each reported claim to the files and parameters that produced it. And verify with agents that are outside the generating context entirely.

The knowledge layer is the part I'd steal first. Rather than writing a checklist themselves, they mined historical peer reviews — the place where the tacit standards actually live, the objections reviewers raise about when evidence doesn't support a claim. Out of that came thirteen judgment principles and two hundred thirty-six specific analytical pitfalls. The scaling behavior is informative. Feeding in forty incremental batches of pre-2025 reviews, the high-level principles all emerged in the first batch and then stayed put; the specific pitfalls climbed from thirty-three to about two hundred twenty before plateauing. Principles saturate fast, pitfalls take a corpus. They then froze the 2024 version of the principles and tested them on held-out 2025 and 2026 material, and the frozen principles still helped models catch methodological flaws in newer papers — significantly for DeepSeek, directionally but not significantly for Kimi. That model dependence is a real caveat and they say so: the same distilled rules buy different amounts for different backbones.

Execution is decomposed into fifty composable analytical units, forty-seven of them carrying tiered validation evidence — unit tests at the low end, planted defects, simulated ground truths and official reference builds at the high end. Each unit logs its preconditions and refuses out-of-bounds data instead of producing a number anyway. On six Nextstrain virus datasets the trees land close to the official references, and in simulations the system detected all ten classes of planted pipeline defects with no false positives on clean controls.

Then the gate most agent systems don't have. A claim-binding agent takes every assertion in the report and maps it to inputs, intermediate artifacts, parameters and logs, and the system distinguishes "results generated" from "evidence sufficient to deliver". Across two hundred thirty-three analyses and nearly thirteen hundred reported claims, about twelve hundred had complete evidence chains; twenty-six analyses were flagged as having gaps, pinpointed to the exact missing file. And here's the detail I find most telling: every single one of those two hundred thirty-three analyses was held unsigned, pending human approval. The default state of this system is undelivered.

The audit itself is split into two blinded channels: a numerical agent that rebuilds summary tables from raw artifacts, and a figure agent that reverse-maps what's drawn back to the underlying arrays. Both are deliberately outside the generating context, because when planning, execution and self-checking share one window, the check inherits the assumptions it's supposed to test. Across blinded defect injections the two channels caught and localized every one of a hundred sixty-two defective replicates, and matched the preset severity rating every time.

They also bounded their own sensitivity, which is rare and worth noting. Injecting numerical drift of varying size into reported values, detection is total once the drift reaches one part in ten thousand, but below that it blurs into four-decimal rounding noise and gets caught only sometimes. So the audit certifies against rounding-scale error, not arbitrary precision — and knowing where it stops is part of what makes it usable.

Now the part that earns the paper its title. They ran the audit on their own figures. A hundred thirty thousand checks, and it came back with seventeen defects — ten minor, four major, three blockers — label-definition mismatches, missing replication evidence, hash-manifest mismatches. They fixed them, re-audited, and open defects went to zero. In the virus case study, Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever, the audit blocked a claim of "no reassortment candidates" and forced it to "no high-confidence candidates", because low-confidence Turkish candidates existed and the phrasing erased them. That is the system narrowing a conclusion its own generative side wanted to state cleanly.

Head to head against the generalist agent Biomni and against an unconstrained model, with the same backbone and the same compute budget, EvoTrace's biggest win isn't accuracy. It's variance. Across random seeds, its estimates of the most recent common ancestor varied by under three years; the baselines ranged up to thirty. It was also about a quarter faster, and its one unfinished run was deliberately halted by a quality gate rather than running out the clock.

Which brings me to the sentence in the discussion I'd put on a poster. Execution consistency does not equal inferential accuracy — workflow stability can mask systematic bias. They are explicit that passing the audit is not the same as being right: the framework certifies computational provenance, not biological truth, and it leaves semantic and domain-level misinterpretation to human review. A tightly constrained agent that gives you the same answer every time is giving you a reproducible answer, which is a prerequisite for a correct one and absolutely not a substitute. Most of what's shipping right now can't even claim the first.
