The biggest item is a T-cell engager transaction that tells you where the myeloma field thinks its real problem is, and that is where the spotlight goes. Eight items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight. G-S-K has acquired global rights to a preclinical trispecific T-cell engager from Chimagen Biosciences for an undisclosed upfront payment, with development and commercial milestones taking the total to seven hundred fifty million dollars. The molecule binds C-D-three on T cells and two separate tumor antigens on the myeloma cell, and it is expected to enter Phase one in twenty twenty-seven. This is the second asset G-S-K has bought from the same Chinese antibody shop: in October twenty twenty-four it paid three hundred million dollars upfront plus up to five hundred fifty million in milestones for C-M-G-one-A-forty-six, a trispecific that binds C-D-nineteen and C-D-twenty on B cells with high affinity and C-D-three with low affinity, which G-S-K took into lupus and lupus nephritis. The same architecture is showing up again, and the reason it keeps showing up is the interesting part. Hesham Abdullah, who runs global oncology at G-S-K, framed it as complementing the existing myeloma portfolio — which is Blenrep, belantamab mafodotin, the B-C-M-A antibody-drug conjugate that was pulled from the U-S market in twenty twenty-three and came back in October twenty twenty-five on the DREAMM-seven combination data. Separately, and in the other direction, G-S-K is cutting six hundred fifty jobs as it closes a vaccine manufacturing site in Germany.

Second, a regulatory first that matters more than the drug does. The F-D-A approved Curium's Bexlutry, lutetium-one-seventy-seven dotatate injection, for adults with somatostatin-receptor-positive gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumors. What makes it notable is the pathway: this is the first radioligand equivalent ever approved, cleared under five-oh-five-b-two against Novartis's Lutathera, on published evidence plus targeted bridging data showing a comparable biological and chemical profile rather than a fresh outcomes trial. Radiopharmaceuticals have been treated as effectively un-genericizable because the product decays on the way to the patient and the barrier is logistics, not chemistry. Curium's pitch is exactly that: Mike Patterson, the North American chief executive, is selling reliable supply, consistent delivery and predictable scheduling for sites of care, on the back of four manufacturing sites and full vertical integration in lutetium. Read this as the opening of a second front in radiopharma economics — if a five-oh-five-b-two can take share from Lutathera on delivery reliability, the same logic eventually points at Pluvicto.

Third, portfolio surgery at Sanofi. Sanofi and Cheplapharm are creating a strategic partnership under which Cheplapharm takes over twenty mature medicines and three manufacturing sites — Csanyikvolgy in Hungary with about four hundred employees, Jurong in Singapore with about a hundred, and Ploermel in France with about sixty-five. Lovenox, enoxaparin, is in the package. Sanofi is not taking cash; it is taking a twenty-six point four percent equity stake in Cheplapharm, building on a relationship that started in twenty fourteen. Commercial transfer begins in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven and the whole thing should close by the third quarter. This is the new chief executive putting the innovation-versus-legacy trade in writing, and the equity structure is the tell — Sanofi wants the tail economics without the tail operating burden.

Fourth, out of Seoul, a checkpoint result worth flagging. BioNTech and OncoC4 presented updated stage-one data from PRESERVE-oh-oh-three, the global Phase three of gotistobart — a C-T-L-A-four antibody engineered to preferentially deplete regulatory T cells inside the tumor microenvironment rather than block the receptor systemically — against docetaxel in previously treated metastatic squamous non-small cell lung cancer, in patients who had already progressed on a checkpoint inhibitor and platinum chemotherapy. Median overall survival was eighteen point five months against ten point zero for chemotherapy, with what the companies describe as a manageable safety profile. Nearly doubling survival post-checkpoint is a serious number, and the design point is the one to hold onto: the C-T-L-A-four class has always been limited by toxicity, and the engineering thesis here is to get the regulatory-T-cell depletion without the systemic immune cost. Stage two is enrolling globally.

Fifth, the cystic fibrosis story reaches its conclusion. Sionna Therapeutics is cutting forty-six percent of its staff and discontinuing every program except the dual combination of its two remaining correctors, which will go into a Phase two-a proof-of-concept study. Chief Business Officer Caroline Stark Beer departs this week, and the Chief Medical Officer picks up R and D on top of her existing job. You will recall the August failure: Sionna's differentiated pitch was stabilizing the N-B-D-one folding domain of the C-F-T-R protein, a domain Vertex's correctors do not address, and layering that on top of Trikafta. The monotherapy readout moved the needle one point where analysts wanted ten, and the stock fell about ninety-two percent, below the company's own cash. With two hundred sixty-eight million dollars on hand as of June thirtieth and runway into twenty twenty-eight, the company is buying itself one more shot on the combination rather than winding down. That is the right call financially and a long shot scientifically.

Sixth, the I-P-O window. Electra Therapeutics set terms for its Nasdaq debut: twenty-one point seven million shares at fourteen to sixteen dollars, roughly three hundred twenty-five million dollars at the midpoint, for a fully diluted value just under a billion. That is a large raise for this market. Electra is the pan-S-I-R-P antibody company — the lead, ipsoprubart, selectively depletes pathological myeloid cells and T cells, and it is in the registrational Phase two-three SURPASS trial in secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, a hyperinflammatory syndrome with no approved therapy in that setting. The Phase one-b number that is powering the story is a hundred percent eight-week overall survival and a hundred percent response rate in twelve frontline malignancy-associated patients — small, uncontrolled, and still striking in a disease that kills most patients within weeks. Sanofi is an investor and the company had raised a hundred eighty-three million dollars privately. Proceeds fund SURPASS, an oncology expansion and a second molecule. Worth noting the contrast with the C-D-forty-seven era: the myeloid checkpoint field spent years and billions trying to block the don't-eat-me signal and largely failed. Electra is doing the opposite thing with the same axis — depleting the pathological myeloid compartment outright.

Seventh, on the policy side, the F-D-A opened applications for Operation TrialBlazer, the expedited investigational new drug pilot it announced in June. The agency will select eight to ten pairs of drug developers and qualified research institutions — academic medical centers or contract research organizations with specialized scientific expertise — and allow rolling review of I-N-D components during the pre-I-N-D phase instead of waiting on a complete submission. Acting Commissioner Kyle Diamantas described it as a test of whether the partnership can accelerate what happens after a trial is allowed to proceed, and C-B-E-R director Karim Mikhail framed the goal as resolving issues earlier and reducing the risk of clinical holds. The stated target is shaving up to twelve months off the path from identified molecule to first-in-human. The subtext is reshoring — this is the agency's answer to early-phase work migrating to Australia and China. Applications close October thirtieth, which is a tight window if you want in.

Eighth, and on the same day the F-D-A held its public hearing on the therapeutic use of psychedelics. Definium Therapeutics reported positive topline results from Panorama, a Phase three of D-T-one-twenty — an orally disintegrating tablet formulation of lysergide — in generalized anxiety disorder. Two hundred forty-five patients across thirty-two centers; the hundred-microgram arm improved nine point eight points on the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale from baseline against four point seven on placebo, a placebo-adjusted five point one points at week twelve, p less than zero point zero zero zero one, effect size zero point six four. Discontinuation ran about ten percent in every arm, and the most common adverse event, in sixty-eight percent of treated patients, was illusion — which is a fair reminder that the dosing-day experience, not the efficacy, is what the regulatory framework still has to solve. That is the company's third positive Phase three for this molecule, after Emerge in major depression in June and Voyage in generalized anxiety in August. A pre-N-D-A meeting is set for the fourth quarter with a filing targeted in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. Meanwhile at White Oak, nearly a dozen federal officials and more than thirty public commenters worked through what a regulatory framework would actually look like, under the April executive order directing agencies to expedite this class. The new C-D-E-R director, Michael Davis, who spent years as chief medical officer at the Usona Institute, was in the room. Written comments stay open through October fifth.
