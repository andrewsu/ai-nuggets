Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Friday, October ninth, twenty twenty-six, covering Thursday. The lead and today's spotlight is argenx: an independent monitoring committee stopped its Phase three trial of Vyvgart in Sjögren's disease for futility, and the stock lost about fourteen percent, even though a celiac drug from its Forte acquisition won the same morning. Around it: Endeavor says its Hedgehog inhibitor improved lung function in pulmonary fibrosis, Viatris buys Pacira for one point six five billion dollars, Bristol's iberdomide more than doubles progression-free survival in myeloma, Newron discloses four deaths on its schizophrenia drug, and two biotechs price I-P-Os. Ten items, then the week ahead.

First, argenx. The company is ending UNITY, its six-hundred-thirty-patient Phase three study of subcutaneous efgartigimod in Sjögren's disease, after an interim look found it cannot meet its primary endpoint, change in systemic disease activity at week forty-eight. Efgartigimod blocks the neonatal Fc receptor, which recycles I-g-G antibodies, so it lowers circulating autoantibodies. The readout wasn't expected until twenty twenty-seven. William Blair said the stop removes the next potential blockbuster expansion opportunity for Vyvgart. The good news is FB102, an antibody against C-D-one-twenty-two, the receptor subunit shared by interleukin-two and interleukin-fifteen. It protected the gut lining in a hundred twenty-six celiac patients during an eight-week gluten challenge, and argenx is taking it into Phase three. The spotlight covers what the Sjögren's failure says about which diseases antibody-lowering drugs can treat.

Second, pulmonary fibrosis. Private Endeavor BioMedicines said its Phase two-b WHISTLE-PF trial of taladegib met its primary endpoint. Taladegib is an oral inhibitor of the Hedgehog pathway, a developmental signal that fibroblasts reuse in wound healing. In two hundred thirteen patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, three-quarters of them on standard therapy, taladegib improved percent predicted forced vital capacity versus placebo at twenty-four weeks. On treatment, lung function rose above baseline, and C-T measures of fibrosis and total lung capacity also improved. Current drugs only slow decline, so a drug that reverses it would be a first, and Endeavor says reversal is the claim. The company hasn't released the size of the effect, and Hedgehog inhibitors cause hair loss, muscle spasms and taste changes, which matter for a drug taken for years. Endeavor's twenty twenty-four Series C was one hundred thirty-two million dollars, led by an affiliate of Matrix Capital, with Fidelity, Perceptive and T. Rowe among the investors. It plans to talk to regulators about Phase three.

Third, M and A. Viatris will pay thirty-six fifty a share in cash, one point six five billion dollars in equity value, for Pacira BioSciences. Pacira sells Exparel, a long-acting liposomal form of the local anesthetic bupivacaine for pain after surgery, and Zilretta, an extended-release steroid injection for knee osteoarthritis. Together they brought in about seven hundred forty-six million dollars in revenue over the past year. The catch is patents: under settlements, Exparel generics can launch in early twenty thirty, and Zilretta's patents expire in twenty thirty-one. Leerink's Daniel Clark called the deal immediately accretive and said it fits Viatris' strategy of buying products already on the market. It also pairs with Viatris' fast-acting meloxicam, which has an F-D-A decision due December twenty-seventh.

Fourth, myeloma. Bristol Myers Squibb said Zenbexus, iberdomide, hit its second primary endpoint in the Phase three EXCALIBER trial. Iberdomide is a cereblon modulator, a next-generation relative of Revlimid that degrades the transcription factors Ikaros and Aiolos. Combined with daratumumab and dexamethasone, it produced a median progression-free survival of forty-two months versus twenty on a daratumumab, bortezomib and dexamethasone regimen, a fifty-one percent cut in the risk of progression or death. Zenbexus got accelerated approval in August on the trial's other endpoint, minimal residual disease, the first time that endpoint supported a myeloma approval. Now progression-free survival backs it up. Full results come at the hematology meeting in December.

Fifth, a safety story. Newron disclosed that four patients on evenamide have died across its studies, a rate of about half a percent, against one death on placebo. Evenamide is a sodium channel blocker that dampens excess glutamate release, tested as an add-on for treatment-resistant schizophrenia. Investigators consider three deaths unrelated, and one possibly related because two autopsies found no definitive cause. The F-D-A has kept U-S enrollment on hold since spring over concern that sodium channel blockers can cause heart rhythm problems. Newron says it has seen no pattern in more than ten thousand E-C-Gs, and enrollment continues outside the U-S.

Sixth, policy. PhRMA said the new user-fee deal sets aside F-D-A money that can only be spent on hiring and keeping staff, to rebuild reviewer numbers after this year's H-H-S layoffs. STAT reports that the one hundred percent Section two-thirty-two tariff on imported patented drugs took effect at the end of September, and that big drugmakers with most-favored-nation pricing deals don't owe anything until early twenty twenty-nine, which leaves smaller biotechs paying it and asking for guidance. And the U-K revoked the license for Valneva's chikungunya vaccine Ixchiq, citing earlier safety data rather than a new signal. The U-S suspended it last year.

Seventh, capital markets. TRexBio priced its Nasdaq I-P-O at fourteen dollars, the bottom of its range, raising about a hundred seventeen million dollars. It works on tissue regulatory T cells and is partnered with Lilly and Johnson and Johnson, with Lilly having indicated it would buy about thirteen million dollars of the offering. Retension Pharmaceuticals upsized its I-P-O to forty-five million dollars at twelve. Its RTN-zero-zero-one is a next-generation P-D-E-five inhibitor designed to reach the central arteries in uncontrolled hypertension. Both start trading today. In private money, Multiply Labs raised seventy-five million dollars for robotic cell and gene therapy manufacturing, led by Patrick Soon-Shiong, with AstraZeneca new on the cap table. And General Medicine raised a hundred twenty million dollars led by Andreessen Horowitz for an online health store it says A-I shopping agents can use as well as people.

Eighth, A-I and automation. Ginkgo Bioworks will give teams in the National Science Foundation's X-Labs, a one point five billion dollar, decade-long program for breakthrough science, remote access to its robotic lab in Boston, plus protein testing for designs that A-I models generate. The White House science adviser, Michael Kratsios, announced it. Coming a day after Biohub's one point eight billion dollar data initiative, it shows the federal A-I-for-science push now pays for measurement capacity, not just models.

Ninth, thyroid eye disease. Innovent's Sycume, an antibody against the I-G-F-one receptor, the same target as Amgen's Tepezza, met its Phase three endpoint in inactive disease, the burned-out stage that makes up about two-thirds of patients. In a hundred sixteen Chinese patients, sixty percent had a meaningful reduction in eye bulging at twenty-four weeks versus twenty-four percent on placebo. Sycume is already on China's national reimbursement list, and the data strengthen China's lower-cost version of a very expensive drug class.

Tenth, the quick ones. Biohaven licensed its I-g-G degrader BHV-thirteen-hundred and two follow-on molecules to Ono for Japan and much of Asia, with eighty million dollars up front, more on that in the spotlight. Caribou, which ended R and D on Tuesday, is now being described as a likely reverse-merger shell: by BioPharma Dive's math its enterprise value is about minus twenty million dollars, meaning it holds more cash than its market value, and Leerink's Daina Graybosch names a reverse merger or a buyout by Tang Capital as the likely endings. And in case you missed it on Wednesday, the Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for work on how one mirror-image form of a molecule comes to dominate, chemistry that underpins how drugmakers make single-enantiomer drugs.
