Yesterday was mostly about trial readouts, and pulmonary fibrosis had a bad day. Bristol Myers said its L-P-A-one antagonist admilparant won a Phase three in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, but only at the lower of two doses. Nine items, then the week ahead.

First, Bristol Myers had two Phase three readouts on the same morning. The first is admilparant, an oral drug that blocks lysophosphatidic acid receptor one, a lipid-signaling receptor that drives fibroblast recruitment and scarring in the lung. ALOFT-IPF enrolled about twelve hundred fifty patients, and the sixty milligram twice-daily dose met the primary endpoint, absolute change in forced vital capacity at fifty-two weeks. The hundred-twenty milligram dose slowed decline but did not reach significance. Bristol released no effect sizes. Leerink's David Risinger wrote that the data could be sufficient for approval, but that the lack of a dose response raises questions about the drug's profile. The spotlight covers what that means for the drug and for the L-P-A-one class. The second readout was cleaner. RYZ-one-oh-one is actinium-two-twenty-five attached to DOTATATE, the same somatostatin-receptor-targeting peptide used in Novartis's Lutathera. At an interim analysis it improved progression-free survival in gastroenteropancreatic neuroendocrine tumors that had progressed after lutetium-based therapy. Lutetium emits beta particles, and actinium emits alpha particles, which travel a very short distance and are far more potent. RayzeBio's chief medical officer says this is the first Phase three result for any alpha-emitting targeted radiopharmaceutical, in any indication. Bristol paid four point one billion dollars for San Diego's RayzeBio in twenty twenty-four, largely for this asset and the actinium supply chain behind it. There are no numbers yet, and overall survival is still being followed. Remember that Curium launched a generic Lutathera last Friday at a five percent discount. RYZ-one-oh-one is going after the patients who progress on that drug.

Second, the other half of the bad day for pulmonary fibrosis. Syndax said Monday night that MAXPIRe failed. In this Phase two, a hundred forty-five patients were randomized two to one to axatilimab, its antibody against the colony-stimulating factor one receptor, which depletes the monocyte-derived macrophages thought to sustain fibrosis, or to placebo. It did not slow the annualized decline in forced vital capacity. Safety was clean, and Syndax is dropping the indication. The rationale was lung improvement in chronic graft-versus-host disease, where the drug is approved as Niktimvo with Incyte. That signal did not carry over to idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

Third, First Tracks Biotherapeutics, the San Diego company AnaptysBio spun out in April, came through in celiac disease. A-N-B-zero-thirty-three is a subcutaneous antibody against C-D-one-twenty-two, the receptor chain shared by interleukin-two and interleukin-fifteen. In the first cohort of its Phase one-b, thirty-two patients got three loading doses and then a fourteen-day challenge of six grams of gluten a day, about two slices of bread. Villus-height-to-crypt-depth ratio fell zero point six six on placebo and only zero point one seven on drug. Intraepithelial lymphocyte density rose nearly nineteen points on placebo and stayed flat on drug. Symptoms separated from day one, and forty-seven percent of placebo patients needed rescue medication against six percent on drug. There were no serious adverse events. Leerink's Risinger called it "impressive" and wrote that he sees "pipeline-in-a-product potential." There are now three positive celiac programs on this axis: this one, argenx's F-B-one-oh-two against the same target, and Teva's interleukin-fifteen antibody. First Tracks plans late-stage development in twenty twenty-seven, eosinophilic esophagitis data in the third quarter of twenty twenty-seven, and a vitiligo Phase two.

Fourth, Mirum's EXPAND Phase three of Livmarli also hit. Livmarli is maralixibat, an inhibitor of the ileal bile acid transporter, which blocks bile acid reabsorption in the gut so the body's total bile acid load falls. The trial enrolled fifty-two patients with ultra-rare cholestatic liver diseases, including biliary atresia. Itch scores improved by a placebo-adjusted zero point nine eight points on a four-point scale, and serum bile acids fell sixty-nine percent relative to placebo. The biliary atresia subgroup was significant on its own, with a p-value of zero point zero zero zero two. That subgroup matters, because biliary atresia is the most common reason children need liver transplants, and it would take the drug well beyond Alagille syndrome and P-F-I-C. Mirum plans the supplemental filing in the first half of twenty twenty-seven.

Fifth, the biggest schizophrenia dataset of the weekend came from a private company. Tortugas Neuroscience, which launched in April with a hundred six million dollars led by Cure Ventures, with The Column Group and A-N Venture Partners co-leading the Series A, presented Phase two-b data at the European neuropsychopharmacology congress for obuprazine. The drug is a partial agonist at dopamine D-two and D-three receptors and at serotonin one-A, and an antagonist at serotonin two-A. That's the same broad family as aripiprazole and cariprazine. Partner Hansoh Pharma ran the trial in three hundred sixty-three hospitalized Chinese adults. The lowest dose produced a sixteen point seven point placebo-adjusted reduction on the PANSS total score at day forty-two, and the higher doses gave thirteen point two and fourteen point seven. Two cautions. The lowest dose was the best, so there's no dose response. And it was only numerically better than the aripiprazole reference arm. Placebo-adjusted effects in acute inpatient trials run outside the U-S tend to be larger than in American trials, so the real test is the U-S I-N-D Tortugas plans to file.

Sixth, Amgen and AstraZeneca put numbers on the eosinophilic esophagitis win for Tezspire, their antibody against T-S-L-P, the alarmin released by stressed epithelium. In the CROSSING Phase three, nearly seventy percent of patients on the high dose were in histologic remission at twenty-four weeks, against three point five percent on placebo. Cross-trial comparisons are risky, but that's above what Sanofi and Regeneron's Dupixent showed, and Dupixent is the only approved biologic in the disease. William Blair's Matt Phipps called it "best-in-disease" and expects "another blockbuster indication." Full data come Wednesday. This matters beyond Tezspire, because T-S-L-P is one of the most crowded antibody targets in immunology right now.

Seventh, Cytokinetics presented more data at the heart failure meeting from ACACIA-HCM, the trial of Myqorzo, its cardiac myosin inhibitor, in the non-obstructive form of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Bristol's Camzyos failed in that population last year. Fifty-three percent of patients on drug responded in three or more clinical domains, against thirteen percent on placebo. The cost is systolic function. Ejection fraction fell below fifty percent in ten and a half percent of treated patients versus under one percent on placebo, and heart failure serious adverse events ran four point seven versus one point two percent, all during titration. Cytokinetics files the supplemental application this quarter. Non-obstructive disease is the bigger half of the market, and the label will hinge on how the F-D-A wants titration monitored.

Eighth, on the A-I file. China's regulator cleared an I-N-D for C-S-four-three-one-five-eight from Shenzhen Chipscreen, the company behind the H-D-A-C inhibitor chidamide. It's an oral multikinase inhibitor for acute myeloid leukemia. It hits F-L-T-three, including the I-T-D and kinase-domain mutants, along with the bypass signaling pathways from the bone marrow microenvironment that drive resistance to current F-L-T-three drugs. Chipscreen says the compound came from its A-I-enabled chemical genomics platform, which it built on two decades of gene-expression profiling of compound effects. The company discloses nothing about the model. A deliberately broad kinase profile is the opposite of the selectivity most A-I chemistry programs optimize for, and selectivity is what decides tolerability in A-M-L, often in combination with venetoclax, which Chipscreen says the compound synergizes with.
