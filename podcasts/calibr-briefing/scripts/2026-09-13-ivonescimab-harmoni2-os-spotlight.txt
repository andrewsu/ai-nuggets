Today's spotlight is ivonescimab, and the overall survival data from HARMONi-two that Akeso released into the World Conference on Lung Cancer overnight. I want to spend the time on three things: what the number actually is, why the squamous data may matter more than the headline, and what this does and does not settle about a class of drugs that four large pharma companies have collectively spent north of twenty billion dollars buying into.

Start with the molecule, because the mechanism is the whole argument. Ivonescimab is a bispecific antibody with two binding jobs — programmed death one on T cells, and vascular endothelial growth factor A in the tumor stroma. It came out of Akeso's tetravalent antibody platform, so it carries two arms against each target rather than one. That architecture is not decoration. V-E-G-F is abundant inside tumors and scarce in normal tissue, so when the molecule binds V-E-G-F it becomes locally concentrated, and having a second P-D-one arm means that local concentration converts into higher avidity for P-D-one right where the tumor is. The design intent is a drug that behaves like a checkpoint inhibitor plus an anti-angiogenic in tumor tissue and like neither of them very much anywhere else. That is the theory. Until this weekend, the evidence for it was progression-free survival.

Now the number. HARMONi-two randomized three hundred ninety-eight previously untreated patients at fifty-five hospitals in China, all with P-D-L-one tumor proportion score of one percent or higher and no E-G-F-R or A-L-K alterations, to ivonescimab at twenty milligrams per kilogram or pembrolizumab at two hundred milligrams, every three weeks. At the August twentieth data cut, with thirty-six months of median follow-up and two hundred thirty-four deaths, median overall survival was thirty point eight months on ivonescimab against twenty-two point six on Keytruda. Hazard ratio zero point seven three, confidence interval zero point five seven to zero point nine five, p equals zero point zero zero nine against a prespecified one-sided alpha of zero point zero one four one. The earlier progression-free survival result, which is what got this drug approved in China in twenty twenty-five, was eleven point one months against five point eight, hazard ratio zero point five one.

That combination — significant on both progression-free survival and overall survival, randomized, double-blind, against pembrolizumab monotherapy — has not been done before by anything. Akeso is claiming it as a first and I have not found a counterexample. And it matters specifically because the loudest objection to the twenty twenty-four result was mechanistic rather than statistical. Anti-V-E-G-F agents shrink tumors and normalize vasculature; they can push a R-E-C-I-S-T measurement around without touching the biology that kills the patient. Bevacizumab has a long history of exactly that. Survival is the endpoint that objection cannot be raised against, and the drug cleared it.

Here is the part I think is under-discussed. Forty-five and a half percent of the HARMONi-two population had squamous histology, and among the squamous patients on ivonescimab, seventy-two percent had central tumors, ten percent had cavitation or necrosis, and six point seven percent had tumors encasing major vessels. Those are the exact patients that anti-V-E-G-F therapy has been contraindicated in since bevacizumab's fatal pulmonary hemorrhages in the early two thousands. The entire anti-angiogenic class has been locked out of squamous lung cancer for twenty years on that basis. Akeso reports no apparent increase in bleeding risk in that group — and the survival hazard ratio in squamous patients was zero point six five, better than the zero point seven nine in non-squamous. If that safety finding holds up in a Western population, it is not a subgroup footnote. It reopens a market segment that an entire mechanism was excluded from, and it is the single strongest piece of evidence that the bispecific format is doing something a combination of two separate drugs would not.

The P-D-L-one subgroups cut the other way, and you need to hold both. In tumor proportion score fifty percent or above, the hazard ratio was zero point five eight — a forty-two percent reduction in the risk of death. In the one-to-forty-nine percent group, it was zero point eight five with a confidence interval from zero point six one to one point one eight, which crosses one. So essentially all of the benefit sits in the P-D-L-one-high population. On first read that sounds like a narrowing. On second read it is the opposite of a problem, because pembrolizumab monotherapy is only genuinely standard of care in the P-D-L-one-high group. In the one-to-forty-nine percent band, Western practice is chemotherapy plus a checkpoint inhibitor, not pembrolizumab alone — so in that subgroup HARMONi-two was testing against a comparator no Western oncologist uses, and it does not much matter that the result is equivocal. The benefit concentrated exactly where the comparator was legitimate. And Akeso's global confirmatory trial, HARMONi-seven, is precisely that study: ivonescimab against pembrolizumab in P-D-L-one-high disease, run internationally.

Safety was unremarkable in the way you want. Any-grade treatment-related events ran ninety-three point four percent on ivonescimab against eighty-four point nine on pembrolizumab, serious events twenty-nine point nine against twenty-one point six, and discontinuation for toxicity four point one percent against five point zero — so the extra events were mostly not the kind that make people stop. Immune-related adverse events were identical at about thirty-four percent in both arms, which is a useful data point on its own: adding a V-E-G-F arm to a P-D-one antibody did not amplify autoimmunity.

Now the caveats, and they are real. This is a China-only trial. Three hundred ninety-eight patients at Chinese sites, and the comparator arm's median overall survival of twenty-two point six months is notably better than what pembrolizumab monotherapy produced in KEYNOTE-oh-four-two, which was about sixteen point seven months in the same tumor proportion score one-percent-and-above population. A control arm running six months ahead of the Western benchmark usually means a fitter population, more post-progression therapy, or both — and if the control arm is that much healthier, the absolute eight-month gain will not transfer intact. It does not invalidate the hazard ratio, but it does mean the median is not a number you can promise a U-S patient.

And the global program has not been clean. HARMONi, the international Phase three in E-G-F-R-mutant disease after third-generation T-K-I failure, missed statistical significance on overall survival at its primary analysis — the Western subgroup hazard ratio was zero point nine eight — and improved to about zero point seven six on the June twenty twenty-six data cut without ever clearing the bar. That is the study underpinning Summit's biologics license application, which has a Food and Drug Administration action date of November fourteenth. And HARMONi-three, the global first-line trial, did not clear its interim progression-free survival threshold in May, with non-squamous enrollment finishing this year and the read now expected in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. So the pattern across the whole program is that the Chinese trials keep winning and the global trials keep coming in softer. HARMONi-two is the best Chinese datapoint yet. It is still a Chinese datapoint.

The competitive frame is why anyone outside Summit should care. Everyone who bought into P-D-one-by-V-E-G-F bought a China-originated asset on China-originated data. Summit paid Akeso five hundred million dollars up front against five billion total in December twenty twenty-four. Bristol Myers Squibb committed up to eleven point one billion dollars to BioNTech for pumitamig, which is the P-D-L-one version of the same idea. Pfizer took S-S-G-J-seven-oh-seven from three S-Bio and has registered two global Phase threes including a head-to-head against Keytruda in first-line lung cancer. Merck licensed LaNova's molecule, now M-K-two-oh-one-zero, and showed its first clinical data this year. That is four of the largest oncology franchises in the world hedging against their own checkpoint inhibitors, and the class is expanding into colorectal, biliary, pancreatic and triple-negative breast cancer in China. HARMONi-two is the strongest evidence any of them has that the mechanism is real. It is also, for all four of them, evidence generated in a country none of them will be selling into.

Jefferies' read after the topline ten days ago was that future lung cancer regimens may increasingly be judged against ivonescimab rather than pembrolizumab, with the caveat that the China results still have to translate. With the numbers in hand that framing looks right, and the translation question is now concentrated into two trials — HARMONi-seven in P-D-L-one-high disease and HARMONi-three in the broader first line.

So the bottom line. What changed this weekend is that the mechanism is no longer speculative: a P-D-one-by-V-E-G-F bispecific extended life against the best single agent in first-line lung cancer, in a properly blinded randomized trial, with a safety profile that did not punish squamous patients the way twenty years of anti-angiogenic experience said it would. What did not change is that the drug has never done this outside China, that its two global Phase threes have both underdelivered relative to their Chinese counterparts, and that the November fourteenth Food and Drug Administration decision rests on a different trial with a weaker result.
