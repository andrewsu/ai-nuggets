Welcome to the spotlight. Today it's petosemtamab. Genmab paid eight billion dollars for Merus to get this bispecific antibody, and yesterday it produced the first positive Phase 3 in first-line recurrent or metastatic head and neck cancer in years. The main question is what a hazard ratio of zero point five at a first interim analysis actually tells you. The second is what it means for everyone else trying to add something to PD-one in this disease.

Start with what Genmab disclosed. LiGeR-H-N-one is an open-label Phase 3 that randomized six hundred ninety-nine patients one to one. All had recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma that was PD-L-one positive, and all were being treated for the first time. One arm got petosemtamab plus Merck's Keytruda, the other got Keytruda alone. The trial had two primary endpoints, overall survival and response rate. At the first interim analysis, run by the independent data monitoring committee, both were met. The survival hazard ratio was zero point five oh, with a confidence interval of zero point three seven to zero point six seven, and the p-value was below one in ten thousand. The odds ratio for response was three point one seven. Genmab says the benefit held regardless of H-P-V status, and that there were no new safety signals.

Here is what Genmab did not disclose: the median survival in either arm, the actual response rates, and the side-effect table. Truist's analysts filled in the first two from history. Keytruda alone in this setting has a median survival of roughly a year, from the KEYNOTE-oh-four-eight trial that made it the standard in twenty nineteen. Applying a hazard ratio of zero point five to that, Truist estimates the combination landed at about thirty months, in line with the twenty-eight point two months seen in Phase 2. They put the response rate somewhere between forty-four and fifty-eight percent. Treat those as analyst models, not data.

The mechanism explains why this is more than another E-G-F-R antibody. Head and neck cancer is the disease where E-G-F-R was validated, with cetuximab, two decades ago, and also where E-G-F-R blockade turned out to be a modest benefit. Petosemtamab works three ways. The E-G-F-R arm blocks ligand binding on any tumor cell. The second arm binds L-G-R-five, a receptor found on stem-like cancer cells that tend to drive recurrence and resistance. On cells that express both receptors, L-G-R-five is naturally internalized, so binding both drags E-G-F-R into the cell to be degraded instead of only blocking it. And the antibody's Fc region is engineered to be low in fucose, which makes it much better at recruiting natural killer cells to kill the tumor. That third mechanism is why it pairs well with Keytruda. It brings in innate immune killing at the same time PD-one blockade releases the T cells. It is also chemotherapy-free, which matters in a population that is often frail, malnourished and recovering from surgery and radiation.

Now the caveats, and there are three. First, this is an interim analysis. A trial that stops at its first look tends to overestimate the effect, because it stops on a high point. The confidence interval runs from about a one-third to a nearly two-thirds reduction in the risk of death. That is unambiguously positive, but the final hazard ratio could plausibly settle somewhere in the zero point six range. Truist said the same thing more politely: full data remain important, and they want to see how mature the survival data are, along with absolute response rates, safety and regulatory timing.

Second, the control arm. Keytruda alone is a legitimate standard for PD-L-one-positive disease. But in U.S. practice, many patients, especially those with heavy disease burden, get Keytruda plus platinum chemotherapy, and LiGeR-H-N-one did not test against that. The first debate at the medical meeting will be which patients a chemo-free doublet can safely take away from chemotherapy.

Third, safety. E-G-F-R antibodies bring skin toxicity, and earlier petosemtamab studies reported infusion reactions. "No new safety signals" in a press release only means nothing surprising turned up. It does not tell you the rates, and in a first-line, chemo-free regimen given for long periods, the rash and fatigue rates will shape how often doctors use it.

Next, the business context, which is why the stock moved twenty-nine percent. Genmab is facing the loss of its Darzalex royalties later this decade, and it bet its next phase on three programs reading out in a few weeks of each other. All three have now hit. Epkinly, its C-D-twenty by C-D-three bispecific with AbbVie, cut the risk of progression or death by about half when added to R-CHOP in newly diagnosed lymphoma. Rina-S, its folate receptor alpha drug conjugate, beat chemotherapy on progression and survival in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. And now petosemtamab. Truist had modeled petosemtamab's approval for twenty twenty-eight, and it now sees twenty twenty-seven. When Genmab bought Merus in twenty twenty-five, petosemtamab was the centerpiece and the most expensive of those bets. There are also two Phase 3 trials in colorectal cancer and one in second- and third-line head and neck cancer, so this readout reduces risk across the whole franchise, not just one label.

Now Bicara, which is the interesting part. Bicara's ficerafusp alfa is also built on E-G-F-R. It's an E-G-F-R antibody fused to a trap that soaks up T-G-F-beta, meant to break the immunosuppressive barrier around the tumor. Its Phase 3, FORTIFI-H-N-one, is also testing it with Keytruda in first-line PD-L-one-positive disease. There are two important differences. It is placebo-controlled with about six hundred fifty patients, and it enrolls only H-P-V-negative patients. Its interim readout is expected in mid-twenty twenty-seven. Bicara fell forty-eight percent yesterday to about nine dollars. That repeats May of last year, when the two companies' Phase 2 data came out within a day of each other. Petosemtamab showed a sixty-three percent response rate and seventy-nine percent one-year survival, against fifty-three and about sixty-two for ficerafusp, and Merus rose thirty-two percent while Bicara fell forty-one.

The market reads yesterday as winner takes most. Petosemtamab is likely to reach market at least a year earlier, in a broader population, with a big company's commercial infrastructure behind it. BMO argued the opposite. Its view is that a positive survival result for E-G-F-R plus PD-one is the best evidence yet that Bicara's trial will also succeed, and that the stock drop doesn't match the outlook. Both readings can be true. The biology risk for Bicara fell yesterday, and the commercial risk rose. In H-P-V-negative disease, the subgroup where both drugs looked strongest in Phase 2, Bicara will be the second to arrive with a similar mechanism. It will need a clearly better survival result or a better tolerability profile to win share, not just a positive trial.

There are three takeaways for Calibr. First, this is a Phase 3 survival validation of a design idea, not just a target. One arm of a bispecific uses its partner receptor's trafficking to degrade the other target, and the Fc does the immune killing. That template is available for other receptor pairs where one partner internalizes. Second, adding a mechanism to PD-one beat replacing PD-one. Both LiGeR and FORTIFI kept Keytruda and tried to make it work better, and that has been the more productive strategy in solid tumors this year. Third, the interim result changes how the head and neck field gets designed. The next trial in this setting will probably have to beat petosemtamab plus Keytruda, not Keytruda alone, and that is a much higher bar for everyone still in development.

Watch for the full data, which Genmab says it will present at a future medical meeting, including medians, the H-P-V subgroups, and the rash and infusion reaction rates. Watch also for whether the F-D-A gives this a fast review on the interim data. That's the spotlight.
