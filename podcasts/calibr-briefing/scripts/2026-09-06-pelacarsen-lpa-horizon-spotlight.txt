Today's spotlight is pelacarsen, and the question it was supposed to settle.

Start with what makes this different from an ordinary Phase three miss. Most failed trials fail because the drug did not do what it was designed to do — it missed the target, or it could not reach the tissue, or the dose was capped by toxicity before it did anything useful. None of that happened here. Pelacarsen is an antisense oligonucleotide, discovered at Ionis, licensed to Novartis in February twenty nineteen. It is a second-generation chemistry with a triantennary N-acetylgalactosamine conjugate on it, which is the sugar handle that the asialoglycoprotein receptor on hepatocytes grabs. That gets the molecule into liver cells, where apolipoprotein little a is made, and essentially nowhere else. It works. In the Phase two-b it dropped lipoprotein little a by up to eighty percent, dose-dependently, with injection site reactions as the main complaint and no platelet, liver or kidney signal. On Friday Novartis said the same thing happened in the Phase three: lower lipoprotein little a levels were achieved, in the range previously observed, with an acceptable safety profile. The drug hit its target, in the right organ, to the expected depth, in eight thousand people, for years.

And the patients did not benefit. That is the expensive kind of negative result — the one where every engineering step worked and the biological premise is what broke.

Here is the trial. L-P little a HORIZON enrolled eight thousand three hundred twenty-three patients with established cardiovascular disease — prior myocardial infarction, prior ischemic stroke, or symptomatic peripheral artery disease — and screening lipoprotein little a at or above seventy milligrams per deciliter, which is about a hundred forty-nine nanomoles per liter on an isoform-independent assay. Randomized one-to-one to eighty milligrams of pelacarsen subcutaneously every four weeks or matching placebo, on top of optimized guideline-directed therapy including lipid lowering and antihypertensives. Nine hundred two sites. First patient December twelfth, twenty nineteen. Minimum follow-up two and a half years, event-driven, and the study was designed to end when nine hundred ninety-three adjudicated primary events had accrued. Primary completion was July sixteenth of this year. The endpoint is four-point major adverse cardiovascular events: cardiovascular death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke, and urgent coronary revascularization requiring hospitalization. And critically, there were two co-primary populations with formal multiplicity control — the overall population at seventy milligrams per deciliter and above, and a subpopulation at ninety and above. Neither company claimed the subpopulation. If the high-level group had hit, Friday's release would have said so.

Now the part that should have been the field's central argument for the last eight years, and largely was not.

Lipoprotein little a became a drug target on the strength of human genetics. Levels are about ninety percent determined by the L-P-A locus, essentially fixed at birth, unmoved by diet or exercise, which makes it close to a natural randomized experiment. Mendelian randomization studies established a linear, dose-proportional association with coronary heart disease. Textbook causal inference — that is exactly why it was such an attractive target, and exactly why Novartis put nearly seven years and eight thousand patients behind it.

But the same body of work also told you how much lowering you would need, and the number is uncomfortable. In a two thousand eighteen analysis in JAMA Cardiology, Burgess and colleagues used individual participant data on nearly twenty-one thousand coronary cases plus summary data from forty-eight further studies to calibrate the two exposures against each other. A ten milligram per deciliter lower genetically predicted lipoprotein little a associates with a five point eight percent lower risk of coronary heart disease. A ten milligram per deciliter lower L-D-L cholesterol associates with fourteen and a half percent. Lipoprotein little a is, per milligram, roughly two and a half times weaker. Run that out and you get the headline figure: you need a hundred one and a half milligrams per deciliter of absolute lipoprotein little a lowering to buy the same expected benefit as one millimole per liter — about thirty-nine milligrams per deciliter — of L-D-L lowering. The confidence interval on that runs from seventy-one to a hundred thirty-seven.

Hold that against what pelacarsen actually delivered. The trial let people in at seventy milligrams per deciliter. In the Phase two-b, median baselines across the dose groups ran roughly two hundred to two hundred fifty nanomoles per liter, which is somewhere around eighty-five to a hundred milligrams per deciliter in mass units. Take eighty percent off a baseline in that neighborhood and you get an absolute reduction of perhaps seventy to eighty milligrams per deciliter. That is at the bottom edge of the confidence interval for a single L-D-L-equivalent. So on the field's own genetic arithmetic, the maximum benefit this trial could have been chasing was something like three-quarters of what you get from one millimole per liter of L-D-L lowering.

Then apply the second discount, which is the one that really matters. Mendelian randomization measures lifetime exposure — sixty years of a slightly different lipid particle, acting on an artery from adolescence. Drug trials measure four or five years of exposure in someone who already has plaque. For L-D-L we know the size of that gap: the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists meta-analyses put the trial-horizon benefit at roughly a fifth per millimole per liter, while lifetime genetic estimates are two to three times larger. Apply the same haircut here, and the effect pelacarsen could plausibly have produced over four years in secondary prevention is on the order of a ten to fifteen percent relative risk reduction. Nine hundred ninety-three events gives you comfortable power at twenty percent and marginal power at fifteen.

That is my arithmetic, not Novartis's, and the full dataset will either support it or demolish it. But it frames the honest reading of Friday: we do not yet know whether this was a biology failure or a dosing-and-power failure wearing a biology failure's clothes. Those have opposite implications for the forty-six thousand patients still enrolled behind it.

There is a second explanation that deserves equal weight, and it comes from a trial this show covered a week ago. The prespecified lipoprotein little a analysis from VESALIUS-CV, published in Circulation in June, measured baseline levels in seven thousand five hundred fifty-seven patients on evolocumab or placebo. Two findings matter here. First, higher lipoprotein little a predicted major coronary events — a hazard ratio of one point one five per hundred nanomoles per liter, and one point two three for myocardial infarction specifically — but showed no association whatsoever with ischemic stroke. Hazard ratio one point zero zero. L-P little a HORIZON's primary endpoint includes non-fatal stroke and urgent revascularization, two components with weak or absent attribution to the risk factor the drug removes. Any true coronary signal gets diluted by them. Second, and more awkward: evolocumab produced the same relative benefit regardless of baseline lipoprotein little a — forty-one percent versus thirty-five percent, interaction p of point four five. In other words, in a population already on aggressive L-D-L lowering, much of the risk that tracks with lipoprotein little a is apparently already being harvested by the L-D-L drug. Optimized background therapy may have left very little residual, attributable risk on the table.

So: what does this do to the rest of the field?

There are four Phase three outcomes trials still running and they are not equally exposed. Amgen's olpasiran is a small interfering R-N-A that has cleared ninety-five percent lowering in mid-stage work, versus pelacarsen's eighty. If depth is the problem, olpasiran is the clean test — but note that ninety-five percent of ninety milligrams per deciliter is about eighty-six, still short of a hundred one and a half. Depth alone may not be enough; you may need depth plus a higher-baseline population. Its secondary prevention trial has seven thousand two hundred ninety-seven patients and reads out in twenty twenty-eight. The more interesting Amgen asset is now the eleven thousand patient primary prevention study that started in August of last year and runs to twenty thirty-one, because primary prevention is where the genetic logic is strongest — you are intervening before the plaque, which is the regime Mendelian randomization is actually describing. Lilly has lepodisiran, another siRNA, in the seventeen thousand three hundred patient ACCLAIM trial due in twenty twenty-nine. And Lilly dosed the first patients in a ten thousand four hundred fifty patient Phase three of muvalaplin — an oral small molecule that prevents assembly of apo little a onto the apoB particle rather than silencing its message — on September second. Four days ago. Lilly now has to decide whether to keep writing those checks with Friday's result on the table.

Watch the imaging trials, which nobody was paying attention to a week ago and which just became the cheapest way to learn anything. Amgen has a four hundred six patient coronary C-T angiography study of plaque burden with olpasiran, and Lilly started a two hundred fifty-two patient coronary plaque study with lepodisiran in June. Those are intermediate readouts on whether removing the particle changes the artery at all, and they land years before the outcome trials do.

For Ionis specifically, the financial damage is contained — pelacarsen was Novartis-funded, and Ionis' economics were milestones and royalties it had not yet booked. Brett Monia's quote made the pivot explicit: he spent one sentence on the miss and the next listing Tryngolza, Dawnzera and Zanvastro, the three wholly owned commercial products, the last of which was approved three days before this news broke. That is the right story for Ionis. But it is the second time this summer that an Ionis-partnered cardiovascular outcomes trial produced an argument rather than a verdict, after the debate over eplontersen in transthyretin cardiomyopathy. The chemistry keeps working. The cardiovascular translation keeps being harder than the biomarker suggested.

And that is the durable lesson, well beyond these two companies. Lipoprotein little a was the single best case anyone had for human genetics as a sufficient basis for target validation. Causal, dose-proportional, unconfounded by lifestyle, replicated across cohorts, with a clean druggable node and an assay that reads out in weeks. If genetic validation plus confirmed target engagement plus a moved biomarker were enough, this trial wins. It did not. What the genetics could not tell you — and structurally cannot tell you — is whether an exposure that matters over sixty years still matters over four, in an artery that has already remodeled, in a patient whose L-D-L is already at goal. PCSK9 survived that translation. Lipoprotein little a, so far, has not.

Which is worth sitting with if you are looking at any liver-directed R-N-A program whose value rests on a biomarker rather than an event. ADARx filed to go public on Friday, the same day as this print, on a platform of exactly that shape — including a factor eleven silencer aimed at secondary stroke prevention, where the biomarker is factor eleven activity and the thing you are selling is strokes that do not happen. The chemistry to knock down almost any liver-made protein is now close to commodity. What is scarce, and what Friday just repriced, is confidence that the protein you chose is doing the damage on the timescale your trial can measure.

The full dataset goes to a medical congress, most likely the American Heart Association in November. The three numbers to look for are the hazard ratio in the ninety-milligram-and-above subpopulation, the achieved absolute reduction in milligrams per deciliter rather than percent, and whether benefit tracks with either baseline level or magnitude of lowering. If any of those three points the right way, this is a dosing problem and the class is alive. If none of them do, twenty years of genetic epidemiology has been describing a passenger.
