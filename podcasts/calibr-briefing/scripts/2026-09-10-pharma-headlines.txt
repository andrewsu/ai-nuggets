Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Thursday, September tenth, twenty twenty-six. The F-D-A moved a targeted lung cancer drug into first line on the strength of a sixty-nine-patient single-arm cohort, a drug Bristol Myers licensed out in the middle of its own Phase two just posted the cleanest lupus readout anyone has produced in years, and Kura took its menin inhibitor out of oncology and put it into diabetes with Eli Lilly's money behind it.

First, Bayer. The F-D-A granted accelerated approval to Hyrnuo — sevabertinib — for the first-line treatment of adults with locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer whose tumors carry activating HER-two tyrosine kinase domain mutations. Sevabertinib is an oral, reversible small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor against mutant HER-two, covering both exon twenty insertions and point mutations, and it also hits mutant E-G-F-R while largely sparing the wild-type receptor. It came out of Bayer's research alliance with the Broad Institute. The approval rests on Cohort F of the ongoing Phase one-two SOHO-oh-one trial: sixty-nine treatment-naive patients, objective response rate seventy-five percent, with complete responses in six percent and partial responses in seventy percent. Seventy-three percent of responders held that response at least six months, thirty-eight percent at least twelve months. The confirmatory study, the randomized Phase three SOHO-oh-two against standard of care in the same first-line population, is already running. Two things to hold onto here. One, this is Bayer's second accelerated approval for the same molecule — it got the previously-treated indication in November of last year on a seventy-one percent response rate — and the effect of yesterday's action is simply to delete the prior-therapy requirement from the label. Two, granting accelerated approval in the first line is a different act from granting it in the second, because in the first line there is a standard of care: platinum, pemetrexed and pembrolizumab. The agency has effectively accepted a cross-trial comparison of a seventy-five percent single-arm response rate against chemo-immunotherapy in a population that is two to four percent of advanced non-small cell lung cancer and enriched for never-smokers. Xiuning Le at M-D Anderson, the lead investigator, used the moment to press on molecular testing at diagnosis, which is the real gating item — you cannot prescribe this without an N-G-S panel that reports HER-two kinase domain mutations. The tolerability is the soft spot for a chronic first-line oral: eighty-nine percent diarrhea including sixteen percent grade three, with a median onset of four days; hepatotoxicity in twenty-nine percent by adverse event and A-L-T elevations in forty-one percent by lab; ocular toxicity in seventeen percent; and lipase elevations in forty-eight percent. Against Boehringer's zongertinib, which is HER-two-selective and spares wild-type E-G-F-R, that gastrointestinal and skin burden is the axis on which this gets fought — and Boehringer is running a randomized trial to get to first line while Bayer got there on a single-arm cohort.

Second, today's spotlight, so I will just set it up. Beeline Medicines reported positive topline results from the global Phase two of afimetoran in systemic lupus erythematosus. Afimetoran is a selective, oral, once-daily, equipotent small-molecule inhibitor of Toll-like receptors seven and eight — the endosomal single-stranded R-N-A sensors that sit upstream of the type one interferon axis, and the mechanism human genetics implicates most directly in lupus. Two hundred forty-eight adults with active moderate-to-severe disease, randomized one-to-one-to-one-to-one to three doses or placebo for forty-eight weeks on top of background care with a mandatory corticosteroid taper. The primary endpoint was S-R-I four response at week forty-eight, and all three dose groups beat placebo with a p-value below zero point zero zero one. No response rates were released; those go to a congress. Beeline is taking it into pivotal development in both systemic and cutaneous lupus. Bristol Myers discovered this molecule, licensed it to Beeline in July of last year while this very trial was running, and kept equity. I will take the whole thing apart in the second episode.

Third, Kura Oncology launched Caspian Therapeutics with a fifty-million-dollar financing to develop menin inhibitors for diabetes and cardiometabolic disease. The lead compound is K-O-seven-two-four-six, a next-generation, highly selective, orally bioavailable menin inhibitor purpose-built for chronic dosing rather than oncology. Menin is a scaffold protein that regulates gene expression and acts as a brake on pancreatic beta-cell proliferation, so inhibiting it is an attempt to expand functional beta-cell mass and restore endogenous insulin production rather than replace or supplement insulin. Kura reports selective beta-cell proliferation, expanded beta-cell mass and improved glycemic control across multiple type one and type two models, with improvements persisting after treatment stopped, plus selective beta-cell proliferation in human islet microtissues and organoids with no proliferation of non-beta cells. B-V-F Partners led, with the T-one-D Fund, Invus, Montanova, Eli Lilly, Kura itself and members of Kura's leadership team. Kura keeps roughly half the company on a fully diluted basis and Caspian runs on an intercompany services arrangement — which is why fifty million is enough. Robert Spencer, formerly of Neurommune and before that eighteen years at Cara, is president and chief operating officer. The preclinical package gets an oral presentation at the European Association for the Study of Diabetes meeting on September twenty-ninth. Note the credibility this borrows: Kura's own ziftomenib, marketed as Komzifti, is an approved menin inhibitor in relapsed refractory N-P-M-one-mutated acute myeloid leukemia, so the medicinal chemistry and the human safety experience are real. What is unproven is whether you can dose that mechanism chronically in a non-lethal disease, and whether expanding beta cells in type one diabetes does anything if you have not also stopped the autoimmunity that killed the first set.

Fourth, financing. Encoded Therapeutics closed a two-hundred-seventy-five-million-dollar Series F co-led by G-V and an undisclosed healthcare investor, with ARCH, Illumina Ventures, SoftBank Vision Fund Two, R-T-W, Venrock, Matrix, Braidwell, Farallon, Janus Henderson, Nolan Capital and Invus participating. The money funds pivotal development of E-T-X-one-oh-one in S-C-N-one-A-positive Dravet syndrome plus an expansion study up to age eighteen. E-T-X-one-oh-one is not a gene replacement — Dravet is caused by haploinsufficiency of S-C-N-one-A, which encodes the sodium channel Nav one point one, and the gene is too large for conventional A-A-V packaging. So Encoded delivers an engineered transcription factor by A-A-V-nine intracerebroventricularly, under a regulatory element called REGABA that restricts expression to G-A-B-A-ergic interneurons, and that transcription factor upregulates the patient's own remaining S-C-N-one-A allele. It is gene regulation rather than gene addition, and cell-type restriction is the entire safety argument, because you do not want to raise sodium channel density in excitatory neurons. Interim Phase one-two POLARIS data at the European Epilepsy Congress showed substantial and sustained seizure frequency reductions with developmental gains, which is what unlocked this round.

Fifth, two more raises worth your attention. Solstice Oncology came out of stealth with a two-hundred-twenty-five-million-dollar Series A from R-A Capital, Canaan and Forbion, around porustobart, a C-T-L-A-four antibody licensed from Harbour BioMed in February for a hundred five million dollars up front and up to one point two billion in total. Porustobart is a fully human heavy-chain-only antibody from Harbour's platform, engineered for enhanced antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity so that it preferentially depletes the C-T-L-A-four-high regulatory T cells sitting inside the tumor rather than blocking the checkpoint systemically — which is the standard argument for why the next generation of C-T-L-A-four drugs should be more effective and less toxic than Yervoy. Caroline Loew, formerly of Mural Oncology, Bristol Myers and Merck, is chief executive, and the unusual choice is the setting: they are going into colon cancer patients before any other treatment, neoadjuvantly, with mid-stage testing by year end. Harbour already has positive Phase two data for the same antibody with tislelizumab in microsatellite-stable metastatic colorectal cancer, which is the population Agenus built the botensilimab story on — so this is the third serious attempt to make C-T-L-A-four work in cold colorectal tumors. And TwoStep Therapeutics raised an oversubscribed sixty-two-and-a-half-million-dollar Series A alongside F-D-A clearance of its I-N-D for T-S-one-oh-four. Insight Partners and Medical Excellence Capital led, with M Ventures and Pfizer Ventures as co-leads, plus Stanford itself, where the science came from — the company was founded by Caitlyn Miller with Jennifer Cochran, Nobel laureate Carolyn Bertozzi and Ronald Levy. The idea addresses the actual failure mode of solid-tumor targeting: instead of one antigen that has to be uniformly and highly expressed, they use a polyspecific integrin-binding peptide that engages five different tumor-associated integrins, so target-low cells have fewer ways to escape. T-S-one-oh-four couples that peptide to M-M-A-E, the validated auristatin payload, and the same scaffold is being pointed at radioligands next. First patients this year.

Sixth, NodThera renamed itself Rezera and announced a Phase three program for ruvonoflast, its N-L-R-P-three inflammasome inhibitor, in peripheral artery disease — roughly eight hundred patients starting in twenty twenty-seven. N-L-R-P-three is the sensor that assembles the inflammasome and licenses interleukin-one beta and interleukin-eighteen release, so this is the small-molecule oral version of the thesis canakinumab proved and could not commercialize. Choosing peripheral artery disease rather than coronary outcomes is the interesting call: smaller trials, harder endpoints, and a population where nothing anti-inflammatory has worked.

Seventh, Compass Pathways posted fifty-two-week open-label data from Part C of the Phase three COMP-oh-oh-five trial of COMP-three-sixty, its synthetic psilocybin, in treatment-resistant depression. Patients originally randomized to the twenty-five-milligram arm who took an additional dose in Part C showed an average thirteen-point reduction from baseline on the Montgomery-Asberg depression rating scale at week fifty-two — durability out to a year, which has been the whole question for a single-administration psychedelic. Two hundred fifty-eight patients in the original randomization. Launch readiness is targeted for the end of this year with commercialization in the first half of twenty twenty-seven.

Eighth, the miss. Tyra Biosciences fell nearly twenty percent after initial Phase two SURF-three-oh-two results for dabogratinib, its oral F-G-F-R-three inhibitor, in non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer with F-G-F-R-three mutations. Fifty-seven percent of patients had a complete response at three months against a bar management itself had set at seventy percent. The commercial pitch is a pill instead of a procedure — the alternatives, UroGen's Zusduri and Johnson and Johnson's Inlexzo, both require repeated instrumentation of the bladder — so the question is whether fifty-seven percent is enough to sell convenience against a catheter. Some analysts think the late-stage numbers improve at higher exposure.

Ninth, on the artificial intelligence side, two items. ARPA-H selected UpDoc, Tempus A-I and Atman Health for a program to build F-D-A-authorized agentic A-I tools to manage cardiovascular disease — note that the deliverable is regulatory clearance for an agent, not a research prototype, which is a materially harder ask than anything the clinical A-I field has cleared so far. And Insilico Medicine dosed the first patient in GENESIS-I-P-F-three, the Phase three of rentosertib, at Peking Union Medical College Hospital, with Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital enrolling the same day. That is the T-NIK inhibitor whose target and molecule both came out of Insilico's platform, and it is the first generative-A-I-derived drug to enter Phase three: three hundred twenty patients across forty-seven sites, fifty-two weeks, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled, primary endpoint the annual rate of decline in forced vital capacity, with Zuojun Xu as lead investigator. We covered the aging-clock paper on Monday; this is the registrational clock actually starting.

Tenth, awards and market color. The Lasker Awards went to Emmanuel Mignot at Stanford and Masashi Yanagisawa at Tsukuba for identifying orexin and showing that orexin deficiency causes narcolepsy — the discovery that produced the entire orexin agonist class now in late-stage development — and the clinical award went to Kunihiro Hattori, Tomoyuki Igawa and Takehisa Kitazawa at Chugai for inventing the bispecific antibody that bridges factors nine and ten to substitute for factor eight in hemophilia A, which is emicizumab. Michael J. Fox took the public service award. If you want one data point on antibody engineering as a discipline, a Lasker for a bispecific geometry is it.
