Fifth, in A-I. A disclosure first: this briefing is produced with Claude, so weigh my framing accordingly. 10x Genomics launched Sentira Single Cell, a cloud-based agent for analyzing data from its Chromium single-cell platform, built on Anthropic's Claude models. It works through a chat interface: the user asks questions and the agent writes and runs the analysis code behind the scenes. Pricing is credit-based, with roughly sixty credits to get from raw data to experimental conclusions and a hundred free credits at launch. 10x's chief technology officer, Michael Schnall-Levin, told GEN that work "that might have taken someone weeks before could take hours," and he also said people "are still on the hook" for checking the output. The most useful comment came from an early user, Henry Long at Dana-Farber. He said 10x's challenge will be keeping up with the general-purpose tools from the foundation model companies, and that his lab has increasingly relied on Claude Science over Sentira. That's the strategic question for every instrument company building an A-I layer. And Luciano Martelotto at Waters, speaking about A-I single-cell analysis in general, put the risk well: "nothing in an A-I-generated analysis looks broken... The mistakes that matter are the silent ones."

The same day, Recursion presented updated Phase two data for R-E-C forty-eight eighty-one, an oral MEK one-two inhibitor, in familial adenomatous polyposis. That's an inherited condition driven by A-P-C mutations, with no approved drug; patients get repeated endoscopies and eventually surgery.
