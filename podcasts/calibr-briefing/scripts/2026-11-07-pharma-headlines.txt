On the list: Sarepta won't restart the Phase 3 trial of Elevidys in older and non-ambulatory boys, Moderna beats on vaccines and says its cancer vaccine filing is coming by year end, Lilly puts Zepbound on Hims and Hers, Alnylam's commercial chief exits, and Gilead posts Phase 3 data that could widen seladelpar's label in primary biliary cholangitis. Plus a real-world Repatha study, Agios cutting more than a third of R&D, a fourth try for a liver cancer combination, and the F-D-A's crowded November. Here's what you need to know.

First, and this is today's spotlight. On Thursday's earnings call, Sarepta said it will not resume enrollment in ENVISION, the Phase 3 trial of Elevidys, its A-A-V micro-dystrophin gene therapy for Duchenne, in non-ambulatory boys and ambulatory boys aged eight to seventeen. The trial was paused in the summer of 2025, after two liver-failure deaths, with about half its hundred-fifty patients enrolled. Sarepta's path back to non-ambulatory patients now runs through twelve-week safety data from a sirolimus-prophylaxis cohort, due to the F-D-A in the first quarter. Elevidys did eighty-three and a half million dollars in the quarter, down from a hundred thirty-one and a half million a year ago, though William Blair sees the franchise stabilizing. The spotlight goes deep on what the company is giving up, and why Duchenne has become the stress test for accelerated approval.

Second, Moderna. Third-quarter revenue rose twenty-nine percent to one point three billion dollars, and vaccine sales of one point two seven billion beat a consensus of eight hundred ninety-one million, according to William Blair. CFO Jamey Mock credited global COVID uptake and timing of shipments; Jefferies noted U-S sales of mNEXSPIKE are actually declining and the growth is international. Moderna raised full-year revenue growth guidance to fifteen to twenty-five percent, from ten. The bigger story is oncology. President Stephen Hoge said Moderna and Merck are on track to file intismeran autogene, the personalized m-R-N-A neoantigen vaccine given with Keytruda, in melanoma by year end, with a decision in 2027. Next up is a two-hundred-eighty-two-patient Phase 2 in kidney cancer; development chief David Berman said if it's very strong, the partners could take it straight to the F-D-A. And the norovirus vaccine won't enroll another cohort this season after missing its early bar.

Third, Eli Lilly will offer its obesity drugs Zepbound and Foundayo on the Hims and Hers telehealth platform, at the same self-pay prices available elsewhere: two ninety-nine a month to start for Zepbound and a hundred forty-nine for Foundayo. This is the same Hims that built a business on compounded G-L-P-one copies, until it changed course in March after a legal challenge from Novo Nordisk. For Lilly, it's another distribution channel for cash-pay patients; for Hims, it's the transition from compounder to licensed storefront.

Fourth, Alnylam's chief commercial officer, Tolga Tanguler, is leaving. Alnylam is running a search for a replacement. The stock is down about sixty percent this year on worries about Amvuttra, its T-T-R-silencing s-i-R-N-A for transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy. William Blair's Myles Minter wrote that the drug's growth rate visibility is "murky at best." When the commercial head of the best-selling R-N-A-i drug walks in the middle of a launch debate, investors read it as a verdict.

Fifth, Gilead's seladelpar, sold as Livdelzi, a P-P-A-R-delta agonist for primary biliary cholangitis, hit in the Phase 3 IDEAL study in a population that's been excluded from second-line trials: patients whose alkaline phosphatase is elevated but below one point six seven times the upper limit of normal. Seventy-five percent normalized alkaline phosphatase at a year, versus sixteen percent on placebo, and more than half got there within a month. No treatment-related serious adverse events. Miami's Cynthia Levy said these patients "have been largely excluded from previous pivotal trials." Livdelzi has accelerated approval from 2024; Gilead will take the data to regulators, and the bet here is that normalization, not just improvement, becomes the treatment goal, which would pull many more patients into second-line therapy.

Sixth, Amgen reported a real-world study of Repatha, its P-C-S-K-nine antibody, against ezetimibe in more than half a million statin-treated patients with atherosclerotic disease. Over four years, heart attack risk was twenty percent lower with Repatha, a hazard ratio of point eight. It's an observational cohort from claims data, so treat it as supporting evidence, not a trial. But the commercial purpose is clear: the add-on decision after a statin is still overwhelmingly cheap generic ezetimibe, and Amgen wants that first add-on slot, especially as oral P-C-S-K-nine drugs approach.

Seventh, Agios is cutting more than thirty-five percent of its R&D staff, more than a hundred roles, about eleven million in charges. It comes a week after the F-D-A pushed its decision on mitapivat, the oral pyruvate kinase activator, in sickle cell disease to February first, while it weighs whether a liver-safety R-E-M-S is needed. The franchise did fifty-five million in the quarter, ahead of consensus. Leerink called the cut "a sharper allocation of capital toward commercialization," and Agios now targets profitability in 2028 with nine hundred seven million in cash.

Eighth, the F-D-A set an April ninth, 2027 decision date for Elevar's rivoceranib, an oral V-E-G-F-R-two inhibitor, with Hengrui's P-D-one antibody camrelizumab in first-line liver cancer. This is the fourth resubmission, following complete response letters, the most recent in July, tied to manufacturing issues. The data have never been the problem: in CARES-three-ten, median overall survival was twenty-three point eight months versus fifteen point two on sorafenib. It's a test of whether a China-manufactured biologic can clear F-D-A inspection, which matters for every company in-licensing from China right now.

Ninth, two quick layoffs: Voyager Therapeutics, the A-A-V capsid company partnered with Novartis, disclosed unquantified third-quarter cuts with one point four million in restructuring charges, and C4 Therapeutics, the targeted protein degradation company, reduced research headcount. Both are the familiar pattern: preserve runway, narrow to the clinic.

Looking ahead, the F-D-A has at least eleven decisions this month. Summit and Akeso's ivonescimab, the P-D-one by V-E-G-F bispecific, in E-G-F-R-mutant lung cancer by November fourteenth. Capricor's deramiocel, a cell therapy for Duchenne, on November twenty-second, after a nine-to-three advisory vote against. And Vertex's povetacicept, a dual B-A-F-F and APRIL inhibitor, in I-g-A nephropathy, by November thirtieth, the drug BMO says could make Vertex's next chapter "nothing short of iconic."
