Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Tuesday, September first, twenty twenty-six.

The European Society of Cardiology congress closed yesterday and the tape immediately handed the field something bigger. Late last night the Wall Street Journal reported that Novartis has paused eight clinical trials of its autoimmune C-A-R-T program after three patient deaths, and Bristol Myers has paused its own autoimmune cell therapy enrollment alongside it. That is the story of the day and the spotlight. Then, this morning in Basel, the same company put out positive Phase three data on an oral pill in the same disease area, which makes for a very unusual forty-eight hours in neuroimmunology. Nine items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight. Novartis temporarily halted eight trials of rap-cel, its C-D-nineteen-directed autologous C-A-R-T therapy, in late August — the holds went in on August twenty-fourth — following three serious cases of immune effector cell-associated hemophagocytic syndrome. That is a macrophage-activation syndrome, a hyperinflammatory cascade distinct from ordinary cytokine release, and per the Journal all three patients died. A Novartis spokesman described the fatal immune response as a known safety risk of C-A-R-T therapy and said the company is conducting a comprehensive review, working with the independent safety boards on each study and sharing what it knows with regulators. The eight paused studies are the Phase two programs in lupus and lupus nephritis, systemic sclerosis, A-N-C-A-associated vasculitis and inflammatory myopathies, plus Phase one-slash-two studies in rheumatoid arthritis, Sjogren's disease, generalized myasthenia gravis, and both relapsing and non-active progressive multiple sclerosis. The two oncology trials of the same product are unaffected and continue. Separately, Bristol Myers voluntarily paused enrollment in its zola-cel autoimmune program out of what it called an abundance of caution, citing transient and reversible inflammatory events picked up in routine safety surveillance, and said it believes the profile remains consistent with the known profile of C-A-R-T therapies. Bristol had planned to open a systemic sclerosis study this month. The mechanistic hypothesis that matters here comes from William Blair's Sami Corwin, who wrote in a Monday client note that the newer rapid-manufacturing technology, which produces a less differentiated and more expansion-competent cell product, could be driving the increased expansion and the reported toxicities — while cautioning that other factors could be involved. Both Novartis and Bristol are running exactly that kind of fast process. That distinction is the whole read-across question, and it is where the spotlight goes.

Second, the strange counterpoint, from the same company twelve hours later. Novartis this morning issued an ad hoc announcement that remibrutinib met the primary endpoint in both REMODEL-one and REMODEL-two, its identical Phase three trials in relapsing multiple sclerosis. Remibrutinib is a highly selective covalent oral Bruton's tyrosine kinase inhibitor — it blocks signaling in B cells and innate immune cells rather than deleting the B cells outright — and it beat teriflunomide on annualized relapse rate in both studies, plus every key secondary endpoint within each trial including M-R-I lesion counts. On disability, there was a positive trend on three-month confirmed progression and nominal significance on six-month confirmed progression in a preplanned combined analysis of the two trials. Roughly two thousand patients, randomized one-to-one, Expanded Disability Status Scale zero to five point five. The safety line is the commercially important one: no liver safety signal and no Hy's-law cases, across a program that now spans more than forty-five hundred trial participants. That matters because hepatotoxicity is what has dogged this class — it is why the F-D-A put a partial clinical hold on Sanofi's tolebrutinib over drug-induced liver injury, and why the whole B-T-K-in-M-S thesis has been treated as a safety bet at least as much as an efficacy bet. Two cautions. Novartis released no numbers — not the annualized relapse rates, not the hazard ratios — and the late-breaker is not until MSToronto twenty twenty-six, the joint A-C-T-R-I-M-S-E-C-T-R-I-M-S meeting on October twenty-first through twenty-third. So there are seven weeks between the claim and the data. And a positive trend on three-month progression that becomes only nominal significance at six months is a soft disability story, which is precisely the ground where the anti-C-D-twenty antibodies are strongest. Remibrutinib is already approved as Rhapsido in chronic spontaneous urticaria, F-D-A last September and E-M-A in April. Novartis will file in relapsing M-S globally. Note the shape: rap-cel's paused trials include both relapsing and progressive M-S, so Novartis de-risked its neuroimmunology franchise on the small-molecule side the morning after the cell-therapy side stopped.

Third, the deal. Lilly is acquiring Merida Biosciences for up to two point eight seven five billion dollars in cash, covering an upfront payment plus contingent milestones, with no breakdown disclosed. Closing is expected in the fourth quarter. Merida is a Massachusetts precision-immunology company seeded by Third Rock in twenty twenty-two and launched publicly in April twenty twenty-five with a hundred twenty-one million dollar Series A led by Bain Capital Life Sciences, B-V-F Partners and Third Rock, with G-V and the Perceptive Xontogeny funds also in. The lead asset, M-E-R five-one-one, is in Phase one for Graves' disease and thyroid eye disease. Both are driven by thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins — autoantibodies that agonize the T-S-H receptor — and Merida's approach is to selectively degrade the pathogenic antibody itself and deplete the B cells producing it, rather than suppress immunity broadly. Lilly also gets M-E-R seven-six-nine, a preclinical antibody aimed at the underlying biology of allergy, with intent in food allergy, asthma and chronic spontaneous urticaria, plus the platform. Francisco Ramírez-Valle, who runs Lilly's immunology research and early clinical development, framed the buy as a platform play across antibody-driven disease. B-M-O Capital Markets called it a strategic use of capital that diversifies the immunology and inflammation pipeline. Two things to hold. This is Lilly's thirteenth acquisition of twenty twenty-six — after AtaiBeckley at up to three point eight billion last month, Abbisko at up to one point nine billion, Centessa at six point three billion in March and Kelonia at seven billion in April, the year's business-development commitment is north of twenty-five billion dollars. And note the target class: thyroid eye disease is the tepezza franchise, an I-G-F-one-receptor antibody, and the pathogenic-antibody-degrader thesis is the same conceptual space that F-c-R-n blockers occupy from the other direction. Selective degradation of one autoantibody species, if it works, is a cleaner instrument than either.

Fourth, policy. The White House announced most-favored-nation pricing agreements with nine more manufacturers — Alcon, Astellas, BeOne Medicines, BridgeBio, C-S-L, Kyowa Kirin, Sun Pharma, Teva and U-C-B. Under the deals the companies will charge state Medicaid programs the same prices they charge foreign countries for certain outpatient drugs, and in exchange they get exemption from a comparable most-favored-nation program in Medicare plus protection from pharmaceutical tariffs. That brings the total to twenty-six manufacturers covering eighty-nine percent of the branded market. The notable shift is size: this round is midsize companies, not the top ten, which means the exemption-plus-tariff-shield trade is now being taken by companies with far less negotiating room, and the remaining eleven percent of the market is going to look increasingly exposed.

Fifth, and this one is close to home. A-R-C-H Venture Partners is out raising roughly three billion dollars for its fourteenth fund. Worth remembering that A-R-C-H and Foresite Labs were the joint incubators and lead investors behind Xaira's billion-dollar launch, so the fourteenth fund is the reload on the same early-stage, platform-and-A-I-heavy thesis. In a market where seed and Series A have been the thinnest part of the stack all year, a three-billion-dollar A-R-C-H fund is the single most consequential supply-side signal for company formation.

Sixth, Electra Therapeutics filed its S-one for a Nasdaq listing, seeking up to a hundred million dollars. Electra is a pan-S-I-R-P antibody company; the lead, ipsoprubart, selectively depletes pathologic myeloid cells and T cells and is in the registrational Phase two-slash-three SURPASS trial in secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis. Sanofi is an investor, and the company raised a hundred eighty-three million dollars previously. A second candidate, E-L-A eight-two-two, targeting activated T cells, started Phase one in healthy volunteers in Europe last month. There is an accidental resonance worth noting: the exact syndrome Electra is trying to treat, hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, is the disease that the C-A-R-T deaths are a drug-induced version of.

Seventh, eighth and ninth, the smaller wire. A-N-two Therapeutics presented clinical and translational data supporting epetraborole in polycythemia vera at the Society of Hematologic Oncology meeting — that is a leucyl-t-R-N-A synthetase inhibitor originally developed as an antibacterial, now being repurposed against a myeloproliferative neoplasm, which is an unusual mechanistic bet in a space where hepcidin mimetics and interferons own the conversation. P-M-V Pharma priced an oversubscribed fifty-million-dollar offering, which for a p-fifty-three Y-two-twenty-C reactivator company is a real vote of confidence in a mutant-selective reactivation thesis that has taken a long time to mature. And Canada's Drug Agency issued a final positive recommendation supporting public reimbursement of Leqembi, lecanemab, in early Alzheimer's disease — the anti-amyloid class continues to win reimbursement one national payer at a time.

Two things that did not happen: no material A-I-in-drug-discovery news since Friday, and no readouts of consequence beyond the ones above. The week ahead: the European Respiratory Society meets September fifth through ninth, where Generate Biomedicines' golukibart posters get their formal presentation; Ultragenyx has a September nineteenth action date in Sanfilippo A; Scholar Rock's apitegromab is September thirtieth; Capricor's deramiocel is November twenty-second; MSToronto is October twenty-first through twenty-third and now carries the remibrutinib late-breaker; Cytokinetics files in non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy in the fourth quarter; and Arrowhead files in severe hypertriglyceridemia before year-end.

The spotlight goes deep on the autoimmune C-A-R-T halt — what the hemophagocytic syndrome actually is and why it is not just severe cytokine release, why rapid manufacturing is the leading suspect, how much of this reads across to Cabaletta, Kyverna, Cartesian and the in vivo players versus how much is specific to two platforms, and why the differentiator the field is about to start paying for is control rather than potency.
