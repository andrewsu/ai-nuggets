First, and today's spotlight. At Psych Congress twenty twenty-six in New Orleans, which ran Tuesday through Saturday, Lilly presented on Friday its Phase one dose-selection data for brenipatide, its G-I-P and G-L-P-one dual receptor agonist, together with the designs of two Phase three programs — one in recurrent major depressive disorder, one in alcohol use disorder. Same receptor pair as tirzepatide, but engineered toward central nervous system and inflammatory pathways rather than metabolic ones, including the receptors implicated in reward and addiction. The detail that matters is buried in the Phase one design, and I will unpack it in the second episode, because it tells you exactly which scientific objection Lilly is trying to close.

Second, and this is a strange coincidence of timing, a company launched on Thursday that is attacking the same organ system from the opposite end. G-O-A Therapeutics came out of stealth in Dallas with fifteen and a half million dollars raised and more than a decade of quiet work behind it, unveiling G-O-A twenty-six for acute alcohol intoxication and poisoning. It is a dual-action intravenous agent — an engineered enzyme that accelerates ethanol metabolism paired with a chemical scavenger that sequesters acetaldehyde, which is the toxic intermediate you generate when you speed that metabolism up. In an I-N-D-enabling pig model dosed with one point two grams of ethanol per kilogram, blood alcohol concentration was sixty-one point three percent lower in treated animals than untreated controls at twenty minutes. Chief executive Andrew Altschuler's framing is that in an alcohol emergency, physicians currently stabilize the patient and wait for the body to clear the alcohol, and that wait can run many hours. His stated ambition is to make this as widely available as naloxone. The data were presented at the trauma surgeons' annual meeting, I-N-D is planned by year end, and first-in-human in twenty twenty-seven. There is no approved drug that rapidly lowers blood alcohol, so the regulatory path is genuinely open — the hard part will be showing that faster clearance changes outcomes rather than just numbers.

Third, a venture studio spun out its first company, and the target is one almost nobody is working on. Palomar Labs, formerly Negev Labs, launched Ariadne Bio on Thursday around A-B three hundred, a clinical-stage non-hallucinogenic serotonin two-A receptor agonist, aimed at apathy in Parkinson's disease. Shlomi Raz is chief executive; he previously founded Eleusis, which went to Beckley Psytech, then to Atai, and Lilly agreed to acquire Atai Beckley in July. So this is a psychedelics operator making a deliberate bet on the third generation of that class — compounds that hit the same receptor without the hallucinogenic pathway. A paper in Nature in January attributed that split to signaling bias at the receptor. The clinical thesis is the interesting part: apathy affects roughly forty percent of Parkinson's patients over the course of the disease, caregiver-burden studies rank it above motor symptoms as a driver of distress, there is no approved therapy for apathy in any indication, and the S-S-R-Is used for Parkinson's depression have been linked to emotional blunting that can make apathy worse. Avoiding dopamine entirely is the design choice, because dopaminergic stimulants are poorly tolerated in older adults.

Fourth, a non-incretin obesity readout that deserves more attention than it got. Syntis Bio reported the multiple-ascending-dose segment of its Phase one-Ib SYNTIETY-one trial of S-Y-N-T one-oh-one on Wednesday — twenty-three overweight or obese adults, twenty-eight days, three dose groups. No withdrawals, no dose reductions, and gastrointestinal adverse events at placebo-like rates. The mechanism is the story: the drug forms a temporary lining in the duodenum and is designed explicitly not to enter systemic circulation. Participants showed two- to five-fold increases in G-L-P-one and peptide Y-Y and reductions in ghrelin, which chief development officer David Rosenbaum characterized as mimicking the hormonal signature of gastric bypass through a once-daily tablet. Weight loss beat placebo in all three cohorts and was described as comparable to G-L-P-one therapies over the same period. Twenty-three patients over twenty-eight days is not an efficacy claim, but a locally acting oral that produces a bariatric hormone profile without systemic exposure is a genuinely different shot on goal, and the tolerability profile is the whole pitch.

Fifth, staying in obesity, a preclinical result with an unusual shape. Artelo Biosciences reported Wednesday that A-R-T twenty-seven point one three, a peripherally selective dual cannabinoid receptor one and two agonist originally out of AstraZeneca, cut body weight roughly twenty percent over four weeks in diet-induced obese mice, matching semaglutide as monotherapy. Combined with semaglutide it produced about forty percent weight loss — roughly double either alone, taking the animals down to lean body weight. Composition of the loss favored the cannabinoid arm, around eighty percent fat versus about seventy percent for semaglutide alone. And it did nothing at all in lean controls, which is the finding that makes it interesting: an agent that only acts on the obese metabolic state. Mice are mice, and the cannabinoid receptor field carries the rimonabant psychiatric legacy, though peripheral selectivity is the entire point of this generation. Worth a line in your combination-therapy file, not more.

Sixth, an artificial intelligence result in oncology trials that is about endpoints rather than discovery. Altis Labs presented data at the lung cancer meeting in Seoul showing that its automated imaging system, called IPRO, ran about ten thousand radiology scans from Johnson and Johnson's Phase three MARIPOSA study — amivantamab plus lazertinib against osimertinib in first-line E-G-F-R-mutated non-small-cell lung cancer. Rather than automating RECIST, IPRO reads three-dimensional scans for tumor burden, body composition and organ health, and predicts survival directly. It favored the investigational arm from week sixteen onward, while RECIST-based objective response rate never anticipated the eventual overall survival benefit. Founder Felix Baldauf-Lenschen's claim is that artificial intelligence can see meaningful clinical benefit that conventional imaging endpoints miss. This is a retrospective analysis of a trial that already read out, so it proves concordance and not prospective validity — but with the F-D-A pushing overall survival as a pre-specified endpoint in every cancer trial, anything that gives you an early, survival-correlated read on a trial in flight has obvious value for go, no-go decisions.

Seventh, two European label extensions from Friday's committee meeting that are commercially larger than the twelve new approvals I covered yesterday. The European regulator's human medicines committee recommended Enhertu, trastuzumab deruxtecan from Daiichi Sankyo and AstraZeneca, as adjuvant monotherapy for resected H-E-R-two-positive breast cancer with residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant treatment. That rests on DESTINY-Breast-oh-five, which cut the risk of invasive disease recurrence or death by fifty-three percent against T-D-M-one, hazard ratio zero point four seven, with three-year invasive disease-free survival of ninety-two point four percent versus eighty-three point seven. Interstitial lung disease ran nine point six percent, mostly low grade, with two fatal cases. That moves the leading H-E-R-two antibody-drug conjugate into curative-intent treatment in Europe. Separately, Merck got a positive opinion for Keytruda plus Padcev as perioperative treatment in resectable muscle-invasive bladder cancer regardless of cisplatin eligibility, on KEYNOTE-B-fifteen — event-free survival hazard ratio zero point five three, overall survival hazard ratio zero point six five, and a pathologic complete response rate of fifty-five point eight percent versus thirty-two point five for neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Commission decision expected in the fourth quarter.

Eighth, briefly, China's regulator cleared Akeso's A-K one fifty-eight D one into Phase one in solid tumors on Friday. It is the company's fourth antibody-drug conjugate to reach the clinic. Akeso is the ivonescimab company, so the read-through is that the P-D-one-V-E-G-F bispecific is not the whole pipeline, and the A-D-C engine behind it is now producing at a steady clip.
