Third, a cautionary tale in manufacturing. Xspray Pharma took its fourth complete response letter for Dasynoc, an amorphous-solid-dispersion reformulation of Bristol Myers Squibb's dasatinib, the BCR-ABL kinase inhibitor sold as Sprycel. The point of the product is better, less variable absorption without the acid-reducer interaction that constrains the original. The FDA raised no questions about clinical data, bioequivalence or stability. The problem is, again, the contract manufacturer, NerPharMa in Italy, and the agency now wants additional commercial-scale batch data. That's rejections in twenty twenty-three, twenty twenty-four, October twenty twenty-five and now. Management says it will resubmit before year end. The lesson worth carrying: when your entire value proposition is a formulation, your C-M-C file is your clinical file, and outsourcing it to a single site is the whole risk.

Fourth, a first-in-human worth flagging in a crowded mechanism. AC Immune reported interim phase one data on ACI-nineteen-seven-six-four, an oral, brain-penetrant small-molecule inhibitor of the NLRP3 inflammasome, with in vitro potency in the low nanomolar range. Safe and tolerated through single and multiple ascending doses up to twenty milligrams a day, serum half-life over thirty hours, no serious adverse events, no withdrawals, dose-dependent suppression of interleukin one beta release as proof of target engagement, and measurable drug in cerebrospinal fluid. That last point is the differentiator: most NLRP3 programs are peripheral, and the reason to want a C-N-S-penetrant one is that inflammasome activation in microglia is a candidate driver in neurodegeneration. AC Immune is pointing the phase one-b at type two diabetes and obesity with cardiovascular risk first, with more data in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. Keep it in your peripheral vision rather than your foreground.

Fifth, an investigator-initiated result that is more interesting than its sample size. Mount Sinai reported the ASPIRE trial, a single-arm phase one-two in hormone-receptor-positive, HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer, testing a completely chemotherapy-free four-drug regimen: anastrozole to shut down estrogen synthesis, palbociclib against C-D-K four and six, and trastuzumab plus pertuzumab against two different HER2 epitopes. Among twenty-nine evaluable patients, ninety-seven percent had clinical benefit, median progression-free survival was just under twenty-five months, and median overall survival was not reached after more than thirty-nine months, with one discontinuation and no deaths. Twenty-nine patients, no control arm, so this is a hypothesis, not a practice change. But the hypothesis is the right one: in the double-positive subset, the two pathways feed each other, and hitting both may make the taxane optional. That matters most for exactly the patients least able to tolerate chemotherapy.

Lilly is running near twenty-three billion in that franchise, up forty-eight percent. The read: the clinical hierarchy and the commercial hierarchy have converged on tirzepatide, and the binding constraint on the whole category is now price and persistence rather than efficacy, which is precisely why the oral formulations matter more for their cost structure than for their weight loss.
