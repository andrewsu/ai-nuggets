The F-D-A approved Cogent's bezuclastinib, the first new second-line drug for G-I-S-T in twenty years, and that's today's spotlight. AbbVie's Rinvoq became the first pill approved for vitiligo. Kymera raised one point seven five billion dollars the day after its eczema data. The F-D-A approved PolyPid's slow-release antibiotic for colorectal surgery and made two transparency moves. Bristol Myers is losing its finance chief, and Caribou is winding down. Here's what you need to know.

First, Cogent. On Tuesday morning, six days before its November thirtieth goal date, the F-D-A approved Ekitna, or bezuclastinib, with the old generic sunitinib, for advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumors after imatinib. Bezuclastinib is a KIT inhibitor built to hit the activation-loop mutations that sunitinib misses, so the two together cover most of the ways these tumors escape imatinib. In the Phase three PEAK trial, the pair cut the risk of progression or death in half against sunitinib alone. Median progression-free survival was sixteen and a half months against nine point two. Cogent launched the same day at a list price of just over forty-nine thousand dollars a month. The stock closed up seventeen percent at thirty-three dollars and one cent. The label carries a boxed warning for liver toxicity, and that matters more for Cogent's next decision, in mastocytosis on December thirtieth. That's the spotlight.

Second, an approval from Monday evening that we didn't catch yesterday. The F-D-A approved AbbVie's Rinvoq, or upadacitinib, for nonsegmental vitiligo in patients twelve and older. It's the first systemic drug approved in the U-S for the disease. Rinvoq blocks JAK-one. In vitiligo, interferon-gamma signals through the JAK-STAT pathway to draw in the T cells that destroy pigment cells, so blocking JAK lets the skin slowly repigment. In the two Phase three Viti-Up trials, nineteen and twenty-one percent of patients regained at least half of their total-body pigment at forty-eight weeks, against six percent on placebo. Response rates roughly doubled by week seventy-six. Those are modest numbers that build slowly, and Rinvoq carries the JAK class boxed warning. But until now the only approved drug was Incyte's Opzelura, a topical JAK cream. Incyte's oral povorcitinib and Pfizer's ritlecitinib are still in Phase three. Europe approved Rinvoq for vitiligo in July.

Third, Kymera cashed in. After Monday's Phase two-b eczema data for KT-six-twenty-one, its oral STAT-six degrader, Kymera priced an upsized offering of one point seven five billion dollars. It sold about eleven point six million shares at one hundred fifty dollars and thirty-seven cents, which was Monday's closing price, so there was no discount. Morgan Stanley and J-P Morgan led a syndicate of seven banks, and the deal closes today. Endpoints called it historic, and it is one of the largest follow-on offerings a biotech has ever done. The money means Kymera can run Phase three in eczema and asthma itself, without a partner.

Fourth, PolyPid. On Tuesday the F-D-A approved Bacfenssi, a doxycycline paste that surgeons apply at wound closure. It releases the antibiotic locally for about thirty days to reduce surgical-site infections after colorectal surgery. The approval came ahead of the November twenty-eighth goal date. In the Phase three SHIELD-two trial of about eight hundred patients, infections fell from nine point five percent with standard care to three point eight percent with the paste on top. It is PolyPid's first approved product. Azurity Pharmaceuticals will sell it in the U-S starting early next year.

Fifth, two quieter moves from the F-D-A. Starting Monday, patient information leaflets for drugs cleared through accelerated approval have to say so and point patients to an F-D-A explainer. The prescribing information always said it. The leaflet patients actually take home did not, and Endpoints reports PhRMA had pushed back on this for years. Separately, STAT reports the agency issued its first notices of noncompliance in more than a year for trial results never posted to Clinical Trials dot gov. Addex got two notices, for Parkinson's trials of dipraglurant, and Duchesnay got one, for a study of Diclegis in pregnant adolescents. Both companies had started submitting results and never finished, and the F-D-A says that doesn't count. Penalties are possible but haven't been announced.

Sixth, more turnover at Bristol Myers Squibb. Chief financial officer David Elkins, who came over with the seventy-four billion dollar Celgene deal, leaves March first to become C-F-O of Coca-Cola. Kevin Dillon, head of corporate finance, takes over. It comes less than two weeks after research head Robert Plenge announced his exit and B-M-S merged research and development under development chief Cristian Massacesi. That puts new people in key jobs just as Eliquis and Opdivo, about half of revenue, head toward patent expiry. B-M-S reaffirmed its 2026 guidance.

And a sad end for a CRISPR pioneer. Berkeleyside reports Caribou Biosciences, which Jennifer Doudna co-founded, is winding down. It stopped its off-the-shelf CRISPR-edited CAR-T programs in October after it couldn't fund a Phase three lymphoma trial. Most of its roughly ninety staff leave in early December. Caribou had about ninety-five million dollars in cash at the end of September and says it is still weighing a merger, sale or liquidation.
