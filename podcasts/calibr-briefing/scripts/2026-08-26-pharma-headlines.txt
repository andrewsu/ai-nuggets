Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, twenty twenty-six.

Yesterday delivered the approval the whole oncology calendar had been pointing at, and it is bigger than a label expansion — it is the first time in sixteen years that anything has displaced trastuzumab from the front line of a gastrointestinal cancer. Around it, a China NewCo built by two American venture firms, Bristol Myers walking away from the flagship customer relationship in automated cell therapy manufacturing, a third Alzheimer's blood test cleared, and a banker putting a number on how close the I-P-O window is to staying open. Eight items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight. The F-D-A approved two Ziihera-containing regimens from Jazz Pharmaceuticals for first-line, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic H-E-R-two-positive gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma. Ziihera is zanidatamab, a bispecific H-E-R-two antibody that binds two different sites on the same receptor. The approved regimens are zanidatamab plus Tevimbra — that is tislelizumab, BeOne Medicines' P-D-one antibody — plus fluoropyrimidine and platinum chemotherapy, for both I-H-C three-plus and I-H-C two-plus slash I-S-H-positive disease; and zanidatamab plus chemotherapy alone for I-H-C three-plus. The registrational trial is HERIZON-G-E-A-oh-one, nine hundred fourteen patients, more than thirty countries, published in the New England Journal of Medicine earlier this year. Median progression-free survival was twelve point four months for both zanidatamab arms against eight point one months for trastuzumab plus chemotherapy. Median overall survival in the triplet arm was twenty-six point four months against nineteen point two, a hazard ratio of zero point seven two. Jazz's Rob Iannone called it the longest median overall survival ever reported in a phase three in this setting, and he is right. R-B-C's Leonid Timashev sees a market north of one and a half billion dollars; Stifel's Annabel Samimy models six hundred fifty to seven hundred million dollars in U.S. gastroesophageal sales and points out the eligible population goes from about fifteen hundred patients in biliary tract cancer to roughly twelve thousand. Zymeworks, which invented the molecule in Vancouver, collected a two hundred fifty million dollar milestone on the approval. We take the whole thing apart in the spotlight, including the one subgroup number that is either the most important result in the dataset or the least trustworthy.

Second, a China NewCo with an American venture signature on it. Haisco Pharmaceutical granted exclusive global rights outside Greater China to a preclinical immunology asset to Sentivera, a brand-new U.S. company founded by Population Health Partners and ARCH Venture Partners. The consideration is seventy-five point nine million dollars in cash up front plus equity in Sentivera, up to one point four six billion dollars in development, regulatory and commercial milestones, and tiered royalties from mid-single digits into the low double digits. The target is not disclosed. What is disclosed is the indication set: type two inflammatory disease — asthma, atopic dermatitis, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps, eosinophilic esophagitis — and a Chinese I-N-D clearance earlier this month. Haisco is explicit that this is a NewCo structure rather than a straight license-out, which is the interesting part: Haisco keeps an equity position and therefore keeps the upside if Sentivera works, instead of selling the asset and watching someone else mark it up. This is Haisco's fourth large Western transaction of the year, after roughly three billion dollars with Lilly and one point four billion with Nuvectis in June, and seven hundred fifteen million with AbbVie in April. The signal for you is on the venture side, not the pharma side: ARCH is now doing what the specialists have been doing, which is treating Chinese preclinical immunology as the cheapest source of clinical-ready assets on earth.

Third, the hardest blow yet to automated cell therapy manufacturing. Bristol Myers Squibb has terminated its relationship with Cellares. A B-M-S spokesperson said the Cell Shuttle system could not meet the necessary requirements to manufacture commercial batches of Breyanzi, the C-D-nineteen C-A-R-T. Cellares C-E-O Fabian Gerlinghaus announced layoffs on LinkedIn without naming the customer; a W-A-R-N filing obtained by BioPharma Dive puts the number at roughly a hundred employees. The two companies signed a three hundred eighty million dollar capacity reservation in twenty twenty-four, and Cellares has raised on the thesis that closed, cassette-based automation is how autologous cell therapy escapes its cost structure. This is the first time a large pharma has said publicly that the box did not clear commercial G-M-P. It does not falsify the thesis, but it does mean the burden of proof has moved back to the vendors — and every allogeneic and in-vivo C-A-R-T program just got a slightly better argument.

Fourth, a third Alzheimer's blood test through the F-D-A. Roche and Lilly received clearance for the Elecsys p-Tau-two-seventeen assay, a single-biomarker plasma test for amyloid pathology in people fifty-five and over who already have cognitive symptoms. It carries validated cutoffs meant to work identically in primary care and in specialty clinics, which is the whole point — P-E-T and C-S-F remain effectively unavailable outside academic centers. Labcorp says nationwide availability in the coming months; Quest launches its A-D-Detect version in the fourth quarter. This is the third cleared blood test in the space, after Fujirebio's Lumipulse in May of last year and C-two-N's Precivity-A-D-two this month. Three cleared tests in sixteen months is a diagnostics market forming in real time, and it is the rate-limiting step on lecanemab and donanemab volumes.

Fifth, a number on the I-P-O window. Leerink's Jack Bannister told BioSpace the biotech I-P-O market is approaching escape velocity: twenty-five deals year to date, five of them in August alone, on pace for about thirty by year end. For scale, twenty twenty-one produced seventy-eight and twenty twenty-five produced eight, so this is a return to twenty eighteen and twenty nineteen normal rather than a bubble. Every August deal is trading above its offer price. His two yellow flags are worth writing down: preclinical companies rushing the window, and teams pushing valuations too hard. His read on why this cycle is healthier is that conviction is showing up as concentrated hundred-million-dollar-plus commitments rather than as broad momentum buying.

Sixth, the F-D-A's expedited I-N-D pilot is getting a real fight over governance. The agency's proposal would create a network of qualified research institutions — academic medical centers, health systems, C-R-Os, regulatory advisers — that would co-develop and review first-in-human protocols alongside sponsors before the I-N-D goes to the agency. Endpoints reports that Amgen and the academic centers, Memorial Sloan Kettering and Cleveland Clinic among them, are diverging on exactly the question you would expect: whether an institution that helps design and review a protocol can also run the trial, and how conflicts of interest get walled off. Amgen's own real-time-review pilot is the phase one-b STREAM-S-C-L-C study of Imdelltra, tarlatamab, its D-L-L-three by C-D-three T-cell engager, in limited-stage small cell lung cancer. Watch this one — if it works it compresses the I-N-D-enabling timeline for everyone, and if the conflict question is not solved cleanly it will not work at all.

Seventh, Bausch and Lomb is taking its dry eye candidate into phase three despite missing the primary endpoint in the mid-stage study, according to an Endpoints exclusive. That is the second time this week we have seen a sponsor advance on a missed primary. It is becoming a pattern, and the pattern has a common structure: the company argues the endpoint or the population was wrong rather than the molecule.

Eighth, Lilly launched Foundayo — orforglipron, the oral small-molecule G-L-P-one agonist — in the United Kingdom. The oral obesity race is now an ex-U.S. race as much as a domestic one, and given that seventy-four percent of U.S. discontinuations get attributed to cost, the geographies where a pill can be priced against a national payer rather than a pharmacy benefit manager are where the volume thesis actually gets tested.

The week ahead. Gilead's once-daily oral bictegravir plus lenacapavir combination has an action date tomorrow, the twenty-seventh. The European Society of Cardiology congress opens in Munich on Friday and runs through Monday, with AstraZeneca, Cytokinetics and Novartis all carrying late-breakers. And Jazz hosts its investor webcast on the zanidatamab development program, which is where you will hear what indications they are chasing next.
