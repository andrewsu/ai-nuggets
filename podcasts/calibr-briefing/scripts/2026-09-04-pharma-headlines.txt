Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Friday, September fourth, twenty twenty-six.

After three days of wreckage — Alumis in lupus, Ultragenyx in Angelman syndrome, and a CAR-T safety halt before that — Thursday broke the streak. Ionis got the first approval ever granted in Alexander disease. Summit and Akeso put an overall survival win on the board against Keytruda. AbbVie cleared Phase three in myeloma. GSK spent a hundred ten million dollars up front on a molecule that has not yet been given to a human being. And Revolution Medicines published lung cancer data in the New England Journal that reframes what its newly approved drug is actually worth. Ten items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight. The Food and Drug Administration approved Zanvastro, zilganersen, from Ionis, as the first and only disease-modifying treatment for Alexander disease, in children and adults. Alexander disease is an ultra-rare leukodystrophy — roughly one in a million to one in three million people, fewer than a thousand patients in the United States — caused by gain-of-function mutations in G-F-A-P, the intermediate filament protein that gives astrocytes their skeleton. Mutant G-F-A-P accumulates into aggregates called Rosenthal fibers, the astrocytes stop doing their job, and white matter fails behind them. Zanvastro is an antisense oligonucleotide delivered into the spinal fluid that lowers G-F-A-P production, dosed at fifty milligrams once every twelve weeks. The pivotal study randomized fifty-four participants aged one and a half to fifty-three, across thirteen sites in eight countries, two-to-one against a matching intrathecal placebo, double-blind for sixty weeks. Gait speed on the ten-meter walk test stabilized in treated patients while the control arm declined, a mean difference of thirty-three point three percent with a p-value of zero point zero four one. Plasma G-F-A-P fell thirty-three point six percent versus control. The approval came nineteen days ahead of the September twenty-second action date and carried a rare pediatric disease priority review voucher with it. William Blair had modeled about two hundred ninety-five million dollars in peak annual sales. Ionis closed down five point two percent at fifty-eight thirteen — the approval was expected — and recovered a couple of percent overnight. Recordati has rights outside the United States with European and Japanese submissions planned for twenty twenty-seven. The spotlight takes this apart, because coming twenty-four hours after the Angelman failure, it isolates exactly which variable killed that program.

Second, Summit Therapeutics and Akeso reported that ivonescimab — the P-D-one by V-E-G-F bispecific antibody — met its key secondary endpoint of overall survival against Keytruda in HARMONi-two, the Chinese Phase three in first-line P-D-L-one-positive non-small cell lung cancer. No hazard ratio yet; the detailed data go to a medical meeting. This matters because the original HARMONi-two readout in May twenty twenty-four was a progression-free survival win — eleven point one months against five point eight — and the standing objection was that radiographic progression in an anti-V-E-G-F regimen can flatter a drug that shrinks tumors without extending lives. Survival answers that objection on its own terms. The caveat is unchanged and it is a real one: this is a China-only trial, and the global HARMONi-three study's interim progression-free survival analysis failed to reach significance back in May, which is what pushed the regulatory timeline out. Summit rose more than seven percent to fifteen sixty-five. Jefferies wrote that future lung cancer regimens may increasingly be judged against ivonescimab, while cautioning that the China results still have to translate. The Food and Drug Administration decision in E-G-F-R-mutant lung cancer comes in November.

Third, GSK licensed HMPL-A-eight-thirty from HUTCHMED for a hundred ten million dollars up front and up to one point one eight five billion in milestones — call it one point three billion all in — plus tiered royalties, for worldwide rights outside mainland China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan. The molecule is worth understanding because it is a genuinely different shape of drug. HUTCHMED calls it an antibody-targeted therapy conjugate: instead of a cytotoxic payload, the anti-E-G-F-R antibody carries a selective small-molecule K-RAS inhibitor. So you get tumor-selective delivery of a RAS inhibitor to cells that overexpress E-G-F-R, and you block both pathways at once — which is notable given that E-G-F-R reactivation is the canonical resistance mechanism when you inhibit K-RAS in colorectal cancer. Initial indications are colorectal, pancreatic and lung, where K-RAS mutation rates run roughly forty-four percent, up to eighty-nine percent, and thirty-four percent respectively. Here is the striking part: the global Phase one has not started. It is expected in the second half of this year. GSK paid a hundred ten million dollars up front for a preclinical asset, which tells you both how tight the competition for differentiated RAS mechanisms has become and how far the price of Chinese-originated assets has moved this year.

Fourth, AbbVie reported a Phase three win for etentamig in multiple myeloma. Etentamig is a second-generation B-C-M-A by C-D-three T-cell engager, engineered with a high-avidity B-C-M-A arm and a deliberately low-affinity C-D-three arm to blunt cytokine release, and it retains neonatal Fc receptor binding so it can be dosed monthly after step-up. The CERVINO trial randomized three hundred ninety-three heavily pretreated patients — median three prior lines, all exposed to proteasome inhibitors, immunomodulators and anti-C-D-thirty-eight antibodies — against investigator's choice. Objective response rate was seventy-four percent versus forty-six; progression-free survival improved sixty percent; twelve-month overall survival was eighty-eight percent against seventy-two. Cytokine release syndrome ran twenty-eight percent, predominantly grade one. Grade three or four infections were higher on etentamig, twenty-eight percent against nineteen, but fatal infections were lower, one point five percent against three point one. That safety shape is the whole competitive argument: J and J's Tecvayli and Pfizer's Elrexfio are already approved, Pfizer paused enrollment over peripheral neuropathy, and both Amgen and Bristol Myers discontinued B-C-M-A programs. AbbVie's pitch is a bispecific that community oncologists can actually give monthly in an outpatient setting.

Fifth, and relevant to the Foresite portfolio, Revolution Medicines published its Phase one-two lung cancer data in the New England Journal of Medicine. In the pivotal-relevant slice — thirty-eight docetaxel-naive patients at a hundred sixty to two hundred twenty milligrams, all previously treated with platinum chemotherapy and a P-D-one or P-D-L-one agent — daraxonrasib produced a forty-two percent response rate, median progression-free survival of eight point three months, and median overall survival of sixteen months. No grade four or five treatment-related events; grade three in twenty-five percent, mostly rash and diarrhea. Truist called that cohort the most Phase-three-relevant subgroup. RAS mutations drive roughly thirty percent of non-small cell lung cancer and there is no targeted option today outside K-RAS G-twelve-C. Rasonque was approved in pancreatic cancer just over a week ago; the lung indication runs through RASolve three-oh-one against chemotherapy, reading out December twenty twenty-seven. That is the difference between a niche second-line pancreatic drug and a franchise.

Sixth, on artificial intelligence in drug discovery: Superluminal Medicines closed an oversubscribed sixty-million-dollar Series B led by B-V-F Partners, with R-A Capital, Nvidia and Eli Lilly participating, bringing total venture funding to two hundred thirteen million. Superluminal's platform models G-protein-coupled receptor activation — the conformational dynamics of turning a receptor on, not just static docking — and its lead program is an M-C-four-R agonist for genetic obesity, starting in Bardet-Biedl syndrome and hypothalamic obesity, with Prader-Willi and broader obesity behind it. Clinic by year end. The incumbents are Rhythm's Imcivree, itself an M-C-four-R agonist, and Neurocrine's Vykat X-R. Chief executive Cony D'Cruz framed the differentiation as tolerability rather than potency — efficacy alone is not enough, and a chronic therapy has to combine weight and hyperphagia effects with a safety profile you can live on. That is the right thing for a structure-based platform to be claiming, and the receptor activation problem is a defensible place to point machine learning.

Seventh, Pfizer found a home for another discarded Seagen asset. Medicus Pharma licensed P-F-oh-eight-oh-four-six-oh-three-one, a C-D-two-two-eight-directed antibody-drug conjugate, for twelve million dollars up front and another fifteen million a year from now, against more than a billion in milestones plus royalties. C-D-two-two-eight is melanotransferrin, highly expressed in melanoma and several other solid tumors. Pfizer had discontinued the program earlier this year in its broader reassessment of the forty-three-billion-dollar Seagen portfolio, and is contributing two million dollars and staying on as co-developer. Twelve million dollars up front for a clinical-stage A-D-C from Seagen's bench is a striking price, and it says something about how much of that acquisition Pfizer is now willing to let go at salvage value.

Eighth, Novartis signed an option and license agreement with Alteogen worth up to three point two billion dollars plus royalties for A-L-T-B-four, berahyaluronidase alfa, under the Hybrozyme platform — the enzyme that transiently depolymerizes hyaluronan so an intravenous biologic can be given subcutaneously. Novartis takes options across multiple products. This is Alteogen's fourth licensing deal this year, and it is the clearest sign yet that subcutaneous conversion has become a standard lifecycle instrument rather than a Halozyme monopoly.
