Welcome to the spotlight, Travis. Today it's Iovance, the tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte company, and Thursday's update on its lung cancer program. Iovance called the data positive. The market took a third off the stock. The reason sits in one table, and it's a lesson for anyone developing a cell therapy in solid tumors.

Start with the product. Lifileucel, sold as Amtagvi in melanoma, is a TIL therapy. Surgeons remove a piece of the patient's tumor, Iovance grows up the T cells that had already infiltrated it, and those cells go back into the patient as a single infusion. Before the infusion comes lymphodepleting chemotherapy, and afterward high-dose interleukin-two. The idea is that a polyclonal T-cell product, already trained on the patient's own tumor, can recognize many tumor antigens at once. Amtagvi is Iovance's commercial base. In September the company raised its twenty twenty-six revenue guidance to four hundred ten to four hundred twenty million dollars. Lung cancer is the expansion it has been promising for years.

The trial is IOV-LUN-two-oh-two. It enrolls patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer whose disease progressed after both platinum chemotherapy and a checkpoint inhibitor. That setting has few good options. As of an October fourteenth data cutoff, seventeen of sixty-nine patients with non-squamous disease responded. That's twenty-four point six percent, and it includes two unconfirmed partial responses. Last November, in the first thirty-nine patients, the rate was twenty-five point six. So the headline number held as the trial grew.

Then comes the table that matters. Iovance went back through the genomics after the fact. In patients whose tumors had normal, or wild-type, KRAS, thirteen of thirty responded, forty-three percent. In KRAS-mutant tumors, four of thirty-five responded, eleven percent. Iovance then removed patients with HER-two alterations, which left thirteen of twenty-five. Then it kept only current or former smokers, which left thirteen of twenty-two. That's fifty-nine percent, and both complete responses were in that group. So under a third of the patients produced more than three quarters of the responses. Iovance is now starting a new cohort called POSITILITY, which will enroll only patients who meet all three criteria. It starts in November at more than seventy existing sites and is meant to be registrational, subject to F-D-A feedback. Iovance also plans a frontline trial in the same enriched population next year. In a small frontline cohort, six of seven such patients responded to lifileucel plus pembrolizumab, though HER-two wasn't checked in those patients.

Now, the market. Iovance closed at nine fifty-six, down thirty-five percent, on about six times its normal volume. It was the stock's worst day since May of last year, and it came after a run of more than three hundred percent this year. Some of that is expectations. On Wednesday evening Iovance announced a webcast to discuss "positive data", and the stock rose more than ten percent after hours. What arrived was a flat response rate and a new subgroup story. But three substantive things moved too.

The first is durability. Last November Iovance said the median duration of response had not been reached after twenty-five months of follow-up. This time the release gave no duration figure at all. On the webcast, interim chief executive Fred Vogt said the data are immature, that the swimmer plot appears to show responses lasting roughly a year so far, and that a final median of eighteen months or more could be "reasonable." Investor's Business Daily summed it up as new durability questions. For a one-time therapy that carries chemotherapy and interleukin-two toxicity, durability is the whole value case. Without a median, the chart is the only evidence.

The second is timing. Last November Iovance guided to a launch in the second half of twenty twenty-seven. Now it plans to file a supplemental application during twenty twenty-seven. The final data cutoff is early next year, and Vogt said the F-D-A generally wants six months of follow-up after the last response. A decision could slip toward late twenty twenty-seven or into twenty twenty-eight.

The third is the most important, and it's the subgroup analysis itself. Look at how the numbers move. KRAS wild-type gives thirteen of thirty. Adding HER-two gives thirteen of twenty-five. Adding smoking history gives thirteen of twenty-two. The numerator never changes. Each new filter removed only non-responders, and none of the thirteen responders. That's what you get when you choose the cuts after looking at the outcomes. Every step can only raise the rate, because the rule was picked to raise it. The sample is also small. Thirteen of twenty-two has an exact ninety-five percent confidence interval of roughly thirty-six to seventy-nine percent. So the honest prior for POSITILITY isn't fifty-nine percent. It's somewhere between the forty-three percent KRAS result and the twenty-five percent overall rate, until someone enrolls prospectively and finds out.

To be fair to Iovance, the biology is coherent, and that's why this isn't just data dredging. Oncogenic KRAS signaling has been linked in lung cancer models to weaker antigen presentation and an immunosuppressive tumor environment. The company cites a twenty twenty-two Science Advances paper showing that KRAS G-twelve-C inhibition switched on interferon-driven antitumor immunity. Smoking-associated tumors carry more mutations, which means more neoantigens for a polyclonal TIL product to see. The KRAS split is also the cleanest number in the table: forty-three versus eleven percent, defined by a test that is already routine in lung cancer. If any of this replicates, I'd bet on KRAS. The HER-two and smoking refinements are the parts I'd discount most.

There's a less obvious cost to publishing the split. The broad sBLA, covering all non-squamous patients, is still the near-term plan. Now the F-D-A has an eleven percent response rate in KRAS-mutant patients in front of it. Iovance's own materials put docetaxel, the standard second-line chemotherapy, at about thirteen percent in this setting. A reviewer will ask why a KRAS-mutant patient should go through tumor surgery, lymphodepletion and interleukin-two to get chemotherapy-level response odds. By showing the label might work best narrow, Iovance may have made the broad label harder to get. That may be the real reason for the thirty-five percent.

On the competition, the bar after chemo and immunotherapy is low but rising. Daiichi and AstraZeneca's TROP-two antibody-drug conjugate, datopotamab deruxtecan, beat docetaxel on progression-free survival in TROPION-Lung-oh-one, but not on overall survival. Merck said on Thursday's earnings call that it plans to go "quite aggressively" into lung cancer with sac-T-M-T, its own TROP-two A-D-C. Those are off-the-shelf drugs given in an infusion chair. Lifileucel needs surgery, a manufacturing slot and an inpatient stay. To win, a one-time cell therapy has to deliver responses that are deeper and much longer lasting, not just a higher rate in a selected group. That's why the missing duration number matters more than the fifty-nine percent.

A note on sentiment. I couldn't find a named analyst note dated after the data. The latest ratings I found were from earlier in October: Barclays raised its target to eighteen dollars, and Goldman had resumed coverage at Buy with a fifteen-dollar target. Both are now well above the stock. One equity research blog pointed out that the KRAS-mutant rate is below docetaxel's historical benchmark. And there's a governance point. Fred Vogt, Iovance's former general counsel, became interim chief executive in May twenty twenty-one, when Maria Fardis resigned after an F-D-A setback on the potency assay. He still holds the interim title five years later, while the company tries to turn one approved product into a platform.

What should you take from this? First, Iovance is now really two programs. One is a broad lung filing that the company's own subgroup data weakens. The other is a biomarker-selected study whose hypothesis was built from thirteen responders. Second, the useful lesson isn't to avoid enrichment. Prospective enrichment on a biologically grounded, routinely tested marker is exactly right. The lesson is to look at how the hypothesis was built. When each added filter keeps the same numerator and only shrinks the denominator, read the final rate as an upper bound, not an estimate. That applies to any cell therapy or immuno-oncology program heading into solid tumors, Calibr's switchable platforms included. Nobody will believe a subgroup until it holds up prospectively.

What to watch: Iovance's third-quarter results in early November for Amtagvi demand and any F-D-A feedback on POSITILITY, the first enrollment numbers for the enriched cohort, and the early twenty twenty-seven data cutoff, which should finally give a median duration of response. Until then, the market is treating fifty-nine percent as a hypothesis, which is the right call.
