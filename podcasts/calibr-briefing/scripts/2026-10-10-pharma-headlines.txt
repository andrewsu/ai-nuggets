First, Chai and G-S-K. Chai Discovery is the San Francisco company founded in twenty twenty-four by four people out of OpenAI, Meta's F-A-I-R lab and Stripe, backed by Index, Kleiner Perkins, Sequoia and OpenAI itself. G-S-K now gets access to Chai's platform — the structure prediction models and the design models — across its discovery pipeline. The detail that matters is how the deal happened. Chai's models generated designs against G-S-K targets with no target-specific training, G-S-K tested them in its own laboratories, and found binders to all the targets it ran. Second, Revolution Medicines. The F-D-A posted its review documents for Rasonque, the RAS-on multi-selective inhibitor approved in August for previously treated pancreatic cancer, and the stock fell between six and eight percent across Thursday and into Friday. The issue is a mutation-subtype split. Response rate in the K-RAS G-twelve-D subgroup came in at twenty-four percent, against fifty percent in G-twelve-V. The agency noted the subgroup analyses were not designed for formal comparisons, which is the correct caveat and also the reason nobody can resolve the question from this data set. It matters commercially because G-twelve-D is the dominant mutation in pancreatic cancer, so a real efficacy gradient would shrink the addressable population inside the approved indication and leave an opening for G-twelve-D-selective competitors. Analysts pushed back on the selloff. R-B-C called it overdone, pointing to the overall survival result that drove the approval — Rasonque nearly doubled survival versus chemotherapy — and kept an Outperform with a two hundred fifty-nine dollar target. Truist raised its target to two hundred forty. Hold that G-twelve-D versus G-twelve-V distinction, because it comes back in the spotlight from an unexpected direction.

Fourth, schizophrenia. The F-D-A approved Weltruza, Teva's once-monthly olanzapine extended-release injectable, built on Medincell's B-E-P-O polymer depot technology. The clinical differentiator is not efficacy — it matches oral olanzapine, as you would expect from a reformulation of a sixty-year-old antipsychotic pharmacology. It is the administration. Weltruza is subcutaneous, needs no loading dose, no oral supplementation at initiation, and critically, no post-injection monitoring. The incumbent long-acting olanzapine, Lilly's Zyprexa Relprevv, carries post-injection delirium sedation syndrome in its label and requires patients to sit in a healthcare facility for three hours after every shot under a restricted distribution program. That requirement is why Relprevv never worked commercially. If Weltruza's label genuinely drops it, Teva has the first monthly olanzapine a community clinic can actually deliver. Medincell gets mid to high single-digit royalties on worldwide net sales plus up to one hundred twelve million in milestones, four million of it on this approval. Teva says U-S availability in the coming weeks.

Fifth, radiopharmaceuticals, and a pricing signal worth noting. Curium priced Bexlutry, its lutetium one-seventy-seven dotatate product, at a five percent discount to Novartis' Lutathera. Bexlutry was approved in September as the first what the F-D-A is calling a radioligand equivalent, cleared through the five-oh-five-b-two pathway after a Delaware court found the Novartis patents invalid and not infringed — a verdict Novartis is appealing. Lutathera did eight hundred sixteen million dollars globally last year and four hundred thirty-six million in the first half of twenty twenty-six. A five percent discount is not generic pricing. It is brand-adjacent pricing, and it reflects the real economics of radioligands: you are not selling a molecule, you are selling a supply chain with a six-and-a-half-day half-life and a fixed number of production slots. Curium is also acquiring Lantheus, which holds the first-to-file A-N-D-A on the same drug, for up to eight billion dollars, closing expected in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. So the first competitor and the second competitor are about to be the same company.
