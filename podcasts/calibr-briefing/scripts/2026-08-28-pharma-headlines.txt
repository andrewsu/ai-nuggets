Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Friday, August twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six.

Yesterday was approval day twice over. A rare autoimmune muscle-and-skin disease that has never had a targeted therapy in its history got one, and Gilead extended lenacapavir into a fourth product. Around that, the incumbent anti-T-S-L-P antibody won a third indication, an A-I-designed anti-T-S-L-P antibody had three unpublished clinical posters leak onto a conference website nearly two weeks before embargo, Moderna cashed in its cancer-vaccine rally for two billion dollars, and the Fifth Circuit shut the last door on the industry's constitutional case against Medicare negotiation. Nine items, then the week ahead.

First, the approval of the day. The F-D-A approved Lisraya, brepocitinib, from Priovant Therapeutics, for adults with dermatomyositis. It is a once-daily oral pill, and it is the first targeted therapy ever approved in that disease. Dermatomyositis is a systemic autoimmune disease with progressive proximal muscle weakness and a characteristic rash, and it can pull in the lungs, the heart and the gut. Until yesterday the standard of care was corticosteroids and broad immunosuppression, plus intravenous immunoglobulin — nothing aimed at the pathway. Brepocitinib is a dual T-Y-K-two and JAK-one inhibitor, which is the mechanistic point: T-Y-K-two carries interferon-alpha and interleukin-twelve and twenty-three signaling, JAK-one carries the interferon-gamma and interleukin-six arms, and dermatomyositis is as close to a pure type-one interferon disease as the myositis family gets. Hitting both is the thesis. The approval rests on VALOR, the largest dermatomyositis trial ever run and the first positive fifty-two-week placebo-controlled study in the disease. Separation on the myositis total improvement score appeared by week four and held to week fifty-two. Fifty-five percent of patients on drug reached moderate-or-better improvement while on minimal or no steroids, against thirty percent on placebo, and among patients taking at least seven and a half milligrams of prednisone equivalent a day at baseline, sixty-two percent tapered to minimal or none. The label carries the full JAK-class boxed warning — serious infection, mortality, malignancy, major cardiovascular events, thrombosis — so this is a specialty drug with a real risk conversation attached. On ownership, note the structure: Priovant is the Roivant vehicle built in September twenty twenty-one around brepocitinib, licensed from Pfizer, with Pfizer holding roughly a twenty-five percent stake. This is the first major approval the Roivant hub-and-spoke has produced since Dermavant's V-TAMA in twenty twenty-two, and Endpoints framed it as the first regulatory nod capable of turning the model into a multibillion-dollar revenue stream. Priovant is already pushing brepocitinib into non-infectious uveitis, cutaneous sarcoidosis and lichen planopilaris. For your purposes, that is a company assembling an orphan autoimmune franchise off one molecule and two kinases. And it lands five days after our spotlight on Abcuro's anti-K-L-R-G-one antibody in inclusion body myositis, which is the sibling disease that still has nothing.

Second, and today's spotlight. On Monday the twenty-fifth, three draft posters that Generate Biomedicines had submitted to the European Respiratory Society were inadvertently published on the society's own website, nearly two weeks before the embargo was due to lift. They contained unpublished phase one clinical data on golukibart — the company calls it G-B-zero-eight-nine-five — an A-I-engineered anti-T-S-L-P antibody now in two global phase three trials in severe asthma. Generate filed an eight-K the same day furnishing the final posters, which is the correct move and which is why we can read the whole dataset this morning. The stock had touched an all-time high of twenty dollars and thirty-eight cents on the twenty-fifth and was at sixteen fifty-seven yesterday morning. Generate is the Flagship-founded generative protein design company that went public in February at sixteen dollars a share, raising four hundred million against a two-billion-dollar valuation. We will spend the spotlight on what the posters actually say the drug is, why the twenty-fold affinity improvement is probably the least commercially important thing about it, and why a competitor lost forty-seven percent of its market value in one day this February on the exact question Generate is now betting a sixteen-hundred-patient phase three on.

Third, the incumbent in that same pathway had a good day. AstraZeneca and Amgen reported that the phase three CROSSING trial of Tezspire, tezepelumab, met both co-primary endpoints in eosinophilic esophagitis — histologic remission and dysphagia frequency and severity — in roughly three hundred seventy patients, with effect at twenty-four weeks maintained through fifty-two, and all key secondary endpoints hit. Tezspire binds T-S-L-P, the epithelial alarmin that sits at the very top of the airway and mucosal inflammatory cascade, which is why it keeps travelling across indications rather than being confined to one. This would be its third: severe asthma since December twenty twenty-one, chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps last year on the WAYPOINT trial, and now an eosinophilic esophagitis population above four hundred seventy thousand in the United States. William Blair called it an attractive third indication with blockbuster potential on its own. Leerink had flagged the readout as a key Amgen catalyst going in. Consensus has Tezspire around four point four billion dollars at peak, against Dupixent's roughly twenty-five point seven.

Fourth, Gilead. The F-D-A approved Bixlenvo, a fixed-dose combination of bictegravir seventy-five milligrams and lenacapavir fifty milligrams, as a once-daily single tablet for virologically suppressed adults with H-I-V. Bictegravir is the integrase strand transfer inhibitor at the center of Biktarvy; lenacapavir is the first-in-class capsid inhibitor that Gilead has been extending across its whole franchise. The point of this particular pill is switch: it is the first and only single-tablet regimen for suppressed patients on complex multi-pill regimens who could not take any existing single-tablet option, typically because of resistance history or renal or bone constraints that rule out the tenofovir backbone. Approval rests on ARTISTRY-one and ARTISTRY-two, both showing maintenance of virologic suppression at week forty-eight, presented as late-breakers at C-R-O-I earlier this year. Strategically this is Gilead doing what it does — taking a molecule with a novel mechanism and a long dosing interval and monetizing it in every format the market will absorb, from twice-yearly prevention to a daily combination pill.

Fifth, Moderna. The company announced a two-billion-dollar private placement of convertible senior notes due twenty thirty-two, with an option for purchasers to take up to three hundred million more, proceeds earmarked for the oncology business and debt repayment. Context is everything here. On August nineteenth Moderna's stock rose one hundred seventy-seven percent in a single session, adding roughly forty-four and a half billion dollars of market capitalization, on the late-stage readout of the individualized neoantigen therapy it co-develops with Merck — which was our spotlight on the twentieth. Eight days later, they sold paper into it. That is textbook and it is the right call, but the market took the point: shares fell about five percent on the announcement, which is a dilution reaction, not a doubt about the data. Watch what this does to the financing calendar generally — a hundred-and-seventy-seven-percent single-day move followed immediately by a two-billion-dollar raise is the clearest signal yet that the convertible window is wide open for anyone with a catalyst behind them.

Sixth, the legal story is over. On Wednesday the Fifth Circuit affirmed summary judgment against the Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America and the National Infusion Center Association in their challenge to Medicare drug price negotiation, rejecting the separation-of-powers, due-process and excessive-fines theories together. The court's reasoning is the same one every court has reached: participation in Medicare and Medicaid is voluntary, so manufacturers have no protected interest in selling to Medicare beneficiaries at a price of their choosing. This is the twenty-fifth courtroom loss for the industry on this program, and it comes four days after Judge Kollar-Kotelly rejected Merck's parallel First and Fifth Amendment case in Washington — which we covered Tuesday. The Supreme Court has already declined to take the earlier appeals. Endpoints called it a dead end and that is the accurate word. Plan pricing strategy around the statute, not around litigation risk to the statute.

Eighth, a company launch worth your attention on the A-I side. Outer Bio came out of stealth with twenty-three million dollars from Wing Venture Capital, Initialized Capital, Sozo Ventures and Hawktail, run by chief executive Michael Polansky, who managed Sean Parker's family office for fifteen years and helped stand up the Parker Institute for Cancer Immunotherapy. His co-founders are chief scientific officer K-J Jang and chief technology officer Chris Hinojosa, both formerly of Emulate and originally out of Harvard's Wyss Institute. The technical claim is the interesting part: they keep explanted living human skin alive for about a month rather than the usual week, then screen compounds against it and read out more than thirty thousand data points per compound-and-sample pairing. They say they have generated over ten terabytes across three hundred-plus donors and ten thousand candidate treatments. Polansky is explicit that the drug is not the asset — quote, the ultimate data corpus and the A-I models are going to be much more valuable than any one active chemical we validate. The commercial route is cosmetics first, because the timeline and cost are a fraction of pharma, with pharma partnerships held open for real disease indications. Nineteen employees, formed in twenty twenty. Whether or not you believe the model, the structure — sell into cosmetics to fund the data flywheel, then arbitrage into therapeutics — is a template you are going to see more of.

Ninth, two quick manufacturing and disclosure notes. RayzeBio, the Bristol Myers radiopharmaceutical subsidiary, is putting more than a hundred seventy-three million dollars into a new radiopharmaceutical plant in Indiana, about a hundred jobs — which is the unglamorous constraint on that entire field, since isotope supply and same-week distribution are what actually gate a radioligand launch. And separately, Eli Lilly has stopped listing its phase one assets on its public pipeline page as of the August fifth update, citing competitive reasons. Reaction has been genuinely split. The defensible version is that early-stage disclosure mostly serves competitors; the cost is that Lilly is now the most valuable pharmaceutical company in the world running a phase one program that outsiders cannot see, at exactly the moment when everyone is trying to model where the obesity franchise goes next.
