The through-line is a landmark cancer filing that finally cracks a target the field called undruggable, a big obesity readout with a wrinkle, a myeloma combo that keeps raising the bar, an ADHD decision due today, and a bioprocessing deal. The cancer story gets the spotlight.

First, and it's the biggest one for you, Revolution Medicines. The F-D-A has accepted for review the company's new drug application for daraxonrasib in previously-treated metastatic pancreatic cancer. This matters on two levels. The drug itself is a first-of-its-kind RAS inhibitor, and the filing went in through the F-D-A Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot, the new expedited pathway, on top of Breakthrough Therapy and Orphan Drug designations it already held. Pancreatic cancer is about as hard as oncology gets, and second-line patients have almost nothing. In the Phase 3 RASolute three-oh-two trial, daraxonrasib roughly doubled median overall survival, thirteen point two months versus six point seven on chemotherapy, a hazard ratio of zero point four. The plenary presentation at this year's A-S-C-O reportedly got a standing ovation. This is a named focus company for you, it works with the A-I firm Iambic on its discovery engine, and I'll go deep in the spotlight on why RAS being druggable at all reshapes a huge slice of oncology.

Second, obesity, and this one is nuanced. Eli Lilly reported positive topline results from two more Phase 3 studies of retatrutide, its triple agonist that hits the G-I-P, G-L-P-one, and glucagon receptors all at once. The weight loss was powerful, up to roughly twenty-two to thirty percent depending on the study and dose, extending Lilly's dominance in the class beyond tirzepatide. The wrinkle is on the heart. In the trial that enrolled people with established cardiovascular disease, major cardiac events came in lower than expected in both the drug and placebo arms, so the cardiovascular outcomes read as inconclusive rather than a clean win, even though the drug sharply improved risk factors like triglycerides. Lilly plans to file with the F-D-A in the first quarter of next year. The takeaway: retatrutide is the most potent weight-loss agent yet, but the outcomes story that payers increasingly demand is still a work in progress.

Third, multiple myeloma. Johnson and Johnson reported topline Phase 3 results from MonumenTAL-six, testing its two bispecific antibodies together, Tecvayli, which targets B-C-M-A, and Talvey, which targets G-P-R-C-five-D, in patients with relapsed or refractory disease who'd had one to four prior lines. The combination cut the risk of progression or death by eighty-nine percent and the risk of death by sixty-two percent against standard of care. Those are striking numbers, and the strategic point is that J-and-J is pushing its bispecific franchise earlier into the treatment course and showing that hitting two myeloma targets at once may beat sequencing them, which pressures both the C-A-R-T players and everyone else in that space.

Fourth, watch the calendar today. Otsuka has an F-D-A decision due on centanafadine for A-D-H-D, with the target action date landing today. If it clears, it would be the first A-D-H-D medicine that works by blocking reuptake of three neurotransmitters at once, norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin, a mechanism distinct from both the stimulants and the existing non-stimulants. It's backed by four pivotal Phase 3 trials, and Otsuka has been building extra data, including a win in adults with A-D-H-D and comorbid anxiety. A new non-stimulant option in a market that leans heavily on controlled substances is worth tracking.

And fifth, a tools-and-supply deal. Repligen agreed to acquire BioLife Solutions for about one and a half billion dollars, roughly two-thirds stock and one-third cash. BioLife's biopreservation media, its CryoStor line, is used to freeze and ship cells and sits under the majority of U-S commercially-sponsored cell-therapy trials and eighteen approved therapies. Repligen is buying the picks-and-shovels layer of cell therapy, one of the fastest-growing corners of manufacturing, and management expects it to add to earnings in year one. It's a reminder that as cell therapies scale, the supply chain underneath them is consolidating into a real prize.
