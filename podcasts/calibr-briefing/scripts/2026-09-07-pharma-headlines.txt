Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Monday, September seventh, twenty twenty-six. It is Labor Day, so U-S markets are closed and the domestic news flow is thin — but Europe is working, the European Respiratory Society congress is in its third day in Barcelona, and the two most consequential items on the tape both broke while nobody in New York was watching.

First, and this is this morning's news. Endpoints News reports that Novo Nordisk has stopped two more late-stage trials of ziltivekimab, its once-monthly anti-interleukin-six antibody, after the independent data monitoring committee determined futility. The two trials are HERMES and ATHENA, and together they were the entire heart failure wing of the program. HERMES was the big one — roughly forty-nine hundred patients with heart failure with mildly reduced or preserved ejection fraction, systemic inflammation and elevated N-T-pro-B-N-P, randomized to fifteen milligrams of ziltivekimab monthly or placebo, with morbidity and mortality as the endpoint and a primary completion date that had been sitting in July of next year. ATHENA was the companion, six hundred eighty patients, same population, but powered on health status and physical function rather than events. Both are gone. Recall the sequence: on July thirty-first Novo reported that ZEUS, the sixty-three-hundred-patient cardiovascular outcomes trial in atherosclerotic disease plus chronic kidney disease plus inflammation, came in at a hazard ratio of zero point nine nine, confidence interval zero point eight eight to one point one one — a flat null, despite the drug doing exactly what it was designed to do to free interleukin six and to high-sensitivity C-reactive protein. At that time Novo explicitly said the heart failure and post-heart-attack trials would continue and read out in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. Five weeks later the heart failure pair is closed. What survives is ARTEMIS — ten thousand patients, dosed after an acute myocardial infarction, still recruiting across nine hundred seventy sites, with primary completion in December — plus two small serial-imaging studies using intravascular ultrasound, near-infrared spectroscopy and optical coherence tomography to look at plaque directly. So Novo is keeping the acute post-infarct hypothesis and the mechanistic imaging, and has abandoned the chronic-inflammation-in-heart-failure hypothesis entirely. The read-across matters beyond Novo. Nine days ago Novartis took pacibekitug, its own anti-interleukin-six antibody, into Phase three anyway, on the argument that ZEUS enrolled the wrong population. Novartis now has a much lonelier position. And put this next to Friday's other print: pelacarsen lowered lipoprotein little a by eighty percent and prevented nothing. In six weeks the field has lost the two largest post-statin residual-risk theses — inflammation and lipoprotein little a — in trials that both demonstrated flawless target engagement. That is not a drug-development problem. That is a biology-to-outcome problem, and it should change how you underwrite any cardiovascular program whose entire case is a biomarker plus Mendelian genetics.

Second, the item that got buried by the holiday weekend and that today's spotlight takes apart. On Friday the F-D-A granted accelerated approval to camizestrant — brand name Etcamah, from AstraZeneca — in combination with a C-D-K four six inhibitor, for adults with hormone-receptor-positive, H-E-R two negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer whose tumors develop an E-S-R one mutation detected in circulating tumor D-N-A during first-line aromatase inhibitor plus C-D-K four six therapy. Read that indication carefully, because the words doing the work are "detected in circulating tumor D-N-A." This is the first cancer therapy ever approved on the basis of a resistance mutation found in blood before imaging shows the disease progressing. The F-D-A simultaneously approved Guardant Health's Guardant three-sixty C-Dx as the companion diagnostic, with repeat testing every three months — Guardant's twenty-ninth companion diagnostic indication globally, and its first approved for longitudinal, serial testing in breast cancer. The supporting trial, SERENA-six, randomized three hundred fifteen patients and cut the risk of progression or death by fifty-six percent, sixteen months of progression-free survival versus nine point two, hazard ratio zero point four four. And here is the part that makes this a story rather than an approval: the agency's own Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee voted six to three against this in April. AstraZeneca's Dave Fredrickson notes it is the company's tenth F-D-A approval this year and fourth in breast cancer. Kevin Kalinsky at Winship called it an important option for the one in three patients whose tumors develop these mutations. The label carries a boxed warning. More on all of it in the spotlight.

Third, from Barcelona. GRI Bio presented late-breaking Phase two-a data on G-R-I oh-six-two-one, an oral retinoic acid receptor beta-gamma selective agonist for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. The mechanism is unusual for this space — rather than blocking a fibrotic growth factor, it aims at invariant natural killer T cells, an innate-like lymphocyte population implicated in driving fibrotic remodeling. Across a late-breaking oral presentation and three posters, the company reported a placebo-adjusted increase in forced vital capacity at twelve weeks, reductions in gene-expression and protein markers of fibrotic remodeling, and tolerability favorable to placebo. Twelve weeks and Phase two-a scale means the lung function number is directionally interesting and statistically fragile — but tolerability is the real currency in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, where nintedanib and pirfenidone are limited far more by gastrointestinal intolerance than by efficacy, and where the anti-fibrotic graveyard is deep. Watch whether this gets a partner rather than whether it gets a Phase three.

Fourth, also at the respiratory congress, Insmed presented a late-breaking twelve-month analysis from ENCORE, the Phase three-b trial of Arikayce — amikacin liposome inhalation suspension — added to guideline-based multidrug therapy in patients with newly diagnosed mycobacterium avium complex lung infection who had never received antibiotics. The topline came in March: four hundred twenty-five patients, primary endpoint met on respiratory symptom score, seventeen point seven seven versus fourteen point six six, a three-point-one-one-point difference at a p of zero point zero two nine nine, with culture conversion at eighty-seven point eight percent versus fifty-seven percent by month six. The strategic point is regulatory rather than clinical: ENCORE was run to satisfy an F-D-A post-marketing requirement, and Insmed intends to file a supplemental application in the second half of this year both to expand the label into newly diagnosed disease and to convert the existing refractory indication from accelerated to traditional approval. A conditional approval becoming a full one, plus a much larger front-line population.

Fifth, AstraZeneca presented mechanistic data at the congress from FRONTIER-four on tozorakimab, its anti-interleukin-thirty-three antibody, arguing the drug modulates airway epithelial remodeling in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease through pathways outside type-two inflammation. That is the biological argument behind the March readout, when tozorakimab hit its primary endpoint in both OBERON and TITANIA across former and current smokers and across all eosinophil counts. Hold the interleukin-thirty-three scoreboard in your head, because it is getting complicated: tozorakimab, an antibody against the ligand, wins two Phase threes. Itepekimab, from Sanofi and Regeneron, also against the ligand, split its two Phase threes. Astegolimab, from Roche, against the receptor, won its Phase two-b and missed its Phase three at a numerically similar fourteen and a half percent exacerbation reduction. And yesterday we covered Mabwell's anti-S-T-two antibody posting steep effects in eighty low-eosinophil patients. Same pathway, four different verdicts, and the differences look more like trial design and population than biology.

Sixth, on the capital side, Claret Capital Partners in London closed Fund four at five hundred seventy-five million euros this morning, its largest growth-lending vehicle to date, and said roughly a third is earmarked for biotech, medtech and diagnostics. Thirty-two percent is already deployed across twenty-seven companies including the clinical-stage names Cinclus Pharma and Inventiva. That takes Claret to one point three billion euros raised since inception. The relevant point for a U-S investor is structural: European growth lending is where a lot of clinical-stage European biotech is now getting non-dilutive capital, and a dedicated third of a five-hundred-seventy-five-million-euro fund is a meaningful expansion of that channel at exactly the moment the U-S I-P-O window has reopened and European companies are choosing whether to cross the Atlantic.

And seventh, briefly logged: Tarsus Pharmaceuticals closed its acquisition of Alkeus Pharmaceuticals on Friday. The deal was announced August sixth — two hundred seventy million dollars in cash, one hundred eighty million in Tarsus stock, up to three hundred fifty million in milestones for a headline of eight hundred million — and brings in gildeuretinol, a once-daily oral for Stargardt disease. The Phase three does not read out until twenty twenty-nine. This is a close, not news, but it puts two Phase three retinal programs on one integration plan.
