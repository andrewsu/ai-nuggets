Novartis said yesterday morning that the Phase three HARBOR study of del-desiran in myotonic dystrophy type one missed its primary endpoint. Del-desiran — delpacibart etedesiran, if you want the full name — is an antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate: a monoclonal antibody against transferrin receptor one, which is how you get into skeletal muscle, chemically tethered to a small interfering RNA that degrades the toxic DMPK messenger RNA that causes the disease. About one hundred fifty patients, dosed intravenously every eight weeks for fifty-four weeks. It did not separate from placebo. Novartis says there was clinical activity in secondary endpoints and exploratory analyses and released no numbers, which is exactly what a company says when the secondaries are directionally right and nowhere near a filing. Third, Amgen. The Phase three DeLLphi-three-oh-five study of Imdelltra — tarlatamab, the DLL3-by-CD3 bispecific T-cell engager — met its primary endpoint of overall survival at a prespecified interim analysis, in first-line maintenance for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer. Fourth, Bristol Myers Squibb reported positive topline results from QUINTESSENTIAL, the registrational Phase two of arlocabtagene autoleucel — arlo-cel, a G-protein-coupled receptor class C group five member D-directed CAR-T. In quadruple-class-exposed relapsed refractory myeloma, patients who have already failed an immunomodulator, a proteasome inhibitor, an anti-CD38 antibody and a BCMA-targeted agent, it met its primary endpoint of overall response rate and its key secondary of complete response rate, and hit both again in the three-prior-lines population. If this is approved it is the first GPRC5D CAR-T, and it gives Bristol Myers an answer for patients relapsing after its own Abecma and after Carvykti, which is where the myeloma market is now living.
