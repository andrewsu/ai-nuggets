Today's spotlight is Abcuro's sixty-six million dollar Series D, and the reason it deserves your full attention is not the size of the round. It's that a syndicate including Foresite just put fresh money into re-testing a drug that missed its primary endpoint in a two hundred seventy-two patient phase two-three trial six months ago. That is the second time this week we have looked at a company raising capital on the claim that the population was wrong rather than the molecule. Yesterday it was neridronate in complex regional pain syndrome. Today it's an anti-K-L-R-G-one antibody in inclusion body myositis, and the argument here is built differently. It's worth walking through carefully, because if it holds it tells you something general about how we design trials in slowly progressive diseases.

Start with the disease, because it is the most instructive part. Inclusion body myositis typically presents after fifty with a signature pattern of weakness: the quadriceps and the deep finger flexors. Patients lose the ability to rise from a chair and to grip, then fall, then in many cases develop swallowing difficulty. It progresses monotonically. There are roughly forty thousand diagnosed patients in the United States and around thirty-five thousand across the major European markets and Japan. There is no approved therapy anywhere.

Here is the fact that has shaped thirty years of failure. Inclusion body myositis is the one inflammatory myopathy that does not respond to immunosuppression. Corticosteroids, intravenous immunoglobulin, methotrexate, azathioprine — all of them work to varying degrees in dermatomyositis and polymyositis, and none of them work here. That paradox split the field. One camp looked at the endomysial T cell infiltrate and said autoimmune. The other looked at the rimmed vacuoles, the protein aggregates, the amyloid, and said this is a degenerative myopathy that happens to have inflammation in it. Both got their shot. Bimagrumab, an antibody against the activin type two receptor that blocks myostatin signaling and grows muscle, missed on the six-minute walk and later found a second life as an obesity asset at Lilly. Arimoclomol, meant to boost the heat-shock response and clear protein aggregates, missed. Sirolimus showed limited efficacy with real toxicity.

So the K-L-R-G-one hypothesis arrives after every other mechanism has been tried and has failed, and it is the first one that actually explains the treatment resistance rather than working around it. The science comes out of Steven Greenberg's lab at Brigham and Women's and Harvard; he co-founded Abcuro and is its chief scientific adviser. His observation is that the T cells attacking muscle in this disease are not ordinary autoreactive T cells. They are terminally differentiated, immunosenescent cytotoxic CD8 cells. They have lost CD5 and CD28 and gained CD57 and K-L-R-G-one. That phenotype is the answer to the paradox: cells at that stage of differentiation no longer require costimulation to kill. Every conventional immunosuppressant works upstream, on activation and proliferation, on cells that still need permission. These cells don't need permission. You cannot suppress them. You can only remove them.

That is what ulviprubart is designed to do. K-L-R-G-one is the marker that identifies this differentiation state, and it is essentially absent from naive, central memory and regulatory T cells. The antibody is humanized and afucosylated — sugar removed from the Fc region to tighten binding to Fc-gamma receptor three-a and maximize antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. The therapeutic act is subtraction of one cell state, not blanket immunosuppression, and the first-in-human data at the American College of Rheumatology in twenty twenty-one showed it does deplete the target selectively.

One more piece of biology, because it changes how you read the company. Greenberg reported in Brain in twenty sixteen that fifty-eight percent of inclusion body myositis patients had populations of large granular lymphocytes in blood meeting the diagnostic criteria for T cell large granular lymphocytic leukemia — clonal in twenty of twenty patients tested, and still there a median of three hundred fifty days later. Confirmed by T cell receptor gene rearrangement the rate is about a third, which is still remarkable. In a substantial fraction of patients this disease is a clonal cytotoxic T cell disorder wearing a myopathy's clothes. That is why the same antibody is also in a phase one-two leukemia trial.

So to the trial itself. MUSCLE randomized two hundred seventy-two patients — ninety-four to zero point five milligrams per kilogram, ninety-two to two milligrams per kilogram, eighty-six to placebo — dosed once every eight weeks, with the primary endpoint being change from baseline on the forty-point functional rating scale at seventy-six weeks. The low dose declined one point seven points against two point four on placebo, a p-value of zero point zero eight six. The high dose declined two point one, p-value zero point three seven three. That is a miss, and it is a miss with an inverted dose response, which is the shape you least want to see. Safety was unremarkable: falls in fifty-two, fifty-five and fifty-one percent of the low dose, high dose and placebo arms respectively — that's the disease, not the drug — plus chills, headache, fever and nasopharyngitis. Two percent of treated patients discontinued early versus seven percent on placebo, and nearly everyone who finished rolled into the open-label extension.

Then the pre-specified subgroup: patients whose baseline functional score was twenty-nine or higher out of forty, meaning less advanced disease. Both dose arms declined one point three points. Placebo declined two point six. Exactly fifty percent slowing, with p-values of zero point zero six six and zero point zero seven five.

Three things make that a better argument than the average subgroup rescue. First, it was pre-specified, not dredged. Second, both doses landed on the identical one point three — the inverted dose ordering that made the overall result look incoherent dissolves entirely in the less-severe group, which is what you would expect if that ordering was noise contributed by the severe patients. Third, and this is the load-bearing one, the placebo arm declined faster in the less-severe subgroup, two point six points, than in the overall population, two point four. That is the fingerprint of a floor effect. Patients who have already lost the functions the scale measures cannot lose much more of them, so advanced patients compress the measurable placebo decline and shrink the window in which any drug can separate. Restore the dynamic range and the same biological effect becomes a bigger number: the treatment effect went from zero point seven points to one point three. And the biology says the same thing from the other direction. Depleting the killer T cells can protect muscle fibers that still exist. It cannot regrow a fiber that is already gone.

Now for the honest counter-case, because zero point zero six six is not a win. The subgroup missed too. Abcuro has not disclosed how many patients were in it, and if roughly half the enrolled population sat above that threshold you are looking at something like forty-odd patients per arm defending a one point three point difference on a ten-item ordinal scale over seventy-six weeks. That is roughly one grade on one item, and whether it is meaningful to a patient is a question the agency will ask. Enrichment strategies reverse-engineered from a failed trial have a poor forward record. And the population with the most measurement headroom is also the population with the slowest natural history, where placebo variance is hardest to predict.

Which brings us to the money, because the financing is the clearest read on what the insiders actually believe. Abcuro raised a hundred fifty-five million dollar Series B in twenty twenty-three, then two hundred million in February of twenty twenty-five, led by NEA with Foresite joining, to complete MUSCLE. Roughly four hundred sixty-three million in total. This round is sixty-six million — a third of the last one — led by New Leaf, which was already an investor, with Rock Springs Capital as the single new outside name. There is a benign reading: one trial in a narrower population costs less than a full pivotal program, and disciplined sizing is the right call. But notice what did not happen. No crossover round. No step-up. No S-one, in a week when Scribe's gene-editing I-P-O was trading at roughly twice its fifteen dollar price. If this syndicate believed the subgroup was a high-probability win with a clean regulatory path, the round would have been larger and it would have had a new lead. Sixty-six million from insiders is enough to buy one more look, and it is priced like one more look.

The timeline is the other cost. The trial starts in the fourth quarter, topline lands in the second half of twenty twenty-eight, and a filing follows that. Approval no earlier than twenty twenty-nine — eight years after the first human data showing the antibody depletes its target — in a disease that only goes one direction.

There are two more things worth holding onto. The first is that nobody is going to race them. Argenx just won ALKIVIA in dermatomyositis and immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy with efgartigimod, and that drug works by blocking the neonatal Fc receptor to strip circulating IgG. It requires antibody-driven disease. Inclusion body myositis is cell-mediated, so the most successful myositis franchise in the world has no mechanistic route into this indication. Abcuro has the field to itself, which is the opportunity and also the reason there is no validating competitive bid to point at.

Second, the mechanism is bigger than the indication. Selectively removing terminally differentiated cytotoxic T cells while sparing the naive, regulatory and central memory compartments is a general tool, and the leukemia program is the cleaner test of whether the antibody does what it claims, because in a clonal malignancy you can count the cells you killed. For Foresite's purposes, this is a mechanism position, not an indication position — which is the right way to be exposed to a coin flip.

Watch the protocol when it posts. Whether "less severe" stays at the twenty-nine cutoff or tightens. Whether they carry one dose or two. Whether the agency has agreed a single trial can support a filing. And most of all, whether they select patients on the clonal T cell burden itself, which is measurable in blood. Abcuro has spent four hundred sixty-three million dollars establishing that this disease is driven by an identifiable, countable population of cells. The most valuable thing it could do with the next trial is enroll on that number rather than on a functional score.
