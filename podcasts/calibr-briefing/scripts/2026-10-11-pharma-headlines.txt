Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Sunday, October eleventh, twenty twenty-six. The newswires were quiet over the weekend, so today's briefing does two things: it picks up the Thursday and Friday items that got crowded out by the Chai Discovery and G-S-K news, and it covers two medical meetings that ran straight through the weekend — the Fall Clinical Dermatology Conference in Las Vegas and the American Academy of Ophthalmology meeting in New Orleans. The lead, and today's spotlight, is the first human data ever reported for partial epigenetic reprogramming. Eight items, then the week ahead.

First, Life Biosciences. On Wednesday the company reported interim results from the first-in-human Phase 1 trial of E-R one hundred, an adeno-associated virus carrying three of the four Yamanaka transcription factors — Oct four, Sox two and Klf four, collectively O-S-K — injected once into the eye, with the genes switched on by eight weeks of oral doxycycline. Three patients with open-angle glaucoma received the low dose. Through day fifty-six there were no dose-limiting toxicities, no serious adverse events and no adverse events of special interest, and the independent safety monitoring board cleared escalation to the higher dose. Two of the three patients showed improvement on Humphrey visual field testing, with at least twenty-eight percent of tested points improving by more than four decibels. That is three patients, open-label, with no control group, so hold the result loosely. But the concept is enormous, and when we covered the NewLimit Series C in June, the flat statement was that cellular reprogramming had zero human data in any indication. It no longer does. Full analysis in today's spotlight.

Second, the oral psoriasis pill war got two data drops on the same day at Fall Clinical. Alumis presented forty-eight-week results from ONWARD three, the open-label extension of its twin Phase 3 trials of envudeucitinib, its next-generation T-Y-K two inhibitor. Among patients treated continuously, seventy-five percent reached ninety percent skin clearance and fifty-four percent reached complete clearance, with about eighty percent of patients holding a clear or almost-clear scalp at week forty-eight, one in three hitting a meaningful itch reduction as early as week two, and no new safety signals. Alumis is on track to file with the F-D-A this quarter. Here is the thing to watch: in the broader population, including patients who did not roll into the extension, the numbers were sixty-six percent and forty-seven percent — a reminder that extension cohorts select for responders. And analysts are not fully convinced. Jefferies flagged that envudeucitinib is dosed twice daily, and that its efficacy appears to sit below both Johnson and Johnson's icotrokinra and Takeda's zasocitinib, which limits the differentiation story. This is the same T-Y-K two asset that failed in lupus in September, which we covered in detail at the time. Psoriasis was always the commercial engine; the question is whether it clears a crowded field.

Third, the comparator answered the same morning. Johnson and Johnson reported two-year data from ICONIC-LEAD for Icotyde, generic name icotrokinra, the first targeted oral peptide that blocks the interleukin twenty-three receptor rather than inhibiting a kinase. At week one hundred twelve, at least seventy-two percent of treated patients held ninety percent clearance, at least seventy percent were clear or almost clear, and at least forty-four percent had complete clearance, sustained from week sixty-four. Among patients withdrawn from drug at week twenty-four, eighty-five percent regained their response within twenty-four weeks of restarting. Icotrokinra already beat Bristol Myers Squibb's Sotyktu head-to-head in the ICONIC-ADVANCE trials. Once-daily, biologic-like clearance, two years of durability, in adolescents as well as adults — that is the bar envudeucitinib has to clear commercially.

Fourth, Lilly used the same meeting to make an immunometabolic argument. New exploratory biomarker data from TOGETHER-PsO, a two hundred seventy-four patient Phase 3b trial, compared Taltz, its interleukin seventeen-A antibody, given alongside Zepbound, tirzepatide, versus Taltz alone in patients with both psoriasis and obesity. Combining the two changed four hundred eighty-two circulating proteins by week twelve versus one hundred forty with Taltz alone, with greater reductions in neutrophil-associated inflammatory markers — and a portion of the extra skin-clearance benefit was statistically mediated by those neutrophil markers. James Krueger of Rockefeller, who is as close to the center of psoriasis immunology as anyone, called out the pathway-level differences as the compelling part. This is Lilly building the scientific case for treating the metabolic comorbidity as part of the immune disease, which also happens to be a case for prescribing two Lilly drugs.

Fifth, in ophthalmology, Glaukos reported positive Phase 4 results for iDose T-R, its titanium intracameral implant that elutes travoprost inside the eye for up to three years. Combined with cataract surgery, it lowered mean diurnal eye pressure by eleven point one millimeters of mercury at three months versus seven point four for cataract surgery alone — an incremental three point six millimeters, highly statistically significant, with no corneal endothelial cell loss and no macular edema. One hundred forty-nine eyes. It is worth holding this next to the Life Biosciences result, because the two represent opposite theories of the same disease: lower the pressure more conveniently, or try to restore the neurons that the pressure already killed.

Sixth, Natera said the F-D-A granted Breakthrough Device designation to its multi-cancer early detection blood test, a cell-free D-N-A assay. The company says the agency assessed data across twelve cancer types, weighing sensitivity, specificity and cancer-signal-origin performance. Natera is the dominant player in minimal residual disease testing with Signatera, and this is its move into screening — the same market where Grail's test has drawn public criticism over clinical utility. Note the designation is a development-and-review accelerator, not evidence of benefit, and no performance numbers were disclosed.

Seventh, on policy, the Department of Health and Human Services launched a broad vaccine-injury initiative: a new N-I-H clinic in Bethesda that opened on October fifth to investigate health problems reported after immunization, reimbursement for physicians who file reports to the passive adverse-event system, an overhaul of that system, and a requirement that electronic health records capture vaccine-related adverse events. Paul Offit of Children's Hospital of Philadelphia told BioPharma Dive he expects misleading, poorly performed studies that further shake parental confidence, and Kawsar Talaat of Johns Hopkins said it is unclear how the initiative improves scientific understanding rather than increasing confusion. For anyone with a vaccine program or a vaccine-adjacent asset, the practical risk is a noisier passive surveillance signal feeding policy decisions.

Eighth, two smaller items worth filing. Sagimet will present full fifty-two-week results from license partner Ascletis's Phase 3 open-label extension of denifanstat in acne, plus mechanistic data, at Fall Clinical — a fatty acid synthase inhibitor finding a dermatology indication after a long M-A-S-H journey. And Seaport Therapeutics presented additional Phase 1 pharmacokinetic data on GlyphAgo at the European College of Neuropsychopharmacology congress, continuing to build out its glycoside prodrug approach for routing C-N-S drugs around first-pass liver metabolism.

For the week ahead. The I-P-O window is the main event: City Therapeutics and the A-I-native small-molecule company Iambic are both expected to price this week, after setting terms on Friday, with TRexBio's one hundred seventeen million dollar offering closing on the thirteenth. That would put the year's biotech I-P-O count past thirty. Fall Clinical and the Academy of Ophthalmology meeting both wrap in the next couple of days, so expect more late-breaking derm and retina data to surface Monday and Tuesday. And mark November fourth: Charles River reports third-quarter results before the open, which remains the cleanest single read on whether preclinical demand is actually recovering. That is your Sunday briefing. The spotlight goes deep on the Life Biosciences data and what it does and does not establish.
