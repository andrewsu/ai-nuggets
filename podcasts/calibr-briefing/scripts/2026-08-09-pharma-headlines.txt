Travis' biotech and pharma headlines for Sunday, August 9, 2026. It's a quiet weekend on the wire, so today is about one big regulatory catalyst landing this month, a fresh IPO, and where the money is still flowing. Five things.

First, and today's spotlight: Regeneron is days to weeks away from an FDA decision that could redefine treatment for one of medicine's cruelest rare diseases. The drug is garetosmab, an antibody against a signaling protein called Activin A, and the disease is fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva, or F-O-P — a genetic disorder in which soft tissue turns to bone, building a second skeleton that locks the body in place. There are only about nine hundred people with it worldwide. Garetosmab's target action date is this month, and what makes the story worth a deep dive is the arc: this program nearly died in 2020 after patient deaths in an early trial, then came back with a phase 3 that stopped more than ninety percent of new bone lesions. We go deep in today's spotlight.

Second, the biotech IPO window is still open, if a little cooler. BlossomHill Therapeutics priced an upsized offering Thursday at sixteen dollars a share, raising one hundred fifty million dollars, and began trading on the Nasdaq — though shares slipped slightly on debut, a more restrained reception than the pops we've seen this window. BlossomHill is an oncology company built by Jean Cui, the medicinal chemist who founded Turning Point Therapeutics, which Bristol Myers Squibb bought for four point one billion dollars in 2022. Its lead drug is a macrocyclic inhibitor aimed at the broad spectrum of E-G-F-R mutations in lung cancer — including the resistance mutations that defeat today's drugs — while sparing normal E-G-F-R to limit toxicity.

Third, the oral obesity race added a starter. Structure Therapeutics dosed its first patients in a phase 3 program for aleniglipron, an oral small-molecule G-L-P-1 receptor agonist — the same mechanism as the injectable blockbusters, but in a pill. That puts Structure into direct competition with Eli Lilly's oral orforglipron and Novo Nordisk's oral programs, in what is shaping up to be the defining commercial battle of the decade. A convenient, scalable, oral G-L-P-1 is the prize everyone is chasing.

Fourth, a readout in central nervous system drugs, with a weekend-sized asterisk. Vistagen reported results from an exploratory phase 2 study of fasedienol, a nasal spray for the acute treatment of social anxiety disorder. The interesting part is the mechanism: fasedienol is a pherine, designed to act on chemosensory neurons in the nose rather than being absorbed into the bloodstream and hitting the brain systemically — a fundamentally different approach to anxiety than the pills we have. This study met its safety goal for repeat dosing and showed encouraging numerical trends, but it was not powered for statistical significance, and it follows a phase 3 that missed — so read it as supportive, not definitive.

And fifth, the macro number that frames all of this: drug startups have now raised roughly six billion dollars in U-S IPOs so far in 2026 — more than the previous four years combined at this point in the calendar. BlossomHill's softer debut is the nuance in that story: the window is open, but investors are pricing with a bit more discipline than they were a few weeks ago.
