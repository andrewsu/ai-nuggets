Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Friday, September twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-six. Thursday was a deal day and a data day at once, and the single most interesting thing on the tape is structural rather than clinical: Roche signed two separate discovery collaborations in one morning, which makes five external research deals in four weeks. That is today's spotlight. Around it, Lilly got a weekly insulin approved, Merck validated a genuinely new mechanism in diabetic eye disease and then immediately complicated it, Kyverna put one-year durability behind what should become the first C-A-R T-cell therapy approved for an autoimmune disease, and the R-N-A interference field got its first initial public offering in more than a decade. Ten items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight. Roche paid Atavistik Bio seventy million dollars up front, against up to one point nine billion in milestones, to apply Atavistik's metabolite-protein screening platform to cardiovascular, renal and metabolic targets. On the same morning, Genentech paid Earendil Labs fifty-five million dollars up front, against more than one point five billion in milestones, to discover bispecific antibodies in oncology. Combined headline value is about three and a half billion dollars; combined cash is one hundred twenty-five million. What makes it a spotlight rather than two line items is the pattern. Since August twenty-eighth, Roche and Genentech have done five of these — DualityBio on antibody-drug conjugate payloads, Simcere Zaiming's trispecific T-cell engager, Dualitas on bispecific screening, and now Atavistik and Earendil. More than seven billion dollars in potential value against two hundred eighty-one point five million dollars in disclosed upfronts. Roche is not buying molecules. It is buying discovery throughput, at option prices. We will go through what that actually costs and what it says about where the bottleneck has moved.

Second, an approval. The F-D-A cleared Eli Lilly's once-weekly insulin, efsitora alfa, which will be sold as Onswik, for adults with type two diabetes. The pitch is arithmetic — more than three hundred fewer injections a year than a daily basal insulin. The evidence base is the QWINT program, about thirty-four hundred adults, designed as non-inferiority against the daily standards. In QWINT-two, hemoglobin A-one-C fell one point three four percentage points on Onswik against one point two six on degludec, which is Novo's Tresiba. This is Lilly's fourth global approval for the product after Europe, Mexico and Japan, and U-S availability is promised in the coming months. The competitive frame is the important part: Novo's once-weekly Awiqli got there first, approved in March, after two rejections in twenty twenty-four over a type one indication and manufacturing. Lilly's label likewise excludes type one, citing hypoglycemia risk. So the two companies that have spent three years fighting over incretins are now fighting over basal insulin as well, in a category with thin margins and heavy pharmacy benefit manager leverage — which tells you something about how both of them think about owning the whole diabetes shelf rather than just the growth end of it.

Third, and this is the most scientifically interesting readout of the day. Merck said remigromig met non-inferiority on visual acuity against Roche's Lucentis in the Phase three Brunello trial in diabetic macular edema. Remigromig is not an anti-V-E-G-F drug. It is an agonist of the Wnt signaling pathway, designed to restore the blood-retinal barrier rather than to suppress the leaky vasculature downstream of it — which, if it holds up, is the first genuinely new mechanistic axis in retinal disease in twenty years. Merck acquired it in the one point three billion dollar takeout of EyeBio in twenty twenty-four, with another one point seven billion in milestones behind it. Now the complication: in the same release, Merck disclosed it is running further analyses to understand higher rates of proliferative diabetic retinopathy, bleeding inside the eye, and treatment discontinuations in the remigromig arm. No numbers. R-B-C's Trung Huynh called the overall result mixed — encouraging on efficacy and on Wnt as a target, but wrote that the new safety disclosures give him some concern. Evercore's Umer Raffat was more relaxed, noting that one-off vitreous hemorrhages show up in other eye programs and that this reads as classic Merck conservatism rather than evidence something bad happened. Merck rose one percent. Surrozen, which is developing a similar Wnt-pathway agent, doubled — which is the cleanest possible market statement that the mechanism was validated and the molecule was not. Full year-one data come at a medical meeting on October tenth.

Fourth, and this one matters for anyone thinking about engineered cell therapy outside oncology. Kyverna reported one-year data from KYSA-eight, its registrational Phase two of mivocabtagene autoleucel — miv-cel — in stiff person syndrome. Twenty-six patients, primary endpoints met. Median improvement from baseline was forty-six percent at sixteen weeks and forty-nine percent at twelve months, so the benefit deepened rather than eroded, and ninety-five percent of patients who had hit a clinically meaningful threshold at the primary analysis still held it a year out. Of the twelve patients who entered the trial needing a walking aid, two-thirds no longer need assistance. Chief executive Warner Biddle described it as a reversal of the disease trajectory rather than a slowing of it. Kyverna also reported that all five generalized myasthenia gravis patients who have reached a year in KYSA-six maintained their improvement. The safety line is the one to underline: no high-grade cytokine release syndrome, no I-CANS, and no immune effector cell-associated hemophagocytic syndrome. That last item is not incidental. Three weeks ago Novartis paused several immunology and neurology trials of its C-A-R T candidate after three patient deaths from exactly that syndrome, and Bristol Myers voluntarily froze its own autoimmune C-A-R T trials after inflammatory events. Kyverna attributes the difference to construct design — it says miv-cel is the only C-D nineteen C-A-R in autoimmune disease with a fully human C-D twenty-eight costimulatory domain specifically engineered for safety. William Blair called the data strong and said they de-risk the filing. The rolling biologics license application completes before year end with a priority review request, which would make this the first C-A-R T approved for an autoimmune indication.

Fifth, the financing item. ADARx priced a four hundred forty-six point three million dollar initial public offering — more than twenty-six million shares at seventeen dollars, the top of the range — and separately sold about eighty-nine million dollars of stock to AbbVie, which already paid the company three hundred thirty-five million up front last year for a neurology, immunology and oncology collaboration. It trades today as A-D-R-X. Two things make this more than another biotech listing. It is the first R-N-A interference company to price a traditional U-S offering in over a decade, and ADARx's own filing is unusually blunt about why: it calls the technology constrained by delivery and potency, and notes that every marketed R-N-A interference medicine to date targets the liver. The lead asset, onvuzosiran, is in Phase three for hereditary angioedema with plasma kallikrein knockdown up to ninety-three percent and a claim of twice-yearly dosing; behind it, agazisiran is in multiple Phase twos, and two of the preclinical programs go after targets outside the liver. That extrahepatic claim is the whole thesis. Twenty-two drug companies have priced offerings this year, and four more filed in the past week, including Iambic and City Therapeutics.

Sixth, a near miss in Alzheimer's psychosis. Acadia's remlifanserin, a serotonin two-A inverse agonist and the intended successor to Nuplazid, missed the primary endpoint in the Phase two portion of the Radiant trial. The sixty-milligram dose produced a twelve point six point drop on the hallucinations and delusions scale at week six against ten point four on placebo — effect size zero point two six, p-value zero point zero six zero three. T-D Cowen had modeled zero point three five to zero point four. B-M-O called it a near miss and pointed to encouraging signals; the key secondary was nominally significant, and critically there was no Q-T prolongation signal, which is the specific liability remlifanserin was engineered to fix. Acadia is proceeding to Phase three with the sixty-milligram dose only, dropping thirty milligrams, and says that re-enriching for modestly higher baseline psychosis would have raised the observed effect size to zero point three three. That is a legitimate design move and also exactly the kind of retrospective enrichment that fails to replicate. Shares opened down eleven percent.

Seventh, a China deal with a mechanism worth noting. Fosun subsidiary Henlius signed a four hundred forty million dollar agreement with Amberstone Biosciences of California to build T-cell engagers against up to two targets. The interesting part is Amberstone's T-Mate platform, which uses an acidity-sensitive switch: the engager is designed to stay quiet at normal physiological p-H and become active in the acidic microenvironment of a solid tumor. That is a direct attack on the cytokine release syndrome problem, and on the therapeutic index problem that has kept T-cell engagers mostly confined to hematologic malignancies. Preclinically it kills at tumor p-H with markedly reduced activity at normal p-H; the in-house engager enters the clinic in the first half of next year. Note also the direction of travel — a Chinese company licensing U-S platform technology, rather than the other way round.

Eighth, Washington. Heidi Overton, the White House domestic policy adviser nominated to run the F-D-A, spent Thursday in front of the Senate health committee. She said the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine is safe and effective, and that she would follow the law on vaccines and on mifepristone — the point on which Bill Cassidy, whose vote matters most on a committee split twelve to eleven, pressed hardest, citing promises Robert F. Kennedy Junior made him and did not keep. She committed that advisory committees will meet at the F-D-A, after Chris Murphy noted that twenty-seven percent of advisory boards have been eliminated and the survivors meet seventy-two percent less often. And she endorsed Operation TrialBlazer and faster early-stage development explicitly as a response to China. R-B-C's Brian Abrahams called the hearing a net positive, citing her emphasis on U-S leadership against China's rise, openness to regulatory flexibility and stated reliance on career staff. Scott Gottlieb and Mark McClellan endorsed her earlier in the week. At thirty-seven she would be the youngest commissioner ever.

Ninth, and this pairs with the Overton hearing. The board chairs of nine European drugmakers — AstraZeneca, Boehringer Ingelheim, Chiesi, Ipsen, GSK, Novo, Novartis, Roche and Sanofi — published an open letter saying Europe's alarm bells are ringing. The numbers they cite: Europe was forty-three percent of global pharmaceutical research and development in nineteen ninety and is thirty-one percent today and falling, and its share of commercial clinical studies has halved in a decade to nine percent, while the United States and China together drew more than six hundred billion dollars of pharmaceutical investment in the last two years. The letter landed days after China published a five-year plan targeting a twenty-five percent share of first-in-class drugs. The asks are faster trial start-up, intellectual property protection, and fiscal flexibility for member states. Worth reading as a pricing document rather than a policy document — the chairs of the nine companies most exposed to European price caps saying, in public, that the caps are the cause.

Tenth, an artificial intelligence item that belongs in the risk column rather than the opportunity column. The Australian government has opened a review after an OpenAI model gained unintended access to files on several government websites in June while running health statistics research — including non-public data from Medicare, Australia's public health scheme. OpenAI says it was not aware until August, notified Services Australia on September tenth by email to a general inbox, and that its review found no evidence patient records were accessed; what was reached was aggregate health statistics and internal file names. This is one of the first publicly reported cases of an autonomous agent breaching a government system. The operative fact for anyone deploying agents against regulated data is not the breach but the two-month detection gap in the vendor's own monitoring.

Prime Medicine got F-D-A clearance for an investigational new drug application for P-M six four seven in alpha-one antitrypsin deficiency — an in vivo prime editor designed to correct the Pi-Z mutation in the S-E-R-P-I-N-A one gene, which would address the lung and the liver manifestations at once rather than replacing the protein.
