First, Rapport Therapeutics. Its lead drug RAP two nineteen met its primary endpoint in a Phase 2 trial in acute bipolar mania. RAP two nineteen is a pill that dampens AMPA glutamate receptors only where they are paired with a helper protein called TARP gamma eight, which is concentrated in the hippocampus and cortex and largely absent from the hindbrain. The drug cut mania scores by three point one points more than placebo after three weeks, with a p-value of point zero four five. Stifel's verdict was that it "just hits." The stock was up twenty-five percent before the open and finished the day up less than three percent. That is today's spotlight.

Second, two AI drug discovery deals. Eli Lilly signed with METiS TechBio, which is listed in Hong Kong and uses machine learning to design the ionizable lipids that make up lipid nanoparticles. The goal is nanoparticles that reach specific tissues, not just the liver, for programs Lilly will pick. METiS gets an upfront payment in the low double-digit millions, up to one point one billion dollars in milestones, and single-digit royalties. The upfront is small next to the milestone headline, which is typical for a platform deal at this stage. Separately, Endpoints reported exclusively that Astellas has partnered with Boston-based AI Proteins for up to four hundred million dollars to develop de novo miniproteins, small designed binders that fall between peptides and antibodies. CEO Chris Bahl named TNF receptor one and multi-target cancer drugs as areas of interest. AI Proteins signed a deal of the same headline size with Bristol Myers Squibb in late twenty twenty-four, so this is its second big-pharma validation.

Third, Arcus Biosciences gave a broad update on casdatifan, its pill that blocks HIF two alpha, a protein that kidney cancer cells rely on to grow. That is the same target as Merck's approved Welireg. In late-line patients on casdatifan alone, median overall survival was thirty-five months and median progression-free survival was thirteen months, across a hundred twenty-one patients with twenty-eight months of follow-up. In second line, casdatifan plus the kinase inhibitor cabozantinib gave fourteen and a half months of median progression-free survival, whether or not patients had already had a kinase inhibitor. That matters because about sixty percent of second-line patients have. As a first-line regimen with no kinase inhibitor, casdatifan plus Arcus's PD-1 antibody produced a fifty percent response rate in thirty patients. The stock swung both ways during the day and closed up about seven percent. Gilead let its option on casdatifan expire in February twenty twenty-five, though it bought into Arcus's stock offering that same day. Arcus now holds the rights alone, and data like these will set what they're worth.

Fourth, the IPO window stays open. Lycia Therapeutics set terms for an IPO of about two hundred twenty-seven million dollars: fourteen point seven million shares at sixteen to eighteen dollars each, to trade on Nasdaq as L Y C A. Lycia is built on LYTACs, Carolyn Bertozzi's Stanford discovery of molecules that drag proteins outside the cell into the lysosome to be destroyed. That extends protein degradation beyond the cell interior, where PROTACs and molecular glues work. Its lead programs remove IgE, the antibody behind food allergy, and the autoantibodies that drive Graves' disease, and both are in Phase 1. Versant is the largest holder at about nineteen percent before the offering, followed by Venrock Healthcare Capital Partners, Redmile, R T W and Janus Henderson. This comes just a week after Iambic's IPO.

Fifth, drug pricing. ICER, the nonprofit that judges whether drug prices match their value, reviewed twenty-six drugs approved from twenty twenty-two through twenty twenty-five. Adjusted for inflation, the median net launch price rose from a hundred ninety thousand dollars to two hundred fifty-seven thousand. Only one of the twenty-six fell within ICER's value benchmarks. By ICER's math, Insmed's Brinsupri and Merck's Winrevair would need discounts of about ninety percent. ICER also says four in ten prescriptions for these drugs were initially rejected by insurers. Industry will dispute the methodology, as it always does, but the report lands as most-favored-nation pricing talks continue.

Sixth, Novo Nordisk presented two post hoc analyses of its REDEFINE one trial at the Menopause Society meeting. Women on the top dose of CagriSema, Novo's combination of semaglutide with the amylin analog cagrilintide, lost twenty-five percent of their body weight at sixty-eight weeks, versus three percent on placebo. Weight loss was similar before, during and after menopause. Read this as marketing ahead of an FDA decision expected late this year, not new efficacy. The whole-trial number was twenty-two point seven percent, below Novo's own twenty-five percent target, and CagriSema failed to match tirzepatide head to head in February.
