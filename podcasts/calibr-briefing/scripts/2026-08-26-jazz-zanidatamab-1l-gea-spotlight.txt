This is your spotlight for Wednesday, August twenty-sixth, twenty twenty-six. Trastuzumab has been the front-line H-E-R-two agent in gastroesophageal cancer since the ToGA trial in twenty ten. As of yesterday afternoon, it is not.

Here are the mechanics first. The F-D-A approved two Ziihera regimens from Jazz Pharmaceuticals for adults with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic H-E-R-two-positive gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, previously untreated. Regimen one is zanidatamab plus tislelizumab — Tevimbra, BeOne Medicines' P-D-one antibody — plus fluoropyrimidine and platinum chemotherapy, and that one covers the full H-E-R-two-positive population, I-H-C three-plus and I-H-C two-plus with I-S-H positivity. Regimen two is zanidatamab plus chemotherapy alone, restricted to I-H-C three-plus. The label carries boxed warnings for diarrhea and for embryo-fetal toxicity. The drug is commercially available now.

Now the molecule, because the molecule is the entire argument. Zanidatamab is what the field calls biparatopic. It is a single I-g-G-one-like antibody whose two arms bind two different epitopes on the same protein — extracellular domain two and extracellular domain four of H-E-R-two. If those coordinates sound familiar, they should: domain four is where trastuzumab binds and domain two is where pertuzumab binds. So the obvious reading is that zanidatamab is trastuzumab and pertuzumab fused into one molecule, and the obvious reading is wrong in the way that matters.

When you give a patient trastuzumab and pertuzumab together, you get two independent blockades on the same receptor. Useful, additive, and that is roughly the whole effect. What zanidatamab does is different because of geometry. A single antibody reaching two epitopes cannot easily reach both on the same receptor molecule, so it reaches across — one arm on one H-E-R-two, the other arm on the neighbor. That crosslinks adjacent receptors, and crosslinking propagates. You get receptor clustering on the tumor cell surface, and clustering does two things a blockade cannot. It drives internalization and downregulation, so the surface H-E-R-two density actually falls rather than merely being occupied. And it produces something no two-antibody combination produces efficiently, which is a dense, ordered array of F-c domains sitting in one patch of membrane. That array is what complement and F-c-receptor-bearing effector cells recognize. Hence the three acronyms Jazz keeps repeating — complement-dependent cytotoxicity, antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity, antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis. Daniel Lin of Thomas Jefferson, discussing the data at ASCO GI, put it as plainly as anyone: receptor clustering, potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity, enhanced A-D-C-C and A-D-C-P, all of which may lead to greater immune activation and potentially greater synergy with a P-D-one antibody. Hold onto that last clause, because it is where this gets interesting.

The trial. HERIZON-G-E-A-oh-one randomized nine hundred fourteen previously untreated patients one-to-one-to-one across roughly two hundred twenty-five sites in more than thirty countries, enrolling from December twenty twenty-one to February twenty twenty-five. Arm A was trastuzumab plus chemotherapy — the standard. Arm B was zanidatamab plus chemotherapy. Arm C was zanidatamab plus tislelizumab plus chemotherapy. More than two-thirds of the patients had gastric primaries; ninety percent got CAPOX as the chemotherapy backbone. H-E-R-two status was centrally confirmed. Dual primary endpoints, progression-free survival by blinded independent review and overall survival. Critically, enrollment was open regardless of P-D-L-one status.

At a twenty-six-month median follow-up, median progression-free survival was twelve point four months in both zanidatamab arms against eight point one months in the trastuzumab arm — hazard ratios of zero point six three to zero point six five, a thirty-five percent reduction in the risk of progression or death, and an absolute gain of just over four months. Overall survival in Arm C, the triplet, was twenty-six point four months against nineteen point two, hazard ratio zero point seven two, a seven-month absolute gain. Elena Elimova of Princess Margaret, the lead author, framed the historical marker precisely: this is the first phase three in metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma with a median progression-free survival above one year and a median overall survival above two years.

The response data are where I would spend my attention if I were modeling uptake, because they say something about depth rather than just delay. Confirmed objective response rates were seventy point seven percent for the triplet, sixty-nine point six for zanidatamab plus chemotherapy, sixty-five point seven for the control — a modest spread. But complete responses were nineteen point six, seventeen point one and eleven point zero percent, and median duration of response was twenty point seven months, fourteen point three months, and eight point three months. Eight point three to twenty point seven is not an incremental improvement, that is a different clinical experience. Rachna Shroff at Arizona, ASCO's designated expert on the data, singled out exactly that: what is remarkable is a duration of response that is truly meaningful.

Now the number that is either the most important result in this dataset or the least trustworthy, and you should decide which. In the prespecified P-D-L-one subgroup analysis, patients whose tumors were P-D-L-one negative — tumor area positivity under one percent — had a median overall survival of twenty-nine point seven months on the triplet against fifteen point eight months on trastuzumab plus chemotherapy. In P-D-L-one positive patients, the same comparison was twenty-six point four against twenty-one point two. Read that twice. The absolute benefit is larger in the patients who, by every prior in immuno-oncology, should have benefited least from adding a P-D-one antibody. Kohei Shitara, co-lead author, called it out directly — survival benefit with the addition of tislelizumab was suggested across P-D-L-one-defined subgroups, including in patients with P-D-L-one-negative tumors, a population that has historically derived limited benefit from P-D-one-based approaches. Jazz's Rob Iannone went further and called it the first immuno-oncology combination to show efficacy across both P-D-L-one-positive and negative tumors in this setting, and he explicitly tied it to the clustering mechanism.

The mechanistic story is coherent enough to be worth taking seriously. P-D-L-one expression is a proxy for a pre-existing, exhausted T-cell response that a checkpoint antibody can release. If zanidatamab's clustering is generating innate immune engagement — complement, macrophage phagocytosis, N-K-mediated killing — then it may be manufacturing tumor antigen release and priming in tumors that had no pre-existing T-cell response to release. In that world, P-D-L-one stops being the right selection biomarker because the drug creates the substrate the checkpoint inhibitor needs. That is a real hypothesis, and if it holds it is worth far more than this indication.

Here is why you should not spend it yet. The trial was not stratified by P-D-L-one status. That was Lin's central caveat and it is the correct one — an unstratified subgroup split in a three-arm trial invites imbalance, and the tell is in the control arm, where P-D-L-one-negative patients did dramatically worse than P-D-L-one-positive patients, fifteen point eight months against twenty-one point two. Some of the apparent triplet benefit in that subgroup is the control arm's floor, not the drug's ceiling. Second, there is no head-to-head against the actual current standard for P-D-L-one-positive patients, which is trastuzumab plus pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy per KEYNOTE-eight-eleven. As Shroff noted, HERIZON was designed before those data existed. Cross-trial, KEYNOTE-eight-eleven's final median overall survival was twenty point zero months against sixteen point eight — so twenty-six point four looks commanding, but it is a different trial, different era, different population. Third, Arm B's overall survival is still immature. Elimova described a strong trend, about five months of separation; Jazz guided the second interim analysis for mid-twenty twenty-six, so it is either imminent or already in hand. Until that reads out, the zanidatamab-plus-chemotherapy regimen — the one P-D-L-one-negative patients would get if you did not believe the subgroup — is supported on progression-free survival and a trend. And fourth, to Jazz's credit they disclosed it: the control arm received more subsequent checkpoint inhibitors and more subsequent H-E-R-two therapy, because more of those patients progressed. That confound runs against the zanidatamab arms on overall survival, which is the reassuring direction, but it is a confound.

The toxicity is a real cost and I want to state it without softening. Grade three or higher treatment-related adverse events were seventy-one point eight percent in the triplet against fifty-nine point six percent in the control, and discontinuations for treatment-related toxicity were forty-two point five percent against twenty-nine point one. That is roughly twelve points of severe toxicity and thirteen points of discontinuation bought with seven months of median survival. Diarrhea is the driver and it carries a boxed warning: severe, life-threatening and fatal cases occur despite loperamide prophylaxis, the risk is higher with tislelizumab in the regimen, and higher again in patients sixty-five and over. Cycle-one loperamide prophylaxis is mandated in the label. Practically, the events cluster early, generally resolve within about three weeks, and rarely cause discontinuation of the H-E-R-two agent specifically. But in a seventy-something gastric cancer patient on CAPOX, front-loaded severe diarrhea is not a footnote, and community oncology adoption will be governed by how well that first cycle is managed.

The competitive read. Trastuzumab plus chemotherapy has held this indication since twenty ten. In March twenty twenty-five, pembrolizumab was added for P-D-L-one-positive patients on KEYNOTE-eight-eleven, and Enhertu holds later lines. Yesterday's approval displaces trastuzumab as the H-E-R-two backbone and, in the same stroke, installs a non-Merck P-D-one antibody in the front line of a solid tumor where Merck had the franchise. Tislelizumab is a BeOne Medicines molecule — a China-origin P-D-one that Novartis licensed and returned, now written into a U.S. label as part of a first-line standard of care in a thirty-country phase three. Set that against the same week's news flow, with two Republican lawmakers asking the F-D-A to increase scrutiny of Chinese trial data and ARCH standing up a NewCo around a Haisco asset. The China-origin question is not one question. There is a real difference between single-country investigator-initiated data and a global randomized phase three with central pathology review, and this label is the cleanest available illustration of it.

The economics tell you how the value is actually distributed, and it is not to Jazz alone. Zanidatamab was discovered and engineered in Vancouver on Zymeworks' Azymetric platform, and Zymeworks carried it through I-N-D before licensing Asia-Pacific to BeOne in twenty eighteen and the rest of the world to Jazz in twenty twenty-two. Approval triggered a two hundred fifty million dollar milestone, taking total Jazz payments to Zymeworks to six hundred fifty million, with up to one point three billion more available and tiered royalties of ten to twenty percent. From BeOne, Zymeworks has taken eighty-one million to date with up to a hundred forty-four million more and royalties up to nineteen and a half percent. Ken Galbraith has explicitly turned Zymeworks into a royalty-and-asset aggregator funding a wholly-owned pipeline of trispecific T-cell engagers and A-D-Cs. Whatever you think of that strategy, yesterday it was validated in cash.

The street's disagreement is the thing worth watching. Stifel's Annabel Samimy models six hundred fifty to seven hundred million dollars in U.S. gastroesophageal sales, and observes that eligibility goes from about fifteen hundred biliary tract patients to roughly twelve thousand. R-B-C's Leonid Timashev sees the opportunity north of one and a half billion and expects relatively quick uptake. Broader peak estimates across all indications run three to five billion. That spread is not a disagreement about the data. It is a disagreement about how fast a community oncologist stops reaching for trastuzumab, a drug they have used for sixteen years and can now buy as a biosimilar for very little, in favor of a branded bispecific with a boxed warning for diarrhea. Jazz has submitted the manuscript to N-C-C-N for guideline inclusion, and that is the switch that matters more than any sales force.

One last thread for your world specifically. Iambic's I-A-M-one-three-six-three is a brain-penetrant, H-E-R-two-selective small-molecule kinase inhibitor — more than five thousand-fold selective over E-G-F-R, roughly ten-fold better C-N-S penetration than approved T-K-Is, active against wild-type and mutant H-E-R-two — and it came out of an A-I-driven discovery platform. Last October, Jazz agreed to supply zanidatamab at no cost for a combination cohort in Iambic's phase one study, in H-E-R-two-positive breast cancer patients who have already progressed on T-D-X-d. That cohort just got more valuable, because its partner molecule stopped being an interesting bispecific and became a validated backbone with a label, a guideline submission, and a commercial organization behind it. The generalizable point is that the scarce resource for an A-I-native discovery company is not compute or candidates — it is a combination slot next to a drug that works. That is what Jazz gave Iambic for free, and it is worth watching who else starts paying that particular currency.

What to watch: the second interim overall survival readout for zanidatamab plus chemotherapy, which decides whether the doublet stands on its own for P-D-L-one-negative patients; N-C-C-N guideline language, which decides uptake; the ongoing confirmatory work Zymeworks references for global registration; and the colorectal program, where zanidatamab already holds a breakthrough therapy designation. That is your spotlight.
