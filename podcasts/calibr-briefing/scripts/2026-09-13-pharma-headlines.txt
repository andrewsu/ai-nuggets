Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Sunday, September thirteenth, twenty twenty-six. Almost everything today comes out of Seoul, where the World Conference on Lung Cancer opened Saturday and runs through Tuesday. In about thirty-six hours the small cell program alone has produced two positive Phase three trials against topotecan, a de-escalation result that takes a forty-year-old radiation practice off the table, and a dosing study that could decide how widely a T-cell engager can actually be used. And overnight Akeso put numbers on the survival win against Keytruda that it announced without any numbers ten days ago. Seven items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight, so I will give you the shape here and take it apart in the second episode. Akeso published the overall survival data from HARMONi-two, the Chinese Phase three of ivonescimab against pembrolizumab in first-line P-D-L-one-positive non-small cell lung cancer. Ivonescimab is the P-D-one by V-E-G-F-A bispecific antibody that Summit Therapeutics licensed for five billion dollars in late twenty twenty-four. Median overall survival was thirty point eight months on ivonescimab against twenty-two point six months on Keytruda, a hazard ratio of zero point seven three, confidence interval zero point five seven to zero point nine five, p equals zero point zero zero nine. Three hundred ninety-eight patients, fifty-five hospitals in China, thirty-six months of median follow-up, two hundred thirty-four deaths. That is the first time any agent has beaten pembrolizumab monotherapy on survival in a randomized, double-blind head-to-head, and it answers the standing objection to the May twenty twenty-four progression-free survival result — that an anti-V-E-G-F regimen can flatter itself on radiographic progression without extending lives. The subgroups are where the interesting problems are, and that is the spotlight.

Second, MediLink and Roche put a B7-H3 antibody-drug conjugate through topotecan in relapsed small cell lung cancer, and the margin is not subtle. In TAISHAN-three-oh-two, four hundred fifty-one patients randomized roughly evenly, tambotatug pelitecan — tam-peli, the compound Roche bought ex-China rights to for five hundred seventy million dollars in upfront and near-term payments — produced median overall survival of thirteen point three months against nine point four for topotecan, hazard ratio zero point four six, a fifty-four percent reduction in the risk of death, p less than zero point zero zero zero one. Progression-free survival was seven point four months against two point eight, hazard ratio zero point two nine. Response rate was fifty-nine point one percent against nine point seven. And the tolerability ran the right direction: grade three or higher treatment-related adverse events in forty-six point four percent of the A-D-C arm against seventy-four point seven percent on topotecan, with no treatment-related deaths. Interstitial lung disease or pneumonitis was four point nine percent versus one point four, grade three in under one percent of both arms. B7-H3 is a surface protein broadly expressed on small cell tumors, and tam-peli uses MediLink's tumor-microenvironment-activated linker, which is designed to release payload preferentially in tumor tissue. Li Zhang, presenting, called it among the first Phase three studies of an antibody-drug conjugate to show this in the disease. Topotecan has been the relapsed small cell comparator since the late nineteen nineties. It should not survive this year.

Third, and on the same target, Hansoh presented ARTEMIS-oh-oh-eight, the Phase three of risvutatug rezetecan — the B7-H3 A-D-C that GSK licensed in late twenty twenty-three — also against topotecan in relapsed small cell, also in China, roughly four hundred sixty patients. It met its overall survival primary endpoint with consistent progression-free survival benefit and no new safety signals. Hesham Abdullah, who runs oncology R and D at GSK, framed it as the first positive Phase three survival data for a B7-H3-targeted A-D-C in any tumor type. Both trials are China-only, both beat the same weak comparator, and the two of them together mean the relapsed small cell market is about to be contested by two very similar molecules with two large-pharma partners behind them. The differentiation is going to have to come from interstitial lung disease rates and from who gets a global trial done first.

Fourth, the de-escalation result. MAVERICK, the SWOG S-eighteen-twenty-seven trial, randomized three hundred four small cell patients who had completed initial therapy and had no brain metastases on M-R-I to surveillance imaging alone or surveillance plus prophylactic cranial irradiation at twenty-five gray in ten fractions. The primary endpoint was cognitive-failure-free survival, and M-R-I alone won it: hazard ratio zero point six zero, a forty percent reduction in the risk of cognitive failure or death, p equals zero point zero zero one at twenty-two months median follow-up. Overall survival, preliminary after a hundred twenty-eight deaths, showed no difference — hazard ratio zero point nine zero — with the final analysis due at a hundred ninety deaths. Brain-metastasis-free survival also showed no difference. Grade three to five adverse events were zero point eight percent with imaging alone against seven point nine percent with radiation, including one fatal encephalopathy in the radiation arm. Chad Rusthoven at Colorado presented it. Prophylactic cranial irradiation has been standard for decades on the argument that you cannot see micrometastases; modern M-R-I says you largely can. This is the rare trial whose product is removing a treatment rather than adding one, and it is the single most immediately actionable result of the meeting.

Fifth, staying with Amgen's DLL-three engager. DeLLphi-three-oh-nine randomized two hundred fifty-two previously treated small cell patients across three tarlatamab schedules — ten milligrams every two weeks, twenty every three weeks, thirty every four weeks, all after a one-milligram step dose. Blinded central response rates were forty, thirty-one and twenty-seven percent; investigator-assessed, thirty-six, thirty-seven and thirty-one. Median progression-free survival was four point two, four point one and two point seven months. Cytokine release syndrome ran sixty to seventy percent across all three, predominantly grade one or two, with neurotoxicity in six to twelve percent. Jonathan Goldman at U-C-L-A read the every-three-week arm as holding up. This looks like a minor study and it is not: T-cell engagers in solid tumors are gated by monitoring burden, and a regimen you can give monthly is a different drug commercially than one you give fortnightly. Separately, the numbers from DeLLphi-three-oh-five — the first-line maintenance trial of tarlatamab plus durvalumab that Amgen and AstraZeneca said hit overall survival on September eighth — are still not public. Five hundred sixty-three patients, positive on survival, progression-free survival and response rate, and no hazard ratio yet. Until that number lands you cannot size what is arguably the most important small cell result of the year.

Sixth, the negative lesson, which is worth as much as the wins. EVOKE-oh-three, the Phase three of Gilead's Trodelvy — sacituzumab govitecan, the T-ROP-two A-D-C — plus pembrolizumab against pembrolizumab alone in untreated non-small cell lung cancer with P-D-L-one at fifty percent or above, is being presented in Seoul after Merck and Gilead discontinued it in June on a data monitoring committee review. Progression-free survival numerically favored the combination and did not reach significance, and the committee judged a survival win unlikely at final analysis. Two active drugs, a biomarker-selected population, and no additivity. That is the counterweight to every checkpoint-plus-A-D-C deal signed in the last two years, and it is why the ivonescimab result matters so much — a single molecule engineered to do both things is a different bet than two molecules given together.
