Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Monday, August thirty-first, twenty twenty-six.

It is the closing day of the European Society of Cardiology congress in Munich, and unlike the weekend, the business wire actually woke up. Three genuine corporate items landed overnight, including a royalty settlement that reprices an entire rare-disease market, and the congress delivered one more late-breaker that Amgen is turning into the biggest marketing claim of its cardiovascular franchise. Ten items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight. Amgen presented a prespecified mortality analysis of VESALIUS-C-V in a late-breaking session this morning, published simultaneously in Circulation, and the press release headline is that Repatha reduces the risk of death in patients at high risk for a first heart attack or stroke. The number is a twenty percent relative reduction in death from any cause, in twelve thousand two hundred fifty-seven patients with atherosclerosis or high-risk diabetes and no prior heart attack or stroke, treated with evolocumab a hundred forty milligrams every two weeks on top of optimized lipid-lowering therapy, followed a median four point six years. The curves separate at about eighteen months. Now, the trial's primary results, published in the New England Journal in January, were unambiguous — a twenty-five percent reduction in the three-point composite of coronary death, heart attack or ischemic stroke, nineteen percent on the four-point composite that adds revascularization, and a thirty-six percent reduction in heart attack, all at p less than zero point zero zero one. Mortality is a different matter. The prespecified testing hierarchy broke one step above it, at death from coronary heart disease, which came in at a hazard ratio of zero point eight nine with a p value of zero point three nine. Everything below that step in the sequence — cardiovascular death and all-cause death included — is formally exploratory, and the New England Journal paper says so in plain language. So the question for today is whether a twenty percent mortality number that the trial's own statistical plan will not certify should change how you think about this drug, and I think the answer is more interesting than either yes or no. That is the whole spotlight.

Second, the readout with actual money moving behind it. Arrowhead presented the full twelve-month SHASTA-three and SHASTA-four datasets yesterday, and the stock rose twenty-three percent to just over ninety-one dollars. Plozasiran, brand name Redemplo, is a quarterly R-N-A-interference injection that silences apolipoprotein C-three in the liver — apo-C-three is the brake on triglyceride clearance, so turning it down releases the brake. Seven hundred fifty-seven patients across twenty-four countries with severe hypertriglyceridemia. Median triglycerides fell seventy-nine and eighty-one percent at month twelve against roughly twenty-seven percent on placebo. But the number that matters is the prespecified pooled analysis of acute pancreatitis, which is the event that actually hospitalizes these patients: a seventy-eight percent reduction in event rate, rate ratio zero point two two, an absolute risk reduction of four point one percent over one year, and a number needed to treat of twenty-four. In the highest-risk subgroup — triglycerides above eight hundred eighty with a prior pancreatitis episode — there were zero events on drug, and in the broader prior-pancreatitis group the number needed to treat is three. Safety was clean with one blemish: worsening glycemic control in fourteen point three percent versus eight point seven on placebo. No platelet changes, no Hy's-law liver cases, and specifically no increase in liver fat on the magnetic resonance substudy. That last point is the competitive knife. Ionis and Sobi's Tryngolza, olezarsen, was approved in this indication on June twenty-fourth — same target, antisense rather than R-N-A interference, dosed monthly, with an eighty-five percent pancreatitis reduction on its own label and a documented mean increase in liver fat. R-B-C's Luca Issi wrote that physicians will likely see the two as equally efficacious and gave Arrowhead the dosing advantage; T-D Cowen's Joseph Thome has said plozasiran could take majority share. Arrowhead files its supplemental application before year-end and will burn the priority review voucher it bought on August fourth to compress the review. Management hosts a call on the data today.

Third, and this is the one with the most structural read-through. BioMarin and Ascendis announced a binding global settlement resolving every patent dispute over Yuviwel, Ascendis's once-weekly C-type natriuretic peptide prodrug for achondroplasia. Ascendis will pay BioMarin a twenty percent royalty on U-S net sales, retroactive to first commercial sale, and eighteen percent in the European Union, Brazil and South Korea, running through May twenty thirty. That resolves a Section three-thirty-seven investigation at the International Trade Commission plus litigation in Brazil, Denmark, Germany, South Korea and the Northern District of California, and it licenses Ascendis for achondroplasia, hypochondroplasia and combination use. Hold the strategic shape of this. Voxzogo, vosoritide, is a daily injection and BioMarin's top-selling product; Yuviwel won accelerated approval in February as a once-weekly, and BioMarin has already paused three Voxzogo Phase two programs in Turner syndrome, S-H-O-X deficiency and aggrecan deficiency and walked back its four-billion-dollar sales ambition for the franchise. So BioMarin is losing the convenience war on the product and simultaneously taxing the winner one dollar in five. For a company facing competitive displacement, converting patent leverage into a fifth of your competitor's revenue is close to the best available outcome, and it is worth remembering the next time someone tells you a rare-disease franchise dies the day a better-dosed rival lands.

Fourth and fifth, the transthyretin amyloidosis pair, and read them together because the field's central question is now open. BridgeBio presented the cardiac magnetic resonance substudy and open-label extension of ATTRibute-C-M for Attruby, acoramidis — the near-complete oral T-T-R stabilizer — and is claiming the first evidence of actual reversal of cardiac structural disease progression over forty-two months, plus a significant increase in days alive and free from hospitalization. The company dosed the first participant in ASCEND-ATTR, a Phase three-b-slash-four built specifically to test regression prospectively, on August twenty-sixth. On the other side of the mechanism, Alnylam presented HELIOS-B analyses for Amvuttra, vutrisiran, showing a twenty-eight point two percent reduction in all-cause mortality and recurrent cardiovascular events overall and thirty-two point eight percent in the monotherapy population through thirty-six months, plus a post hoc analysis on preservation of intrinsic capacity. Set against AstraZeneca and Ionis's eplontersen missing CARDIO-T-T-Ransform on Thursday — and looking numerically worse on a tafamidis background — the emerging picture is that both stabilizers and silencers can carry this disease, and that the silencer-plus-stabilizer combination thesis is the thing that broke, not either mechanism alone.

Sixth, a result that should change how symptom trials are designed. PVI-SHAM-A-F randomized two hundred sixty-two symptomatic atrial fibrillation patients two-to-one to pulmonary vein isolation or a sham procedure across nine sites in Germany and Poland — a genuinely blinded ablation trial, which the field has almost never done. Quality of life improved substantially in both arms and the difference was not significant: the atrial fibrillation quality-of-life score went from sixty-one point three to eighty-one point one after ablation and from fifty-nine point two to seventy-four point nine after sham, p equals zero point three six. Rhythm control did differ — seventy-three percent freedom from atrial fibrillation versus fifty-two percent. Rolf Wachter of Leipzig put it directly: most of the improvement is not attributable to the ablation. Funded by Leipzig University and Helios. If you are running any trial with a patient-reported symptom endpoint in a procedural or device-adjacent indication, this is the citation that will be used against an open-label design.

Seventh and eighth, two clean randomized negatives on the final day. DANISH-C-R-T randomized a thousand heart failure patients with left bundle branch block across all five Danish implanting centers to electrically targeted versus standard left ventricular lead placement, median follow-up forty-five point eight months: death or unplanned heart failure hospitalization in twenty-seven point nine versus twenty-five point five percent, p equals zero point four five. Jens Cosedis Nielsen's conclusion is that routine electrical mapping is not supported. And the TAVI-P-C-I trial randomized nine hundred eighty-six patients, mean age eighty-two, across forty-eight European centers to valve-first or stent-first sequencing in severe aortic stenosis with concomitant coronary disease: twenty-two point two versus twenty-four point two percent at one year, noninferior. Barbara Stähli of Zurich frames it as license to individualize rather than follow a protocol.

Ninth, two prevention datasets with very different implications. The REACT initiative imaged sixteen thousand eight hundred eight adults aged eighteen to seventy in Denmark and Spain with no known cardiovascular disease and found silent atherosclerosis in fifty-seven point one percent — about one in thirteen in the eighteen-to-twenty-nine group rising to nine in ten in the sixty-to-seventy group. Funded by the Novo Nordisk Foundation. Keep that number next to the Repatha story, because it is the denominator problem in one line. And the China Rural Hypertension Control Program, thirty-three thousand nine hundred ninety-five participants across three hundred twenty-six villages, showed that a non-physician-delivered intensive blood pressure intervention reduces dementia risk, not just blood pressure. Also worth noting from the U-K, ASPIRED randomized two thousand two hundred thirty-three unexplained-syncope patients at forty-five hospitals to fourteen days of wearable E-C-G monitoring started in the emergency department versus standard care, funded by the British Heart Foundation, and found better arrhythmia diagnosis plus a survival signal the investigators are careful to call unexplained.

Tenth, the quiet regulatory item. Alvotech announced F-D-A acceptance of its application for A-V-T-eighty, a proposed interchangeable biosimilar to Entyvio, vedolizumab, in prefilled syringe and autoinjector form — the subcutaneous presentations, following the intravenous filing accepted in June. Teva commercializes. Vedolizumab is the gut-selective alpha-four-beta-seven integrin antibody, and the subcutaneous format is where the remaining volume is, so this is the filing that matters for the franchise rather than the earlier one.

On the two things that did not happen: no new A-I-in-drug-discovery news of substance over the weekend, and no deals beyond the BioMarin settlement. The week ahead: the congress closes today; the European Respiratory Society meets September fifth through ninth, where Generate Biomedicines' golukibart posters get their formal presentation; Ultragenyx's Sanfilippo A gene therapy has a September nineteenth action date; Scholar Rock's apitegromab is September thirtieth; Capricor's deramiocel remains November twenty-second; Cytokinetics files in non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy in the fourth quarter; and Arrowhead files in severe hypertriglyceridemia before year-end.
