Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Tuesday, October sixth, twenty twenty-six. The lead and today's spotlight is Vaxcyte: its thirty-one-strain pneumococcal vaccine came out of its pivotal adult trial cleaner than Wall Street expected, the stock rose about half, and by the evening the company was raising a billion dollars. Around it: Shionogi pays two billion dollars in cash for a rare-disease company with sixty-eight million dollars in sales, Genentech buys a brain-penetrant enzyme for Parkinson's from Alector, Genentech's head of A-I drug discovery is out, Lilly doubles down on a company that stops proteins from leaving the cell, and the F-D-A clears the first treatment of any kind for the negative symptoms of schizophrenia. Nine items, then the week ahead.

First, Vaxcyte. The O-P-U-S-one trial put VAX-thirty-one, a thirty-one-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, head-to-head against Pfizer's Prevnar twenty and Merck's Capvaxive in about thirty-six hundred adults fifty and older. Every one of the twenty-eight strains it shares with those vaccines met non-inferiority, and the three strains only Vaxcyte covers met superiority. Against Merck's shot alone, two strains, serotype three and serotype twelve-F, missed the stricter margin the trial used but cleared the looser margin that earlier vaccines were approved on. Mizuho's Salim Syed called it a near best-case scenario, and Leerink's David Risinger said it raises the bar for every future adult pneumococcal developer. Shares jumped about fifty percent, past a twelve-billion-dollar market value, and Vaxcyte then launched five hundred million dollars of stock and five hundred million of convertible notes. The filing isn't planned until the first half of twenty twenty-eight. The spotlight covers why the trial design matters as much as the result, and the policy risk nobody on the call wanted to talk about.

Second, Shionogi is buying IntraBio, a private company based in Texas, for two billion dollars in cash. What it gets is Aqneursa, levacetylleucine, an acetylated form of the amino acid leucine that came out of Oxford research and is taken by mouth. It was approved in twenty twenty-four for Niemann-Pick disease type C, a lysosomal lipid-storage disorder affecting roughly one in a million Americans, on an improvement in a neurological movement-disorder score, and was cleared last month for ataxia-telangiectasia, a larger population. IntraBio booked sixty-eight million dollars in sales last year, so Shionogi is paying about thirty times trailing revenue, and the second label is what makes the price work. The Niemann-Pick field is getting crowded: Zevra's arimoclomol was approved days before Aqneursa in twenty twenty-four, and Cyclo Therapeutics says it will file its cyclodextrin even though its Phase three missed. This follows Shionogi's purchase of the A-L-S drug Radicava last year, and a Phase three in Fragile X from its twenty twenty Tetra buyout. A Japanese pharma assembling a U.S. rare-neurology business one marketed asset at a time is a buyer profile worth remembering for late-stage rare-disease companies.

Third, Genentech and Alector. Genentech pays a hundred million dollars up front, with up to one point one seven billion in milestones plus royalties, for worldwide rights to A-L-fifty. It is an engineered version of glucocerebrosidase, the lysosomal enzyme that the G-B-A-one gene encodes, fused to Alector's brain-carrier technology so the enzyme gets across the blood-brain barrier. G-B-A-one mutations are the most common genetic risk factor for Parkinson's and are linked to a more aggressive course, so the idea is to replace the missing enzyme in the brain rather than try to boost the patient's own. The program is preclinical. Roche already has its own shuttle, Brainshuttle, carrying the late-stage amyloid antibody trontinemab, so this buys a second delivery system in the same building. For Alector it is a lifeline after a long run of failures: AbbVie's partnered Alzheimer's antibody failed in twenty twenty-four, the Phase three of latozinemab in frontotemporal dementia failed a year ago, and G-S-K walked away in July. The company says cash now lasts into twenty twenty-nine, and it is moving a subcutaneous brain-shuttled anti-amyloid antibody forward.

Fourth, and relevant to anyone building A-I discovery inside a large company: Endpoints reports that Richard Bonneau, Genentech's global head of A-I drug discovery, has left, amid what sources describe as tension with research chief Aviv Regev. John Marioni steps in as interim head. Bonneau came to Genentech when it bought Prescient Design in twenty twenty-one, along with Kyunghyun Cho, whose own departure to start a health A-I lab we covered last Wednesday. In a few weeks Genentech has lost both of the people most associated with lab-in-the-loop antibody design. Whether the design platform survives the people who built it is the question every pharma that acquired an A-I team is now facing, and Genentech is the most visible test case.

Fifth, Lilly and Gate Bioscience. Lilly is putting up more than eight hundred seventy million dollars in potential value, with an undisclosed up-front, for one new target plus an option on a second, on top of an eight-hundred-fifty-six-million-dollar deal from July twenty twenty-five. Gate makes oral small molecules it calls molecular gates. Almost every secreted and membrane protein enters the cell's secretory pathway through a single channel in the endoplasmic reticulum, the Sec-sixty-one translocon. Gate's compounds bind that channel and selectively block one target protein as it tries to pass through, so the cell degrades it instead of exporting it, while other proteins go through normally. The appeal is that it turns targets normally reserved for antibodies or injectables into pills. Forbion led Gate's sixty-five-million-dollar Series B last November with Lilly participating. A pharma coming back for a second target within fifteen months, before any clinical data, is the clearest validation a discovery platform can get.

Sixth, the F-D-A cleared Motivista, Click Therapeutics' prescription digital therapeutic formerly called C-T-one-fifty-five, for the negative symptoms of schizophrenia: low motivation, loss of pleasure, social withdrawal. It is the first authorized treatment of any kind for that indication, and it is software. In the Phase three CONVOKE trial, patients on stable antipsychotics improved six point eight points on the negative-symptom scale at sixteen weeks against four point two for a sham control app. That effect size is modest, a Cohen's d of about a third. But negative symptoms are where antipsychotics and the new muscarinic drugs have done the least, and having a cleared comparator now sets a bar for every drug program aimed at that indication.

Seventh, Bristol Myers. Leerink's David Risinger downgraded the company to market perform and cut his price target to fifty-nine dollars, citing doubts about three assets: admilparant, the L-P-A-one antagonist in pulmonary fibrosis whose trial was amended after liver-injury events; milvexian, the factor eleven-a anticoagulant; and Cobenfy, the muscarinic schizophrenia drug. Combined with BMO's bearish view of Bristol's patent-cliff plan last week, the sell side is increasingly saying that the company's post-Eliquis bridge rests on drugs it doesn't trust.

Eighth, three I-P-Os in the queue. TRex Bio is targeting about a hundred eleven million dollars and Retension about thirty-four million, and Lycia Therapeutics has filed. Lycia develops lysosome-targeting chimeras, or LYTACs, which are the extracellular cousin of PROTACs: they link a target protein on the cell surface or in the blood to a receptor that pulls it into the lysosome for destruction. Notice that Gate and Lycia are both after the same territory, removing extracellular proteins without an antibody, one at the point of exit and one after the protein is already outside.

Ninth, the quick ones. Spyre priced a three-hundred-fifty-million-dollar offering at eighty-five dollars a share to fund its long-acting anti-T-L-one-A and combination programs, including a new late-stage push for SPY-oh-seven-two in hidradenitis suppurativa. AstraZeneca opened an eighteen-story R and D center in Kendall Square that, with its genomic medicine site, will house nearly two thousand scientists, with labs built around robotics and what it calls agentic A-I, as part of a billion-dollar Massachusetts investment. ORIC handed its first-in-class C-I-P-two-A program to Cancer Research U-K, which will fully fund development through Phase one-two while ORIC keeps an option to buy it back. ORIC-two-fifty-nine is an oral splicing modulator that inserts a premature stop codon so the C-I-P-two-A message gets degraded, and it's aimed at homologous-recombination-deficient tumors, including PARP-inhibitor-resistant ones. That structure, where a charity carries the clinical risk and the company keeps a buy-back option, is a useful capital-light template. The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to Peter Hegemann, Georg Nagel and Karl Deisseroth for optogenetics, the light-switch channels in algae that Deisseroth turned into a tool for switching neurons on and off. And in STAT this morning, cancer-screening researcher Gilbert Welch argues the F-D-A should not approve GRAIL's Galleri multi-cancer test, noting the randomized N-H-S trial of a hundred forty thousand people missed even its surrogate endpoint, and that the new Medicare coverage law means taxpayers would pay for it anyway.
