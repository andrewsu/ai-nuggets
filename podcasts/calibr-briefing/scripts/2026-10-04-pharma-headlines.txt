Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Sunday, October fourth, twenty twenty-six. It's a weekend tape, so most of what's new comes from the back half of the European dermatology congress in Vienna, which ended Saturday, plus the Friday and Saturday wire releases. The lead and today's spotlight is Nektar's Treg drug in alopecia areata, where the company says the data shows durability, the stock fell twenty-one percent, and the slide deck explains both. Around it: Biogen's year-long lupus skin data, two-year data for J and J's oral I-L-twenty-three receptor peptide, a new Chinese I-L-four receptor antibody, an AI model for proteins that have no structure, and Europe's first industrial plant for lead-two-twelve alpha therapies.

First, Nektar. Rezpegaldesleukin is a pegylated interleukin-two that preferentially expands regulatory T cells. In the Phase two-b REZOLVE-AA trial in severe alopecia areata, eight patients reached at least eighty percent scalp coverage after a year of treatment and then stopped. Five of the eight still held it at six months off drug, and the share of the cohort with near-complete regrowth went from seven to nineteen percent with no drug at all. Shares fell about twenty-one percent on Thursday anyway. Jefferies called the selloff unwarranted, and William Blair said the data adds to evidence of a material durability advantage. The spotlight goes through what the press release left out, and why six months off drug can't yet tell a reset disease from a slow relapse.

Second, Biogen's litifilimab in cutaneous lupus. This is an antibody against B-D-C-A-two, a receptor found only on plasmacytoid dendritic cells, the cells that make most of the type one interferon that drives lupus skin disease. Binding it shuts down their interferon output without killing them. At the congress, Biogen showed fifty-two-week results from the Phase two portion of the AMETHYST trial, in ninety-three patients who had failed or couldn't tolerate antimalarials. Clear or almost clear skin rose from nineteen percent at week twenty-four to twenty-seven percent at week fifty-two, and seventy percent improvement in disease activity rose from about twenty-two to twenty-nine percent. Patients who crossed over from placebo improved within four weeks, and a third of them were clear or almost clear by week fifty-two. Serious adverse events were three point four percent, with no new signals. Litifilimab got breakthrough designation in January, and the Phase three part of AMETHYST reads out in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. As we covered on Thursday, Amgen's daxdilimab goes after the same cell by depleting it rather than silencing it. The field now has two shots at the interferon factory, and the disease still has no approved targeted therapy. The last drug approved for it dates to the nineteen fifties.

Third, Johnson and Johnson's icotrokinra, sold as Icotyde. It's the once-daily oral peptide that blocks the I-L-twenty-three receptor, discovered by Protagonist and approved by the F-D-A in March. The new data is two-year results from ICONIC-TOTAL, a trial in patients whose psoriasis affects hard-to-treat sites. Clear or almost clear skin went from fifty-seven percent at week sixteen to seventy percent at week one hundred twelve. Complete clearance at two years was sixty percent on the scalp, eighty-nine percent on the genitals, and sixty-three percent on the hands and feet. Nail psoriasis kept improving, from a thirty-three percent mean improvement at week sixteen to seventy-one percent at two years. No new safety signals. Put this next to Takeda's head-to-head win for zasocitinib over Sotyktu, which we covered Wednesday, and oral psoriasis therapy now has two new mechanisms, both with durable data, competing for the patients who would rather not inject.

Fourth, another entrant in the I-L-four receptor alpha race out of China. Telikibart, also called G-R-eighteen-oh-two, from Chongqing-based Genrix, is a fully human antibody against the same receptor subunit as Dupixent, with three Fc mutations to eliminate antibody-dependent cell killing. A four-hundred-fifty-patient Phase three in Chinese adults with moderate-to-severe eczema, presented as a late-breaker, hit both coprimary endpoints. At week sixteen, seventy percent reached seventy-five percent skin improvement against twenty-two and a half percent on placebo, and thirty-three percent reached clear or almost clear against seven percent. More than ninety-four percent reached seventy-five percent improvement by week fifty-two. HCPLive noted that the abstract left out serious adverse event and discontinuation rates. The numbers look like Dupixent's. What matters here is less this molecule than the fact that the Dupixent mechanism is now a commodity in China, and that sets a price reference for every Western I-L-four or I-L-thirteen deal, including the Sanofi-Regeneron expansion we covered on Thursday.

Fifth, on the AI file. Seattle-based Talus Bio released Ptarmigan-one, which it describes as the first structure-free model to predict whether a small molecule binds a protein and where, across the whole human proteome. The point is the roughly forty percent of human proteins, many of them transcription factors, that have no stable three-D structure for AlphaFold-style docking to work with. Talus trains instead on its own proteomics assay, which reads proteins label-free in their native cellular context, and the model embeds proteins and compounds in a shared space rather than fitting molecules into pockets. The company says it runs about five thousand times faster than structure-based methods, enough to screen three billion compounds against the proteome in a day. In a proof of concept on a STAT-six inhibitor series the model hadn't seen in training, it beat structure-based methods at picking winners, and binders were confirmed in a third-party lab. Its lead internal program targets brachyury, the transcription factor that drives chordoma. A preprint describes the architecture, and there's a free public tier. The caveats are the usual ones: the benchmarks are internal, and a STAT-six pocket is hard to model but not disordered. Still, this is the first serious attempt to make "undruggable because unstructured" a data problem rather than a physics problem.

Sixth, the isotope supply chain. Orano Med opened ATLab Valenciennes in northern France on Saturday. It's Europe's first industrial plant for lead-two-twelve alpha therapies: a twenty-nine-million-euro site that can make up to ten thousand doses a year in the medium term, with five times that possible at full capacity. Lead-two-twelve has a ten point six hour half-life, so doses have to be made, shipped and given within twenty-four hours. The site was chosen because it can reach hospitals covering about seventy percent of Europe's population inside that window. A matching plant is being commissioned in Indianapolis. Orano has four candidates in the clinic, its own or co-developed with Sanofi, Roche and Molecular Partners. For anyone thinking about radiopharma, it's a reminder that in alpha emitters, manufacturing is where the competitive advantage sits.

Seventh, gene therapy in Europe. The European Medicines Agency validated Ultragenyx's marketing application for rebisufligene etisparvovec, its A-A-V gene therapy for Sanfilippo syndrome type A, the fatal childhood neurodegenerative disease caused by loss of the enzyme sulfamidase. It has PRIME and orphan designation. The detail worth noting is that, as an advanced therapy, it falls under the E-U's new Joint Clinical Assessment framework, so it will be one of the early tests of whether pan-European health technology assessment speeds up gene therapy access or adds another gate.

Eighth, a commercial launch. Replimune's Tudriqev, vusolimogene oderparepvec, an engineered herpes simplex oncolytic virus that carries an immune-activating fusogenic payload, is now available in the U-S for melanoma that has progressed on a PD-one antibody, given with nivolumab. It got accelerated approval in early August on the IGNYTE cohort: a twenty-four percent response rate and a fourteen-month median duration of response in ninety-one evaluable patients. The confirmatory Phase three, IGNYTE-three, is ongoing. After the rejection and resubmission, the question now is whether community oncologists will do image-guided injections into deep lesions, and that's a logistics question more than a data one.

Ninth, Cullinan and Taiho started a rolling real-time oncology review submission for zipalertinib plus chemotherapy in first-line E-G-F-R exon twenty insertion lung cancer, based on the REZILIENT-three Phase three we covered from the world lung conference in September. They expect the filing to be complete by year end. The second-line monotherapy application already has an action date of February twenty-seventh. Cullinan gets thirty million dollars on a second-line approval and a hundred million on a first-line one, and that money funds its pivot to T-cell engagers in autoimmunity.

Tenth, public health with a product angle. Congo's Ebola outbreak has passed four thousand deaths among eighty-three hundred cases since it was declared in mid-May, and the Associated Press calls it the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak in history. A treatment center was burned down and unpaid surveillance workers are protesting. The AP's reporting identifies it as the Bundibugyo species, and the licensed Ebola vaccine, Merck's Ervebo, targets the Zaire species. So there's no approved vaccine for this outbreak, which makes it a real-world test of the platform vaccines and antibodies built for a moment like this.

Three quick ones. CRISPR Therapeutics will present clinical data for zugo-cel, its allogeneic CD-nineteen CAR-T, in autoimmune disease at the American College of Rheumatology meeting, a direct test of whether off-the-shelf cells can match the autologous reset results. BioMarin showed five-year Phase three data for its Pompe disease combination, Pombiliti plus Opfolda, at the World Muscle Society. And Verastem has new molecular profiling and external-control analyses for avutometinib plus defactinib in low-grade serous ovarian cancer coming at the gynecologic cancer society meeting.

Looking ahead. The International Parkinson and Movement Disorder congress opens today in Seoul, and on Wednesday Arvinas presents a late-breaker with biomarker and eye-movement data from the Phase one of A-R-V-one-oh-two, its brain-penetrant PROTAC degrader of LRRK2. That's the first real look at whether degrading LRRK2 does anything an inhibitor can't. Merck's diabetic macular edema data and Beacon's X-linked retinitis pigmentosa dataset are due October tenth. Bristol Myers has its lupus readout around the nineteenth, ESMO opens in Madrid on the twenty-third, and Summit's ivonescimab action date is November fourteenth. That's your Sunday briefing.
