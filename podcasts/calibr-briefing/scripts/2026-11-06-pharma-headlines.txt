First, and this is today's spotlight. Novartis has confirmed that since Zolgensma's 2019 approval it has received nine reports of tumors in children previously treated with the spinal muscular atrophy gene therapy. In two of those cases, the company says there is evidence of vector D-N-A integrating into the patient's own D-N-A. More than six thousand patients have been dosed. What makes this story unusual is how it came out: Novartis notified neurologists earlier in the week, families heard it in clinic appointments, and the news reached the public through a parent's LinkedIn post and a statement from SMA Europe, which said bluntly that the community should not have to piece a safety signal together from social media. Novartis ADRs fell Thursday. Second, AstraZeneca has reportedly made a ten billion dollar offer for Rhythm Pharmaceuticals. The Wall Street Journal put the bid at a hundred twenty-four dollars a share, citing people familiar. Rhythm closed Wednesday up twenty-four percent. The asset is Imcivree, setmelanotide, an M-C-four-R agonist that won its third F-D-A indication in March, this one for acquired hypothalamic obesity. William Blair had flagged Rhythm as a takeout target earlier this year and models the drug at three point six billion dollars by 2032, writing that what it likes about the hypothalamic obesity opportunity is that there's no competition in front of it or behind it: G-L-P-ones don't work there. The firm also thinks any large-cap with a metabolic franchise could be interested, which it says is "basically every biopharma company out there." Worth remembering this is the same Rhythm that we covered two days ago rewriting its bivamelagon deal with L-G Chem, and whose Phase 3 basket trial in genetic obesities failed earlier this year. Soriot said on last week's earnings call he's looking at business development in oncology, cardiovascular, rare disease, immunology and respiratory.

Third, Revolution Medicines. Rasonque, daraxonrasib, the R-A-S-ON inhibitor approved in August for second-line metastatic pancreatic cancer, did a hundred ninety-eight million dollars in the third quarter on five weeks of selling. Truist had modeled nineteen million. More than five thousand patients were treated through September thirtieth, from twenty-seven hundred prescribers, forty percent of them academic. T-D Cowen's Tyler Van Buren called it the best oncology drug launch in history. R-B-C's Leonid Timashev now models four hundred twenty-six million in the fourth quarter alone and more than three billion next year. The list price is thirty-nine thousand eight hundred dollars for a thirty-day supply, above what analysts expected, and plans covering more than ninety percent of patients are reimbursing. The company's own commercial chief pushed back on the extrapolation: a chunk of that revenue was converting the early access program, and those patients were sicker and may come off treatment sooner. Net loss was four hundred seven million, cash three point seven billion. This is the clearest validation yet that a hard target plus a desperate population equals a vertical launch curve.

Fourth, Evommune in-licensed an oral molecular glue degrader from China's Hinova Pharmaceuticals. Thirty million dollars upfront, structured as twenty million cash and ten million in pre-funded warrants, with up to one point one billion in milestones and royalties outside China. The candidate, now E-V-O-five-seventy, degrades V-A-V-one, a signaling protein in immune cells that sits upstream of both T-cell and B-cell activation. The pitch is a cleaner oral than J-A-K inhibitors, which are the only approved orals in rheumatoid arthritis and carry boxed warnings. Oppenheimer called it a low-cost, high-probability-of-success deal and said preclinical data put it at twenty times the potency of Novartis' M-R-T-six-one-six-zero, the V-A-V-one glue Novartis licensed from Monte Rosa in 2024 for a hundred fifty million upfront. Context matters here: Evommune needed this. Its lead oral failed in chronic hives and atopic dermatitis earlier this year. Phase 2 in rheumatoid arthritis is slated for 2027.

Fifth, C-M-S finalized the 2027 outpatient payment rule. Medicare will reimburse three-forty-B participants at average sales price minus twenty-eight percent, which the agency says cuts drug spending by about five and a half billion dollars next year, and it broadens site-neutral payment so hospital outpatient departments get paid the same as independent sites for more procedures. Outpatient departments get a two point three percent rate increase, slightly below the proposed two point four. The American Hospital Association called the continued assault on three-forty-B deeply disturbing. For manufacturers this is directionally favorable — it shrinks the spread hospitals capture on discounted drugs — but it also lands in a week when Democrats just won both chambers, so expect litigation and legislative pushback.

Sixth, the ASH abstracts dropped and the theme is trispecifics. Johnson and Johnson's ramantamig binds C-D-three on T cells and both B-C-M-A and G-P-R-C-five-D on myeloma cells — the two targets J-and-J already sells separate bispecifics against, Tecvayli and Talvey. In a mid-stage trial as induction with Darzalex and Revlimid in newly diagnosed transplant-eligible patients, all twenty evaluable patients were minimal residual disease negative after three cycles. AbbVie has a Phase 1 trispecific, and Innovent and Shanghai Xianxiang are presenting early data too. The thesis is that hitting two tumor antigens at once blunts antigen-escape, which is the dominant resistance mechanism for the approved bispecifics. Small numbers, early setting, but this is where the myeloma field is going.

Seventh, the F-D-A granted accelerated approval to subcutaneous amivantamab with hyaluronidase, Rybrevant Faspro, for recurrent or metastatic head and neck squamous cell carcinoma after platinum chemotherapy and a P-D-one or P-D-L-one antibody. The basis is cohort one of the OrigAMI-four trial: a forty-two percent response rate in a hundred two patients, fifteen percent complete responses, and more than half of responders past six months. Amivantamab is the E-G-F-R by M-E-T bispecific that made its name in lung cancer, and this is J-and-J extending the same biology into a tumor where E-G-F-R has always been the obvious target and cetuximab has always been the disappointing answer. Confirmation rides on OrigAMI-five in the first line with carboplatin and pembrolizumab. Safety is the usual amivantamab profile: seventy-nine percent rash, and six treatment-related deaths.

Eighth, on the A-I-in-discovery beat, a Cambridge team and the spinout DIOSynVax reported in Cell Reports Medicine that a computationally designed multi-antigen m-R-N-A bird flu vaccine completely protected mice and ferrets against antigenically distinct H-five-N-one challenges spanning two decades of viral evolution. The World Health Organization's stockpiled comparator protected about half the animals. The design approach pools global surveillance sequence data and builds consensus antigens across hemagglutinin, neuraminidase and M-two rather than matching one isolate. It's preclinical and it needs a human trial, but it's the third pathogen this group has run the same playbook on, and it's a concrete example of A-I used to design the biologic itself rather than to triage small molecules.
