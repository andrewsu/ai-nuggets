The drug is a deuterated form of psilocin, the active molecule that psilocybin turns into in the body. Ollin's lead drug, O-L-N-three-twenty-four, is a bispecific antibody against V-E-G-F and angiopoietin-two, the same two targets as Roche's Vabysmo. Innovent discovered it. Third, Lilly kept buying. It agreed to acquire Calytrix Bio for up to one and a half billion dollars in cash. Calytrix's lead drug is an unnamed biparatopic antibody, meaning it binds two separate sites on its target, mutant calreticulin. That mutation drives a large share of essential thrombocythemia and myelofibrosis. Today's drugs mostly manage the symptoms of these blood cancers, and a mutant-specific antibody aims to change the course of the disease instead. Incyte is the main rival here, with its own mutant calreticulin antibody. Calytrix launched last year out of J-P Morgan's life sciences private capital arm. BioSpace counts this as Lilly's fourteenth acquisition of the year. That evening Lilly also signed a research deal with Stipple Bio of Cambridge, Massachusetts, worth up to five point nine billion dollars. Stipple's Pointillist platform looks for epitopes, the specific spots on a protein that an antibody binds, that appear only on tumor cells, even when the protein itself also sits on healthy tissue. Lilly will pair those binders with its own oncology technology across several drug types. Stipple gets an upfront payment and an equity investment, but the deal didn't disclose how big either is.

Fourth, an approval in breast cancer. The F-D-A approved Roche's Wilserdly, giredestrant, in combination with the m-TOR inhibitor everolimus, for E-S-R-one-mutated, E-R-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer after endocrine therapy. Giredestrant is an oral selective estrogen receptor degrader, or S-E-R-D. In the Phase three evERA trial's E-S-R-one-mutated group, median progression-free survival was ten months against five and a half on standard endocrine therapy plus everolimus, a hazard ratio of zero point three eight. Overall survival data are immature. Approval came four weeks early, with Foundation Medicine's liquid biopsy as the companion test. It's the fifth oral E-R degrader approved in the U-S, and the first approved as a partner for everolimus. The bigger prize for Roche is still early breast cancer, where giredestrant's lidERA trial is already positive.

Fifth, bladder cancer. CG Oncology's cretostimogene, an oncolytic adenovirus instilled into the bladder, won its second Phase three. In PIVOT-zero-zero-six, three hundred sixty-seven patients had intermediate-risk or high-grade Ta tumors removed. Cretostimogene cut the risk of recurrence by sixty-eight percent against surveillance alone, with no grade three or higher treatment-related side effects. No adjuvant therapy is approved for these patients, and the company puts the U-S population at about fifty thousand. CG Oncology plans to file in the second quarter of 2027 and present full data at the Society of Urologic Oncology meeting in San Diego on December second.

Sixth, from the Alzheimer's meeting CTAD in Boston. AriBio's AR-one-thousand-one is an oral P-D-E-five inhibitor, the same drug class as erectile dysfunction pills.
