Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Sunday, October eighteenth, twenty twenty-six. The weekend tape was mostly dark, as it usually is, but not empty. India's drug pricing regulator published a gazette notification on Saturday capping trade margins at thirty percent on a hundred and thirty-seven cancer drugs, including pembrolizumab, nivolumab, osimertinib and bevacizumab. That's the spotlight. AbbVie dropped twenty abstracts of long-term inflammatory bowel disease data as United European Gastroenterology Week opened in Barcelona, Viatris picked up a presbyopia approval after Friday's close and immediately said it wants a partner to sell it, UCB got an autoinjector cleared for its complement inhibitor in myasthenia gravis, and a Shanghai in vivo CAR-T company got orphan drug designation for an autoimmune indication. Five items, then the week ahead.

First, the spotlight. India's National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority invoked paragraph nineteen of the Drugs Prices Control Order, its extraordinary-powers clause, to cap trade margins at thirty percent of maximum retail price on a hundred and thirty-seven non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs. Trade margin here means the spread between what the manufacturer sells to the stockist or distributor for and what the patient pays, excluding tax. The reference price is the March twenty twenty-six price to stockist. The regulator's worked example is blunt: a product sold into the channel at seventy rupees can carry a maximum retail price of a hundred, down from three hundred in the illustration. Officials say some prices fall by up to seventy percent. It applies to patented and off-patent, domestic and imported, any strength, any dosage form, and even when the drug is used for a non-cancer indication. Revised prices take effect November ninth, and manufacturers have seven days from publication to report. The spotlight goes into who actually eats this cut, why it may not be the manufacturers, and what it tells you about where emerging-market oncology pricing is heading.

Second, AbbVie. United European Gastroenterology Week opened in Barcelona on Saturday, and AbbVie came with twenty abstracts and a clear message about durability. In the COMMAND maintenance study of Skyrizi in ulcerative colitis, that's risankizumab, the antibody against the p-nineteen subunit of interleukin twenty-three, week ninety-six clinical remission ran from fifty-four to seventy-three percent on the one-eighty milligram dose depending on whether patients had failed advanced therapy before, with endoscopic remission from thirty-nine to fifty-four percent. In U-ENDURE, the Crohn's maintenance study of Rinvoq, upadacitinib, the JAK-one inhibitor, they went out to week one hundred forty-four: clinical remission seventy-nine percent on fifteen milligrams and eighty-seven percent on thirty, endoscopic remission forty-seven and sixty-four percent. One important caveat you should hold onto. These are as-observed analyses. Patients who dropped out are simply not counted. That is the convention for long-term extensions, but it is not the non-responder-imputation math that regulators use for registrational endpoints, so these numbers will read better than an intent-to-treat version of the same data. The more forward-looking item in the drop is the first clinical look at A-B-B-V-four-six-six, a dual-targeting molecule combining risankizumab with trosunilimab, an alpha-four-beta-seven integrin blocker, in moderate to severe Crohn's. No numbers in the release, just an abstract title promising endoscopic remission. That's AbbVie building the successor to a franchise whose patents start falling in twenty thirty-two. Remember, this is the same company that showed subcutaneous Skyrizi induction data in Crohn's earlier this month. The IL-twenty-three plus gut-selective integrin combination is the hypothesis a lot of this field is converging on.

Third, Viatris. After Friday's close, the F-D-A approved Ryzumvi, phentolamine ophthalmic solution at zero point seven five percent, for presbyopia in adults. Phentolamine is a non-selective alpha-adrenergic blocker, and in the eye it works by constricting the pupil, creating a pinhole effect that extends depth of focus. The drug was already approved to reverse pharmacologically dilated pupils; this is the indication that has commercial weight. The interesting part is what Viatris said next, which is that it is exploring a partnership to commercialize the presbyopia indication in the United States. A company that just won an approval in a large cash-pay market announcing on day one that it is looking for someone else to sell it tells you something about Viatris' appetite for building an eye-care sales force, and about how hard the first entrant in this category found the commercial reality. Opus Genetics is the development partner on the asset.

Fourth, UCB. The F-D-A approved an autoinjector presentation of Zilbrysq, zilucoplan, for acetylcholine receptor antibody-positive generalized myasthenia gravis. Zilucoplan is a macrocyclic peptide that blocks complement C-five, and it is the only once-daily subcutaneous C-five inhibitor in the class, competing against infused and longer-interval antibodies. The approval rests on a fourteen-subject bioequivalence crossover against the prefilled syringe, with complete dose delivery in essentially all of four hundred forty-nine administrations. Commercial availability is guided to the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven. This is a device approval, not a new indication, and it is worth noting only because in chronic self-administered neurology, delivery is a real share driver.

Fifth, in vivo CAR-T. Vivacta Bio, a Shanghai company spun out of Beijing Grit Biotherapeutics last year, said the F-D-A granted orphan drug designation to G-T-eight-oh-one in autoimmune hemolytic anemia. G-T-eight-oh-one delivers messenger RNA encoding an anti-C-D-nineteen chimeric antigen receptor inside a lipid nanoparticle that is targeted to T cells using a single-domain antibody, so the CAR-T cells are generated inside the patient. No apheresis, no ex vivo expansion, no lymphodepletion, and the company says it supports repeat dosing. The clinical evidence so far is two patients from an ASGCT poster, both complete responses, with sustained B-cell depletion and falling hemolysis markers. Two patients and a company press release is not a dataset. But designation in warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia is a signal about where the in vivo CAR-T field is pointing: not oncology first, but B-cell-driven autoimmunity, where the tolerability bar is high and the ex vivo logistics are the thing standing in the way.
