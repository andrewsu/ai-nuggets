Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Wednesday, October fourteenth, twenty twenty-six, covering Tuesday. It was a deal day with an obesity theme. Zealand Pharma went to Generate Biomedicines for A-I-designed proteins, Roche bought a fat-tissue R-N-A interference drug from China, and two oral G-L-P-one programs stumbled. Also on the list are BioNTech's third messenger R-N-A setback, a Paragon company created by reverse merger, and Beam's first clinical correction of a disease mutation in the liver. The Generate–Zealand deal is today's spotlight. Nine items, then the week ahead.

First, BioNTech. Its Phase two-three AHEAD-MERIT trial tested BNT-one-thirteen with Merck's Keytruda against Keytruda alone in first-line metastatic head and neck cancer that is positive for H-P-V sixteen. BNT-one-thirteen is an messenger R-N-A immunotherapy that encodes antigens from two H-P-V-sixteen viral proteins. At an interim analysis, the combination did not significantly improve progression-free survival, which is one of two dual primary endpoints. BioNTech disclosed no hazard ratio. The monitoring committee let the trial continue to overall survival, and under the pre-specified design a win on survival alone would still make the trial positive. BMO wrote that a survival win is "still possible" but "less likely given today's PFS miss." Back in August, Leerink's Daina Graybosch had called the evidence for the program "sparse" when the Phase three started. BMO counts this as BioNTech's third messenger R-N-A setback, after two autogene cevumeran trials were stopped, one in colorectal cancer for futility and one in bladder cancer. The shares fell about five percent premarket. BioNTech is increasingly a pumitamig story, which is its bispecific against P-D-L-one and V-E-G-F.

Second, today's spotlight. Zealand Pharma signed a deal with Generate Biomedicines to design multispecific proteins for obesity and metabolic disease. Generate gets thirty million dollars up front for two programs and nearly one point two billion in total milestones, and Zealand has an option on a third program. Generate's shares rose about five percent after hours. It's a peptide company buying a protein-engineering capability after two disappointing readouts this month. The spotlight covers what it tells you about the next wave in obesity and about pricing for A-I design.

Third, the same day, Roche licensed worldwide rights to an R-N-A interference drug against A-L-K-seven from China's Rona Therapeutics. A-L-K-seven is an activin receptor on fat cells. People whose A-L-K-seven works less well tend to carry less visceral fat, so the idea is to cut visceral fat while sparing muscle. Roche pays up to a hundred million dollars in upfront and near-term milestones, with up to one point zero six billion more in milestones. BioPharma Dive counts it as Roche's fifth deal with a China-based drugmaker since the end of August. The target is getting crowded. Arrowhead showed a fourteen percent placebo-adjusted cut in visceral fat with its A-L-K-seven drug in January, Alnylam has a program, and G-S-K licensed one from SiranBio in May. Roche also recently handed its anti-myostatin antibody emugrobart back to Chugai after an interim look found it unlikely to produce meaningful weight loss, so the company is betting on fat-side biology over muscle-side biology.

Fourth, two oral G-L-P-one programs went the wrong way. Verdiva Bio, one of the best-funded private obesity companies, stopped a Phase two switching trial of ecnoglutide, its once-weekly oral G-L-P-one, after enrolling just eight patients. Verdiva cited "market evolution," and its EVOLVE-two Phase two-b data still haven't been released. Separately, after the close, Biomea Fusion reported Phase one data for B-M-F-six-fifty, an oral small-molecule G-L-P-one agonist. Outpatient cohorts lost one and a half to three point two percent of body weight at day twenty-nine, against a tenth of a percent on placebo. The highest-dose cohort, held as inpatients, lost six point one percent, but the two placebo patients in that cohort lost about the same amount while eating a third of their food. Tolerability was clean, with no serious events. Biomea wants a partner for the drug and is keeping its own focus on its menin inhibitor. With Lilly's orforglipron setting the bar, a new oral small molecule needs a clear advantage to stand out.

Fifth, Paragon's company-building machine scaled up. Velaris Therapeutics, the ninth company Paragon has spun out, merged into the Nasdaq-listed Alaunos the same day the deal was signed. Fairmount led a private placement of about three hundred fifty million dollars, with Venrock Healthcare Capital Partners, Wellington, Janus Henderson, Perceptive and T. Rowe Price also in. The pipeline targets pulmonary arterial hypertension with activin receptor type two ligand traps, the class of Merck's Winrevair. Two of them are engineered to spare B-M-P-nine and B-M-P-ten, which should cut the bleeding and blood-vessel side effects seen with Winrevair. There's also an antibody against P-D-G-F-B. The runway reaches into twenty twenty-nine. On the same day, the leadership team from Apogee, which AbbVie bought this year for more than ten point nine billion dollars, joined Paragon to scale up its research and company creation. That includes C-E-O Michael Henderson and R-and-D head Carl Dambkowski. BioPharma Dive notes there have been more than two dozen biotech reverse mergers this year, against ten in all of last year.

Sixth, Beam reported the first clinical proof of concept for BEAM-three-oh-one. It's a lipid-nanoparticle base editor delivered to the liver that corrects the R-eighty-three-C mutation in G-six-P-C-one, the cause of glycogen storage disease type one-a. Patients with this disease can't release glucose from the liver, so they live on cornstarch doses every few hours. Six patients were dosed. Both evaluable patients at the higher dose went from hypoglycemia after about two and a half hours of fasting to the protocol maximum of fifteen hours or more. Prescribed daily starch fell by seventy-one to ninety-two percent in most patients. There were five serious adverse events, all of which resolved, including three grade three infusion reactions that Beam ties to its steroid premedication, which it has since changed. Beam gave no numbers on editing efficiency. Full data come at the European gene therapy congress on October twenty-eighth.

Seventh, two data points in fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva, the ultra-rare disease in which muscle and connective tissue turn to bone. Regeneron's Pasatru, garetosmab, is an antibody that blocks activin A, the ligand that the mutant A-C-V-R-one receptor misreads as a bone-forming signal. In the eighty-four-week extension of the OPTIMA trial, there were no new bone lesions on drug between weeks fifty-six and eighty-four, against nine on placebo. The cost was skin and soft-tissue infections in seventy-eight percent of patients on the high dose, against thirty-three percent on placebo. Regeneron closed up two percent. Separately, the small company āshibio reported an eighty-six percent drop in flare-up rate for andecaliximab, an antibody against the protease M-M-P-nine. That came from twelve patients compared with their own pre-treatment history, with no placebo arm.

Eighth, on the A-I file, Pfizer signed a deal worth more than five hundred million dollars with Paris-based Enodia Therapeutics. The deal covers small molecules that block Sec-sixty-one, the channel that newly made secreted and membrane proteins pass through, so that disease proteins are destroyed as they're made. Enodia designs selectivity with a machine-learning engine. Pfizer Ventures co-led Enodia's twenty-five-million-dollar seed round in January. Eli Lilly expanded its own Sec-sixty-one deal with Gate Bioscience last week, so two big pharmas are now behind this mechanism.

Ninth, quick ones. Soufflé Therapeutics raised a hundred thirty-five million dollar Series B led by the new firm Juvia Group, with T-C-G-X, Fidelity and B-B Biotech joining Amgen Ventures, ARCH and Polaris. Soufflé uses receptor-mediated delivery to carry oligonucleotides to muscle and heart. Its lead program is in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy, and next is phospholamban silencing in heart failure. Next, Johnson and Johnson opened earnings season. Pharma sales grew about eight percent, led by Tremfya, up sixty-eight percent, while medtech missed and the shares slipped about four percent. Next, the F-T-C and seven states asked a federal court to hold Martin Shkreli in contempt of his lifetime pharma ban, alleging he traded at least sixty pharma stocks. Finally, AbbVie showed that subcutaneous induction of Skyrizi in Crohn's disease reached thirty-six percent endoscopic remission at week twenty-four in induction responders, against nine percent on placebo. That filing is under review in the U-S and Europe.

For the week ahead: Iambic, the A-I-native cancer drug developer, and John Maraganore's City Therapeutics both filed their final listing paperwork Tuesday afternoon, so pricing is likely imminent. Iambic's range is fifteen to seventeen dollars. Full Tezspire esophagitis data are due today, and E-S-M-O opens in Madrid on the twenty-third. That's your Wednesday briefing.
