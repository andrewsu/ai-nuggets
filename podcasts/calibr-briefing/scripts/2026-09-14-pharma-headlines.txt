Second, the durability result. AstraZeneca presented the eight-year landmark analysis from ADAURA, the adjuvant trial of Tagrisso — osimertinib, the third-generation E-G-F-R inhibitor — in six hundred eighty-two patients with completely resected stage one-B through three-A E-G-F-R-mutant disease, three years of treatment or until recurrence. At eight years, seventy-four percent of patients on osimertinib were alive against fifty-eight percent on placebo in the stage two to three-A primary population, a forty-seven percent reduction in the risk of death. Across the full stage one-B to three-A population it was seventy-nine percent against sixty-four, a forty-eight percent reduction. Roy Herbst, the principal investigator, framed the useful version of that number: a sixteen percentage point absolute improvement in survival at eight years from three years of a pill given after surgery. Simultaneous publication in the Journal of Thoracic Oncology, final safety consistent with the known profile. For a drug that has been the adjuvant standard since twenty twenty, this is the analysis that closes the argument about whether early E-G-F-R intervention is curative or merely deferring recurrence. The curves are still separated at eight years.

The competitive question is not efficacy. It is whether a twice-daily pill added to chemotherapy displaces a bispecific antibody that requires split first-dose infusions and prophylactic anticoagulation.

Fifth, Nuvalent. The TKI-naive cohort of ARROS-one read out: zidesamtinib, the brain-penetrant R-O-S-one-selective inhibitor the F-D-A approved in July for previously treated patients, produced a ninety-four percent response rate in ninety-four evaluable treatment-naive patients — eighty-eight responders, fifteen percent complete responses. Those are first-line numbers that would justify displacing crizotinib and entrectinib outright, and a first-line filing follows. Nuvalent is now a company with an approved drug, a frontline dataset, and an A-L-K program behind it.

Sixth, the negative-space result. Roche presented the final analysis of IMpower-oh-three-zero, perioperative atezolizumab plus platinum chemotherapy in resectable stage two through three-B disease. Median event-free survival was sixty-two point eight months against thirty-four point nine, pathologic complete response twenty-nine point six percent against eight point five, major pathologic response fifty-three point six against twenty-four point four. And it did not cross its prespecified threshold for statistical significance. Surgical cancellation rates were low and comparable, no new safety signals. A twenty-eight-month numerical event-free survival difference that the trial cannot claim is a statistics-and-timing problem, not a biology problem, but in a perioperative landscape that already has three approved regimens it means this one arrives without a clean label claim.

DeLLphi-three-oh-five — tarlatamab, Amgen's D-L-L-three T-cell engager, plus durvalumab as first-line maintenance in five hundred sixty-three extensive-stage small cell patients — was presented in Seoul and the companies still have not released a median, a hazard ratio, or a p-value. Eighth, the two approvals I owe you. On Friday the eleventh the F-D-A approved Scholar Rock's Isembyld — apitegromab, a monoclonal antibody that binds the latent, inactive form of myostatin and prevents its activation — for spinal muscular atrophy in patients two and older who are already on an S-M-N-two-targeted therapy. That is the first muscle-targeted treatment in S-M-A and, as C-E-O David Hallal put it, the first drug to break through decades of failed industry-wide attempts at myostatin inhibition. The SAPPHIRE trial showed a two point two point gain on the Hammersmith functional motor scale at one year, with thirty-four point two percent of treated patients gaining three points or more against thirteen point five on placebo. Note the history here, because it is the real lesson: this drug got a complete response letter last year on nothing but inspection findings at the Catalent Indiana fill-finish site Novo Nordisk had just acquired — pest infestation, contamination, equipment failures. Scholar Rock resubmitted with two fill-finish facilities in parallel, which is what bought the approval nineteen days early. Also on Friday, Pharming got Joenja — leniolisib, a selective P-I-three-K-delta inhibitor — approved down to age four in activated P-I-three-K-delta syndrome, the first treatment for that age group, on a single-arm study showing reduced lymphadenopathy and restored naive B cells. Doses available in October.
