Good morning, Travis. It's Wednesday, October 28th, 2026. ESMO is over and earnings season is underway. Today's briefing covers a San Diego cell therapy company shutting down, Novartis putting a price on the Avidity setback, another KRAS G-twelve-D contender in pancreatic cancer, and a small AI acquisition by Anthropic. Here's what you need to know.

First, Artiva Biotherapeutics is winding down. The San Diego company is ending development of AlloNK, its off-the-shelf natural killer cell therapy given with the anti-C-D-twenty antibody rituximab to deplete B cells in rheumatoid arthritis and other autoimmune diseases. It is cutting about ninety-four percent of its staff and has hired Leerink to run a strategic review that could end in a sale, a merger or liquidation. Two patients in its Phase 2a basket trial died. The investigator and an independent safety review judged the first death, a fatal leukoencephalopathy, likely related to fludarabine, the lymphodepleting chemotherapy given before the cells. The second was a sudden death about two months into treatment that the investigator judged unrelated, cause not yet determined. The FDA asked Artiva to pause the study, and the planned Phase 3 is cancelled. Artiva also says longer follow-up didn't show the durability it needed. The stock fell about thirty percent, and Wedbush cut its price target from forty dollars to four.

Second, Novartis reported third-quarter results and took a one point nine billion dollar impairment on its twelve billion dollar Avidity acquisition. The charge reflects del-desiran, Avidity's antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate that targets transferrin receptor one to get into muscle and knocks down D-M-P-K messenger RNA in myotonic dystrophy type one. It missed its primary endpoint on hand function in the Phase 3 HARBOR trial last month. Chief executive Vas Narasimhan said Novartis remains "very, very happy" with the deal. He called the write-down a "mathematical shift" for the cost and time of another pivotal study, and said Novartis is talking with the FDA about accelerated approval for del-brax in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy. Separately, the autoimmune and neurology trials of its CAR-T therapy rapcabtagene autoleucel, paused after three deaths, are still on hold in the U.S. and Europe, and Novartis still has no root cause to report. Sales were fourteen and a half billion dollars, up four percent at constant currency, as Entresto generics took thirty-eight percent off that brand and Kisqali grew thirty-seven percent to one point eight billion.

Third, the KRAS G-twelve-D race in pancreatic cancer. Incyte presented Phase 1 data at ESMO for I-N-C-B-one-six-one-seven-three-four, also called zecdarasib, a selective KRAS G-twelve-D inhibitor. Combined with modified FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy in first-line metastatic pancreatic cancer, the response rate was eighty-nine percent, every patient had disease control, and ninety percent were progression-free at six months. With gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel it was sixty-nine percent. Revolution Medicines' G-twelve-D inhibitor zoldonrasib posted eighty-two and sixty-one percent on the same two regimens, so RBC says Incyte "appears at least competitive." BMO called it a "firm contender" and sizes the pancreatic opportunity at one point four billion dollars. These are early, single-arm numbers in small cohorts. That race is today's spotlight.

Fourth, in AI drug discovery, Endpoints reports that Anthropic has acquired Mithril Medicine, a stealth drug discovery startup backed by Atlas Venture. Endpoints describes it as a one-person company run by a pseudonymous poster on X. The terms are behind Endpoints' paywall, and I couldn't confirm them anywhere else. This follows Anthropic's roughly four hundred million dollar purchase of Coefficient Bio in April and the life sciences push in its S-one filing on Monday. Fifth, Ferring Pharmaceuticals will buy Gameto for up to about one point four billion dollars, with the upfront payment undisclosed. Gameto's product, Fertilo, uses stem cell-derived ovarian support cells to mature eggs outside the body. The goal is to cut the hormone injections in IVF and shorten a cycle to as little as three days. Fertilo is cleared in Australia and parts of Latin America and is in a U.S. Phase 3. Gameto had raised about a hundred twenty-seven million dollars since twenty twenty.

Sixth, Astellas signed an option deal with Australia's Audax Biosciences for DAX-oh-forty-four, an interleukin-twelve antibody-cytokine fusion. Interleukin-twelve is a potent anti-tumor cytokine that has been too toxic to give systemically, and Audax's design aims to concentrate its activity in tumors. Audax gets up to two hundred eighty-five million dollars across the upfront and option exercise payments, plus up to one point zero five billion in milestones, for a molecule that won't enter Phase 1 until twenty twenty-seven. That's a large price for a preclinical cytokine program.

Seventh, Rapport Therapeutics is raising money a week after its spotlight here. Rapport launched two hundred million dollars in stock and a hundred fifty million in convertible notes due twenty thirty-two, plus a concurrent private placement, to fund RAP two-nineteen. That's its precision anticonvulsant targeting the AMPA receptor through its TARP-gamma-eight auxiliary subunit, which just posted positive data in bipolar mania. Raising right after a readout is the usual move. The convertible piece shows Rapport wants to limit dilution while the stock is up.

Eighth, a New Jersey federal judge denied Novo Nordisk's request for a preliminary injunction against Lilly's ads comparing Zepbound with Wegovy. Novo argued the ads used outdated, lower-dose data and ignored high-dose Wegovy. Judge Zahid Quraishi found no showing of harm, noting that Wegovy prescriptions held steady while the ads ran. Novo says the ruling doesn't decide whether the ads are misleading, and the underlying suit goes on.

Ninth, two quick items on clinical development. Mirum reported positive data in children aged six to under twelve for Atebrioz, or zilurgisertib, its oral inhibitor of the A-L-K-two kinase in fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva. There were no new bone lesions on drug, and Mirum plans to file for this age group in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. And Idorsia started FAB-KLEAR, a sixteen-patient kidney biopsy Phase 3 of lucerastat, an oral substrate-reduction therapy for Fabry disease. Japan's Nxera exercised its option to co-develop the drug, paying seven million Swiss francs.

Tenth, the deal outlook. A new Leerink analysis counts one hundred eighty-eight billion dollars of big pharma M&A firepower. Johnson and Johnson ranks as the most likely buyer, with twenty-one billion in cash. Pharma has spent ninety-seven billion dollars across twenty-eight acquisitions so far this year, against a historical average of sixty-nine billion across nineteen deals. Lilly alone has done eleven deals worth thirty-one point seven billion.

Looking ahead, Biogen and GSK report third-quarter results today. Lilly, Merck, Bristol Myers and Gilead are expected tomorrow. On the regulatory calendar, Inovio has an FDA decision due Friday for I-N-O three-one-oh-seven in recurrent respiratory papillomatosis, and Agios has one Sunday for mitapivat in sickle cell disease.
