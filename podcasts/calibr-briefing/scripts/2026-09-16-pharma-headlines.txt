Good morning Travis. Here are your biotech and pharma headlines for Wednesday, September sixteenth, twenty twenty-six. Seoul is over and the news has rotated hard toward cardiometabolic medicine — two separate companies made two very different bets on the same pair of genes inside twenty-four hours, and that is where the spotlight goes. There is also a two-year kidney readout that is about as clean as Phase three data gets, a partnership Novo Nordisk walked away from, and a leadership change at the biggest name in cell therapy. Nine items, then the week ahead.

First, and today's spotlight. Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals reported the first human data for A-R-O dimer P-A, which the company is calling the first dual-functional R-N-A interference therapeutic — one molecule, two silencing payloads, aimed at P-C-S-K-nine and A-P-O-C-three simultaneously. In the interim single-dose analysis from the Phase one-two-a study, mean maximal reductions were seventy-two percent for circulating P-C-S-K-nine and eighty-eight percent for A-P-O-C-three. That translated into fifty-four percent lower L-D-L cholesterol, seventy-three percent lower triglycerides, sixty-one percent lower non-H-D-L cholesterol, and fifty percent lower apolipoprotein B. Single-dose escalation is complete through four hundred milligrams, the study is enrolling up to seventy-eight adults with mixed hyperlipidemia, and the most common adverse events were injection-site reactions and headaches, with no drug-related serious adverse events. Chief Executive Chris Anzalone called it an important step forward and a first for the field. The stock fell about nine percent on the day, its worst session since July of last year. That gap between the data and the tape is the episode.

Second, the cleanest number of the day. Vera Therapeutics reported the final efficacy analysis of ORIGIN three, the Phase three of Trutakna — atacicept, a fusion protein that traps both BAFF and APRIL upstream of the B cells that produce the pathogenic antibodies in I-g-A nephropathy. Four hundred twenty-eight patients. Through one hundred four weeks the annualized kidney function slope was a decline of zero point six milliliters per minute on drug against five point six on placebo, a treatment difference of five point zero. At fifty-two weeks the change was essentially flat — zero point one — against five point seven. Composite kidney disease progression events were eleven on Trutakna against thirty-eight on placebo, a hazard ratio of zero point two four, a seventy-six percent risk reduction, p less than zero point zero zero zero one. Dialysis lasting thirty days or more, transplant, or death: zero events on drug against eight on placebo. Proteinuria, galactose-deficient I-g-A-one and hematuria all hit as hierarchically tested endpoints. Infections ran thirty-two percent against twenty-eight, with no opportunistic infections and no clinically significant antibody deficiency — which is the specific worry with a drug that shuts down two B-cell survival factors at once. Vera got accelerated approval in July on thirty-six-week interim data, has booked more than three hundred fifty patient start forms in the ten weeks since, and files a supplemental biologics license application in the fourth quarter for full approval in twenty twenty-seven. Chief Executive Marshall Fordyce's framing is that upstream inhibition of BAFF and APRIL delivers comprehensive disease modification, and the argument he is really making is against the single-cytokine competition — Otsuka, Novartis, Vertex, Calliditas and Travere are all in this market now.

Third, a company betting its existence on a different modality. Cellectis is abandoning donor-derived cell therapy and reinventing itself as an in vivo gene editing company. The board approved it on the eleventh, it was announced on the fourteenth, and the shares fell as much as forty-three percent, to roughly two dollars, near all-time lows. The stated reason is that the commercial and clinical landscape for treating blood cancers with C-A-R-T has changed materially — bispecific antibodies and other approvals have shrunk the addressable population and slowed enrollment. The new pipeline is two preclinical cardiovascular programs delivered intravenously in lipid nanoparticles, both built on TALE-based editors rather than CRISPR nucleases, so neither one cuts both strands of D-N-A. HEAL-one-oh-one is a base editor targeting A-P-O-C-three for severe hypertriglyceridemia; HEAL-two-oh-one is an epigenetic editor that silences the P-C-S-K-nine promoter for severe hypercholesterolemia. First-in-human data are guided to the second half of twenty twenty-seven and the first half of twenty twenty-eight respectively, both via investigator-initiated Phase one trials in China. Cash runway extends into the second half of twenty twenty-eight, helped by keeping the partnered cell therapy commitments with AstraZeneca, Allogene, Servier and Iovance alive while the company shops its own clinical-stage assets. Note the two targets and then go back to the first item.

Fourth, Novo Nordisk is walking away from monthly G-L-P-one. Novo terminated its collaboration with Ascendis Pharma covering TransCon technology in metabolic and cardiovascular disease, less than two years after signing it in November twenty twenty-four. The lead program was TransCon semaglutide, a once-monthly prodrug of the same molecule in Wegovy — the TransCon linker releases the parent peptide slowly enough to stretch a weekly drug to a monthly one. All licenses revert to Ascendis, and neither party has continuing financial obligations to the other. Ascendis says it will now initiate multiple programs in both rare and large metabolic and cardiovascular indications on its own. Read this as portfolio triage rather than a verdict on the chemistry: Novo is deep into an oral Wegovy launch, a seven point two milligram dose, and next-generation amylin programs, and a monthly injectable that competes with its own weekly franchise is a hard thing to prioritize. Amgen's MariTide is still the one carrying the monthly-dosing thesis.

Fifth, an oral drug that appears to have beaten the incumbent oral drug. The F-D-A accepted Takeda's new drug application for zasocitinib in moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis under priority review. Zasocitinib is an allosteric TYK-two inhibitor — it binds the pseudokinase regulatory domain rather than the active site, which is how this class gets selectivity against the rest of the JAK family and avoids the class boxed warning. Across LATITUDE-P-s-O three-oh-oh-one and three-oh-oh-two, nearly three thousand patients, about seventy percent reached clear or almost clear skin at week sixteen and more than thirty-five percent reached complete clearance — more than two and a half times the complete-clearance rate reported for deucravacitinib, which is Bristol Myers Squibb's Sotyktu and the only approved drug in the class. No new safety signals. If that comparison survives regulatory scrutiny, Takeda has a best-in-class oral in a market that has been waiting for one.

Sixth, a miss. Contineum Therapeutics reported that MOONLIGHT-one, the Phase two of J-N-J five-one-two-zero, also known as PIPE-three-oh-seven, did not hit its primary endpoint — change in the Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale at day five — as monotherapy in a hundred seven adults with major depressive disorder. The drug is an oral, brain-penetrant, highly selective M-one muscarinic receptor antagonist, and it is worth remembering where it came from: M-one was identified as a brake on oligodendrocyte differentiation, so the original program was remyelination in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis, published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, and Johnson and Johnson bought global rights in twenty twenty-three. That M-S trial missed. The depression thesis was a different one — rapid onset on a five-day timescale, which is an aggressive bar to set for any antidepressant. No new safety signals. Johnson and Johnson is still working through exploratory endpoints before deciding what to do, with no timeline. Contineum fell about eight percent after hours. Two Phase two misses in two indications is the point at which a mechanism, not a trial design, comes into question.

Seventh, a change at the top of C-A-R-T. Legend Biotech appointed Ingrid Zhang chief executive effective the fifteenth. She was most recently chief commercial officer for Novartis International and previously ran Novartis's China and innovative medicines businesses, with earlier stops at AstraZeneca, Pfizer and McKinsey. Alan Bash, who had been interim chief executive since July, returns to his role as president of Carvykti, the B-C-M-A C-A-R-T that Legend partners with Johnson and Johnson. The signal in that resume is commercial, not scientific — Legend's problem is manufacturing and access, not biology.

Eighth, two smaller pipeline items worth a line each. BioMarin is discontinuing development of Voxzogo — vosoritide, the C-type natriuretic peptide analog that works downstream of the F-G-F-R-three signaling defect — in children with Noonan syndrome, citing study feasibility and the current treatment landscape rather than safety or efficacy. That is distinct from March, when the company stopped dosing in the Turner syndrome, SHOX-deficiency and aggrecan-deficiency studies after safety events in two investigator-led trials. And Ocular Therapeutix reported a positive pre-N-D-A meeting for AXPAXLI in wet age-related macular degeneration, with F-D-A alignment on a five-oh-five-b-two filing built on fifty-two-week efficacy from SOL-one plus interim safety from SOL-R, including repeat dosing. The drug is an intravitreal hydrogel implant that elutes axitinib, a tyrosine kinase inhibitor, so it attacks V-E-G-F signaling inside the cell rather than mopping up the ligand outside it. Submission stays on track for the fourth quarter.

Ninth, on the artificial intelligence side. Xaira Therapeutics took the twenty twenty-six Fierce A-I Innovation Award for preclinical development, for X-Atlas slash Pisces and X-Cell. Pisces is a genome-wide CRISPR-interference Perturb-seq dataset of twenty-five point six million perturbed single-cell transcriptomes across seven cellular contexts — more than three times the scale of the company's previous release — and X-Cell is the four point nine billion parameter model trained on it, which Xaira describes as the largest causal perturbation model built to date and which it says follows the same power-law scaling behavior seen in language models. An award is an award, but the substance underneath it is the thing to watch: this is the clearest public test of whether virtual-cell models generalize to perturbations they have never seen, which is the only version of the claim that matters for target discovery.
