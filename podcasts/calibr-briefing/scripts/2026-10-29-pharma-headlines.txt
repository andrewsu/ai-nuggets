Good morning, Travis. It's Thursday, October 29th, 2026. Today's briefing covers Genmab's third big win of the month, Lilly's first head-to-head Phase 3 for retatrutide, a new F-D-A path for one-patient gene therapies, Washington opening the door to compounded peptides, and two financings in AI drug discovery. Here's what you need to know.

First, Genmab. Its Phase 3 LiGeR-H-N-one trial of petosemtamab met both of its primary endpoints at the first interim analysis. Petosemtamab is a bispecific antibody that binds E-G-F-R and L-G-R-five. Added to Keytruda as first-line treatment for PD-L-one-positive recurrent or metastatic head and neck cancer, it cut the risk of death in half compared with Keytruda alone, with a hazard ratio of zero point five oh, and it tripled the odds of a response. The benefit held regardless of H-P-V status. Truist called the survival result "highly compelling and potentially practice-changing," and William Blair called it "exceedingly positive." Genmab rose twenty-nine percent. Bicara, whose competing drug is still a year from its own Phase 3 interim, fell forty-eight percent. That readout is today's spotlight.

Second, Eli Lilly ran retatrutide against Novo's Ozempic for the first time. Retatrutide is Lilly's once-weekly triple agonist of the G-I-P, G-L-P-one and glucagon receptors. In the TRANSCEND-T-two-D-two trial in nearly thirteen hundred adults with type two diabetes on metformin, both doses of retatrutide lowered A-one-C by two point two points at eighty weeks, against one point eight for Ozempic at two milligrams. Patients lost about twenty percent of their body weight on retatrutide, against nine percent on Ozempic. The cost was tolerability. Diarrhea ran twenty-eight to thirty-three percent against twenty-six on Ozempic, and dysesthesia, a burning or prickling skin sensation that has followed this drug through its program, ran about five to six and a half percent against under three. This is retatrutide's sixth Phase 3 win, and Lilly still plans to file in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven. Lilly reports third-quarter earnings this morning.

Third, the F-D-A issued draft guidance on what it calls the Individualized Feature Space. A sponsor defines in advance how a gene therapy or gene editing product can vary from patient to patient. Usually that means a different guide or sequence for a different mutation. One biologics license can then cover all of those variants. A new variant that stays inside those boundaries and passes its assays can be added to the license without another clinical trial. That is the regulatory version of the personalized CRISPR therapy given to the infant with C-P-S-one deficiency last year. On the same day, C-M-S said it will widen its Cell and Gene Therapy Access Model beyond sickle cell disease, so outcomes-based Medicaid deals can start sooner after approval. RBC, back from the rare disease summit in Washington, said it came away "incrementally more positive" on the regulatory setup.

Fourth, the peptide policy we flagged last week is now official. H-H-S says it will use enforcement discretion to let state-licensed pharmacies, federal facilities and physicians compound six unapproved peptides while the F-D-A works on a rule. These are the six that an F-D-A advisory committee backed in July, over the objections of the agency's own scientists. As STAT notes, there is little evidence these products are safe or effective.

Fifth, Biogen posted a third straight quarter of growth, with two point eight billion dollars in revenue. Spinraza brought in four hundred sixty-one million as patients switched to the high-dose regimen. Leqembi, the anti-amyloid antibody it shares with Eisai, grew sixty-seven percent, and the Apellis products added two hundred forty-three million in their first full quarter. Chief executive Chris Viehbacher says the company can now grow without its pipeline. Analysts spent the call asking about litifilimab anyway. That's Biogen's antibody against B-DCA-two, a receptor on plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and two Phase 3 lupus readouts are due by year end. Truist expects clinical catalysts, not earnings, to drive the stock for the next year.

Sixth, GSK's vaccine business missed. Sales of Arexvy, its R-S-V vaccine, fell more than twenty percent to a hundred ninety-five million pounds, and Shingrix fell five percent. Chief executive Luke Miels says the adult vaccine market is down about eighteen percent from last year. Guggenheim says the quarter shows how hard GSK's turn toward oncology will be with dolutegravir's patent expiry approaching. GSK says Hibsago, the bepirovirsen hepatitis B drug we covered at approval last week, will be on the market within ten days.

Seventh, two financings in AI drug discovery. Endpoints reports that Formation Bio has raised a six hundred million dollar investment fund. Formation uses A-I to in-license outside assets, develop them and hand them off, and it has partnerships with OpenAI and Sanofi. In China, Beijing-based BioGeometry says it raised more than a hundred million dollars in a Series A for generative protein design. Its founder is Jian Tang, and Yoshua Bengio is its chief scientific advisor. And a new Endpoints survey finds seventy-eight percent of scientists now expect A-I to speed drug discovery this year, up from a skeptical baseline a year ago.

Eighth, three overnight items. Ascletis reported a U.S. Phase 2 of A-S-C-thirty, a once-daily oral small-molecule G-L-P-one agonist, in type two diabetes. Placebo-adjusted A-one-C fell about one point seven points at twelve weeks, weight fell about four percent at thirteen weeks, and no patients dropped out for G-I side effects, in a hundred patients. Coherus reported interim data for tagmokitug, its cytolytic antibody that depletes C-C-R-eight-positive regulatory T cells in tumors, given with the PD-one antibody toripalimab plus chemotherapy in first-line esophageal squamous cell cancer. The confirmed response rate was seventy-five percent, but only eight patients could be evaluated. And Takeda reported first-half results with its narcolepsy drug oveporexton approved in three markets, though the U.S. launch is waiting on D-E-A scheduling.

Ninth, BioSpace counts more than four thousand biopharma manufacturing jobs being cut this year or planned, across ten companies, with BioNTech the largest. That's happening while the same companies announce billions of dollars to build plants in the U.S.

Looking ahead, Lilly, Merck, Bristol Myers and Gilead report today. Kymera presents the first human data for K-T-five-seventy-nine, its oral degrader of the transcription factor I-R-F-five, which it is aiming at lupus and other autoimmune diseases. Iovance is presenting registrational data for its tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte therapy in lung cancer. And Inovio's F-D-A decision for papillomatosis is due tomorrow.
