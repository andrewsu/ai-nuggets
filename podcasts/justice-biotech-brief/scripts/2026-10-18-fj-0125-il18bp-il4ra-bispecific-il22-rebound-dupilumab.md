# FJ-0125 — an IL-18-binding-protein × IL-4Rα bispecific, and the argument that dupilumab's own IL-22 rebound is what the second arm is for

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.11.758381v1

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick today, a preprint posted yesterday, and the interesting part is not the molecule — it's the liability the molecule was built to clean up.

The asset is F-J-oh-one-twenty-five, out of Ferry Jam Biotechnology in Shanghai with the Huanghe Science and Technology University faculty of medicine in Zhengzhou. It is described as a bispecific antibody for atopic dermatitis, but the architecture is worth being precise about, because it isn't two Fabs. One arm is a conventional humanized anti–I-L-four receptor alpha antibody — dupilumab's target, raised fresh from mouse hybridomas, five picomolar on the receptor. The other arm is not an antibody at all. It's human I-L-eighteen binding protein, the body's own secreted decoy for I-L-eighteen, fused onto the heavy chain using knob-into-hole heterodimerization on an effector-silenced I-g-G-four backbone. So the second specificity is a natural decoy receptor given antibody pharmacokinetics. That matters commercially, because the free version of that decoy — tadekinig alfa — has been through the clinic in adult-onset Still's disease and in inflammasome-driven hemophagocytic syndromes on a three-times-a-week subcutaneous schedule. Bolting it to an I-g-G is the obvious fix for a protein whose problem was always that it washes out.

Now the finding that earns the episode. The group built an integrated assay — keratinocytes stimulated with Staph aureus, the conditioned medium used to prime monocyte-derived dendritic cells, those dendritic cells then co-cultured with allogeneic naive CD4 T cells. It's an epithelium-to-immune relay in a dish rather than a single recombinant cytokine, and the T cells that come out of it make all three flavors of inflammation. In that system dupilumab did exactly what you'd expect on the type-two cytokines — knocked TARC down to fifteen percent of control, I-L-thirteen to thirty-seven percent. It did nothing to interferon gamma. And it raised I-L-twenty-two roughly two-point-seven fold. Not failed to lower it. Raised it. The same direction, short of statistical significance, showed up in the mouse ear tissue.

The mechanistic story they offer for that is the part I'd want you to carry out of this. I-L-four, signaling through STAT-six, is a negative regulator of I-L-eighteen receptor alpha on activated CD4 T cells. Block I-L-four receptor alpha and you lift that repression — the T cell up-regulates its own I-L-eighteen receptor and becomes more responsive to an alarmin that the colonized, barrier-disrupted skin is already pouring out. So the type-three rebound isn't an off-target quirk of dupilumab. It's a predictable consequence of removing a brake that I-L-four was holding on a parallel axis. The second arm of this molecule isn't there for additive potency. It's there to close a door that the first arm opens. And that reframes what "complementary pathway" ought to mean when you're picking the second specificity for anything — the best partner may not be the one that adds the most independent effect, but the one that neutralizes the compensatory circuit your primary blockade de-represses. Co-formulating the fix into the same molecule, with one half-life, is a defensible reason to build a bispecific rather than run a combination.

Two pieces of craft worth noting. First, the causality control is clean: they stimulated with a decoy-resistant I-L-eighteen variant — the engineered cytokine that signals normally but won't bind the binding protein — and F-J-oh-one-twenty-five lost its ability to suppress interferon gamma, while it still worked against wild-type I-L-eighteen. That nails the activity to the decoy module rather than to anything incidental about the scaffold. Second, and this is the irony I'd flag for your side of the building — that decoy-resistant variant exists because in oncology I-L-eighteen binding protein is the enemy. It's been characterized as a secreted immune checkpoint, up-regulated by tumors, and the reason recombinant I-L-eighteen failed as a cancer drug; the field spent a directed-evolution campaign over hundreds of millions of variants engineering a cytokine that escapes it. Dermatology is now proposing to dose that same decoy as the therapeutic. Same axis, opposite direction, and the two fields are using each other's reagents as controls.

The in vivo work is a human I-L-four and I-L-four-receptor-alpha knock-in mouse so both arms engage, with dermatitis driven by repeated MC-nine-oh-three plus Staph aureus. Ear thickness went from about two hundred microns to five hundred in the isotype group; the bispecific held it near two hundred fifty to two hundred seventy, significantly better than either single-target comparator at equimolar dose. Across the tissue cytokines, each monotherapy covered its own half and missed the other — dupilumab took down I-L-four and I-L-thirteen but not interferon gamma or I-L-twenty-two, the decoy alone took down interferon gamma and I-L-twenty-two but barely touched the type-two cytokines. Serum I-g-E is the cleanest demonstration that the arms are genuinely non-redundant: dupilumab and the bispecific both crushed it, the decoy alone did nothing to it. That's two arms doing different jobs, not two shots at the same job.

The caveats are substantial and the authors are reasonably upfront. This is preclinical, one disease model, one mouse strain. No pharmacokinetics, no stability, no immunogenicity data — and for a fusion of a natural human decoy protein onto an antibody, immunogenicity against the decoy module is a real question, not a box to tick. The epitope of the I-L-four receptor alpha arm isn't disclosed. And the biggest one is the mirror image of the oncology irony: sustained neutralization of I-L-eighteen is sustained suppression of an antimicrobial and antitumor cytokine, in a patient population whose skin is colonized by Staph aureus by definition. The paper waves at that as something for longer-term studies. In a chronic dermatitis indication dosed for years, that's the therapeutic-window question, and nothing here speaks to it.

Net take. The molecule is early and from a small company you haven't heard of, and preclinical bispecifics in atopic dermatitis have a graveyard — we talked through it in June when Talawar's I-L-thirteen by I-L-eighteen asset came out of stealth on a SPAC. What's new here isn't the target pair, it's the evidence for why the pair is right: a specific, mechanistic account in which the standard of care de-represses the receptor for the second target, and a controlled demonstration that adding the second arm abolishes the rebound. That argument is portable. When you're choosing a second specificity, the question worth asking isn't only which pathway is independently validated — it's which pathway your first arm is about to unleash.
