# Tudriqev — RP1's oncolytic virus clears the FDA on the third try, with a political assist and a confirmatory trial as the price of admission

Paper link: https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/replimune-tudriqev-fda-approve-melanoma-rp1/827226/

## Script

Yesterday the F-D-A approved Tudriqev — the oncolytic virus we've talked about before as R-P-one, generic name vusolimogene oderparepvec — paired with Bristol Myers' nivolumab, for advanced melanoma that's already progressed on a P-D-one drug. You know the mechanism: engineered herpes simplex virus, injected into a tumor, bursting cells open and dumping antigen into the field while G-M-C-S-F recruits the immune system to pay attention, all so the checkpoint antibody has something to work with. What's new isn't the biology, it's how this thing actually got across the finish line, and I think the "how" matters more to you than the melanoma indication does.

This is Replimune's third trip to the F-D-A. Rejected twice — most recently just last summer, when the agency's own reviewers wrote that the pivotal IGNYTE trial wasn't an adequate, well-controlled study and that the patient population was too heterogeneous to trust the result. Then, on July thirtieth, an advisory committee looked at the same data and voted ten to three that it was in fact interpretable and clinically meaningful. The agency followed the panel. And by multiple accounts, that reversal wasn't just a panel vote — trial investigators mounted a public defense of the drug, and the White House reportedly intervened on Replimune's behalf. I don't know of another recent oncology approval where that's been reported so directly. Whatever you think of it, that's now a data point sitting in the F-D-A's institutional memory the next time a company with a shakier single-arm dataset and an unmet-need story goes looking for a rehearing.

The label rests on ninety-one patients out of the trial's hundred and forty — the ones who had a tumor lesion that never got injected with virus. Response rate there was about twenty-four percent, median duration past fourteen months. That non-injected-lesion analysis is the whole ballgame, because IGNYTE had no control arm: everyone got R-P-one plus nivolumab, so there was never a clean way to separate what the virus added from what the checkpoint drug alone would have done. Looking only at tumors the needle never touched is the field's best proxy for a systemic, virus-driven effect, and it's a proxy, not a control group. The approval is accelerated, and it's explicitly contingent — a randomized phase three called IGNYTE-three is running now, R-P-one plus nivolumab against standard therapy, and if it misses, this comes back off the shelf.

Here's why I'd flag it for you specifically, past the melanoma niche. Everything you work on — trispecifics, A-D-Cs, CAR-T — eventually runs into this exact contribution-of-components problem the moment you combine a novel agent with an approved backbone like a checkpoint antibody or a standard-of-care chemo. Regulators want to know what your new molecule is actually contributing, and a single-arm trial structurally can't answer that cleanly. Replimune's workaround — mining the data for a sub-analysis that isolates a systemic effect — is a template you'll see other combination-therapy sponsors reach for, because a randomized trial against an active comparator is slower and more expensive than everybody wants it to be. The lesson isn't "single-arm is fine now." It's that the bar for getting a sympathetic hearing on single-arm data is a coherent mechanistic story plus a confirmatory trial you can point to as already enrolling — and it also apparently doesn't hurt to have vocal outside advocates when the agency's own staff is skeptical.

One more thing worth knowing: this is only the second oncolytic virus ever approved in the U.S. The first, Amgen's Imlygic, cleared in twenty fifteen and never scaled — a commercial afterthought, largely because intratumoral dosing is logistically painful and it didn't move the needle much as a near-monotherapy. Replimune priced Tudriqev at four hundred fifty thousand dollars a course and is betting the checkpoint combination is what Imlygic never had. Whether intratumoral oncolytic viruses are a real modality or a one-hit answer to a very specific post-P-D-one gap is still an open question — but IGNYTE-three's readout, expected in late twenty twenty-seven, is what actually settles it, not yesterday's approval.
