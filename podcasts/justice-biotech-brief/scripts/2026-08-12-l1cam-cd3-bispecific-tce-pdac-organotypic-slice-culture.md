# L1CAMxCD3 bispecific T-cell engager — a pancreatic-cancer TCE keeps working in patient tumor slices with the full immunosuppressive microenvironment intact

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.10.743835v1

## Script

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is where immunotherapy goes to die. Cold, antigen-poor, walled off by dense stroma, and packed with myofibroblasts and macrophages that shut T cells down before they do anything. So when a preprint out of Susanne Sebens's group at Kiel posts two T-cell engagers against pancreatic cancer, the reflex is to shrug — we've seen solid-tumor T-cell engagers look great in a dish and then vanish in the clinic. But I want to spend today on this one, because the interesting part isn't the molecule. It's the test bed. They took these engagers all the way into patient-derived tumor slices with the intact microenvironment and the patient's own tumor-infiltrating T cells as the only effector cells — and the drug still worked. That's a much harder bar than almost any preclinical T-cell-engager paper you'll read this year clears.

Start with the molecules, quickly. Both are bispecific antibodies in an I-g-G-with-appended-fragment format: a fully humanized anti-L1CAM antibody, with a single-chain anti-C-D-three fragment fused onto each light chain through a flexible linker. Two versions — one uses the classic OKT3 C-D-three arm, the other a humanized S-P-thirty-four arm — and that's the only real difference between them. Crucially, they engineered mutations into the Fc tail to silence it, killing off antibody-dependent cytotoxicity, complement, and phagocytosis. So any tumor killing you see is pure T-cell redirection through C-D-three, not Fc effector function sneaking in. L1CAM affinity is sub-nanomolar, around a tenth to two-tenths of a nanomolar. And the target choice is the whole thesis: L1-cell-adhesion-molecule is expressed in roughly eighty percent of pancreatic tumors, concentrated at the invasive front, it's a negative prognostic marker, and it actively drives progression and therapy resistance. In a tumor with almost no clean surface antigens, that's a rare halfway-decent handle.

Now the ladder of models, because that's where they earn it. In two-D, they used matched pancreatic lines engineered to be L1CAM-positive or -negative, and the killing was strictly L1CAM-dependent — no antigen, no effect; a control bispecific with a dead C-D-three arm, no effect; no T cells, no effect. Textbook specificity, apoptosis confirmed by caspase markers, dose-dependent release of granzyme B, interferon gamma, and interleukin-two. Then the stress tests. Three-D spheroids, fine. But then they added back the two cell types that make pancreatic cancer immunotherapy-proof — pancreatic myofibroblasts and macrophages — and the killing was not blunted. They also swapped in immune cells drawn from actual pancreatic-cancer patients, whose T cells are functionally beaten down, and it still worked, though more variably and with weaker cytokine output than healthy-donor cells. That patient-cell result matters, because a drug that only works with pristine healthy T cells is a drug that works in everyone except the people who'd get it.

Then the crown jewel, and I'll be honest about its limits. They took freshly resected, treatment-naïve pancreatic tumors from four patients and cut them into organotypic slice cultures — living tissue with its architecture, its stroma, and its own resident T cells all preserved. No add-back of healthy immune cells; the only effectors are the exhausted tumor-infiltrating T cells that were already sitting in that tumor. Dose the slice with the S-P-thirty-four version, and you get significantly more tumor-cell apoptosis by cleaved caspase-three, a drop in proliferating Ki-sixty-seven-positive cells, and a trend toward more interferon gamma and granzyme B in the supernatant. And here's the kicker — the L1CAM levels in those particular tumors were low, and it still moved the needle. Now the caveats, plainly: it's four patients, most of the slice-culture effects are trends rather than hard significance with only the caspase signal reaching it, only one of the two molecules was taken this far, and there's no in-vivo efficacy — which is genuinely hard for a human-specific engager in pancreatic cancer, but it's still missing. And L1CAM lives on normal neurons and some epithelia, so the on-target, off-tumor safety question is wide open and this paper doesn't touch it.

So how should you hold this? Not as "new pancreatic-cancer drug," because it's early and preclinical. Hold it as a raised bar for the whole field. Most solid-tumor T-cell-engager programs never test in a model this stringent before they hit the clinic — they run donor immune cells against a cell line, everything lights up, and then the intact microenvironment eats them alive in phase one. This group built the model that mimics exactly where those drugs fail, autologous exhausted T cells and all, and got a signal at low antigen density with the stroma present. Think back to the IM1240 capped-C-D-three trispecific from last Friday — that was attacking the solid-tumor engager problem from the toxicity side, trying to keep the thing from setting off cytokine release. This is the same problem from the model-validity side: does the engager actually do anything once the real microenvironment is in the room? The molecule here may or may not become a drug. But the way they asked the question is what I'd want every solid-tumor engager to answer before it ever sees a patient.
