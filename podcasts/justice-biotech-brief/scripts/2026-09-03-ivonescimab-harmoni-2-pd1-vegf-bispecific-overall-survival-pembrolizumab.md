# Ivonescimab — PD-1 x VEGF bispecific beats pembrolizumab on overall survival in HARMONi-2, and the endpoint that was supposed to expose it did not

Paper link: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ivonescimab-meets-overall-survival-key-secondary-endpoint-in-interim-analysis-of-phase-iii-harmoni-2-study-demonstrating-statistically-significant-and-clinically-meaningful-benefit-versus-pembrolizumab-in-first-line-pd-l1-positiv-302867930.html

## Script

Yesterday at five in the afternoon Eastern, Akeso announced that a pre-specified interim analysis of overall survival in HARMONi-two, assessed by the independent data monitoring committee, met the key secondary endpoint. Ivonescimab, their P-D-one by V-E-G-F bispecific, beat pembrolizumab monotherapy on survival in first-line P-D-L-one-positive non-small cell lung cancer. Summit confirmed it in a parallel release. No hazard ratio, no medians, no confidence interval — those come at a medical conference. So what we have is a binary, and I want to spend the episode on why this particular binary is worth more than most press-release binaries you will read this year.

Start with what the objection was. HARMONi-two read out its primary endpoint in May of twenty twenty-four: progression-free survival, hazard ratio zero point five one, confidence interval zero point three eight to zero point six nine, p less than zero point zero zero zero one, median eleven point one four months against five point eight two for pembrolizumab. That is a doubling, head to head, double-blind, against the standard of care. And the immediate, entirely reasonable response from a lot of thoracic oncologists was: this is an anti-angiogenic, and progression-free survival is a radiographic endpoint.

That objection is not cynicism, it is twenty years of institutional memory. Blocking V-E-G-F normalizes tumor vasculature. It reduces permeability, it reduces contrast enhancement, it reduces peritumoral edema. Lesions on a C-T scan get smaller and less avid for reasons that are partly vascular physics rather than dead tumor cells, and RECIST cannot tell those apart. The bevacizumab era is littered with anti-angiogenic regimens that produced beautiful response rates and progression-free survival curves and then flatlined on survival — glioblastoma being the canonical embarrassment, where bevacizumab was approved on radiographic response and the confirmatory trials showed no survival benefit at all. So when a molecule whose second arm is an anti-V-E-G-F posts a spectacular progression-free survival win, the only endpoint that can adjudicate whether you are looking at immunology or at imaging is overall survival.

And the first look at overall survival went the wrong way. In April of last year, at the request of Chinese regulators reviewing the label expansion, Summit disclosed an interim survival analysis at thirty-nine percent data maturity: hazard ratio zero point seven seven seven. Directionally favorable, nowhere near the very stringent alpha the protocol demanded at that maturity, and the company had to headline it as a positive trend. The stock fell about thirty-six percent in a day. That number — zero point seven seven seven, against a progression-free survival hazard ratio of zero point five one — is exactly the signature you would expect if a large fraction of the progression-free benefit were radiographic rather than real. It looked like the start of the classic story.

It was not. It was an immature interim. That is the finding, and I would generalize it: at thirty-nine percent maturity in a first-line lung cancer population where a substantial fraction of the pembrolizumab arm goes on to receive effective subsequent therapy, a survival hazard ratio is close to uninformative, and reading one as a verdict was a mistake a lot of people made in public.

Now the mechanism, because this is the part I think is actually transferable to your bench. Ivonescimab is tetravalent — two P-D-one binding sites and two V-E-G-F binding sites in one molecule — and the design claim is cooperative binding: the antibody has multifold higher affinity for P-D-one when V-E-G-F is present. Think about why that works. V-E-G-F-A is a homodimer, so the two anti-V-E-G-F arms can grip a single ligand bivalently, and V-E-G-F concentration in the tumor microenvironment is orders of magnitude above what you find in normal tissue. The molecule is therefore preferentially tethered where V-E-G-F is abundant, and tethering raises the local concentration of the P-D-one arms right where the tumor-infiltrating T cells are. The selectivity is not coming from affinity for a tumor-restricted antigen — P-D-one and V-E-G-F are both perfectly present in healthy tissue. It comes from requiring both at once, and paying for the coincidence in avidity.

That is the same engineering logic as a low-affinity C-D-three arm in a T cell engager, run in the opposite direction. In a trispecific engager you deliberately weaken the C-D-three arm so that potency only reassembles where dual-antigen avidity restores it. Here they made the second arm conditionally stronger rather than the first arm intrinsically weaker, but the therapeutic index is bought the same way, with a coincidence detector. And what today's readout says is that this particular coincidence detector produces a survival benefit, not just a prettier scan. That is the first time an avidity-gated dual-mechanism immuno-oncology molecule has cleared that bar against pembrolizumab.

Here is the caveat, and it is load-bearing. Every ivonescimab survival win so far is a single-region Chinese trial run by Akeso. HARMONi-two is three hundred ninety-eight patients at Chinese sites. HARMONi-six, squamous disease against tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, gave a survival hazard ratio of zero point six six with medians of twenty-seven point nine against twenty-three point seven months — also China. The biliary tract cancer win against durvalumab a week and a half ago — China. Four positive Phase three survival readouts, all from one country, one sponsor, one set of investigators.

The one multiregional trial is the one that missed. Global HARMONi, ivonescimab plus chemotherapy against placebo plus chemotherapy in E-G-F-R-mutant patients after a third-generation T-K-I, was published in Lancet Oncology two weeks ago: progression-free survival hazard ratio zero point five two, clean; overall survival zero point seven nine with a confidence interval running to one point zero one and a p value of zero point zero five seven. Missed. There is a defensible explanation — median follow-up for Western patients was nine point two months against thirty-two point seven for Asian patients at the data cutoff, and the hazard ratio has drifted favorably as Western follow-up accrued — but a defensible explanation for a miss is not a hit. That biologics licence application has a P-D-U-F-A date of November fourteenth, and it is the F-D-A's first real encounter with this molecule.

So what got de-risked yesterday is specific: P-D-one by V-E-G-F monotherapy beats P-D-one monotherapy on survival in Chinese patients with P-D-L-one-positive lung cancer. What did not get de-risked is whether that transfers to a Western population with different smoking histories, different E-G-F-R mutation rates, different access to subsequent therapy and different tumor immunobiology. The trial that answers it is HARMONi-seven — seven hundred eighty patients, two hundred seventy-four sites, ivonescimab monotherapy against pembrolizumab monotherapy in P-D-L-one-high disease, primary completion in twenty twenty-eight. That is a long time to hold a thesis.

For anyone tracking the rest of the field — pumitamig at BioNTech and Bristol, the Pfizer and 3-S-Bio molecule, Merck's LaNova asset — this is the single best piece of news the class has had, because the shared mechanistic risk was always that V-E-G-F blockade buys imaging and not immunology. That risk is now substantially lower. The shared execution risk, which is that the entire survival evidence base for this class was generated in one country, is exactly where it was on Monday.

Two things I would watch. First, the actual hazard ratio when it is presented. If it lands near zero point six five or better, the April interim was pure immaturity and the progression-free survival curve was telling the truth all along. If it lands at zero point seven five with a confidence interval that barely excludes one, the honest reading is that a doubling of progression-free survival bought a modest survival benefit, and the imaging objection was partly right. Second, whether Akeso reports the post-progression therapy breakdown. In a Chinese first-line trial where crossover and second-line access differ substantially from Western practice, that table is what tells you whether the survival benefit will replicate in a HARMONi-seven population — and it is the table that will be hardest to get.
