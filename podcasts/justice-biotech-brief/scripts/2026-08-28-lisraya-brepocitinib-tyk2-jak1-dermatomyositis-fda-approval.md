## Script

Yesterday afternoon the F-D-A approved brepocitinib, brand name Lisraya, for adults with dermatomyositis. It's a thirty-milligram once-daily pill from Priovant, which is a Roivant company, and it is the first approval brepocitinib has ever received anywhere. I want to spend the episode on it, because the interesting thing here isn't the indication — it's the specific pharmacological bet the molecule represents, and the price the label makes them pay for it.

Brepocitinib is a dual T-Y-K-two and JAK-one inhibitor. That combination is not an accident of promiscuity; it's the design. The type-one interferon receptor is a heterodimer, and the two chains recruit two different kinases — T-Y-K-two sits on one, JAK-one sits on the other. You need both to move signal. Dermatomyositis is, as much as any disease in rheumatology, an interferon-signature disease: type-one interferon is elevated in skin and in muscle, and the magnitude of the signature tracks with disease activity. So if you want to actually shut off that axis rather than lean on it, hitting both kinases of the receptor pair with one molecule is the cleanest way to do it. Deucravacitinib is allosteric and T-Y-K-two-selective — it leaves JAK-one alone. Upadacitinib is JAK-one-selective — it leaves T-Y-K-two alone. Each of those covers half a receptor. Brepocitinib covers the whole thing.

Here's the cost, and it's right there in the boxed warning. To get both kinases, brepocitinib binds the A-T-P site in the kinase domain rather than the allosteric pocket that makes deucravacitinib selective. That makes it a JAK inhibitor in the regulatory sense, and the label carries the full class warning — serious infections, all-cause mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events, thrombosis — all of it extrapolated from the tofacitinib rheumatoid arthritis safety study, in a disease that is not rheumatoid arthritis. Sotyktu, by contrast, carries no boxed warning at all, precisely because it stays out of that pocket. So Priovant chose to take the class warning in exchange for complete coverage of the interferon receptor. In psoriasis, where you have half a dozen very safe biologics, that trade would be indefensible. In dermatomyositis, where patients have been living on chronic high-dose steroids, non-specific immunomodulators, and intravenous immunoglobulin, it's a reasonable bet — and the F-D-A evidently agreed, with priority review and orphan drug designation.

Small precision point worth having, because the messaging around this is going to blur it. The company's claim is "first and only targeted therapy," and that's the correct claim. It is not the first drug ever approved for dermatomyositis — intravenous immunoglobulin has been approved for adult D-M since 2021. The distinction matters: the comparator here isn't nothing, it's an infusion with no mechanistic connection to the pathobiology.

The data are from VALOR, two hundred forty-one patients across ninety sites, the largest dermatomyositis trial ever run. Primary endpoint was the myositis Total Improvement Score at week fifty-two — a composite that rolls up muscle strength, physician and patient global activity, muscle enzymes, and extramuscular disease. Thirty milligrams landed at forty-six point five, placebo at thirty-one point two, a fifteen-point-three-point delta with a p-value under point-oh-oh-one. The number I'd actually anchor on, though, is the fifteen-milligram arm: thirty-seven point five, sitting cleanly between the two. A monotonic dose-response across three arms is the single most reassuring pattern you can get out of a composite endpoint in a rare disease with a hundred-plus patients per arm, and it's the thing that separates this from the long history of myositis trials where a composite moved and nobody could tell whether the drug or the scoring did it. Nine of nine key secondaries were positive. Separation started by week four.

But the endpoint that will decide whether rheumatologists actually reach for this is the steroid one, and it's the best result in the package. Fifty-five percent of patients on drug achieved both moderate-or-better improvement on the composite and minimal-or-no steroid use by week fifty-two, versus thirty percent on placebo. Among patients who came in on at least seven and a half milligrams a day of prednisone equivalent, sixty-two percent tapered down to two and a half milligrams a day or less, versus thirty-eight percent on placebo, and forty-five percent came off steroids entirely versus twenty-nine percent. Note what the placebo arm is doing there — a third to nearly forty percent of placebo patients tapered successfully too, which is a useful reminder about how much of steroid reduction in a trial is protocol and attention rather than pharmacology. The drug is still clearly winning, but the delta is roughly twenty-four points, not sixty-two.

Two things about this I think matter beyond the drug itself. The first is that Priovant is treating brepocitinib as a franchise, not a product — the stated pipeline is Phase three in non-infectious uveitis, Phase three in cutaneous sarcoidosis, and Phase two-b-slash-three in lichen planopilaris. All of those are orphan or near-orphan autoimmune diseases where nothing targeted is approved and where both T-Y-K-two and JAK-one signaling are plausibly load-bearing. Uveitis is worth flagging specifically: we talked nine days ago about Regeneron terminating its anti-C-D-three antibody in non-infectious uveitis after a single dosed patient. Brepocitinib is coming at the same indication from the oral small-molecule side, already in Phase three, and now with a commercial approval and a real safety database behind it. That asymmetry — cheap oral pill with a scary label versus a biologic with a clean mechanism and no data — is going to be the shape of a lot of orphan autoimmune competition.

The second is a quieter detail. Priovant was created in September 2021 by Roivant and Pfizer specifically to run brepocitinib in two diseases: dermatomyositis and lupus. Pfizer handed over global development rights plus U-S and Japan commercial rights and kept twenty-five percent of the company. Lupus does not appear on today's pipeline list. So the founding thesis delivered on exactly half of itself, and the half it delivered on is the one where the interferon story is tightest and the endpoint had a validated composite behind it. That's not a criticism — it's a fairly precise illustration of why rare, mechanistically coherent indications are where this class of molecule actually converts.

No list price disclosed yet, which is the number to watch. Limited distribution through specialty pharmacy, and a copay program that gets eligible commercial patients to zero dollars a month — standard orphan launch mechanics. The primary VALOR results were published in the New England Journal in March, and skin-specific secondary endpoints came out in JAMA Dermatology this month, so the full data set is available if you want to go look at the composite domain by domain rather than take the top-line number.
