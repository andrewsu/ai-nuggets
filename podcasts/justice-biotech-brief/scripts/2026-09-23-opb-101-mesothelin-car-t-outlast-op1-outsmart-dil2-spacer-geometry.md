# OPB-101 — the four-module mesothelin CAR T already dosing patients finally publishes its preclinical package, and the reusable result is that a faster off-rate, not a juxtamembrane epitope, is what beats shed antigen

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.18.752754v1

Companion preprint (OUTSMART designed IL-2): https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.18.752703v1

Clinical trial: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07030907

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick, from bioRxiv, posted yesterday out of Outpace Bio in Seattle. It is the full preclinical package for a mesothelin C-A-R T cell called O-P-B one-oh-one, and the first thing worth saying about it is the timing. This molecule has been in a Phase 1 in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer since June of last year. Patients have been dosed. The preclinical paper is arriving after the I-N-D, not before it, which means you should read it as a company explaining its design logic to the field rather than as a discovery narrative. That is actually useful, because the design logic is the interesting part, and one piece of it contradicts something the mesothelin field has treated as settled.

The architecture is four things in one lentiviral vector. A mesothelin C-A-R with a deliberately tuned spacer. An activation-responsive promoter they call O-P-one. A designed I-L-two cytokine encoded downstream of that promoter, so the cell only makes cytokine when it sees antigen. And an E-G-F-R-based safety switch you can trigger with cetuximab. Nothing on that list is conceptually new on its own. The claim is that you need all four, in one construct, because each one individually buys you a benefit with a tradeoff attached and the combination cancels the tradeoffs.

Start with the spacer work, because I think it is the most transferable thing in the paper. They built a panel of C-A-R spacers spanning roughly forty-three to two hundred forty-eight angstroms, taken from human immune proteins with proline and cysteine motifs for rigidity, and paired them combinatorially with binders against two different regions of mesothelin — the membrane-distal region one and the juxtamembrane region three. Mesothelin's ectodomain sticks out about ninety angstroms. The membrane-distal binders won, and they won specifically when paired with short spacers. Here is the sentence that should get your attention. The most potent binder in the entire screen was S-S-one — the same binder that has already been through human trials in mesothelin C-A-R T — but only when it was paired with their shortest spacer. With the C-D-eight alpha spacer that was actually used clinically, it underperformed. That is a quiet but serious claim: part of what looked like a binder problem in the clinic may have been a geometry problem.

Now the shed antigen result, which is the one I would argue with the field about. Soluble mesothelin and its ligand MUC-sixteen are shed from tumors and act as decoys that soak up the C-A-R. The field's standard answer has been epitope selection — target the juxtamembrane stub that stays behind after shedding, or target a region that MUC-sixteen does not touch. Outpace's lead binder, which they call v-nine, does neither. It binds the membrane-distal region, the exposed part, exactly where the decoy problem should be worst. And it has lower affinity than the parental S-S-one, driven mostly by a faster off-rate. It is fully resistant to ten micrograms per milliliter of soluble mesothelin and one microgram per milliliter of MUC-sixteen. Meanwhile a published region-three C-A-R, which was designed to dodge the MUC-sixteen binding site, was impaired by both decoys. And the juxtamembrane C-A-R that was resistant secreted substantially less I-L-two and interferon gamma. So the ranking they end up with is: kinetics beat epitope. A fast off-rate lets the receptor sample, release, and rebind on a surface where avidity is high, which a soluble monomer cannot compete with. If you are designing against any shed target — and Justice, you have plenty of those — that is the design rule to steal.

The promoter is the exhaustion piece. O-P-one is ten repeats of an N-F-kappa-B consensus motif in front of a minimal promoter and an alpha-globin five-prime U-T-R. Because both four-one-B-B and C-D-three zeta signal through N-F-kappa-B, the C-A-R drives its own expression when it engages antigen, and goes quiet when it does not. Against a conventional constitutive promoter in the same cells, the O-P-one version held its cytokine output through repeated stimulation, carried lower P-D-one and TIGIT, resisted the N-R-four-A and EOMES exhaustion program, kept I-L-seven receptor and JUN, and avoided the N-K-like dysfunction signature. The honest read is that this is a cheaper, vector-encoded version of what the field already demonstrated with dasatinib rest experiments and with knocking the C-A-R into the T-C-R-alpha locus — no small molecule, no gene editing, just a promoter. They show it ports to a different mesothelin binder and to a R-O-R-one C-A-R, which is the generalization claim that matters for a platform.

The cytokine sits under that same promoter, and the number I like best in the paper is not an efficacy number. Serum levels of the designed I-L-two peaked around two hundred picograms per milliliter, and they tracked tumor volume — rising as the tumor grew, falling as it shrank. That is a cytokine that turns itself off when it has won. The companion preprint, posted the same day, is the protein design paper behind it: a computationally redesigned I-L-two with a new topology that preserves the beta-gamma receptor interfaces, ablates I-L-two receptor alpha binding, and is targeted to C-D-eight alpha, so it biases toward C-D-eight T cells and N-K cells and away from regulatory T cells.

Efficacy, briefly, because it is the least surprising part. Complete clearance of subcutaneous lung tumors at a dose of fifty thousand C-A-R T cells, and efficacy down to that same dose in an intraperitoneal ovarian model. Contralateral rechallenge rejected at day seventy. And a serial transfer experiment — cells harvested from cured mice by cheek bleed at day forty and moved into new mice carrying established tumors of two hundred fifty to five hundred cubic millimeters, where they cleared every animal at the higher transfer dose and three of four at the lower one. Cetuximab dropped C-A-R T numbers about ninety percent within forty-eight hours.

Here is my problem with it, and it is a structural one. Every in vivo experiment is in N-S-G mice. No endogenous immune system. The authors are explicit that this is deliberate — they want to isolate the cell-intrinsic circuit — and that is a defensible choice. But it means the paper's most ambitious forward claim, that the secreted cytokine will recruit bystander C-D-eight cells and N-K cells while sparing regulatory T cells, is precisely the claim these models cannot test. There is no immunocompetent experiment anywhere in the paper, and there is no limitations section. Sparing regulatory T cells is a statement about a compartment that does not exist in these animals. Likewise, the safety argument for antigen-dependent cytokine production — that you avoid the systemic toxicity of constitutive I-L-two — has no toxicology experiment behind it here, only a pharmacokinetic curve. And the dose-sparing headline, fifty thousand cells, is a xenograft number; it will not transfer.

There are two things I would watch from here. The first is whether the ovarian Phase 1 reports serum designed I-L-two alongside its C-A-R T expansion data, because that pairing in humans is the real test of the whole antigen-gated-cytokine premise and the mouse curve is a promise, not evidence. The second is whether anyone picks up the spacer-geometry finding and retrospectively reexamines the mesothelin C-A-R trials that read out flat. If S-S-one was a good binder in a badly matched frame, that is a different post-mortem than the one the field wrote.
