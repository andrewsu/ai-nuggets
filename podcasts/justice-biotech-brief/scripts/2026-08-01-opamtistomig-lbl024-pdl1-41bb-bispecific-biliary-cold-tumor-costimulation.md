# Opamtistomig (LBL-024) — a PD-L1×4-1BB bispecific completes phase 2 enrollment in biliary tract cancer, reviving conditional 4-1BB costimulation for cold tumors

Paper link: https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/30/3335874/0/en/leads-biolabs-opamtistomig-pd-l1-4-1bb-bispecific-antibody-completes-patient-enrollment-in-phase-ii-study-for-biliary-tract-cancer-further-demonstrating-breakthrough-potential-in-i.html

## Script

The news peg here is small — a completed-enrollment announcement, two days old, from a Chinese biotech you may not have on your radar. On July 30th, Leads Biolabs said its antibody opamtistomig, code name L-B-L-024, finished enrolling all seventy patients in a phase 2 first-line study in biliary tract cancer, with the full readout slated for E-S-M-O in October. Normally I'd skip an enrollment milestone. I'm not skipping this one, because the molecule itself is a clean example of an idea you and I keep coming back to — that the next generation of checkpoint drugs isn't just a better checkpoint, it's a checkpoint welded to a second, localized signal.

So let me spend the time on the design, not the milestone. Opamtistomig is a bispecific antibody that hits P-D-L-one on one arm and four-one-B-B on the other. One arm does the familiar thing — it blocks the P-D-L-one checkpoint, taking the brakes off exhausted T cells. The other arm is the interesting one. Four-one-B-B is a costimulatory receptor; agonize it on a T cell and you drive proliferation, survival, and effector function. That's signal two, the go signal, layered on top of releasing the brake.

Here's why that second arm matters, and why nobody has cleanly pulled it off before. Four-one-B-B agonists are a graveyard. Bristol's urelumab was a genuinely potent agonist and it ran straight into dose-limiting liver toxicity — you cluster four-one-B-B systemically and you get hepatitis. Pfizer's utomilumab dodged the tox by being a weak agonist, and paid for it with almost no efficacy. That's the whole trap: strong enough to work is strong enough to poison the liver. The escape route the field converged on is conditional agonism — build the molecule so four-one-B-B only gets crosslinked where the other target is densely present. Here that anchor is P-D-L-one, which is enriched in the tumor. In principle the four-one-B-B go signal only fires inside the tumor microenvironment, where P-D-L-one holds the antibody in place, and stays quiet in the liver. Checkpoint release and costimulation, both delivered to the same place at the same time, from one molecule.

Now the honest part, because the data so far are a mixed bag. The most mature signal is in extrapulmonary neuroendocrine carcinoma, where across roughly a hundred and forty-six evaluable patients the monotherapy objective response rate was about fifteen percent, with disease control just under half. Fifteen percent is not a number that makes you sit up — as monotherapy in a cold, ugly tumor it's a foot in the door, not a breakthrough. What earned this program a look is that it got an F-D-A fast track in that neuroendocrine setting, and that the company is now deliberately marching the antibody into exactly the tumors where single-agent P-D-L-one barely registers — biliary tract cancer, gastric, esophageal squamous. That's the strategic tell. If your pitch is conditional costimulation, you prove it not in P-D-L-one-hot melanoma or lung, where checkpoint alone already works, but in the immune-cold indications where you need the second signal to do real work.

So what would actually move me. Completed enrollment is not efficacy — it's a logistics update dressed up as a press release, and I want to be clear-eyed that the October E-S-M-O readout is the event, not this. What I'll be watching for there is whether the biliary tract response rate clears what plain chemo-plus-checkpoint already delivers in first line, and — the part that matters for the whole conditional-agonism thesis — whether the liver enzymes stay clean at active doses. If they get efficacy above the P-D-L-one-alone floor without lighting up transaminases, that's real validation that tumor-localized four-one-B-B is finally tractable.

And that's why I flag it to you. This is the same architecture we talked through with the dual-stimulatory-receptor work — release the brake, add a spatially restricted go signal — just packaged as a plain bispecific antibody instead of an engineered cell. It sits right next to the P-D-L-one-by-VEGF bispecifics on your list: both are bets that a second function bolted onto checkpoint blockade, and aimed at the tumor, beats checkpoint blockade alone. The four-one-B-B version has the harder safety problem to solve, which is exactly what makes the October data worth putting on your calendar.
