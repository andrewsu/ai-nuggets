# CD5-centered membrane domain — CD4 T cell surface protein organization, not abundance, tracks anti-PD-1 response in lung cancer

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.26.761814v1

## Script

Every peripheral blood biomarker study in checkpoint blockade asks the same question in the same way: which cells, and how much of which protein. Frequencies, abundances, activation markers. Twenty years of that has produced partial predictors and no replacement for tumor P-D-L-one staining. A preprint posted this week from Andreas Lundqvist's group at Karolinska, with Pixelgen Technologies, asks a different question of the same blood samples. Not how much protein is on the cell, but how the proteins are arranged on it.

The assay is the Proximity Network Assay, and the intuition behind it is worth a minute, because it's the whole reason this paper is interesting. Cells are labeled with a cocktail of antibodies — a hundred and fifty-eight of them here — each carrying a D-N-A tag. Those tags get stitched into a network based on which ones end up physically near each other on the membrane of a single cell. You read it out by sequencing. What you get back per cell is not a vector of intensities but a graph: for every pair of markers, a proximity score saying whether they sit closer together than you'd expect by chance. Abundance comes along for free. Organization is the new layer.

The cohort is sixty-two patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer, pretreatment peripheral blood, all going on to anti-P-D-one therapy. Twenty-nine objective responders, thirty-three non-responders by RECIST, with the expected survival separation and no confounding from age, sex, chemotherapy or P-D-L-one status.

Now the first result, and it is the one that frames everything after it. The C-D-eight compartment — the population this entire field profiles, because C-D-eight T cells are the cells that do the killing — was essentially flat. Responders had fewer circulating C-D-eight T cells, which the authors read as more of them having left for the tumor, consistent with prior work. But re-cluster them into naive, central memory, effector memory and TEMRA, look at C-D-one-twenty-seven, C-D-twenty-eight, Fas, KLRG-one, C-D-fifty-seven, P-D-one — nothing separates responders from non-responders. Not subset frequencies, not phenotype, not membrane organization. N-K cells, nothing. B cells, nothing.

Everything lived in the C-D-four compartment. And in two different registers.

The abundance register is the conventional one, and it reads cleanly. Responders had a more differentiated C-D-four compartment — more effector memory and central memory, fewer naive cells. And a specific protein class was up: adhesion molecules. C-D-forty-three was significantly higher in responders across the whole C-D-four compartment, ICAM-three trending the same way, C-D-forty-nine-e up in responder effector memory and C-D-forty-seven in TEMRA. Higher C-D-forty-three and ICAM-three also tracked with longer overall survival. The story that suggests is a C-D-four compartment already primed to migrate before you ever give the drug.

The organization register is the new one, and it points the other way. Two marker pairs survived multiple-testing correction, and both were *increased in non-responders*: C-D-five with ICAM-three, and C-D-five with C-D-one-sixty-two. Most pronounced in naive C-D-four cells. Then the network analysis, which is where the claim gets interesting — C-D-five turns out to sit at the center of a densely interconnected domain of adhesion molecules and activation regulators: ICAM-three, C-D-one-sixty-two, C-D-eleven-a, C-D-forty-three, C-D-forty-four, C-D-six, C-D-twenty-eight, SLAMF-six. Only two of those pairs clear correction, but essentially the whole domain moves in the same direction at uncorrected p below point-oh-one, and it moves one way: everything in the module is packed tighter together in the patients who don't respond. Tighter packing, worse survival.

Why C-D-five is a satisfying protein to find at the center of that: C-D-five is the canonical rheostat on T cell receptor signaling, the thing that sets activation thresholds and tunes self-reactivity. If membrane organization is going to matter anywhere, a protein whose entire job is setting a threshold is a good candidate. And there's a recent precedent sitting right inside the module — SLAMF-six was shown this year to suppress anti-tumor T cell immunity specifically through cis-interactions, meaning interactions in the plane of its own membrane. That is exactly the kind of effect an abundance measurement is blind to and a proximity measurement is built for. None of which establishes causality here, and the authors are explicit that it doesn't.

There's a second finding that I think is the best demonstration of what the assay buys you, and it's almost a throwaway. In monocytes, the proximity network showed a cluster of platelet markers — C-D-forty-one, C-D-sixty-two-P, C-D-nine — segregated from the rest of the monocyte network. Not mixed into it. Segregated. That's a platelet physically stuck to a monocyte, visible as its own node cluster. Platelet-monocyte aggregates have been a messy readout for years precisely because, by abundance alone, a monocyte with a platelet on it and a monocyte expressing platelet markers look identical. Here they fall out of the topology. And they graded by response: lowest in complete responders, significantly higher in progressive disease.

Now the part I'd push back on, which is the headline claim that organization beats abundance for prediction. They used Stabl Lasso — a feature selection method with injected null features to estimate false discovery, twenty seeds of five-by-ten-fold cross-validation, out-of-fold predictions only. Methodologically that's the careful version. At the stringent data-driven threshold, C-D-four proximity models hit a median A-U-C of point-six-seven against point-five-three for abundance, and point-five-three is a coin flip. That's the number in the abstract. But relax the threshold to just take the top two features, and abundance comes up to point-six-four while proximity goes to point-six-eight. So the real margin between the two modalities is about four points of A-U-C, not fourteen. The fourteen-point version mostly reflects that the stringent threshold starves the abundance model of features entirely. The honest claim is that proximity is somewhat better and a lot more robust to how hard you squeeze the feature selection — which is still a real claim, just a smaller one.

And point-six-seven is not a clinical biomarker. It's roughly where tumor P-D-L-one immunohistochemistry already sits, in a cohort of sixty-two with no independent validation set. The authors say hypothesis-generating, and they mean it. One more thing I'd want controlled before believing the organization numbers: forty-six of the sixty-two samples were frozen and thawed, sixteen were run fresh. Membrane architecture is about the most plausible thing in immunology to be perturbed by a freeze-thaw cycle. They z-score within batch, which helps, but the fresh-frozen split and the batch structure are not independent.

So what's the take. The predictive performance is not the contribution and shouldn't be read as one. The contribution is that the features which carried the signal were mostly ones that were not individually significant — small coordinated shifts across an interconnected set of membrane proteins, invisible to any single-marker comparison. If that holds up in a larger cohort, it says the informative immune state isn't a quantity of anything. It's a configuration. That's a genuinely different place to look for a biomarker, and for anyone building receptors it's a reminder that what a T cell can do is set as much by where its proteins sit as by how many of them there are.
