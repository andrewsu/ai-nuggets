# CT-202 — a dual pH-dependent Nectin-4 x CD3 engager doses its first patient, against the one ADC target whose skin toxicity is a boxed warning

Paper link: https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/context-therapeutics-announces-first-patient-dosed-in-phase-1-trial-of-ct-202

Paper link: https://www.contexttherapeutics.com/pubs/ct202-sitc2025

Paper link: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07545122

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick today, and on the surface it's the most routine press release there is — first patient dosed, Phase one. Except the molecule does something the field has largely decided is a bad idea, and the technical package underneath it is unusually revealing about how narrow its own margin actually is.

Context Therapeutics said Wednesday that the first patient has been dosed in the Phase one study of C-T two-oh-two, a Nectin-four by C-D three T cell engager. You know this company — we covered C-T-I-M seventy-six, their Claudin-six engager, back in June. They've since discontinued internal development of C-T ninety-five, the mesothelin engager, so the company is now these two assets and not much else.

The antigen choice is the whole story. Nectin-four is arguably the most thoroughly validated antibody-drug-conjugate target in solid tumors — enfortumab vedotin is standard of care in urothelial cancer, first line alongside pembrolizumab, and we've covered its label expansions twice this year. It's also the cleanest example in oncology of on-target, off-tumor toxicity, because the protein sits at low level on keratinocytes, sweat glands and hair follicles. Enfortumab vedotin carries a boxed warning for Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis, grade three-to-four skin reactions run around thirteen percent, and roughly half of patients in the second-line Phase three developed peripheral neuropathy.

Now consider what a C-D three engager does to that picture. A conjugate kills the cell that internalizes it, so the damage is bounded by how much payload you delivered. An engager doesn't deliver damage — it licenses it. You recruit a cytotoxic effector that proliferates, releases cytokines and recruits more effectors, and you aim it at an antigen sitting on the patient's skin. On its face that should be strictly worse than the ADC. The field's answer to this problem for a decade has been to go find antigens that are genuinely tumor-restricted. Context's answer is to keep the antigen and mask the molecule.

Three layers to the masking, and only one is novel. The tumor side is bivalent, two Nectin-four Fab arms, which makes binding avidity-driven — two contacts grip a high-density surface and slide off a low-density one. The two C-D three single-chain Fv domains are sterically occluded by an effector-silenced F-c, so one molecule can't crosslink a T cell receptor by itself — physically bivalent, functionally monovalent. Both standard practice now. The third layer is BioAtla's conditionally-active-biologics chemistry, and it's the interesting one — both arms, tumor and C-D three, bind preferentially at acidic pH, so in principle the molecule is inert in normal tissue at pH seven-point-four and switches on in a tumor microenvironment around six.

That's the pitch. Now the number that matters, from their own S-I-T-C poster last November, which is the real technical document here. They measured T cell activation potency across four Nectin-four-expressing lines at pH six versus pH seven-point-four. The ratios were one-point-nine fold, two-point-five fold, four-point-nine fold, and four-point-oh fold. That is the entire functional expression of "conditionally active." Binding ratios by surface plasmon resonance always look more dramatic than functional potency ratios, and the poster shows those sensorgrams too — but the number you'd dose against is the two-to-five. For a mechanism whose whole justification is sparing skin, a four-fold window is less than one step of a dose escalation.

The strongest datum in the package isn't the pH ratio. It's the primate comparison, and they built the right comparator: a wild-type version of the same architecture, with a non-pH-dependent anti-Nectin-four derived from enfortumab and a non-pH-dependent anti-C-D three derived from S-P thirty-four, dosed in cynomolgus monkeys at matched milligrams per kilogram. The wild-type engager produced roughly thirty-fold higher interleukin six, plus M-C-P one and interleukin ten increases, plus measurable drops in T, B and N-K cell subsets after a single dose at or above one milligram per kilogram. C-T two-oh-two, up to twenty-five milligrams per kilogram, gave no test-article-related clinical observations, no body weight or clinical chemistry effects, no movement in the other cytokines they measured, and no cytokine release syndrome in any animal. Linear pharmacokinetics, half-life about five and a half days. To their credit they do show that something moved at the top dose — I-P ten and M-C-P one both rose at twenty-five milligrams per kilogram, which they call a modest T cell response rather than burying it.

Here's the gap. S-P thirty-four is the hot C-D three clone the entire field has spent five years engineering away from. AbbVie's etentamig, which we covered last Friday, got its remarkably clean cytokine table with a deliberately weak C-D three arm and no masking whatsoever. So beating an S-P-thirty-four engager on interleukin six may be measuring C-D three affinity attenuation rather than pH switching. The experiment that separates those explanations is a pH-insensitive variant with matched C-D three affinity and identical format, and it isn't in the public data.

The efficacy side has a mirror-image problem. The in vivo model is B-T four-seventy-four breast carcinoma in N-O-G mice with human peripheral blood mononuclear cells co-injected alongside the tumor, plus a slow-release estrogen pellet. Co-injected effectors at a fixed ratio means no T cell trafficking is required, and a subcutaneous xenograft is acidic anyway — so this interrogates the on-state only. The informative conclusion is narrower than the slide: pH-masking cost nothing in potency or pharmacokinetics relative to the unmasked parent, in a tumor that was already acidic. Necessary, not sufficient. Nothing in the package tests discrimination, because the only tissue that could is human skin.

Which brings me to the detail that tells you how much confidence the company has in its own switch. Read the eligibility criteria on the trial registration. Under exclusions: history of severe skin toxicity. They wrote that into the protocol. A team that believed a four-fold pH window closes the keratinocyte problem doesn't need to exclude the patients who've already proven they can get it.

The rest of the design is sensible — a Bayesian optimal interval escalation, roughly six dose levels, priming dose then full doses every two weeks; then four to six expansion cohorts across triple-negative breast, colorectal and urothelial cancer, a hundred sixty-two patients planned, four sites all in Australia, first data guided to the second half of twenty twenty-seven.

My real complaint is the enrollment criterion, which is Nectin-four-positive with no disclosed density cutoff. Unlike the D-L-L three argument we had on this show three days ago, the Nectin-four field has already settled this. In metastatic urothelial cancer, patients with Nectin-four gene amplification responded to enfortumab vedotin in twenty-seven of twenty-eight cases — ninety-six percent — versus thirty-two percent in the non-amplified group. And thirty-nine percent of metastatic lesions show reduced Nectin-four relative to the matched primary. Density predicts response to Nectin-four agents and it drifts down as disease progresses. For a molecule whose tumor-versus-normal discrimination is explicitly avidity-driven, density isn't a covariate — it is the mechanism. Enrolling without a cutoff, off archival tissue, in patients who by definition have progressed, is how you get a response rate nobody can interpret.

The indication logic cuts both ways. Urothelial is the strongest cell: everyone enrolled there will be enfortumab-vedotin-experienced, and if their Nectin-four is retained, a mechanism-orthogonal second shot at the same antigen is a real idea, because payload resistance and efflux confer nothing against a T cell. If the antigen is gone, and in a substantial minority it will be, it's the same target and the same failure. Colorectal is the weak cell — Nectin-four is expressed there, no Nectin-four agent has ever worked there, and colorectal is exactly where T cell exclusion has killed nearly every solid-tumor engager that isn't D-L-L three in small cell lung.

Two footnotes. In May, Context paid BioAtla four and a half million dollars up front plus two million by August to buy out all future milestones and royalties and take full economic rights — six and a half million spent weeks before first dose. Read that as conviction or as BioAtla needing cash, but a small-cap doesn't do it casually. And the thesis isn't alone this week: Adagene announced Thursday that Third Arc Bio selected its first lead candidate under a broadened collaboration on masked C-D three engagers, using peptide masking rather than pH-dependent binding.

Net take. The strategic idea is right, and it's where this field has to go — the supply of genuinely tumor-restricted surface antigens is close to exhausted, so the next tranche of targets gets won by conditional activity rather than by expression, and Nectin-four is a good test case precisely because the normal-tissue liability is so well characterized. But the published margin is two-to-five-fold in the assay that matters, the primate control conflates masking with C-D three affinity, and the company's own protocol excludes the patients most likely to expose the failure mode. What I'd watch in the first escalation cohorts isn't the response rate. It's whether dose-limiting toxicity turns out to be dermatologic, and at what multiple of the active dose it shows up. That one number tells you whether dual pH dependence is a therapeutic window or a rounding error.
