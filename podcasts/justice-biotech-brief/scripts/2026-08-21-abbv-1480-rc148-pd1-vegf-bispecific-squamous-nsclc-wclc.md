## Script

Today's pick landed this morning and it sits directly on your V-E-G-F-by-checkpoint beat. AbbVie put out a conference preview ahead of the World Conference on Lung Cancer, and inside it is the first real clinical data on A-B-B-V fourteen-eighty — the molecule you know as R-C one-forty-eight, RemeGen's P-D-1-by-V-E-G-F bispecific, the one AbbVie licensed in January for six hundred fifty million dollars up front and up to four-point-nine-five billion in milestones. Phase 1b, in combination with platinum chemotherapy, first-line advanced non-small-cell lung cancer. At ten milligrams per kilogram, now designated the recommended Phase 3 dose, the response rate was ninety percent in squamous disease — twenty-seven of thirty patients — and seventy-six percent in non-squamous, twenty-two of twenty-nine. Ninety percent will be the number in every headline this week. It is not the number that matters, and I want to spend most of this episode on the one that does.

First the design, because R-C one-forty-eight is not simply another entrant in a crowded class. It's a humanized IgG-one with a silenced Fc, and the mechanistic bet is different from ivonescimab's. Ivonescimab's story is cooperative binding — tetravalent V-E-G-F engagement and P-D-1 engagement reinforcing each other on the same molecule. R-C one-forty-eight's story is closer to address-and-cargo. V-E-G-F is a dimer, so the anti-V-E-G-F arms can crosslink it, and crosslinking a dimeric ligand gives you avidity — the molecule gets progressively trapped wherever V-E-G-F concentration is highest, which is the tumor stroma. RemeGen's own framing is that P-D-1 binding activity is enhanced as a consequence of that enrichment. So the V-E-G-F arm isn't just a second pharmacology, it's the localization mechanism, and checkpoint blockade is what rides along once you've arrived. The silenced Fc is the tell that they meant it — you do not want antibody-dependent killing of the P-D-1-positive T cells you just decorated. Whether the in-vivo behavior actually matches that story is unproven, but it's a coherent design, and it's the same logic we keep coming back to on this show: one arm to decide where, one arm to decide what.

Now, why the ninety percent shouldn't move you much. This is a single-arm Phase 1b whose primary endpoint is response rate, run at twenty-four sites, all of them in China, and the squamous cohort at the recommended dose is thirty patients. The obvious benchmark is KEYNOTE four-oh-seven — pembrolizumab plus carboplatin and a taxane in metastatic squamous disease — where the response rate was fifty-eight-point-four percent. Ninety against fifty-eight looks like a rout. But that fifty-eight came out of a randomized, blinded, five-hundred-fifty-nine-patient global Phase 3, and comparing a thirty-patient open-label Chinese Phase 1b cohort against it is the single most reliable way to fool yourself in oncology. Note also that squamous beat non-squamous by fourteen points here, which is the opposite of what most people would predict, and at thirty versus twenty-nine patients those two confidence intervals overlap across almost their entire range. That difference is noise until a randomized trial says otherwise.

The number that actually earns your attention is one line further down: no grade three or higher hemorrhages at the ten-milligram combination doses. Here is why that's load-bearing. Squamous histology is bevacizumab's absolute contraindication in lung cancer, and it has been for about twenty years, because a Phase 2 trial showed major hemoptysis and fatal pulmonary hemorrhage in squamous patients. Squamous tumors sit centrally, they cavitate, they erode into large vessels. That single finding excluded squamous patients from anti-V-E-G-F lung trials for two decades. The entire expansion of the P-D-one-by-V-E-G-F class into squamous disease is a bet that a bispecific format — with V-E-G-F neutralization concentrated in tumor rather than distributed systemically — declaws that risk. Ivonescimab made that bet first and won it: in HARMONi-six, roughly five hundred patients, grade three or worse hemorrhage came in at one-point-nine percent versus zero-point-eight, with about thirty percent of patients having a hemoptysis history. So R-C one-forty-eight reporting zero events in roughly fifty-nine patients is reassuring in direction and nearly uninformative in magnitude. Zero out of fifty-nine puts your upper bound somewhere around five or six percent. It is entirely consistent with a true two percent rate. It's the right question with an underpowered answer — an entry ticket, not evidence.

What I'd actually watch is that there are now two Phase 3 programs on this molecule with two very different control arms, and the gap between them is the story. RemeGen started a China Phase 3 in April: five hundred seventy-four patients, seventy-eight sites, first-line squamous, R-C one-forty-eight plus carboplatin and paclitaxel against tislelizumab plus the same chemotherapy. That's the identical control arm HARMONi-six used, and it is the softer of the two available bars. AbbVie's own trial, which posted to the registry as not-yet-recruiting and is slated to open on the twenty-second of September, is a different animal: nine hundred forty patients across roughly two hundred fifty sites globally, squamous only, against pembrolizumab plus carboplatin and a taxane — and progression-free survival and overall survival are both co-primary endpoints, with response rate demoted to secondary. That is the honest experiment in this class, and nobody has finished one. Summit's ex-China ivonescimab program produced a progression-free survival win without establishing survival. AbbVie skipped straight to co-primary overall survival against the actual global standard of care. That's either real confidence in the molecule or a very expensive way to find out.

One last thread, because it's the part that explains the price. AbbVie did not pay five-point-six billion for a monotherapy. It bought a chemotherapy-free immune-plus-antiangiogenic backbone to pair with its own conjugates, and the same release makes that explicit. Telisotuzumab adizutecan, their c-Met A-D-C with a topoisomerase-one payload, is going into a Phase 1b/2 with a P-D-1 inhibitor and no platinum at all in first-line non-squamous disease. A-B-B-V seven-oh-six, their SEZ-six A-D-C, showed an eighty-two percent response rate in relapsed or refractory small-cell after platinum in seventeen patients, with SEZ-six expressed in ninety-one percent of small-cell tumors and over ninety-six percent of those with brain or liver metastases. RemeGen has already run R-C one-forty-eight alongside disitamab vedotin and alongside its mesothelin conjugate. And yesterday we flagged Biokin starting a first-line squamous trial pairing a bispecific A-D-C with a P-D-1-by-V-E-G-F bispecific. Everyone is converging on the same architecture from opposite directions: replace chemotherapy in the backbone with a checkpoint-plus-antiangiogenic bispecific, then add a conjugate as the cytotoxic. The ninety percent is the press release. The interesting question is whether the backbone holds.
