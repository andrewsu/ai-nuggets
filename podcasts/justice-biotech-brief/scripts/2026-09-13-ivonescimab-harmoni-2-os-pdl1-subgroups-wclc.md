# Ivonescimab — the HARMONi-2 survival numbers land at WCLC, and the PD-L1 subgroups say which arm did the work

Paper link: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ivonescimab-versus-pembrolizumab-in-first-line-pd-l1-positive-nsclc-positive-overall-survival-results-from-harmoni-2-presented-at-wclc-2026-302876953.html

## Script

Ten days ago on this show I covered Akeso's announcement that ivonescimab, their P-D-one by V-E-G-F bispecific, had beaten pembrolizumab monotherapy on overall survival in HARMONi-two. It was a binary with no numbers attached. I said at the time that the number was the whole story, and I put a marker down: if the hazard ratio landed near zero point six five or better, the progression-free survival curve had been telling the truth all along, and if it landed around zero point seven five with a confidence interval that barely excluded one, the imaging objection to anti-angiogenics was partly right. Overnight the World Conference on Lung Cancer posted the late-breaking abstract, and we have the number.

In the intention-to-treat population, median overall survival was thirty point eight months with ivonescimab against twenty-two point six months with pembrolizumab. Hazard ratio zero point seven three, confidence interval zero point five seven to zero point nine five, p value zero point zero zero nine against a pre-specified one-sided alpha of zero point zero one four one. Data cutoff was the twentieth of August, median follow-up thirty-six months, two hundred thirty-four deaths in a trial of three hundred ninety-eight patients — so roughly sixty percent maturity, which is a real interim rather than the thirty-nine-percent look that spooked everybody last year.

So it is the second scenario, and I am going to be straight about that. Eight point two months of median survival is a large clinical effect and this is the first time any molecule has beaten pembrolizumab monotherapy head to head on survival in a double-blind randomized trial. But a progression-free survival hazard ratio of zero point five one converted into a survival hazard ratio of zero point seven three with an upper confidence bound of zero point nine five. The skeptics who said that some of that doubling in progression-free survival was vascular physics rather than dead tumor were not wrong. They were just not as right as the April interim made them look.

Now here is the part that actually earns the episode, and it is not the headline number. It is an internal comparison that this trial is uniquely positioned to make, because the same patients, the same blinding and the same central radiology committee generated both endpoints.

Look at what progression-free survival did across P-D-L-one strata in the published primary analysis. In the tumour proportion score one to forty-nine percent group, hazard ratio zero point five four. In the fifty percent and above group, zero point four eight. Essentially flat. The progression-free survival benefit did not care how much P-D-L-one the tumor expressed.

Now look at what survival did in the abstract posted today. In the one to forty-nine percent group, hazard ratio zero point eight five, confidence interval zero point six one to one point one eight. In the fifty percent and above group, zero point five eight, confidence interval zero point three eight to zero point eight nine. That is not flat. That is a steep gradient in exactly the variable that the checkpoint arm reads and the anti-angiogenic arm does not.

Sit with the implication. In P-D-L-one-low patients, a progression-free survival hazard ratio of zero point five four converted into a survival hazard ratio of zero point eight five — almost all of it evaporated. In P-D-L-one-high patients, zero point four eight converted into zero point five eight — most of it held. V-E-G-F blockade does not know what a tumor's P-D-L-one score is. P-D-one blockade absolutely does. So the component of ivonescimab's effect that separates curves without extending lives is the P-D-L-one-independent component, and the component that translates into survival tracks checkpoint biology. That is the cleanest natural experiment the whole P-D-one by V-E-G-F class has produced, and it came out of a subgroup table rather than a mechanism paper.

Caveats, because they matter. These are exploratory subgroups, no interaction p value has been shown, both confidence intervals are wide, and the P-D-L-one-high group is a fraction of a three-hundred-ninety-eight-patient trial. This is a hypothesis with unusually good structure, not a proof.

But if you take it seriously, it reframes what the V-E-G-F arm is for, and that reframing is the transferable part for anyone building multispecifics. The naive model is that you have bolted two anti-tumor mechanisms together and you get the sum. Today's data say something narrower and more interesting: the V-E-G-F arm is functioning mainly as an avidity handle. V-E-G-F-A is a homodimer, it is present in the tumor microenvironment at concentrations orders of magnitude above normal tissue, and the two anti-V-E-G-F arms grip it bivalently — so the molecule is tethered where V-E-G-F is abundant and the local concentration of the P-D-one arms goes up right where the tumor-infiltrating T cells are. That is a localization device, not a second mechanism. And a localization device amplifies whatever the primary mechanism can already do, which is exactly why it should help most where there is the most P-D-L-one to block and least where there is least. It is the same engineering grammar as attenuating a C-D-three arm so that potency only reassembles where dual-antigen avidity restores it, run in the other direction. If that is what is happening, then the design lesson is that you should stop picking the second arm for its own pharmacology and start picking it for where it concentrates you.

The other result in this abstract is the one I would not have predicted, and it is the most practically consequential thing here. Squamous patients were forty-five and a half percent of HARMONi-two. Among the squamous patients on ivonescimab, seventy-two percent had central tumors, ten percent had cavitation or necrosis, and six point seven percent had tumors encasing major vessels. If you have ever written an anti-V-E-G-F protocol you recognize that list, because it is the bevacizumab exclusion criteria, verbatim. Squamous histology, central and cavitary lesions and vascular encasement have been ruled out of anti-V-E-G-F lung trials for twenty years because of fatal pulmonary hemorrhage. Akeso says no apparent increase in bleeding risk. And squamous patients got the better survival hazard ratio — zero point six five against zero point seven nine for non-squamous.

If that survives the full presentation, it is the strongest practical argument yet for avidity-gated V-E-G-F blockade: you get the anti-angiogenic contribution without the systemic V-E-G-F neutralization that made this class unusable in half of lung cancer. Two things restrain me. HARMONi-two is monotherapy, and the bevacizumab hemorrhage experience comes from combination with a platinum doublet, so the comparison is not like for like. And roughly a hundred eighty squamous patients cannot exclude a low-single-digit fatal bleeding rate. "No apparent increase" in a press release is not a tabulated adverse event rate, and I want the table.

I want a different table too. The press release says safety characteristics were generally consistent between arms with no new signals. The published primary analysis of this same trial reported grade three or higher treatment-related adverse events in twenty-nine percent of ivonescimab patients against sixteen percent on pembrolizumab. That is close to double, and "generally consistent" is doing some work in that sentence.

Where this leaves the program. The trial that decides whether any of this transfers to a Western population is HARMONi-seven, ivonescimab against pembrolizumab monotherapy, and it enrolls P-D-L-one-high patients specifically. Today's abstract says that in P-D-L-one-high disease the survival hazard ratio is zero point five eight. Whoever chose that population for the global trial chose correctly, and this is a genuine de-risking of the one study that matters. The flip side is that the approved Chinese label covers tumour proportion score one percent and above, and in the one to forty-nine percent slice the confidence interval runs to one point one eight.

Everything still comes with the same asterisk it came with ten days ago. Three hundred ninety-eight patients, fifty-five centers, one country, one sponsor. The single multiregional trial of this molecule missed overall survival at p equals zero point zero five seven, and its biologics licence application has a November fourteenth action date.

Caicun Zhou gives the full oral in Seoul on Monday. Two things I will be reading for. First, the post-progression therapy breakdown, because in a Chinese first-line trial the subsequent-therapy landscape is what tells you whether a survival benefit replicates in a HARMONi-seven population. Second, whether they show a formal interaction test across P-D-L-one strata — because if that gradient is real, it is more important than the headline hazard ratio, and it is a result about how bispecifics work, not just about this one.
