# PD1-IL2v — the effector-versus-exhaustion decision gets made in the lymph node, before the T cell ever sees the tumor

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.08.17.745170v1

## Script

The trade press was completely dark this weekend — nothing net-new out of Fierce, Endpoints, BioPharma Dive, BioSpace or Clinical Trials Arena since Friday afternoon — so today is a preprint day, and I've got a good one for you. It's from Roche pharma research in Zurich, every author is a Roche employee, and the last author is Laura Codarri Deak, which is the name you want attached to this, because she wrote the 2022 paper that defined the whole "better effector" concept in the first place.

The molecule is P-D-one I-L-two-v. If it's been a while: it's an immunocytokine, one protein doing two jobs. One end is a P-D-one blocking antibody. The other end is an interleukin-two variant engineered so it can't bind C-D-twenty-five, the alpha chain of the I-L-two receptor. Killing C-D-twenty-five binding does two useful things — it stops the drug from feeding regulatory T cells, which constitutively wear high C-D-twenty-five, and it stops it from hitting pulmonary endothelium, which is where high-dose I-L-two's vascular leak comes from. So what you're left with is beta-gamma-biased I-L-two agonism delivered in cis — meaning delivered to the same cell the P-D-one arm has grabbed. It's not I-L-two plus checkpoint blockade given together. It's I-L-two delivered specifically and only to P-D-one-positive cells, on the same molecule. Clinically this is eciskafusp alfa, and it's been in trials since 2020.

The thing the group already showed, back in 2022, is that this drug takes stem-like C-D-eight T cells — the P-D-one-positive, T-C-F-one-positive progenitors — and pushes them into a distinctive effector state they called "better effectors," granzyme-B-positive and TIM-three-negative, functionally superior to what you get from anti-P-D-one alone. Anti-P-D-one, by contrast, mostly expands cells that go on to terminal exhaustion.

The question this preprint asks is: where does that decision happen? Everybody, including them, had assumed it happens inside the tumor — the drug reaches the tumor microenvironment and redirects differentiation there. The claim here is that no, the fork in the road is already taken in the tumor-draining lymph node, before those cells ever traffic in.

Here's how they got there. They went back to a mouse pancreatic tumor experiment — subcutaneous Panc-oh-two — where mice got vehicle, or P-D-one I-L-two-v alone, or anti-P-D-one alone, or a stroma-targeted I-L-two-v called F-A-P I-L-two-v, or anti-P-D-one and F-A-P I-L-two-v together. They'd published the tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte data from that experiment already. What they had never analyzed were the draining lymph nodes from the same animals. So they ran single-cell R-N-A sequencing plus paired T-cell-receptor sequencing on the lymph node C-D-eight cells, and then used a computational fate-mapping tool to infer terminal states and the probability that any given cell drifts toward each one.

Two of those inferred states sat inside the stem-like compartment. They call them S-L-one and S-L-two, and the composition is the whole finding. S-L-one was made up almost entirely of cells from P-D-one I-L-two-v-treated mice and was completely absent from vehicle animals — so it's induced by the drug, not a pre-existing population that got bigger. S-L-two was mostly vehicle and anti-P-D-one cells. And the control that matters most: S-L-one was almost exclusively from the cis-targeted arm, which means the arm that got anti-P-D-one and F-A-P I-L-two-v together — both signals, delivered on two separate molecules — did not produce it. Same two pharmacologies, different geometry, different cell state. If you build bispecifics, that's the sentence to sit with.

Transcriptionally the two states are cleanly opposed. S-L-one is interferon-response genes, N-K receptor genes, C-X-three-C-R-one, B-H-L-H-E-forty, and high C-D-twenty-five — which is exactly the positive-feedback signature you'd expect from sustained I-L-two receptor signaling. S-L-two is the inhibitory receptor list — L-A-G-three, P-D-one, C-T-L-A-four, T-I-G-I-T — plus high T-O-X, the master exhaustion transcription factor. Map both onto the Beltra exhaustion hierarchy and S-L-one looks like an intermediate exhausted cell and S-L-two looks like a progenitor exhausted cell. Which is itself odd and worth noting: intermediate effector-like states are supposed to be a tumor phenomenon, and here they are sitting in a lymph node.

One detail I'd flag for your own target hunting: S-L-two selectively expresses C-D-twenty-two. That's the C-D-twenty-two you know from inotuzumab, showing up as an inhibitory receptor on progenitor exhausted C-D-eight cells, apparently helping hold them in a restrained, non-differentiating state.

Then the part that makes the argument more than descriptive. Because they had paired T-cell-receptor data from lymph node and tumor in the same animals, they could ask which lymph node clonotypes show up in which tumor populations. Of the intratumoral better-effector cells, about nine percent shared a clonotype with S-L-one, versus about one percent with S-L-two. Run it the other way: of the exhausted tumor cells, about nine percent shared with S-L-two and well under one percent with S-L-one. The branch in the lymph node maps onto the outcome in the tumor.

Now the skepticism, because there's a fair amount to apply here. First, the word "fate-mapping" in this paper means a computational transition matrix built from transcriptional similarity. It is not genetic lineage tracing, it's not barcoding, and nobody adoptively transferred an S-L-one cell to see what it becomes. The clonal overlap is real evidence, but it's correlative, and single-digit percentages are thinner than they sound. Second, the S-T-A-T-five mechanism — which is the paper's proposed explanation — is inferred entirely from downstream gene signatures. They never show phospho-S-T-A-T-five. They acknowledge that S-T-A-T-five-A messenger R-N-A wouldn't be expected to move, which is correct, but the honest read is that the mechanism is imported from prior literature rather than demonstrated here. Third, and this is the one the abstract doesn't tell you: they ran a second model, K-P-C with the A-D-P-G-K neoantigen, as validation, and the headline result did not reproduce. In that model the P-D-one I-L-two-v clonotypes fed the intratumoral stem-like pool rather than the better effectors. The authors' explanation is plausible — antigen immunogenicity differs, and prior work showed K-P-C tumor control survives blocking lymph node egress entirely, so in that model the drug is probably acting on progenitors already inside the tumor. But a validation experiment that reassigns where the drug works is a real qualifier on "this happens in the lymph node," not a footnote.

What I'd take away for your bench. The generalizable principle is that cis-delivery of a cytokine signal to a checkpoint-marked cell isn't just a safety trick to avoid regulatory T cells and vascular leak — it appears to rewrite lineage commitment at the priming stage, and the trans-delivered version of the same two signals doesn't do it. That's directly transferable to how you'd think about costimulatory arms on T cell engagers and about armoring in cell therapy: the payload matters less than which cell it lands on and whether it lands at the same time as the targeting event. The second thing is a practical biomarker angle — a C-X-three-C-R-one-positive, C-D-twenty-five-high stem-like population in a draining lymph node is a pharmacodynamic readout you could in principle get from a node rather than a tumor biopsy. And the authors note the same signature appears in public data from a structurally different P-D-one-targeted I-L-two, which if it holds makes this a class property rather than a molecule quirk. It's mouse-only, and the authors say plainly that whether human tumors show the same early bifurcation is unanswered. But the design question it poses — where in the response your drug intervenes, not just how hard — is the right one.
