# Zanidatamab (Ziihera) — a biparatopic HER2 bispecific unseats trastuzumab in first-line gastroesophageal cancer, on a control arm the U.S. had already abandoned

## Script

Yesterday the F-D-A approved two Ziihera-containing regimens for first-line, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic H-E-R-two-positive gastric, gastroesophageal junction and esophageal adenocarcinoma. Ziihera is zanidatamab, and the reason this is your story rather than a G-I oncology story is that nothing about the target is new. H-E-R-two is the most thoroughly worked antigen in solid tumors, trastuzumab has held the front line of this disease since two thousand ten, and what just displaced it is not a better target or a better payload. It is a different binding geometry on the same protein. That is the whole lesson, and it transfers directly to engagers and conjugates.

Here is the engineering. Zanidatamab binds the same two extracellular domains that trastuzumab and pertuzumab bind — E-C-D four and E-C-D two — but it puts both specificities on one molecule, and that changes what happens on the membrane. Trastuzumab plus pertuzumab is two antibodies each binding within a single receptor, in cis. A biparatopic with those two arms binds adjacent H-E-R-two molecules in trans, and crosslinking receptors to each other is a categorically different event from occupying them. You get roughly one-and-a-half-fold higher surface saturation, and then you get large H-E-R-two clusters and polarized caps that neither antibody produces alone. Those clusters don't recycle efficiently out of early endosomes, so the receptor gets internalized and degraded rather than merely blocked, and the crosslinking sterically forbids both homodimerization and heterodimerization with the rest of the family, which is why signal inhibition is ligand-independent as well as ligand-dependent.

The part an immunologist should care about most is the effector arm. That clustering licenses complement-dependent cytotoxicity, which trastuzumab and trastuzumab-plus-pertuzumab do not meaningfully do. Complement recruitment is a geometry problem — C-one-q needs a dense, ordered array of Fc regions to engage, not scattered singletons — so forcing receptors into caps is simultaneously a signaling intervention and a way to build a C-one-q docking surface out of the antibody you already dosed. On top of the usual antibody-dependent cell-mediated killing and phagocytosis. So one format change bought better antagonism, better receptor downregulation, and a killing mechanism the standard of care doesn't have. And the internalization piece is exactly why biparatopics are attractive conjugate scaffolds — more trips to the lysosome is the same thing as more payload delivered.

Now the trial, HERIZON-G-E-A-oh-one. Nine hundred fourteen patients randomized between December twenty twenty-one and February twenty twenty-five, across more than thirty countries, three arms, with progression-free and overall survival both primary by blinded independent review. The triplet — zanidatamab plus tislelizumab plus fluoropyrimidine and platinum — gave median progression-free survival of twelve point four months against eight point one, a hazard ratio of zero point six three, and median overall survival of twenty-six point four months against nineteen point two, hazard ratio zero point seven two. The doublet, zanidatamab plus chemotherapy with no checkpoint inhibitor, matched the progression-free survival exactly at twelve point four months, hazard ratio zero point six five, and reached twenty-four point four months of overall survival — hazard ratio zero point eight, p-value zero point oh-six, which missed. The F-D-A approved the triplet for I-H-C three-plus and for I-H-C two-plus with I-S-H positivity, regardless of P-D-L-one status, and approved the doublet for I-H-C three-plus only. Note what that means: the arm that failed its survival boundary at interim still got a label, on the strength of the co-primary progression-free survival win.

Now the criticism, because the headline number is softer than it reads. The control arm was trastuzumab plus chemotherapy with no checkpoint inhibitor. But KEYNOTE eight-eleven had already put pembrolizumab on top of trastuzumab and chemotherapy in this exact setting — accelerated approval in twenty twenty-one, full approval in late twenty twenty-three for P-D-L-one combined positive score of one or greater, final median overall survival of twenty point oh months against sixteen point eight. Enrollment here ran until February of last year, so for most of the accrual period the control arm was not the American standard of care. Twenty-six point four against nineteen point two is a real result against the comparator that was chosen; it is not an answer to the question you actually want answered, which is whether a biparatopic beats trastuzumab when both sit on the same P-D-one backbone. The cross-trial arithmetic still leans zanidatamab's way — twenty-six point four against KEYNOTE eight-eleven's twenty point oh — but that is cross-trial, in a disease where regional differences in outcome are large, and nobody should treat it as the head-to-head.

The subgroup everyone will quote is the one I'd trust least. In P-D-L-one-negative patients the triplet gave twenty-nine point seven months against fifteen point eight; in P-D-L-one-positive patients, twenty-six point four against twenty-one point two. So the apparent benefit is bigger in the patients least likely to respond to the checkpoint inhibitor in the regimen. Read the denominators rather than the deltas: the anomalous number is the control arm's fifteen point eight, not the experimental arm's twenty-nine point seven. A control subgroup running unusually badly inflates a difference without telling you anything about mechanism. And the assay is tislelizumab's tumor area positivity score, not the combined positive score your intuition is calibrated on, so "P-D-L-one negative" here is not the population you think it is. The honest reading of doublet versus triplet is that the checkpoint inhibitor bought about two months of median survival and the difference between clearing an interim boundary and missing it.

The tolerability is a genuine cost and it is shaped strangely for an antibody. Grade three or higher adverse events hit eighty-three point three percent in the triplet against seventy-four point five percent in the control, and diarrhea ran at forty-eight percent overall with about a quarter of patients at grade three or worse — enough for a boxed warning. That is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor's toxicity profile arriving on a monoclonal, and the plausible source is the mechanism itself: an E-C-D-two arm plus forced clustering shuts down H-E-R-family signaling in gut epithelium harder than trastuzumab's single-epitope occupancy ever did. Pertuzumab combinations hint at the same thing at lower intensity. Left ventricular dysfunction, a decline of more than ten points to below fifty percent, showed up in four point three percent and resolved in seventy percent of those.

Zanidatamab's first approval was accelerated, in second-line biliary tract cancer, in late twenty twenty-four. This is its move into a real first-line indication, and the takeaway for your bench is not about gastric cancer. It is that on the single most saturated target in oncology, the win came from changing valency and binding orientation — trans instead of cis, clustering instead of occupancy — and that the payoff arrived in three separable currencies at once: signaling, receptor disposal, and complement. When you are designing the next trispecific, the geometry is not a detail you tune after picking the arms. It may be the entire pharmacology.
