# Chimagen's myeloma trispecific — GSK buys a preclinical T cell engager and won't name its two tumor targets

Paper link: https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/media/press-releases/gsk-to-acquire-potential-best-in-class-t-cell-engager-tce-for-multiple-myeloma-from-chimagen-biosciences/

Paper link: https://www.biospace.com/deals/gsk-bags-chimagens-trispecific-t-cell-engager-in-deal-worth-up-to-750m

## Script

This morning GSK announced it is acquiring a trispecific T cell engager for multiple myeloma from Chimagen Biosciences, the Chinese antibody company. Full global rights, undisclosed upfront, milestones taking the total to as much as seven hundred fifty million dollars. The asset is preclinical. It is expected to enter phase one in twenty twenty-seven. And GSK will not say what its two tumor antigens are.

That last detail is the one I want to spend the episode on, because everything else about this deal is legible and this part is not.

Start with the money, since the headline number is doing more work than it deserves. This is the second time GSK has bought a molecule from Chimagen. In October of twenty twenty-four they paid three hundred million dollars up front, with five hundred fifty in milestones, for C-M-G-one-A-forty-six — a clinical-stage engager that binds C-D-nineteen and C-D-twenty on the tumor side and is now in phase one for B cell malignancies and B cell driven autoimmune disease. Total potential value, eight hundred fifty million. Today's deal has a smaller headline total, seven hundred fifty, and an upfront GSK declined to disclose, for a molecule that has not been in a human. Fierce called it doubling down. I would read the undisclosed upfront the way you normally read an undisclosed upfront, which is that it is small, and that what GSK is really buying here is a second copy of a platform it already decided it liked.

So what is the platform. Chimagen calls it TRIAD, and the architecture is worth knowing because it is the actual asset. It is an I-g-G-like molecule, about a hundred fifty kilodaltons, built in what they write as one-to-one-plus-one valency: a single binding arm against C-D-three, deliberately low affinity, and one arm each against two different tumor antigens, both high affinity. The design bet is the one this show keeps running into — that selectivity should come from avidity rather than from affinity. A cell carrying both tumor antigens gets gripped with two hands and the weak C-D-three arm is enough to assemble a synapse. A cell carrying one antigen, or none, does not hold on long enough for anything to happen.

The preclinical package on the first molecule is the reason to take that seriously, and it is more concrete than most platform claims. Half-maximal tumor cell lysis by human P-B-M-Cs at around zero point three picomolar. In cynomolgus monkeys, tolerated at six times the dose of a conventional C-D-three by C-D-twenty bispecific — three milligrams per kilogram against zero point five — with no noticeable increase in toxicity, and doses up to ten milligrams per kilogram without significant adverse events. C-D-three receptor occupancy that spikes and then settles at twenty to thirty percent rather than saturating. Cytokines peaking four to eight hours after the first dose and back to baseline within twenty-four. Serum half-life over seventy hours. That is a coherent picture of a molecule engineered so that the C-D-three arm is the rate-limiting step on purpose.

Now GSK's stated rationale, which is where I start to disagree. The press release says T cell engagers have shown transformational efficacy in myeloma but have been associated with difficult tolerability profiles, and that the trispecific is designed to address that, enabling broader adoption and earlier use. It is a clean story. It is also, I think, about a year out of date.

Because the leading trispecific in this exact space already reported, and it largely solved the problem GSK is proposing to solve. Johnson and Johnson's J-N-J-five-three-two-two binds B-C-M-A and G-P-R-C-five-D on the myeloma cell plus C-D-three — the same dual-tumor-antigen architecture — and in its first-in-human phase one, a hundred twenty-six patients, eight point two months of median follow-up, the response rate at the recommended phase two dose was eighty-six percent, and a hundred percent in patients naive to both targets. Cytokine release syndrome in fifty-nine percent of patients, and not one event at grade three or higher. One step-up dose of five milligrams, then a hundred milligrams monthly. Monthly, after a single step-up, in a class where the approved bispecifics need weekly dosing and multiple step-ups.

So the cytokine problem, which is what people usually mean when they say difficult tolerability, has an answer on the table. What J-N-J-five-three-two-two did not fix is more interesting, and it is the thing an avidity gate structurally cannot fix. Grade three or higher infections in twenty-eight percent of patients. Taste disturbance in fifty-eight percent. Four treatment-related deaths. Both of those toxicities are on-target on normal tissue: B-C-M-A sits on normal plasma cells, so you get hypogammaglobulinemia and then you get infections, and G-P-R-C-five-D sits on hard keratinized tissue, so you get the taste and nail problems that this show covered when talquetamab read out in June. A two-antigen avidity gate protects tissue that carries one antigen and not the other. It does very little for tissue that carries both. Normal plasma cells carry both.

Which brings me to the crowding, and this is the part I would want on a slide if I were at GSK. The B-C-M-A by G-P-R-C-five-D by C-D-three lane is not empty. Beyond J-N-J-five-three-two-two, Innovent has I-B-I-three-thousand-three in a phase one-two in China and Australia with U-S plans, and M-B-S-three-fourteen has been through dose escalation with updated results at the last hematology meeting. GSK's molecule doses its first patient in twenty twenty-seven. Potential best-in-class is a phrase that means something different when the class leader has a hundred twenty-six patients, fast track designation, and a monthly schedule, and you have a mouse.

Unless, of course, the targets are not B-C-M-A and G-P-R-C-five-D. And that is why the non-disclosure is the most informative thing in the release. Two years ago GSK named C-D-nineteen and C-D-twenty in the headline of the announcement. Today the language is two strategically selected and validated tumor-associated antigens, and nothing else. Validated is a real constraint — in myeloma it narrows you to roughly B-C-M-A, G-P-R-C-five-D, F-c-R-H-five, and C-D-thirty-eight. So there are two readings. Either the pair is the obvious one and GSK would rather not invite the side-by-side with a competitor three years ahead, or the pair is genuinely unusual — something with F-c-R-H-five in it, say, where this show covered cevostamab in July — and they are protecting it. I lean toward the second reading, weakly, on the grounds that GSK has watched this space closely enough to know that arriving fourth with the same two targets is not a strategy. But I would hold that lightly.

The argument GSK should have led with, and did not, is antigen escape. Biallelic loss of B-C-M-A after B-C-M-A-directed therapy is documented. Loss and mutation of G-P-R-C-five-D after G-P-R-C-five-D-directed therapy is documented. A molecule that kills through either antigen covers escape from either one, and that is a durability argument with actual published support behind it. The release gestures at deeper and more durable responses, but it puts tolerability first. Tolerability is the claim that has already been answered by someone else. Durability is the one still open.

One more thing that is GSK's own problem. Their existing myeloma franchise is belantamab mafodotin, a B-C-M-A antibody-drug conjugate. If the trispecific includes B-C-M-A, they will be selling two B-C-M-A-directed agents into overlapping patients, and the sequencing question — whether exposure to the conjugate selects for the antigen-loss clones that then blunt the engager — becomes an internal question rather than a competitive one. Worth asking them about it.

And the design point to carry away, for anyone building these: notice that the thing GSK actually paid for is not the third specificity. It is the attenuated C-D-three arm and the valency that makes attenuation survivable. Eleven days ago on this show we covered AbbVie's etentamig, which is a plain bispecific — one tumor antigen, B-C-M-A — with a deliberately weak C-D-three arm at around one point three micromolar and bivalent high-avidity tumor binding, and it posted twenty-eight percent cytokine release syndrome with zero events at grade three or above in a phase three. Same grammar, one fewer antigen, same safety result. So the second tumor antigen is not what buys you the clean cytokine table. Avidity-gated C-D-three is. The second antigen has to earn its keep on escape and on depth of response, and that is the readout to watch in twenty twenty-seven — not the safety table, which I expect will look fine, and which will not tell you whether this molecule needed to be a trispecific at all.
