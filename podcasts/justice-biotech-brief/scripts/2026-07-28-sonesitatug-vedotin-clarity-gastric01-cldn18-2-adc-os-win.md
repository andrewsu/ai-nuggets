# Sonesitatug vedotin — first Claudin 18.2 ADC to post an overall-survival win in gastric cancer, though the co-primary PFS endpoint missed

Paper link: https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/sone-ve-improved-survival-in-gastric-cancers.html

## Script

Today's story is an antibody-drug conjugate readout that matters both for the specific target and for a bigger question about what a conjugate is actually good for. On Monday, July 27th, AstraZeneca put out high-level results from the CLARITY-Gastric-01 phase three trial of sonesitatug vedotin — they're calling it Sone-Ve — and it's the first phase three anywhere to show an overall-survival benefit with an anti-Claudin 18.2 conjugate in gastric cancer.

Let me set up the asset, because the naming has moved around. Sone-Ve is a Claudin 18.2-targeting conjugate: an anti-Claudin 18.2 antibody, a protease-cleavable linker, and a monomethyl auristatin E payload — so a classic tubulin poison, not a topoisomerase warhead. You may know it by its old codename, A-Z-D-0901, or by its original name from KYM Biosciences, the Chinese biotech AstraZeneca licensed it from back in March 2023. AstraZeneca is pointedly framing this as the first pivotal win from its wholly owned conjugate portfolio — that is, not the Daiichi Sankyo partnership that gave them Enhertu, but a home-built program.

Here's the trial. CLARITY-Gastric-01 enrolled second-line-and-later advanced gastric, gastroesophageal junction, and esophageal adenocarcinoma patients whose tumors express Claudin 18.2 on at least twenty-five percent of cells at any staining intensity, randomized against investigator's choice of therapy — so real chemotherapy comparators, not placebo. And here's the wrinkle you need to hold onto: the trial had two co-primary endpoints. One was overall survival in the third-line-and-later group. The other was progression-free survival, by blinded central review, in the whole second-line-and-later population. The survival endpoint hit — statistically significant and, in their words, highly clinically meaningful — and a key secondary, overall survival across the full second-line-and-later population, also hit. But the progression-free survival co-primary only showed a trend. It did not reach statistical significance.

So sit with that discordance for a second, because it's the interesting part. This is a conjugate that extends life without formally moving progression-free survival. That combination — overall survival positive, PFS not significant — is unusual, and it's the kind of thing that should make you lean in rather than nod along. One reading is that the survival benefit accrues in depth and durability of response and in the post-progression tail, in a way a PFS cut across a mixed second-line-and-later population, diluted by a dose-selection stage early in the trial, just didn't capture cleanly. The more skeptical reading is that regulators will ask hard questions about an open-label trial that missed a co-primary, even a co-primary that's arguably softer than the survival endpoint it did hit. Overall survival is the gold standard and it almost certainly carries the day here — but "positive OS, missed co-primary PFS" is a headline that gets scrutinized, and you should file it as a real, if probably surmountable, wrinkle.

Now the part that should matter most to you. Why does a Claudin 18.2 conjugate winning on survival change anything? Because until now, Claudin 18.2 has been validated mainly as a naked-antibody target — that's zolbetuximab, which works through antibody-dependent killing and complement, and which requires very high expression, staining in roughly seventy-five percent of cells. Sone-Ve is saying the target is good enough to deliver a cytotoxic payload through, and good enough at a much lower bar — twenty-five percent expression, which AstraZeneca estimates covers about sixty percent of patients in this setting versus the thin slice zolbetuximab reaches. That's a modality argument and a biomarker-expansion argument at the same time: the conjugate both broadens who's eligible and shifts the mechanism from immune recruitment to direct payload delivery.

And keep the whole Claudin 18.2 board in view, because this is where your own world intersects it. AstraZeneca isn't just running the conjugate — they've also got A-Z-D-5863, a Claudin 18.2-by-C-D-3 T-cell engager licensed from Harbour BioMed, in phase one. So the same tumor antigen is now being attacked by a conjugate that's shown survival and by a bispecific engager coming up behind it, and eventually somebody has to sequence or combine those. For anyone working on T-cell engagers against solid-tumor antigens, this is a clean case study in a target getting de-risked by one modality while your modality is still in phase one — the antigen biology just got a lot more credible.

The caveats, quickly. This is a high-level press release — no hazard ratios, no median survival numbers, no confidence intervals. Those are being held for a medical meeting and for regulators, so treat the magnitude as unknown until then. Safety was described as consistent with the known profile with no new signals, which for an auristatin payload is the reassuring-but-watch-the-neuropathy answer. And this is second-line-and-later; the first-line question is a separate phase three, CLARITY-Gastric-02, testing Sone-Ve with chemo plus or minus a P-D-1-by-TIGIT bispecific. But as a proof point, this is the one that matters: the first time an anti-Claudin 18.2 conjugate has moved survival in gastric cancer, and it did it while broadening the biomarker net rather than narrowing it.
