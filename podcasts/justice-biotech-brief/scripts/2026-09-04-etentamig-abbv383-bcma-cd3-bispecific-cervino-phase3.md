# Etentamig — a deliberately weak CD3 arm wins the BCMA engager class its first monotherapy Phase 3, and the safety table is the story

## Script

Yesterday morning AbbVie released topline results from CERVINO, a global randomized Phase three of etentamig — you may know it better as A-B-B-V three-eight-three — against investigator's choice of standard therapy in relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. Both primary endpoints hit. Three hundred ninety-three patients, all triple-class exposed, a median of three prior lines, median follow-up eleven point four months. Objective response rate seventy-four percent versus forty-five point seven percent. Progression-free survival hazard ratio zero point four zero, confidence interval zero point two nine to zero point five four. The independent data monitoring committee recommended unblinding the trial at the first planned interim analysis.

And I want to argue those are the least interesting numbers in the release.

Because the B-C-M-A by C-D three engager class has never had an efficacy problem. Teclistamab won accelerated approval on a sixty-three percent response rate. Elranatamab, sixty-one percent. Linvoseltamab, seventy-one percent. Three approved drugs, all redirecting T cells to the same antigen, all producing responses in the majority of patients who have already burned through a proteasome inhibitor, an immunomodulator and an anti-C-D-thirty-eight antibody. Efficacy was never the bottleneck.

What the class has is a tolerability and logistics problem, and it is severe enough to have killed drugs. Amgen walked away from its B-C-M-A engager, pavurutamab, in twenty twenty-two. Pfizer paused enrollment on elranatamab in twenty twenty-one over peripheral neuropathy signals. Bristol Myers axed alnuctamab in July of twenty twenty-four, months after it had already begun a Phase three. And look at the class's other Phase three — MajesTEC-three, teclistamab plus daratumumab in an earlier line, which produced genuinely spectacular efficacy at the American Society of Hematology last December: an eighty-three percent reduction in the risk of progression or death, three-year progression-free survival of eighty-three point four percent against twenty-nine point seven for standard triplets. Grade three or four infections in fifty-four percent of patients. Thirteen infection deaths. That is a combination regimen, in an earlier line, with much longer follow-up, so it is not a fair head-to-head comparison — but it is the number the entire field is now trying to design its way out of, because a drug that induces deep remissions and then kills people with pneumonia has a ceiling on how early in the disease you can use it.

So the design question is: can you keep the redirected killing and lose the collateral immune activation? Etentamig is a specific, testable bet that you can, and the bet lives in one parameter.

It is a two-plus-one molecule. One arm binds C-D three on T cells with deliberately terrible affinity — about one point three micromolar, which is weak on purpose. Two arms bind B-C-M-A with high affinity and, critically, bivalently. It came out of Teneobio's heavy-chain-only antibody platform through a subsidiary called TeneoOne; AbbVie exercised its option to buy the whole thing in twenty twenty-one for four hundred million dollars up front. And there is a third piece of engineering that matters just as much: the molecule retains neonatal F-c receptor binding, so it has a long enough half-life to be dosed once every four weeks after a single step-up dose.

The intuition behind the affinity choice is worth sitting with, because it generalizes far past myeloma. In a T-cell engager, affinity on the C-D three side is the wrong knob to turn up. Crank it and you get productive synapses everywhere the drug goes — including on T cells that have not found a tumor cell, and including in blood, lymph node and marrow where B-C-M-A is scarce or absent. That is what produces cytokine release syndrome, and it is also what drives T cells into exhaustion before they have done much killing. Detune the C-D three arm far enough and a single contact accomplishes essentially nothing on its own. You only get a synapse when the bivalent B-C-M-A arm has already anchored the molecule to a plasma cell at high avidity, which means the tumor side of the molecule is doing all the targeting and the T-cell side is just permission. Selectivity comes from requiring the coincidence, and you pay for it in avidity instead of affinity. This is the same trick, run in the opposite direction, that we talked about yesterday with ivonescimab's cooperative V-E-G-F and P-D-one binding, and it is the same trick disclosed inside Simcere's G-P-R-C-five-D by B-C-M-A by C-D three trispecific that we covered Wednesday.

Here is whether it worked. Cytokine release syndrome occurred in twenty-eight point three percent of patients receiving the single step-up dose — but predominantly grade one, twenty-three point nine percent, and there were no grade three or higher events at all. One patient, zero point nine percent, had grade one neurotoxicity; nothing grade two or above. Grade three or four infections ran twenty-seven point seven percent on etentamig against nineteen point two percent on standard therapy. Grade five infections — fatal ones — one point five percent on etentamig against three point one percent on standard therapy. Lower than the control arm. Discontinuations for treatment-emergent adverse events, three point six percent versus nine point six percent. Twelve-month overall survival eighty-seven point nine percent versus seventy-two percent, hazard ratio zero point four eight, though the prespecified efficacy boundary for survival had not been crossed at this cutoff, so treat that as directional.

That safety table is the actual news. It is the first randomized Phase three evidence that a detuned-C-D-three architecture converts into a clinical therapeutic index, and not merely into a nicer cytokine curve in a preclinical model. Every program trying to gate a C-D three arm behind dual-antigen avidity now has a randomized precedent to point at.

Three things temper it, and the first is the big one. The comparator was investigator's choice from carfilzomib, pomalidomide, elotuzumab, selinexor, bortezomib and dexamethasone — and prior B-C-M-A-directed therapy was an explicit exclusion criterion. So nobody in this trial had ever seen a B-C-M-A drug, and nobody in the control arm got one. What CERVINO proves is that a B-C-M-A engager beats conventional salvage chemotherapy and immunomodulator combinations in triple-class-exposed myeloma, which nobody doubted. It tells you nothing about etentamig versus teclistamab, elranatamab or linvoseltamab, and no one is running that trial. To be fair, forty-five point seven percent in the control arm is a respectable number for this population, so I do not think the comparator was rigged. It is just the wrong comparator for the question a translational immunologist actually cares about.

Second, infections are reduced, not solved. Grade three and four infections were still higher than control, twenty-seven point seven against nineteen point two, and the drug's whole commercial argument is that you dose it for a long time. You are depleting the plasma cell compartment for as long as you dose. The fatal-infection advantage is measured at eleven point four months of median follow-up; ask again at thirty.

Third, on the monthly-dosing claim specifically. Linvoseltamab already reaches every four weeks — but response-adapted, at twenty-four weeks, and only in patients who reached a very good partial response. Etentamig is every four weeks from the start, after one step-up dose. That difference is real and it is the whole commercial thesis: one step-up dose and then a monthly infusion is a regimen a community oncology practice can deliver, and most myeloma in the world is treated in community practice, not at a Dana-Farber or an M-D Anderson. Access, not response rate, is the binding constraint on this class.

Full data go to a plenary at the International Myeloma Society meeting in Glasgow, September twenty-third to twenty-sixth. Four things I would look for. Absolute median progression-free survival and duration of response, not just the hazard ratio. The minimal residual disease negativity rate at ten to the minus five, which is the secondary endpoint that tells you whether a detuned engager still gets deep. The infection curve as a function of time on treatment, and how much intravenous immunoglobulin the etentamig arm received — that is the difference between attributing the survival advantage to protein engineering versus to supportive care. And whether any site gave that single step-up dose in the outpatient setting, because if the answer is no, the community-oncology argument is aspirational rather than demonstrated. AbbVie is also taking this molecule into A-L amyloidosis, where a plasma-cell-directed drug with a monthly schedule and no grade three cytokine release syndrome is arguably worth more than it is in myeloma.
