# IL-2IT — a bispecific IL-2 aimed at oxidized lipids beats native IL-2 in psoriasis, colitis and atherosclerosis, with zero mutations in IL-2

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.10.750022v1

## Script

Posted to bioRxiv yesterday, out of David Klatzmann's group at Sorbonne with Christoph Binder in Vienna and Ziad Mallat's cardiovascular group in Paris and Cambridge: an interleukin-two therapeutic that gets regulatory T cell selectivity without changing a single amino acid in interleukin-two. I want to spend the episode on this because I think the diagnosis in it is more valuable than the molecule, and because the diagnosis indicts a decade of work that a lot of people are still doing.

Here is the problem the field has. Expanding regulatory T cells in a human being is a solved problem. Low-dose native I-L-two does it. Pegylated I-L-two does it. Engineered I-L-two variants do it beautifully. Amgen's efavaleukin alfa expanded T-regs selectively over conventional T cells and N-K cells in patients, exactly as designed — and then failed a Phase 2b in lupus for futility, failed a Phase 2b in ulcerative colitis for futility, and got dropped in rheumatoid arthritis and chronic graft-versus-host disease for insufficient benefit. This group's own first-generation drug, low-dose I-L-two under the name I-L-T-one-oh-one, missed its primary endpoint in a randomized Phase 2 in lupus in the intention-to-treat population, and only reached significance in a post-hoc analysis that dropped two sites. So we can reliably make more T-regs and we cannot reliably make patients better. That dissociation is the actual open question in this entire field.

The standard interpretation has been a dosing and selectivity problem: if we could hit T-regs harder and effector cells less, the benefit would show up. That is what a second-generation mutein is. You take I-L-two and you mutate the surface that contacts the beta chain of the receptor, C-D-one-twenty-two, so the molecule can no longer signal well through the intermediate-affinity dimeric receptor on effector T cells and N-K cells, and is forced through the high-affinity trimeric receptor that T-regs carry because they express C-D-twenty-five constitutively. It works. It also, as this paper puts it bluntly, turns most of these molecules into partial agonists. You bought selectivity by making the drug weaker, and their claim is that what patients are short of is not T-reg numbers but T-reg activation.

So the paper does two things instead, and they are worth separating.

The first is a geometry trick. They fuse wild-type I-L-two to a short piece of the beta chain of C-four binding protein, which has two cysteines that disulfide-link, so the thing comes out as a dimer. AlphaFold three predicts the two I-L-two moieties fold in against each other in a closed state that buries the beta-chain-binding face while leaving the C-D-twenty-five face exposed. The residues doing the burying include asparagine eighty-eight and valine ninety-one — and valine ninety-one is literally the residue efavaleukin alfa mutates to lysine. Same face, same intent, steric instead of covalent. I would normally be skeptical of a structure prediction carrying this much narrative weight, but the model earns some credibility by also independently predicting that plain I-L-two self-dimerizes at that same face, which is a thing crystallography already showed at high concentration.

And the phenotype follows. In mice given a single A-A-V encoding either molecule, plain I-L-two expanded T-regs about threefold and also transiently expanded N-K cells and pushed effector C-D-four cells up threefold. The dimer expanded T-regs more, and barely touched effectors or N-K cells. In cynomolgus macaques the separation was wider: five to fifteen-fold T-reg expansion on the dimer against five-fold or less on plain I-L-two, with effectors and N-K cells spared. There is also a nice piece of plumbing here — I-L-two was detectable in urine at every timepoint across six weeks and the dimer never was, because dimerization pushes it past renal clearance. Plasma levels went from about twenty picograms per milliliter to about three hundred. And in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, plain I-L-two delayed onset without reducing severity, while the dimer held the mean clinical score to zero point five and left about forty percent of animals asymptomatic.

That alone is a better second-generation molecule than the second-generation molecules. But the second thing is the actual argument.

They bolt on a single-chain antibody against oxidation-specific epitopes — E-zero-six, the prototypic antibody against oxidized phosphocholine. Oxidation-specific epitopes are not a lineage marker and not a tumor antigen. They are damage: oxidized phospholipids on injured membranes, on apoptotic cells, on oxidized L-D-L, in the matrix of chronically inflamed tissue. So the address is a tissue state rather than a cell type, which means one targeting arm should work in any inflamed tissue. And because anti-oxidation-epitope antibodies are independently anti-inflammatory, this is a genuine bispecific rather than a cytokine with a zip code on it — both arms have therapeutic activity of their own.

The construct keeps its potency. About one hundred kilodaltons, binds oxidized phosphocholine and not the other oxidation epitope classes, stains both mouse and human atherosclerotic plaque, and retains full STAT-five phosphorylation in T-regs with a proliferation E-C-fifty only about two and a half fold off native I-L-two. Compare that to a partial agonist.

Now the experiment that makes the whole case. Imiquimod psoriasis, applied to one ear with the other ear as an internal control. The targeted molecule showed up by immunofluorescence only at the border of the treated skin and nowhere in the control ear — so the addressing works. Clinically, untreated animals got about fifty percent skin thickening and a scaling score of two. Animals given plain I-L-two got sixty percent thickening and a scaling score of two and a half. Plain systemic I-L-two made psoriasis worse. The targeted version cut thickening below twenty-five percent and scaling to one or less. Same cytokine, same wild-type sequence, same T-reg agonism — helpful in one place and harmful in another, and the only variable is where it acted.

That is the finding. It says the missing variable in this field is spatial, not dose-response.

The rest is consistent. In dextran sulfate colitis, plain I-L-two took the disease activity index from eight down to six; the targeted molecule took it to three and held weight loss to three percent, and was detectable in inflamed colon while plain I-L-two was not. In L-D-L-receptor knockout mice on eight weeks of high-cholesterol diet, T-regs went from five percent of C-D-four cells to thirty, dendritic cell activation and T follicular helper frequency both dropped, cholesterol did not move — and lesion coverage of the aortic arch fell from seventeen percent to seven, with collagen and alpha smooth muscle actin both up. That second part matters more than the first: they stabilized plaque, not just shrank it. Then swapping E-zero-six for the higher-affinity M-three-C-sixty-five antibody, twenty-fold tighter at one point eight nanomolar, improved colitis further and took survival in experimental cerebral malaria to seventy percent against forty for untargeted I-L-two and zero for controls, without touching parasite burden.

Two honest caveats. Most of the efficacy here is delivered by A-A-V, meaning continuous expression from a gene, not a dosed protein with a half-life and a schedule — only the five-day pharmacodynamic study and the M-three-C-sixty-five colitis experiment used purified protein, and the protein half-life is three point seven hours. That is a real gap between this data package and a drug. And the paper's rhetorical claim that muteins "consistently failed" is too strong. Nektar's rezpegaldesleukin hit its primary endpoint in a Phase 2b in atopic dermatitis and showed a modest but real effect in alopecia areata. Although notice what rezpegaldesleukin actually is — pegylated I-L-two, which attenuates the beta-chain face by putting a polymer in front of it rather than by mutating it. The one clinical success in Treg-selective I-L-two already used steric occlusion. Which supports their mechanism better than it supports their framing.

Why this should land for anyone building engagers. This is a T-reg engager with the same architecture logic we apply to T cell engagers and conjugates, aimed in the opposite direction: instead of hunting a protein antigen with acceptable tumor-versus-normal selectivity, they targeted a post-translational lipid modification that marks a tissue state. That collapses the target-discovery problem, because the same arm addresses psoriatic skin, inflamed colon, atherosclerotic plaque and inflamed brain. And it sits on the other side of a debate this show covered two weeks ago, when three deaths halted the two largest autoimmune C-A-R-T programs. The deletion branch of immune reset — go kill the B cells — is running into its toxicity ceiling. The augmentation branch has been failing for a different reason, and this paper's answer is that it has been failing because nobody was controlling where the signal landed.

I-L-T-O-O Pharma is on the author list, so there is a commercial vehicle for this. Watch whether the protein format, not the gene, can reproduce the psoriasis and colitis results on a tolerable schedule. That is the whole translational question, and it is not answered here.
