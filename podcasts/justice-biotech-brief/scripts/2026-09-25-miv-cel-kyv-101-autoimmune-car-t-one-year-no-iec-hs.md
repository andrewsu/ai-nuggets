## Script

Three weeks ago on this show I laid out the two competing explanations for the three fatal hemophagocytic syndrome cases that stopped Novartis's rap-cel and froze Bristol's zola-cel program, and I said the discriminating datum would be whether the problem was the platform or the host. Yesterday Kyverna answered that question from the other direction, and the answer is more interesting than the press release makes it sound.

Here is what was reported. Kyverna put out one-year topline data on miv-cel — mivocabtagene autoleucel, formerly K-Y-V-one-oh-one — from two registrational trials. The stiff person syndrome trial, KYSA-eight, is twenty-six patients, single arm, one dose. The primary endpoint is the timed twenty-five-foot walk. At the week-sixteen primary analysis last December the median improvement from baseline was forty-six percent. At twelve months it is forty-nine percent, p below zero point zero zero zero one. Ninety-five percent of the patients who cleared the twenty-percent clinically-meaningful threshold at the primary analysis still clear it at a year. More than a third of the cohort now completes the walk in under five seconds, which is a normal adult time. Of the twelve patients who came in using a walking aid, two thirds still do not need one. Every secondary endpoint held, with p values between zero point zero zero zero one and zero point zero zero zero three. And ninety-two percent of patients remain off chronic immunotherapy for their disease.

The myasthenia trial, KYSA-six, is smaller and earlier — the Phase two portion is seven patients. All seven hit clinically meaningful improvement at twenty-four weeks on both the activities-of-daily-living scale and the quantitative myasthenia gravis score, which are the two co-primaries of the ongoing Phase three, with mean reductions of eight point three and eleven point seven points. Five patients have reached a year or beyond, out to eighteen months, and all five have held. Six of seven are off immunosuppressants entirely — no steroids above ten milligrams, no F-c-R-n blockers, no complement inhibitors.

In both trials: no high-grade cytokine release syndrome, no neurotoxicity syndrome, and no cases of the hemophagocytic syndrome. The rolling B-L-A completes this quarter and the company is asking for priority review. If it lands, it is the first CAR-T approved for an autoimmune disease.

Now, the company's explanation for the clean safety, which is where I want you to spend your attention. Warner Biddle told BioSpace it comes down to two things — a unique construct and a well-established manufacturing process — and he specifically said miv-cel is the only C-D-nineteen CAR in autoimmunity with a fully human C-D-twenty-eight costimulatory domain designed for safety. Read quickly, that sounds like ordinary corporate attribution. It is not. It is a specific, published, mechanistically grounded claim, and it is being described slightly wrong.

The CAR in miv-cel is Hu-nineteen-C-D-eight-twenty-eight-Z, out of James Kochenderfer's group at the N-C-I. Two things are unusual about it. The binder is a fully human single-chain variable fragment rather than the murine F-M-C-sixty-three that sits in essentially every approved C-D-nineteen product — that matters for immunogenicity and redosing. But the part that lowers cytokines is not the costimulatory domain at all. It is the hinge and transmembrane. The construct keeps a C-D-twenty-eight costimulatory endodomain and a C-D-three-zeta activation domain, and then swaps the C-D-twenty-eight hinge and transmembrane out for the C-D-eight-alpha hinge and transmembrane. That single substitution was engineered explicitly to reduce cytokine output, it did so in vitro, and it carried into patients: in the N-C-I lymphoma trial, grade three and four neurologic toxicity ran five percent with this construct against roughly fifty-five percent for the conventional F-M-C-sixty-three-twenty-eight-zeta CAR, without giving up efficacy.

So the reusable lesson, and I think this is the one to take back to the bench: C-D-twenty-eight versus four-one-B-B is the axis everybody argues about, and it is the wrong axis here. The hinge and transmembrane are not inert spacer. They set synapse geometry and they set cytokine output, and they are a tunable knob that is largely independent of which costimulatory tail you bolt on. That is the same lesson as the spacer-geometry result I covered Tuesday on the mesothelin CAR, arriving from a completely different direction. If you are building a construct for a non-malignant indication where the therapeutic index is brutal and your patient has decades of life expectancy, the hinge is where you should be doing your medicinal chemistry, not the endodomain.

The manufacturing half of the claim is the more subtle one, and Kyverna is quietly running the controlled experiment. Miv-cel is made with a conventional multi-day ex vivo process — that is the "well-established" language, and it is the product going into the B-L-A. Kyverna's rapid process, Ingenui-T, which goes from a whole blood draw to cryopreserved product in under seventy-two hours, is a separate product: K-Y-V-one-oh-two, I-N-D accepted in January. Which means the same company will eventually hold roughly the same CAR manufactured two ways. That is a far cleaner test of the rapid-manufacturing hypothesis than anything Novartis is going to disclose about its three deaths, and K-Y-V-one-oh-two is now the program I would watch most closely in this field.

Three things keep me from calling this settled. First, the arithmetic. Zero events across thirty-three trial patients bounds the true rate at roughly nine percent or below by the rule of three. Novartis's rate was low single digits. Thirty-three patients does not exclude it. The company says more than a hundred patients have received miv-cel across autoimmune indications, which helps, but that is pooled across studies with mixed follow-up, not a one-year safety database. Second, population. Stiff person syndrome and myasthenia gravis are antibody-mediated neurologic diseases. The Novartis holds were concentrated in lupus, systemic sclerosis, vasculitis and inflammatory myopathy — conditions with substantially higher baseline inflammatory tone and, plausibly, a lower threshold for macrophage activation. A construct explanation and a population explanation predict the same result here, and this dataset cannot separate them. Third, none of this changes the architecture. There is still lymphodepletion, still apheresis, still vein-to-vein time, and once it is infused you cannot dose it down. The engager argument I made three weeks ago survives intact — what does not survive is the version of that argument that leans on the word "safer."

Two other things worth logging. The B-L-A guidance has slipped: in December the company said first half of twenty twenty-six, and it is now completing in the fourth quarter. And in the stiff person cohort, freedom from chronic immunotherapy went from one hundred percent at week sixteen to ninety-two percent at a year. That drift is small and it is the single number I would track, because drug-free remission is the entire commercial and clinical case against a chronic biologic — in myasthenia in particular, the comparator is an F-c-R-n blocker that is safe, effective, and does not require a transplant unit.

The full stiff person dataset goes up at the joint A-C-T-R-I-M-S-E-C-T-R-I-M-S meeting in Toronto in late October, and the myasthenia follow-up gets an oral presentation at the neuromuscular medicine meeting in Orlando on Monday. What I want out of those two talks is not more efficacy. It is peak CAR expansion and the cytokine curves. If peak expansion in these patients sits well below what Novartis reported with T-Charge, the construct and manufacturing story is real and portable. If expansion is comparable and the cytokines still stayed flat, then the hinge did the work and everybody building a C-D-nineteen CAR for autoimmunity should be re-reading the twenty-twenty N-C-I paper this weekend.
