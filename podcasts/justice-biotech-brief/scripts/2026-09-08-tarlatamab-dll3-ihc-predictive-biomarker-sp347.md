# Tarlatamab — DLL3 by IHC predicts who benefits, on the exact assay Rova-T left behind

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.03.749238v1

## Script

Slow news day — the trade press this morning is a Beam base-editing update out of the respiratory congress, a Novartis Phase 3 failure in neuromuscular, and the AstraZeneca oral SERD approval that landed over the holiday weekend. None of that is your beat. But a preprint went up out of MD Anderson yesterday that is exactly your beat, and it settles an argument the T cell engager field has been having with itself for two years.

The claim is simple: baseline D-L-L-three expression by immunohistochemistry predicts who benefits from tarlatamab. That's it. And the reason it's news rather than an obvious result is that the field talked itself out of this in 2023 and 2024.

Here's the setup. Tarlatamab is the D-L-L-three by C-D-three T cell engager, approved in 2024 for relapsed small cell lung cancer, confirmed in the Phase 3 last year against chemotherapy. But the objective response rate is thirty-five percent and median progression-free survival is five-point-three months. So more than half of patients don't respond, and a lot of the ones who do are progressing within six months. There is no companion diagnostic. Nobody tests D-L-L-three before prescribing it.

Why not? Because in the early trials some D-L-L-three-low and even D-L-L-three-negative patients responded, and the field read that as "expression doesn't predict." Which is precisely the mistake everyone made about P-D-L-one and then spent a decade unmaking. Some P-D-L-one-low patients respond to checkpoint blockade. P-D-L-one still predicts. The two statements are not in conflict, and treating them as if they were is how you end up with no patient-selection strategy for a drug that fails in the majority of the people who get it.

This is a cohort out of the GEMINI database — Lauren Byers and Carl Gay's group, co-corresponding. A hundred thirty-eight patients with small cell lung cancer who had D-L-L-three tested by a C-L-I-A-validated immunohistochemistry assay, and within that, fifty-four who actually received tarlatamab. First finding, and it's the one people get wrong casually: D-L-L-three is not uniformly high. Percent positive tumor cells ranges from zero to a hundred, median seventy percent. Fifteen percent of the treated patients had D-L-L-three of zero. Another seventeen percent were between one and fifty percent. So a third of this real-world tarlatamab population was, by any reasonable definition, not D-L-L-three high — and got the drug anyway, because nothing in practice tells you not to.

Now the outcomes, split at the cohort median of seventy-five percent. Time on treatment, which they use as a real-world stand-in for progression-free survival, was ten-point-one months above the cutoff versus two-point-nine months below it. That's a p of point-oh-three-six. Median overall survival was not reached in the high group versus nine-point-five months in the low group, p of point-oh-six-six — a trend, not a hit. On response, among the forty-one patients evaluable by RECIST, median D-L-L-three was eighty percent in the partial responders versus forty percent in the progressors, p of point-oh-oh-six. And in the twenty-four patients who got a full H-score from a pathologist rather than just percent positivity, an H-score above a hundred fifty separated twelve-point-three months of survival from five-point-five.

Ten-point-one months versus two-point-nine. Same drug, same disease, same line of therapy. That is not a subtle effect, and it maps onto something you can order from a pathology lab today.

Which brings me to what I think is the actually interesting part of this story, and the authors are too polite to say it this way. The assay already exists. At this exact cutoff. The Ventana S-P-three-four-seven antibody with a seventy-five-percent-positive-cells threshold is not something this group invented — it is the assay AbbVie built and deployed to select patients for rovalpituzumab tesirine. TAHOE and MERU both enrolled D-L-L-three-high patients defined that way, and both failed. TAHOE was stopped early for worse survival on the Rova-T arm. But the reason Rova-T failed was the pyrrolobenzodiazepine payload — serosal effusions, pneumonitis, a therapeutic index that never existed. It did not fail because the target was wrong or because the assay didn't identify the target. The biomarker got buried with the drug. Five years later a different modality against the same antigen is the standard of care and nobody is running the test.

The result also isn't confined to tarlatamab. Obrixtamig, Boehringer's D-L-L-three engager, reported this year in extrapulmonary neuroendocrine carcinoma: forty percent response rate in D-L-L-three-high at a fifty-percent cutoff, three percent in D-L-L-three-low. That's a starker separation than anything in this cohort, and it's prospective within a trial. Two assets, two diseases, same direction.

Caveats, because there are real ones. Fifty-four patients, retrospective, one institution, no control arm. The seventy-five-percent cutoff is this cohort's own median, which means it's data-derived rather than prespecified — though I'll note in their favor that thresholds at ten, twenty-five and fifty percent all trend the same way with p-values that tighten as the cutoff rises, so the relationship is monotone rather than an artifact of one lucky split. Treated as a continuous variable in a univariable Cox model, though, D-L-L-three gives a hazard ratio of point-nine-two per ten percent increase with a confidence interval crossing one. Not significant. And time on treatment overestimates progression-free survival here in a specific way the authors flag themselves: patients in this cohort routinely got radiation to isolated intracranial progression and stayed on tarlatamab. So the ten-point-one months is generous.

Two other things worth your attention. The first is the brain. Seventy-eight percent of this cohort had brain metastases before starting tarlatamab, sixty-one percent had new lesions, and intracranial responses were good — nineteen percent complete, forty-two percent partial, scored by a neuroradiologist before any brain radiation, which is the right way to do it. But D-L-L-three level did not correlate with intracranial response at all, and systemic and intracranial responses were concordant in only seventy-seven percent of cases. Either the D-L-L-three level in a liver or lymph node biopsy doesn't describe the brain lesion, or the rate-limiting step in the C-N-S is T cell access rather than target density. For anyone thinking about engagers in the C-N-S, that discordance is the more important sentence in this paper than the primary result.

The second is resistance, and here I'd be careful. They have longitudinal ctDNA and CyTOF on three patients — three — and see N-E-U-R-O-D-one-subtype circulating tumor cells emerging at progression, which lines up with a separate preprint arguing that transcriptional subtype switching away from ASCL1 drops D-L-L-three and drives acquired resistance. Directionally consistent, mechanistically plausible, and n equals three. Treat it as a hypothesis the paper is honest about rather than a finding.

So what do you do with this. If you work on T cell engagers, the useful inversion is not "test D-L-L-three and give tarlatamab to the high patients." It's the other half: this identifies, prospectively and cheaply, the roughly one-third of patients whose median time on this drug is under three months. Those are the patients who should be going into combination or onto a different surface antigen entirely, and right now they are being handed a monotherapy that a lab test could have told you wouldn't hold. The broader point for the class is that target density is a dose. We accepted that for A-D-Cs immediately — nobody argues that HER2 level is irrelevant to trastuzumab deruxtecan. Engagers got a pass on it because the effector is a T cell and T cells amplify, and this cohort is a reminder that amplification has a floor.
