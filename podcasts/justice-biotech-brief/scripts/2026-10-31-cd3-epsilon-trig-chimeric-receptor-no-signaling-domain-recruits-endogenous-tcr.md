# CD3-epsilon-TRIG — a chimeric receptor with no signaling domain that recruits the endogenous T cell receptor

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.24.761481v1

## Script

Yesterday's episode was about how much of a C-A-R's intracellular domain you can throw away — forty-five amino acids, down from a hundred and fifty-four. A preprint posted yesterday from the Institute of Biophysics at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, with collaborators at Zhejiang University and Harbin Medical University, takes that question past its limit. It asks what happens if you throw away the signaling machinery entirely, and then has the receptor borrow someone else's.

Start with the biology, because the engineering falls straight out of it. The alpha-beta T cell receptor is an eight-subunit assembly: the variable alpha and beta chains plus three signaling dimers, C-D-three epsilon-delta, C-D-three epsilon-gamma, and the C-D-three zeta homodimer. Ten I-TAMs across the complex. Antigen binds on the outside, phosphorylation happens on the inside, and the question nobody had settled is which subunit actually fires first. Because it's a coordinated multi-subunit event, that's a real question with no agreed answer.

This group's answer is C-D-three epsilon. The argument runs like this. The C-D-three epsilon intracellular tail normally sits stuck to the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane, held there by its basic residues. On engagement it lets go — it flips from a membrane-proximal to a membrane-distal conformation. What drives the release is membrane tension, and they show that directly rather than inferring it: giant unilamellar vesicles under osmotic stress, and supported lipid bilayers where they strip cholesterol with methyl-beta-cyclodextrin to raise tension. Both increase release and phase separation. Once free, the C-D-three epsilon tail condenses with Lck, either free Lck or Lck bound to a co-receptor.

And here is the move that makes this a mechanism rather than an observation. That condensate then pulls in C-D-three epsilon tails from neighboring, unengaged T cell receptors, which recruits still more receptors and more Lck. Triggering propagates sideways, from the receptors that saw antigen to the ones that didn't. It's an amplifier built out of phase separation.

Then the control that should make you sit up. The C-D-three epsilon tail supports robust T cell activation independently of its own I-TAM. Mutate both I-TAM tyrosines to phenylalanine and it still works. So whatever this domain is doing, it is not transmitting signal through itself. It is a recruiter.

Which is the entire engineering idea. They build what they call TRIG receptors, for triggering molecules. An anti-C-D-nineteen single-chain antibody, a C-D-eight hinge and transmembrane, and the C-D-three epsilon tail as the whole intracellular domain. No I-TAM that matters, no costimulatory module, nothing capable of transducing a signal on its own. It works by grabbing the endogenous T cell receptor.

They nail that dependency down properly. The construct activates Jurkat cells; it barely activates J-seventy-six cells, which lack the T cell receptor complex, and barely activates J-R-T-three, which lacks T cell receptor beta. Put the one-G-four receptor back into J-seventy-six and the function comes back. And it's specific to epsilon — the equivalent C-D-three delta and C-D-three gamma constructs show no comparable dependence on the endogenous receptor. The imaging agrees. Under C-D-nineteen presented on a supported bilayer, TRIG clusters recruit endogenous T cell receptor into them, while a conventional C-D-three zeta C-A-R does essentially none of that.

Now the functional profile, which is the part worth arguing about. In primary human T cells against Raji targets, the TRIG construct is milder than both the C-D-three zeta and the four-one-B-B C-D-three zeta C-A-Rs — weaker but clearly detectable secretion of interleukin two, interferon gamma, and tumor necrosis factor alpha. On a single challenge it looks like the lesser receptor. Under long-term repeated rechallenge at high effector-to-target ratio, meant to mimic relapsed and refractory disease, it beats both. Less cytokine per encounter, more proliferation across many encounters.

In mice, in a Nalm-six leukemia model, tumor control is comparable to four-one-B-B C-D-three zeta. Not better — comparable. The difference is phenotype: greater T cell expansion, lower P-D-one, TIM-three and LAG-three at day fourteen, and more C-D-forty-five-R-O positive, C-D-sixty-two-L positive central memory cells at day twenty-one. I want to be careful here, because this is exactly where papers like this usually overclaim. The efficacy is equivalent. The persistence and exhaustion story is one liquid tumor model.

Then they generalize it in the way that actually tests the model. They designed six peptides in silico, selected purely for the ability to phase-separate with Lck and recruit C-D-three epsilon. These bear no relationship to any natural signaling motif. Dropped in as the intracellular domain of an anti-C-D-nineteen receptor, they activate T cells specifically against C-D-nineteen positive targets. The best one, T-five, cleared Raji tumors in mice comparably to four-one-B-B C-D-three zeta and to twenty-eight-zeta, with better survival. If the model is right, the capacity to condense with Lck should be sufficient on its own — and apparently it is.

The same logic extends to natural killer cells, where several activating receptors share that architecture: a single-pass chain coupled to an adaptor. They screened the tails of N-K-G-two-D, K-I-R-three-D-S-one, N-C-R-one, N-C-R-three and C-D-sixteen alpha for phase separation with Lck, and the ones that separated strongly made functional N-K triggering receptors.

So what do I actually think. The mechanism is the valuable part, and it's reasonably supported — reconstitution in vesicles and bilayers, a tension dependence they manipulate rather than merely observe, the I-TAM-null control, and a chain of evidence from cell-free systems into cells that mostly holds. It also lands neatly against yesterday's paper, which deleted most of the basic-rich stretch of C-D-three zeta and found that neither of the standing predictions held — not the condensation model, not the membrane-sequestration model. This preprint's answer would be that you were watching the wrong subunit. The condensate action is at epsilon.

The engineering has one dependency the paper does not foreground, and it's a big one. A receptor that works by recruiting the endogenous T cell receptor requires an intact endogenous T cell receptor. The standard move in allogeneic cell therapy is to knock out TRAC to prevent graft-versus-host disease, and you cannot do that and keep this receptor working. The natural killer cell work is arguably the answer to that objection, since N-K cells don't have the problem — but that's an argument the authors make implicitly rather than confront.

And notice what the milder cytokine profile might really be buying. If this holds up, a receptor that signals through the endogenous complex inherits that complex's I-TAM diversity and its tuning, instead of flooding the cell with many copies of a single I-TAM pattern. That is a genuinely different reason to expect better persistence than "bolt on another costimulatory domain," and it is testable. The things that would move me: a solid tumor model, serial killing measured on single cells rather than function averaged over a population, and some evidence that the condensates they see in vitro are the species doing the work inside a primary T cell.
