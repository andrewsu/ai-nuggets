# Q901 (mocaciclib) — a selective CDK7 inhibitor sensitizes tumors to topoisomerase-one ADC payloads by removing the repair trigger rather than adding damage, and rescues an antigen-low tumor the ADC cannot touch

Paper link: https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aeb4023

Preprint link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.23.690049v1

Press release: https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/qurient-announces-publication-in-science-advances-demonstrating-synergistic-effect-of-selective-cdk7-inhibitor-q901-with-top1-inhibitor-based-adcs

Combination trial link: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07788222

Monotherapy trial link: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05394103

First-in-human interim data: https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2024.42.16_suppl.3078

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick today, squarely in your ADC lane. A paper went live in Science Advances with a Qurient press release behind it, out of Tae-Kyung Kim's group at POSTECH and Yonsei, with Qurient's own chemistry team, Anish Thomas's branch at the National Cancer Institute, and the Maryland Greenebaum center. The mechanism underneath it is the most counterintuitive thing I have read in ADC combinations this year.

Here is the setup. The topoisomerase-one payload has eaten the ADC field — two approved agents, and by this paper's own count more than a hundred topoisomerase-one conjugates in clinical trials. Which leaves the field exactly two unsolved problems, both consequences of that success. One is the therapeutic window: the toxicity of these conjugates looks an awful lot like the toxicity of the free chemotherapy, and a decade of linker and conjugation engineering has not meaningfully fixed that. The other is cross-resistance — if a patient gets three of these in sequence and they share a payload, what are you actually offering on the third? This paper goes after the second problem with a small molecule.

The molecule is Q nine-oh-one, also called mocaciclib, a covalent and genuinely selective C-D-K-seven inhibitor that anchors on cysteine three-twelve. The mechanism has two arms. The first is the one you would predict: C-D-K-seven inhibition collapses M-Y-C and E-two-F occupancy at promoters and at enhancers, and because those two factors sit on the promoters of essentially every expressed gene in these cells, what comes down with them is the DNA damage repair program — homologous recombination, checkpoint control, replication. Gamma-H-two-A-X climbs with dose and with time. You have induced a transient, chemically imposed homologous-recombination deficiency. Drug-induced BRCAness, which is familiar combination logic.

The second arm runs backwards from intuition, and it is the reason I am spending your five minutes here. A topoisomerase-one inhibitor kills by trapping the enzyme covalently on DNA. The cell's route to clearing that lesion is transcription-coupled repair, and the trigger for transcription-coupled repair is a collision — an elongating RNA polymerase two runs into the trapped enzyme, stalls, and both the polymerase and the crosslinked enzyme get ubiquitinated and destroyed by the proteasome. The collision is what summons the cleanup crew. And what Q nine-oh-one does to transcription is trap the catalytically dead C-D-K-seven at the promoter-proximal region so that polymerase two never escapes into stable elongation. Fewer elongating polymerases, fewer collisions, fewer clearance events, and the lesions simply sit there. They show it directly: topotecan alone gives you more ubiquitinated lesion at every timepoint than topotecan plus Q nine-oh-one does — the opposite of what you would see if the drug were just adding damage.

So this is sensitization by removing the repair trigger, not by making more lesions. That is the transferable idea, and I will come back to it.

There is one tension the paper handles empirically rather than resolving, and it is that Q nine-oh-one also drops topoisomerase-one loading at M-Y-C target genes, because M-Y-C is what recruits the enzyme there. Less enzyme on DNA should mean fewer trappable complexes. They show the net goes the other way with topotecan on board, which is an answer, but an empirical one — the balance between less substrate and slower clearance is probably cell-context dependent and nobody has mapped it.

Now the datum that actually matters. They took H-C-T one-sixteen colon cancer, which has ultra-low HER-two and does not respond to trastuzumab deruxtecan at all in a dish — flat line at ten micrograms per milliliter, because without the receptor the payload does not get in. Q nine-oh-one alone had an I-C-fifty of eight point four nanomolar. Combine that completely inactive conjugate concentration with Q nine-oh-one across a range of dose ratios and growth inhibition improves at most of them. An inactive ADC concentration becomes active. If that generalizes it is a statement about antigen-low tumors, which is the live problem in your field — you spend years on epitope position and payload design, and here is a claim that you can rescue delivery you never achieved by changing what the cell does with the trace that got in.

And now let me push back, because the in vivo data are the weak part, and the authors are honest enough to hand me the knife themselves. In the same xenograft, the conjugate alone gave seventy-four percent tumor growth inhibition, Q nine-oh-one alone eighty-one, the combination a hundred and two, with monotherapy tumors resuming growth around day eleven while the combination held flat. But the conjugate was inert on those cells in the dish — so where did seventy-four percent come from in the mouse? Their own experiment answers it: the drug-to-antibody ratio of trastuzumab deruxtecan falls from eight to about four over twenty-one days in mouse serum. The linker leaks. Which means that experiment is substantially a free-payload experiment, and the combination being tested is Q nine-oh-one plus loose deruxtecan. The sacituzumab govitecan arms in TROP-two-positive lung cancer are cleaner — three milligrams per kilogram of conjugate gave forty-seven percent and Q nine-oh-one at ten gave eighty, going to ninety together; flip the doses and seventy-two and sixty-one go to ninety-two. But look at the shape. The small molecule carries most of the effect in every arm and the combination increment is single digits. The percentages are not the signal. The durability is — conjugate-alone tumors regrow after about two weeks and the combination stays flat. That is the finding I believe.

I should also flag that the paper's own one-line teaser is overcoming ADC cross-resistance, and there is no ADC-resistant model anywhere in the paper. An antigen-low tumor is not an acquired-resistance tumor. The cross-resistance argument is imported from clinical observations the authors themselves call limited.

Some class history is worth having here, because C-D-K-seven has been a graveyard. The original tool compound and the Syros derivatives produced beautiful apoptosis and global transcriptional shutdown, and the field then worked out that the killing came from off-target inhibition of C-D-K twelve and thirteen, not C-D-K-seven at all. When somebody built a genuinely selective one, it stopped repressing bulk polymerase phosphorylation and gave G-one arrest with no appreciable apoptosis. Selectivity cost potency. Q nine-oh-one sits in that selective bracket, which is exactly why a combination is the honest framing — this was never going to be a monotherapy drug, and its monotherapy mouse numbers are the part I trust least. The human calibration is the first-in-human interim from two years ago: seventeen patients across three dose levels, no treatment-related serious adverse events, no discontinuations, no dose reductions, and one pancreatic partial response at the lowest dose. Tolerability is the asset in that dataset, and tolerability is what a combination partner needs.

Which brings us to the test that exists. A Phase 2 at Yonsei — investigator-initiated, note, not a Qurient registrational study — forty-eight patients, single-arm, advanced triple-negative breast cancer. Sacituzumab govitecan at ten milligrams per kilogram, the unmodified label dose, plus Q nine-oh-one at a hundred and twenty-six milligrams per square meter, both on days one and eight of a twenty-one-day cycle, response rate as the primary endpoint, completing in twenty twenty-eight. Three things I would watch. That Q nine-oh-one dose is more than double the top dose in the interim escalation we have seen, layered onto the full dose of a drug whose boxed warning is neutropenia and diarrhea — the window question is the entire trial. Second, it is single-arm, and the benchmark it will be read against is the thirty-five percent response rate for sacituzumab govitecan alone from ASCENT, so the design cannot separate combination benefit from the conjugate doing what it already does — which is precisely the confound the mouse data just flagged. Third, and this is the subtle one: the mechanism is kinetic. You want the repair deficiency in place while the crosslinks are forming. Mice got the inhibitor daily. Patients get it twice in three weeks, and whether a twice-monthly covalent pulse holds that state across the conjugate's exposure window is not addressed anywhere in this work.

The forward bet is Qurient's strategy rather than this paper's — a dual-payload conjugate that puts a C-D-K-seven inhibitor and a topoisomerase-one inhibitor on the same antibody. Genuinely clever as an answer to the window problem, because co-localizing both mechanisms inside the tumor is how you avoid asking the bone marrow to absorb a systemic transcription inhibitor on top of a systemic topoisomerase payload. But harder than it looks: you split your drug-to-antibody ratio between two warheads, and you give up the one knob this mechanism most obviously needs, which is the interval between setting the repair-deficient state and delivering the lesions.

The read-across for your own work survives whether or not this molecule does. For any payload whose lethality depends on a lesion the cell can repair, the best combination partner may not be the one that makes more lesions — it may be the one that deletes the signal summoning repair. And the corollary is a warning about your own assays. If an inactive concentration of a conjugate can be made active in an antigen-negative line by a partner that has nothing to do with delivery, be very careful about attributing a combination's benefit to your targeting. The drug-to-antibody ratio collapsing from eight to four in mouse serum belongs in the same category. Both are reminders that in a mouse, an ADC experiment is frequently a free-payload experiment wearing a costume.
