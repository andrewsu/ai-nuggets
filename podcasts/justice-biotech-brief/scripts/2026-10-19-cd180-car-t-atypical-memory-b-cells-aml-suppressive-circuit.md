# CD180 CAR T — one antigen on AML blasts and on the suppressive B cells that CAR T therapy itself creates

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.12.758644v1

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick today, posted to bioRxiv yesterday out of Munich — Sebastian Kobold and Adrian Gottschlich at the L-M-U Institute of Clinical Pharmacology, with Marion Subklewe and Michael von Bergwelt-Baildon, and with Saar Gill at Penn and Thorsten Mempel at Mass General on the paper. The claim is that CAR T cells in acute myeloid leukemia manufacture part of their own resistance, and that the suppressor they create and the leukemia they are chasing happen to share one surface antigen.

Start with the population. Atypical memory B cells — the C-D-twenty-seven-negative, I-g-D-negative, T-bet-positive, C-D-eleven-c-high cells you know from lupus, from chronic H-I-V, from malaria. They have been annotated in A-M-L bone marrow atlases before and correlated with bad outcomes, but nobody had asked what they actually do. This group went through two independent single-cell cohorts and a flow cohort and found them consistently enriched in A-M-L marrow, clonally expanded, carrying a transcriptional program that overlaps the chronic-inflammation version of these cells. They also carry an inhibitory ligand set: PD-L-one, PD-L-two, H-V-E-M, B-T-L-A, plus I-L-ten and T-G-F-beta. A four-gene signature of them predicts shorter overall survival in the T-C-G-A leukemia cohort.

Then the functional work, which is where this stops being another atlas paper. Isolated atypical memory B cells suppress autologous T cell proliferation, and the ones from A-M-L patients suppress harder than the ones from healthy donors. The suppression runs through two separable channels. Contact-dependent, through PD-one — nivolumab restores about one-and-a-half-fold of the proliferation, and an anti-B-T-L-A antibody trends the same way. And soluble, through T-G-F-beta — conditioned supernatant alone suppresses, and neutralizing T-G-F-beta reverses it, while neutralizing I-L-ten does not. Both channels also hit CAR T cells: anti-C-D-thirty-three CAR T cells lose expansion and lose killing in the presence of these polarized B cells.

Now the part that earns the episode. These cells are not simply present in the marrow — the leukemia makes them, and the CAR T cells make more of them. Soluble factors from A-M-L blasts alone polarize healthy donor B cells into the suppressive phenotype; plasma from A-M-L patients does it without any cell contact at all. And then they did the clean experiment. Take supernatant from a co-culture of C-D-thirty-three CAR T cells plus A-M-L cells plus B cells, put it on fresh B cells, and you induce the program — PD-L-one up, T-bet up. Take supernatant from CAR T cells plus B cells with no leukemia in the dish, and you get nothing. The polarizing signal requires the CAR T cell to be engaging its target. Ruxolitinib blocks it, a TLR-seven-through-nine antagonist blocks it, a TLR-four inhibitor does not, and the supernatant drives STAT-one phosphorylation. So it is interferon plus innate nucleic-acid sensing, which is the canonical two-signal recipe for these cells, with one addition: plate-bound C-D-seventy plus interferon gamma also induces the program, and C-D-seventy is constitutively on A-M-L blasts. That C-D-seventy-C-D-twenty-seven route is, as far as I can tell, new.

I want to be careful about how strong the causal claim is here. The patient-level association — atypical B cells enriched in non-responders — comes from reanalysis of eleven patients on Penn's C-D-one-twenty-three CAR T trial and a combined seventeen myeloma patients on B-C-M-A CAR T. That is suggestive, not established, and the authors say so. The directional mechanism — CAR T engagement makes the suppressor — is in vitro, from supernatant transfer. But it is the right experiment for the question, and the negative control, the no-leukemia arm, is what makes it interpretable. The uncomfortable implication is the familiar one in a new place: effective killing releases interferon and dying-cell material into a niche that reads both of those as instructions to build a suppressor.

Which brings the therapeutic move. They looked for a transmembrane protein upregulated on both malignant progenitors and on these B cells, and C-D-one-eighty came out — also called R-P-one-oh-five, a TLR family member that sits on the surface without an intracellular signaling domain. Expressed on blasts at levels comparable to C-D-thirty-three and C-D-one-twenty-three, and critically, expressed on the C-D-thirty-four-positive leukemia-initiating fractions at the same level as on bulk blasts. The off-target map is myeloid and B lineage. And the number that should matter most to you: healthy hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells are essentially negative. In co-culture, C-D-one-eighty CAR T cells did not lyse healthy H-S-P-Cs and did not secrete interferon gamma against them, while C-D-thirty-three CAR T cells did both. That is the central structural problem of A-M-L CAR T — the antigens are shared with the stem cell compartment and the toxicity is the myeloablation — and this target appears to step around it.

The in vivo results are strong for preclinical work. Three cell-line xenografts controlled, two patient-derived xenografts including one from relapsed-refractory disease cleared completely, five out of five in each. A PBMC-humanized toxicity model showed no weight loss and no cytokine storm, which was a real question given that the target is a TLR family protein. And then the experiment I'd actually put on a slide: a B-cell-humanized A-M-L mouse, where they engraft human B cells into leukemia-bearing animals before dosing a deliberately low one million CAR T cells. C-D-one-eighty CAR T cells beat C-D-thirty-three CAR T cells there — and adding the B cells did not blunt them, it improved tumor control and survival, with more CAR T expansion than in the non-humanized arm. The same compartment that was a liability for the C-D-thirty-three product becomes co-stimulation once your CAR can kill it. In the head-to-head suppression assay, at an effector-to-target ratio of one-to-four, the polarized B cells cut C-D-thirty-three CAR T killing and left C-D-one-eighty killing untouched.

Three things to hold against it. The C-D-thirty-three comparator underperformed in a cell line that expresses C-D-thirty-three homogeneously, and the authors concede antigen internalization or heterogeneity could be doing some of that work — so some of the margin is comparator-dependent. The primary-sample killing assays were heterogeneous across donors, which is honest but means the effect size is soft. And B cell aplasia plus monocyte depletion is the guaranteed consequence, in a disease where infection is already how people die; the paper leans on the C-D-nineteen CAR T precedent for aplasia being manageable, which is fair for the B cell half and not really an answer for the myeloid half.

One last thing worth knowing. A phase one trial of C-D-one-eighty CAR T cells in relapsed-refractory C-D-one-eighty-positive blood cancers is already recruiting — twelve patients, single center, at the Institute of Hematology in Tianjin, open since June of last year. No published rationale, no preclinical package, nothing. So the clinic got to this target about sixteen months before the biology did, which happens more than anyone likes to admit, and this preprint is now the first serious argument for why that trial should work — and, more usefully, for what a trial like that ought to be measuring in the marrow.
