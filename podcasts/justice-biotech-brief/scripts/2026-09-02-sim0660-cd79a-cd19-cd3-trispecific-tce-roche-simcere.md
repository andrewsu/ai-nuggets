# SIM0660 — a CD79a x CD19 x CD3 trispecific engager goes to Roche for $75M up front, and the target choice is the whole story

Paper link: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/simcere-zaiming-enters-exclusive-license-agreement-with-roche-for-sim0660-global-development-302866230.html

## Script

Yesterday on this show we spent five minutes on three deaths that halted the two largest autoimmune C-A-R-T programs in the world, and I argued the read-across was really an architecture question — that the thing to watch was whether pharma moves toward dose-titratable, off-the-shelf B cell depletion instead of a living drug whose expansion you can't take back. Yesterday morning, Roche answered. And the answer is more interesting than the price tag, because of which two antigens they bought.

Simcere Zaiming, the biologics arm of Simcere Pharmaceutical in Nanjing, licensed global rights to SIM zero-six-six-zero to Roche. Seventy-five million dollars up front, up to one point five three billion dollars in total payments, tiered royalties up to double digits. The molecule is a trispecific antibody: one arm engages C-D three on T cells, and two arms bind two different B cell antigens — C-D nineteen, which you know, and C-D seventy-nine A, which is the choice worth thinking about.

Start with why that pairing is not arbitrary. Every B cell engager in the clinic right now hangs off one of two antigens. Glofitamab, mosunetuzumab, epcoritamab, odronextamab — all C-D twenty. Blinatumomab and the newer autoimmune-directed engagers — C-D nineteen. And the problem with both is the same problem, which is where in B cell development the antigen turns on and where it turns off. C-D seventy-nine A does something neither of those does: it comes on earlier and it goes off later. It's expressed before immunoglobulin heavy chain rearrangement, before C-D twenty appears, all the way back at the pro-B stage — which is why cytoplasmic C-D seventy-nine A is a defining marker for B-lineage acute lymphoblastic leukemia. And critically, it persists past C-D twenty into the late plasma cell stage of differentiation. C-D nineteen, by contrast, is largely lost as B cells become antibody-secreting cells.

Sit with the second half of that, because it's the part that matters for the audience this drug is actually aimed at. In autoimmune disease, the cells that survive rituximab or obinutuzumab and keep making autoantibody are exactly the antibody-secreting compartment that has already stopped expressing C-D twenty. That's not a subtle limitation, it's the central one — it's why B cell depletion in lupus and myositis and Sjogren's produces improvement rather than cure, and it's a large part of why C-A-R-T looked so exciting in autoimmunity in the first place, because a living C-D nineteen drug that trafficks into tissue seemed like it might reach cells an antibody couldn't. Adding C-D seventy-nine A to C-D nineteen is an attempt to buy that same developmental reach with a molecule you can stop dosing. And in lymphoma the logic is the mirror image: pathologists have used C-D seventy-nine A for twenty-five years as the fallback B cell marker precisely when C-D twenty has been lost after anti-C-D-twenty therapy. Simcere's framing — a differentiated option for patients previously treated with C-D twenty or C-D nineteen-directed therapies — is not marketing. The escape lane is the target.

Now the part I'd interrogate, and it's the reason I'd hold my enthusiasm at arm's length. Almost everything clinically known about C-D seventy-nine A was learned with antibodies against an intracellular epitope. The workhorse reagent, H-M fifty-seven, binds inside the cell; the routine assay is cytoplasmic staining on permeabilized cells. Its partner chain, C-D seventy-nine B, is the one with the well-validated extracellular epitope — which is not a coincidence, it's why polatuzumab vedotin, an anti-C-D-seventy-nine-B conjugate, exists and is approved and anti-C-D-seventy-nine-A drugs largely don't. C-D seventy-nine A sits in the B cell receptor complex, disulfide-linked to C-D seventy-nine B and associated with membrane immunoglobulin, and its accessible surface is small and crowded. So the load-bearing engineering claim inside SIM zero-six-six-zero is that you can get a productive extracellular grip on that antigen at the density it's actually presented on a primary B cell. That claim is not supported by anything public. There is no registration for this molecule on ClinicalTrials-dot-gov as of today. What's in the public domain is a target combination and a platform assertion.

The platform assertion, though, is worth taking seriously, because we've seen it spelled out before on the sibling molecule. Simcere's other trispecific, SIM zero-five-zero-zero, targets G-P-R-C-five-D, B-C-M-A and C-D three in myeloma, and AbbVie optioned it in January twenty twenty-five for up to about one point zero five five billion. The disclosed design there is a deliberately low-affinity C-D three arm paired with two tumor antigens. That's the trick, and it's the same trick here: you detune C-D three engagement so you're not flooding the system with cytokine, then you recover potency and selectivity through avidity — you need both antigens on the same cell to get a productive synapse. That's what "potent cytotoxicity while limiting cytokine release" actually means mechanistically, and it's also the most direct answer to a week in which the field just got a very expensive reminder that uncontrolled immune activation is the toxicity that kills people. A dual-antigen avidity gate is a dose-response you can steer. Expansion of an autologous product is not.

For Roche specifically this is a franchise move, not an opportunistic one, and you can see the whole board. They own polatuzumab vedotin, so they already believe in the C-D seventy-nine complex as a druggable surface. They own two approved C-D twenty by C-D three engagers. They got obinutuzumab approved in lupus nephritis last October — the only anti-C-D-twenty antibody with a randomized Phase three complete renal response benefit. And in May of this year they opened a thirty-patient Phase two of mosunetuzumab in systemic lupus with and without nephritis. So Roche is already running a C-D twenty engager in lupus and already knows where it will plateau. Buying the C-D seventy-nine A by C-D nineteen molecule is buying the next line in a story they're actively writing. It also makes three Asia-Pacific deals in eight days for them, after Hanmi and DualityBio, which we covered Monday.

How should you price it? Seventy-five million against one point five three billion is about a five percent up-front ratio, which is option pricing on a preclinical-to-very-early asset, and Roche structured it that way for a reason. Nobody is paying conviction money for a first-in-class extracellular binder against an antigen the field has mostly only ever stained from the inside.

Two numbers would change my read. First, tissue-level depletion: any demonstration that this molecule clears B cells in lymph node or in inflamed tissue, not just in blood, at a dose where cytokine release stays grade one — that's the specific thing engagers have to prove to compete with a trafficking cell therapy. Second, and this is the one I actually care about: evidence that it touches the C-D nineteen-negative plasmablast compartment. If the C-D seventy-nine A arm really does reach into the antibody-secreting stage in vivo, then this isn't another B cell depleter, it's the first engager with a plausible claim on the cells that make autoantibody. If it doesn't, it's a nicer C-D nineteen engager with a second binder for avidity, which is a fine drug and a much smaller one.
