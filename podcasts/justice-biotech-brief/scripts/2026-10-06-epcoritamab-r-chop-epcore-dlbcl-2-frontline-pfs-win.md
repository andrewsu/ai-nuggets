# Epcoritamab — CD20×CD3 bispecific plus R-CHOP halves progression risk in frontline DLBCL, the first T cell engager to win a Phase 3 before relapse

Paper link: https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/genmab-and-abbvie-announce-epcoritamab-in-combination-with-r-chop-demonstrates-statistically-significant-improvement-in-progression-free-survival-in-patients-with-newly-diagnosed-diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma

## Script

Genmab and AbbVie put out topline results yesterday from EPCORE D-L-B-C-L two, and this one deserves your full attention. Epcoritamab, the subcutaneous C-D-twenty by C-D-three T cell engager, added to R-CHOP in newly diagnosed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, against R-CHOP alone. Hazard ratio for progression-free survival of zero point four nine. That is a fifty-one percent cut in the risk of progression or death, in the primary population with an International Prognostic Index of three to five, and the same zero point four nine across the broader I-P-I two to five population. Progression is read by independent review, which matters because the trial is open label.

Here is why that number is startling. Frontline D-L-B-C-L has been a graveyard for about twenty years. R-CHOP became the standard around the turn of the century, and then trial after trial tried to bolt something onto it and failed. Bortezomib, ibrutinib, lenalidomide, obinutuzumab in place of rituximab. All negative. The one success was POLARIX, which swapped vincristine for the C-D-seventy-nine-b antibody-drug conjugate polatuzumab vedotin, and its hazard ratio was around zero point seven three. A real win, a modest effect. Epcoritamab just came in at zero point four nine. If that holds up in the full data, this is the largest progression-free survival effect anyone has shown in this disease's frontline setting.

The intuition for why a T cell engager might work here when the kinase inhibitors didn't is worth sitting with. Every relapsed-setting bispecific result you know was generated in patients whose T cells had already been beaten up by multiple lines of chemotherapy, sometimes a failed CAR T. Frontline is the first time you hand the engager a patient's least-exhausted T cell compartment, and you hand it alongside a regimen that is debulking the tumor at the same time. The early Phase one-two arm hinted at this: forty-seven high-risk patients, eighty-five percent complete responses, about eighty percent progression-free at two years. The Phase 3 says the hint was real. It's also a fixed-duration design, six cycles of the combination and two more of epcoritamab alone, which is the version of a bispecific community oncologists can actually adopt.

Now the opinionated part, because there are two things the press release does not tell you and one thing it quietly does. It gives no absolute progression-free survival rates, no overall survival, and no toxicity numbers, just "generally well tolerated and consistent with the individual agents." In a curable disease, the bar for adding cytokine release syndrome and infection risk to every newly diagnosed patient is higher than in relapse, so the safety table is going to carry as much weight as the hazard ratio.

The thing it quietly tells you is the control arm. This trial compared against plain R-CHOP. But in the United States, after POLARIX, many I-P-I two to five patients now get polatuzumab with R-CHP. So EPCORE D-L-B-C-L two beats the old standard, not the newer one. Roche knows this, which is exactly why its own frontline bispecific trial, glofitamab on top of Pola-R-CHP, roughly eleven hundred patients, is designed against Pola-R-CHP, with primary completion pencilled for late 2027. So the real fight in frontline D-L-B-C-L is now framed: Genmab and AbbVie have the first Phase 3 win and the bigger effect against an older comparator; Roche will have the harder comparator and a later answer. Expect a cross-trial argument about whether a zero point four nine against R-CHOP beats whatever Roche posts against Pola-R-CHP, and expect nobody to resolve it without a head-to-head.

For your own work, the takeaway is broader than lymphoma. This is the cleanest evidence yet that T cell engagers get dramatically better when you move them earlier and combine them with debulking cytotoxic therapy, rather than saving them for heavily pretreated patients. That logic transfers directly to the solid-tumor engagers and trispecifics now in dose escalation. Watch for the full data at a meeting, most likely ASH in December or next year, and look first at the safety table and the I-P-I two subgroup.
