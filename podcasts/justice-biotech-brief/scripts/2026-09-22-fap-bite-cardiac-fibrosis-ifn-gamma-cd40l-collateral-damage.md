# FAP BiTE — a T cell engager clears 95% of its target cells in the heart and makes the heart worse, and the fix is to strip the cytokines, not the binder

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.15.751626v1

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick today, from bioRxiv, posted yesterday out of Kory Lavine's lab at Washington University, with Jonathan Epstein and Joel Rurik from Penn on the author list and a team from Amgen Discovery Research who built the molecule. Here is the result. A F-A-P-directed bispecific T cell engager removed more than ninety-five percent of its target cells from the injured mouse heart, exactly as designed, and the hearts got worse. Bigger scar, more fibrosis, more chamber dilation, lower ejection fraction than control. This is a paper about a liability in your modality, and it is unusually clean about where the liability lives.

Start with the target, because they did the control experiment most papers skip. Fibroblast activation protein marks an activated cardiac fibroblast state that appears only after injury — they show that with a lineage reporter, nothing in the uninjured heart, a burst in the infarct by day eight that persists a month. Then they cross that reporter to a diphtheria toxin receptor line and simply delete those cells genetically after ischemia-reperfusion injury. Less fibrosis, smaller infarct, less dilation, better ejection fraction, no ruptures. So depleting F-A-P positive fibroblasts is a good idea. The target is validated before the drug ever shows up.

Then the drug shows up. Anti-mouse F-A-P on one arm, sub-nanomolar, anti-C-D-three epsilon on the other, dosed weekly starting two days after the injury, at a level chosen to hold trough concentrations above twenty times the in vitro E-C-ninety. It works as an executioner — over ninety-five percent target depletion in the myocardium. And the animals deteriorate.

The mechanism is the interesting part. Bulk sequencing of those hearts puts response to interferon gamma at the top of the upregulated pathway list, and here is the detail I would underline: it is local. They looked for interferon gamma in the periphery and did not find it. This is not systemic cytokine release syndrome. It is a tissue-confined cytokine field that plasma would never show you. Single-cell sequencing says T cells are the source, both C-D-four and C-D-eight expand in the infarct, the memory compartment marked by T-C-F-seven and C-C-R-seven is almost entirely gone, exhausted-phenotype C-D-eight cells appear, and the expanded C-D-four cells are enriched for C-D-forty ligand.

Now the move that makes this a design paper rather than a cautionary tale. They asked whether the killing actually requires those two pathways, and it does not. Neutralize interferon gamma in vitro, neutralize C-D-forty ligand, killing is unaffected. Block TRAIL and killing drops. Block Fas ligand and it does not. So cytotoxicity and the inflammatory program run through separable axes. In vivo they then give the engager together with antibodies against interferon gamma and C-D-forty ligand, and target cell killing is fully preserved while fibrosis, akinetic area, dilation and ejection fraction all improve — not just back to baseline, but past the control engager. The benefit that the genetic deletion promised was there the whole time, buried under the collateral signaling. And to nail causality to a single cell type, they deleted the interferon gamma receptor specifically from fibroblasts and got the same rescue.

What the interferon gamma was doing is the part I find genuinely unsettling. Killing the F-A-P positive fibroblasts did not just remove a bad cell. It specified a new one. Two fibroblast clusters appear only in engager-treated hearts, C-X-C-L-nine positive, periostin positive, F-A-P negative — so invisible to the drug that created them — sitting in the infarct on spatial transcriptomics, and terminal by velocity analysis. You deplete the activated fibroblast, and the cytokine that accompanies the depletion instructs a different fibroblast to become activated by a route that has no analog in the untreated infarct.

There is a second cell type in the loop, and it explains why C-D-forty ligand blockade helped at all. Subclustering the macrophages turns up an expanded C-X-C-L-nine positive population spread through the infarct, and that same population carries the highest C-D-forty. So the picture is a feed-forward circuit — the engager activates T cells against the fibroblast, the C-D-four cells license macrophages through C-D-forty, licensed macrophages push the T cells further toward a Th1 program, and the interferon gamma that falls out of all of it lands on fibroblasts. Ligand-receptor inference run with the fibroblasts as receiver puts T cell interferon gamma as the dominant incoming signal, which is consistent, and worth treating as consistent rather than as proof, since that is a prediction from expression data. The genetic receptor deletion is the experiment that makes it causal. Blocking interferon gamma alone did more than blocking C-D-forty ligand alone, which fits: C-D-forty ligand is the amplifier, interferon gamma is the effector.

Three things to take from this. First, for a T cell engager in a solid tissue, the off-target liability is not only where your antigen is expressed. It is what the killing reaction does to the neighbors — and the neighbors here were the disease. Second, the fix was not a better binder. Affinity was never the problem; the molecule did its job perfectly. The fix was decoupling the cytotoxic axis from the cytokine axis, which the authors reach chemically with two blocking antibodies and then suggest reaching structurally, by building engagers that recruit C-D-eight cells rather than the Th1-flavored C-D-four cells that carry most of the interferon gamma and C-D-forty ligand in an injured heart. That is a tractable engineering brief. Third, note what the same target did with a different modality — F-A-P C-A-R T cells reduced cardiac fibrosis in the papers Epstein and Rurik published in 2019 and 2022, and those authors are on this preprint. Same antigen, same cell killed, opposite outcome. They did not run the head-to-head, so the honest statement is that exposure profile, T cell subset and affinity all differ and we do not yet know which one carries the difference. Their own limitations section concedes both arms are very high affinity and the dosing was deliberately saturating.

The clinical translation of the rescue is not free, either. An anti-interferon gamma antibody exists and is approved in hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, so that half is available. C-D-forty ligand blockade carries a thromboembolism history from the first generation of those antibodies that the field is still engineering around. I would rather see the selectivity built into the engager.

Two markers to watch. Whether anyone running a stromal F-A-P engager in oncology looks at cardiac function in patients with a prior infarct, because injured human hearts carry this antigen and the clinical F-A-P effort in the heart today is a P-E-T tracer, not a therapeutic. And whether the next generation of tissue-directed engagers reports local cytokine fields rather than plasma cytokines — because in this paper, the blood was clean while the heart was on fire.
