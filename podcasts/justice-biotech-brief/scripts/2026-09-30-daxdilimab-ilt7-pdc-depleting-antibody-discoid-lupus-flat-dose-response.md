# Daxdilimab — an afucosylated antibody that deletes the interferon-producing cell wins in discoid lupus after losing in systemic lupus, and the flat dose-response is the tell

Paper link: https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/amgen-reveals-6-point-drop-lupus-severity-behind-phase-2-win-horizon-antibody

Trial link: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05591222

Proof-of-mechanism paper: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34039741/

Competitor trial link: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05531565

Failed systemic lupus trial: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04925934

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick today, and it is a news pick — an abstract that went up a few hours ago at the European dermatology congress. It is a dermatology readout on its face, but the reason I am spending your five minutes on it is that it is a clean, well-instrumented natural experiment in depleting-antibody design, and the design lesson transfers straight to the molecules you build.

Amgen finally put numbers behind its Phase 2 win for daxdilimab in discoid lupus. Daxdilimab is an afucosylated I-g-G-one antibody against I-L-T seven, and I-L-T seven is about as lineage-restricted as a cell-surface receptor gets — it sits essentially only on plasmacytoid dendritic cells, the cells that make the great majority of type one interferon in lupus. Afucosylated means the Fc glycan was stripped to enhance antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. So the drug does not modulate anything. It recruits natural killer cells and macrophages and it deletes the cell.

The trial was seventy-two patients with moderate-to-severe primary discoid lupus, all of them refractory to systemic therapy. Entry required a C-L-A-S-I activity score of at least eight attributable to discoid lesions, plus a centrally adjudicated photograph confirming an active lesion. Systemic lupus was an explicit exclusion — patients had to fail to meet the twenty-twenty classification criteria. Subcutaneous dosing every four weeks from day one through week twenty, two dose levels against placebo, primary endpoint the mean change in C-L-A-S-I activity at week twenty-four.

Both doses hit. Against placebo, the lower dose produced a six-point drop in C-L-A-S-I activity and the higher dose five point seven. Fifty percent or greater reduction in that score was reached by sixty-two percent of patients at each dose, against twenty-four percent on placebo. Clear or almost clear on the investigator's five-point global assessment — sixty percent at the low dose, fifty-two at the high dose, against nine percent on placebo. No serious adverse events, no deaths, one discontinuation in the high-dose arm for treatment-related joint pain. Remember the entry bar was a score of eight, so a six-point placebo-adjusted drop is most of the way back to the threshold these patients had to clear to enroll. And the placebo arm at nine percent on the global assessment gives you a very quiet control.

Now, four things, and the first one is the dose-response.

It is flat. Worse than flat — the lower dose beat the higher dose on two of the three endpoints. Read that as a disappointment and you have misread the mechanism. If your drug works by removing a cell, your efficacy ceiling is set by how completely you clear that cell, and once you saturate depletion, additional exposure buys you nothing. And we know that ceiling is reachable at modest exposure, because the Phase 1b was published in Science Translational Medicine back in twenty twenty-one and it measured the pharmacodynamics directly: a ninety-seven point six percent median reduction in plasmacytoid dendritic cells in lesional skin by day eighty-five, in cohorts dosed at fifty and a hundred fifty milligrams every four weeks. You cannot beat ninety-seven point six percent. So the useful generalization is this — for a depleting biologic, dose-ranging is a pharmacodynamic exercise, not an efficacy exercise. Find the dose that saturates clearance of the target cell in the tissue that matters, then stop, because everything above it is pure exposure and pure immunogenicity risk with no upside. That is the opposite of the logic you live with on engagers and conjugates, where the curve is real and the top of it is set by toxicity, not by saturation.

Second, I-L-T seven is being used as an address, not as a switch. And this is the part I find quietly elegant. I-L-T seven is an inhibitory receptor. Its natural ligand is B-S-T two, and physiological engagement of I-L-T seven shuts interferon production down — it is the brake on the very pathway this drug is trying to suppress. Amgen did not agonize it. They ignored its signaling biology entirely and used it as a zip code, then let an engineered Fc do all the work. The transferable point: when effector function is your mechanism, the signaling biology of your target can be irrelevant. What you are shopping for is lineage exclusivity and surface density, full stop. That is the same logic that makes C-D nineteen and C-D twenty good depletion targets, and it is worth remembering that a naked afucosylated antibody is a legitimate competitor to a cell therapy whenever the pathogenic cell is accessible.

Third, and this is the uncomfortable one. Go look at the registry and you will find that the trial that won is listed as terminated. Sponsor business decision, not related to safety, last updated in May, no results posted. Primary completion was May of last year. So the data have been sitting for about a year and a half, Amgen disclosed the primary-endpoint hit in a February earnings call without numbers, said last month it was taking steps toward registrational development, and the actual numbers surface today in a conference abstract. Now widen out and look at the whole program. Six registered trials. Systemic lupus, two hundred fourteen patients, completed and failed to beat placebo. The systemic lupus open-label extension, terminated on sponsor decision. Proliferative lupus nephritis, terminated on sponsor decision with nineteen patients enrolled. Dermatomyositis and anti-synthetase myositis, completed with twelve patients — too small to read out efficacy, and the company said so. Alopecia areata, a thirty-patient open-label Phase 2a. One win, and it is the narrowest indication in the set. That is what indication-hopping a mechanism actually looks like from the inside: the molecule was right and the organ was wrong five times.

And the reason the organ mattered is worth saying out loud, because it is a general point about endpoints. Discoid lupus is scored with a ruler on one organ. C-L-A-S-I activity is erythema and scale, lesion by lesion, on skin you can photograph and adjudicate centrally. Systemic lupus is scored with composites that average across organs — and a single-mechanism drug that dominates in skin gets diluted to nothing in an index that is also counting arthritis and serositis and complement. The indication was not a marketing choice. It was the only place the assay could see the mechanism.

Fourth, the competition, because there is a second antibody against the same cell running the opposite strategy. Biogen's litifilimab targets B-D-C-A two, which is also plasmacytoid-dendritic-cell-restricted, but litifilimab is not a depleter. It engages the receptor, drives internalization, and shuts down toll-like-receptor-seven and -nine-driven interferon production while leaving the cell alive. Same cell, opposite design philosophy — delete versus silence. And Biogen is ahead, by about two years and by an order of magnitude in operational scale. Breakthrough Therapy designation in cutaneous lupus in January. A positive Phase 2 portion of a seamless Phase 2-3 study reported at the American dermatology meeting in March, and the Phase 3 portion is running blinded right now with primary completion logged for three weeks from today — four hundred fifty patients across three hundred fifteen sites, with a readout expected next year. There is a separate Phase 3 long-term extension with another three hundred six patients. And two Phase 3 systemic lupus readouts are due in the fourth quarter. I want to be explicit that you cannot cross-compare the numbers — different endpoints, different timepoints, cutaneous lupus broadly versus discoid only, so anyone lining up the percentages is doing something invalid. But the strategic read is fair: if the depleter and the modulator land in the same indication with similar effect sizes, the whole differentiation argument collapses onto durability and safety, and a permanent-deletion mechanism has to carry the long-term infection and interferon-deficiency question that a reversible one does not.

Now the discount rate, and it is a real one. Seventy-two patients. An abstract, not a paper, and not peer-reviewed. No results posted on the registry, so you cannot read the tables yourself. And here is the operational number I would actually worry about if I were running this program: sixty-eight sites across twelve countries took two and a half years to enroll those seventy-two patients. Set that next to four hundred fifty patients at three hundred fifteen sites on the other side of the field. A registrational trial in refractory primary discoid lupus is a genuinely hard recruitment problem, and Amgen would be starting it while Biogen finishes.

The read-across for your work is the first point, and I will sharpen it. When your mechanism is cell removal, your potency conversation is over as soon as the pharmacodynamics saturate, and after that the only remaining variables are completeness in the tissue, persistence, and what you do about the compartment you just emptied. Measure the depletion in the tissue, not in the blood — the twenty twenty-one paper made its case on lesional skin biopsies, and that is why the flat dose-response today reads as confirmation rather than as noise.
