# UCHT1 — the CD3 clone behind half the T cell engager field turns out to dimerize on binding, so the "monovalent" CD3 arm may be a valency effect rather than an affinity one

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.08.757522v2

## Script

Today's pick is a cryo-EM structure paper that went up on bioRxiv two days ago from Longfei Wang's group at Wuhan University, with Di Wu and Zezhi Li as co-corresponding authors and Hao Wu's lab at Harvard Medical School and Boston Children's on the author list. It is nominally about how the gamma-delta T cell receptor gets switched on. I am leading with it because buried in the middle of it is a result about UCHT1 — the anti-CD3-epsilon clone that a large fraction of the clinical T cell engager field is built on — that should change how you think about what the CD3 arm of an engager actually does.

Start with the setup. They expressed the full V-gamma-nine V-delta-two receptor complex, the dominant gamma-delta receptor in human peripheral blood, and solved it at three-point-three angstroms in the resting state. The architecture is close to the alpha-beta receptor — one receptor heterodimer plus CD3-delta-epsilon, CD3-gamma-epsilon and the CD3-zeta-zeta homodimer — which already settles an old argument, because people had speculated the CD3 geometry differed between the two receptor classes and it essentially does not. What is different is that the antigen-binding domains of the gamma-delta chains are so flexible they do not resolve at all. That fits what the receptor has to do, which is recognize a structurally promiscuous set of ligands without an M-H-C to hold them in a fixed presentation geometry.

The second resting-state observation is the one worth filing away. They see ordered lipid density in the inner leaflet — two cholesterols in defined pockets and three phospholipids — and they sit wedged between the CD3-zeta-zeta dimer and everything else in the complex. Untargeted lipidomics on the purified receptor confirms it, with cholesterol and phosphatidylethanolamine both markedly enriched relative to whole-cell lysate. So the signaling subunit carrying the highest-value I-T-A-M payload is physically walled off from its partners by a lipid barrier in the resting receptor. The authors read this as an anti-autoactivation device, and when they stimulate the receptor, the cholesterol and the phosphatidylethanolamine species drop. Tonic signaling in a chimeric receptor is usually discussed as a protein problem — hinge length, framework packing, self-association of the binder. This says there is a lipid-level safety catch in the natural receptor that nobody is designing for.

Now the part that matters for engagers. UCHT1 and its Fab fragment are known agonists of gamma-delta T cells — they drive calcium flux, proximal phosphorylation, and tumor killing. OKT3, binding the same subunit, mostly does not. The field has generally explained differences between CD3 clones by epitope and by affinity. So they overlaid the two. OKT3 and UCHT1 engage CD3-epsilon in essentially the same way. The binding mode is not the explanation.

What they found instead is that the UCHT1 Fab dimerizes. Free Fab in solution is a clean monomer by mass photometry, native mass spectrometry and size-exclusion. But on binding the receptor it pairs up, and the cryo-EM shows two-to-two tetramers — two Fabs bridging two complete receptor complexes — through three distinct interfaces, all in the variable domains. One is an antiparallel beta-sheet formed by the same light-chain strand from each copy, across a two-fold axis; the other two are symmetric hydrogen-bond networks between the light-chain N-terminus and the heavy chain. The receptors in these tetramers sit about thirty-nine angstroms apart, and the assemblies are visibly curved, which the authors suggest means Fab binding at a membrane generates mechanical force.

Then they do the experiment that makes this more than a structure. They made recombinant Fabs with point mutations designed to break each interface, and one designed to lock the dimer with an engineered intermolecular disulfide. Every dimer-disrupting mutant loses activity — the worst of them, a triple charge-and-beta-sheet-destabilizing mutant, and a variant with the whole N-terminal region replaced, essentially flatten the response. A bioluminescence-resonance-energy-transfer readout in receptor-expressing Jurkat cells shows the same rank order for receptor clustering, so the signaling loss tracks the clustering loss. And the disulfide-locked dimer out-performs wild-type Fab on both clustering and CD69 upregulation. Enforced dimerization beats spontaneous dimerization.

Here is the translational read, and it is a measurement problem before it is a design problem. The dimer-disrupting mutants did not just lose potency — they showed increased dissociation constants on cells. Which means the apparent affinity of UCHT1 measured in a cellular format is partly avidity contributed by the Fab pairing with itself on the surface. If you have been ranking candidate CD3 binders by cell-based K-D and picking a "low-affinity CD3 arm" to tune down cytokine release, and your binder self-associates when it is concentrated at a synapse, you have not measured what you think you measured. Worse, a CD3 arm with latent self-association gives you antigen-independent clustering — the engager does not need a tumor cell to start crosslinking receptors. That is a tonic-signaling and C-R-S liability sitting inside a component everyone treats as the inert half of the molecule. Run the orthogonal check: mass photometry or native mass spec on your CD3 arm in isolation will miss it, because free UCHT1 Fab looks monomeric. You need the receptor present.

The honest caveats. This is the gamma-delta receptor, not alpha-beta, and the recent framing is that gamma-delta relies on clustering-driven analog activation while alpha-beta also has a digital mode — so the read-across is strongest for the gamma-delta engager programs and only suggestive for conventional CD3 engagers, though CD3-epsilon is the shared subunit and the epitope is the same. Every functional readout here is CD69 upregulation in a Jurkat line plus the B-R-E-T proximity assay. There is no killing assay with primary T cells, and crucially there is no actual bispecific — nobody tethered this Fab to a tumor-targeting arm and asked whether the dimerization survives being built into a real engager format, where geometry and linker constraints may forbid exactly the pairing they see. There is also a single-residue discrepancy between their UCHT1 sequence and the published one, which they handle by testing both.

The transferable point is the one about valency. The authors connect their Fab result to the natural ligand, where the butyrophilin complex presents two spatially separate receptor binding sites and crosslinks two receptors at once — so the engineered and the physiological agonists converge on the same mechanism, which is clustering. For a field that has spent a decade tuning CD3 affinity as the lever for the therapeutic window, this is an argument that valency is the variable doing the work, and that affinity has been partly a proxy for it all along.
