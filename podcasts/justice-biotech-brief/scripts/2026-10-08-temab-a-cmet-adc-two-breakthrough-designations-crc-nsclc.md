# Temab-A — c-Met topoisomerase ADC wins two FDA breakthrough designations, biomarker-selected in lung but not in colorectal

Paper link: https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/abbvies-telisotuzumab-adizutecan-temab-a-receives-two-breakthrough-therapy-designations-from-the-u-s-fda-for-crc-and-nsclc
Paper link: https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-25-01525

## Script

Yesterday the FDA gave AbbVie two Breakthrough Therapy Designations for one antibody-drug conjugate: telisotuzumab adizutecan, which AbbVie shortens to Temab-A. It's a c-Met-directed antibody carrying a topoisomerase-one inhibitor payload at a drug-to-antibody ratio of six. One designation is in refractory metastatic colorectal cancer, in combination with bevacizumab. The other is monotherapy in previously treated, EGFR wild-type, non-squamous lung cancer. A breakthrough designation is a regulatory label, not data. But read the two side by side and they show a split in how AbbVie is thinking about its target. The lung designation is restricted to c-Met protein-expressing tumors. The colorectal one doesn't mention c-Met at all.

Start with the antibody, because you've seen it before. Telisotuzumab is the same antibody inside Emrelis, telisotuzumab vedotin, which got accelerated approval in May 2025. That version carried a microtubule-inhibitor payload, and it only worked well enough to be approved in high c-Met overexpressors: at least half of tumor cells staining three-plus. Response rate was thirty-five percent in that selected group. Temab-A keeps the antibody and swaps in a topoisomerase-one inhibitor, the payload class that, in drugs like Enhertu, leaks out of the target cell and kills its neighbors. The swap is a bet that this bystander effect, plus six payloads per antibody, turns c-Met from a target that only works when it's dense into one that works when it's simply present. In lung, the early numbers support the bet: a forty-eight percent response rate across all c-Met expression levels in EGFR wild-type disease, which, across trials and with all the caveats that implies, beats what the approved drug managed in its hand-picked patients.

Colorectal is where it gets interesting, and the Journal of Clinical Oncology paper from the first-in-human study is the thing to read. A hundred and twenty-two heavily pretreated patients got monotherapy, nearly all of them already exposed to irinotecan, a topoisomerase-one inhibitor. Overall response rate was fifteen point six percent. Then look at the biomarker figure. c-Met is close to universal in colorectal cancer: ninety-five percent of samples had at least ninety percent of tumor cells staining at some intensity. So presence is a given. Density isn't. Among patients dosed at two point four milligrams per kilogram or higher, those with at least ten percent of cells at three-plus responded thirty-six percent of the time. Those below that cutoff responded twelve percent of the time. That's a threefold gradient, from the same drug in the same disease. The bystander payload widened the window, but it didn't flatten the curve.

Now look at what AbbVie did with that. The monotherapy colorectal study that followed enrolls only patients above a c-Met cutoff. The registrational path, though, is the bevacizumab combination, and it's unselected. At ESMO last year, Temab-A plus bevacizumab in biomarker-unselected third-line-plus patients gave a twenty-six point seven percent response rate in thirty patients. The randomized comparator, trifluridine-tipiracil plus bevacizumab, gave zero percent in twenty, with grade three-or-higher adverse events about equal: sixty-seven versus sixty-five percent. The Phase 3, AndroMETa-CRC, started in May with about nine hundred and ninety patients, the same two arms, and no c-Met gate in its title or listed criteria. The lung Phase 3, by contrast, is explicitly restricted to patients "expressing c-Met protein above a defined cutoff."

My read is that the colorectal design is a commercial decision dressed as a biological one. An all-comers label in third-line colorectal is a far bigger market than a thirty-percent-of-patients label. The bet is that bevacizumab, plausibly by normalizing tumor vasculature and getting more conjugate into the tumor, lifts the low-expressers enough that selection isn't needed. That might be true. But nothing public yet shows it, because the combination data haven't been reported split by c-Met level. That split is the number I'd want to see first.

Also look closely at the comparator. Trifluridine-tipiracil plus bevacizumab is a cytostatic regimen. It extends survival, to a median of about ten point eight months in its own registration trial, while almost never shrinking tumors. Beating it on response rate, zero versus twenty-seven, says almost nothing about benefit. The Phase 3 has two primary endpoints, response by blinded central review and overall survival. Response is close to a foregone conclusion. Overall survival is the real test. The monotherapy median survival in the JCO paper was ten point four months, essentially the comparator's number.

There's also a safety cost to keep in view. At the selected two point four milligram dose, grade three-plus anemia hit forty percent of patients and grade three-plus neutropenia twenty-eight percent. Adjudicated interstitial lung disease ran six percent across all doses. All four treatment-related deaths happened at three milligrams per kilogram or above, which is part of why AbbVie stepped down to two point four.

For your own ADC work, take this away. A bystander payload buys you a lower expression threshold, not freedom from one. When a developer drops the biomarker gate in exactly the indication where its own data show the steepest density gradient, that's a business choice, and the overall survival readout will tell you whether the biology agreed.
