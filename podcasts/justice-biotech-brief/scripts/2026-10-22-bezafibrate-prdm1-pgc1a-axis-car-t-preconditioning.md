# Bezafibrate — a decades-old lipid drug reproduces a Blimp-1 knockout's benefit in CAR T, added only during manufacturing

Paper link: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.15.759372v1

## Script

Good morning, Justice. One pick today, out of Justin Kline's group at the University of Chicago, posted to bioRxiv yesterday. On the label it's a Blimp-1 knockout paper, and if that were all it was I'd skip it, because Blimp-1 knockout in CAR T cells has been done twice already. The reason it's worth five minutes is the last figure, where they take the whole knockout phenotype and reproduce most of it with a lipid-lowering drug that's been in clinical use for decades, added to the culture flask during manufacturing and then washed out.

Start with the human data, because that's the part that justifies the rest. Blimp-1, encoded by the gene PRDM1, is a transcriptional repressor that enforces terminal differentiation in CD8 T cells — it shuts down interleukin-2, the interleukin-2 receptor alpha chain, CD27, ID3, TCF1. All of that was established in infection models years ago. What's new here is where they looked for it. In single-cell data from two hundred seventeen lymphoma patients, PRDM1 tracked the exhaustion score across CD8 clusters, highest in the most exhausted and lowest in naive. Fine, expected. Then in biopsies from large B cell lymphoma patients treated with axicabtagene ciloleucel, the CAR-positive clusters carrying exhaustion features had the same signature. Also expected.

The one that matters is the third. In an independent cohort, they measured PRDM1 in the pre-infusion product — in the bag, before the patient ever saw it — and clinical non-responders had significantly higher PRDM1 transcript and correspondingly lower output from the genes Blimp-1 represses. So this isn't only a consequence of chronic antigen exposure in the tumor. Something about the patient's starting material, or how that material responds to a standardized activation and expansion protocol, imprints a higher Blimp-1 setpoint before infusion, and that setpoint tracks with whether the product works. For a group that thinks about manufacturing, that's the sentence to take away: the differentiation decision that predicts failure may already have been made by the time you cryopreserve.

The knockout experiments are competent and mostly confirmatory. CRISPR deletion in CD19 CAR T cells, built on both four-1BB and CD28 costimulatory domains, which is the right control to run because those two platforms diverge under chronic stimulation. Serial restimulation gave more proliferative capacity, enrichment of stem-cell-memory and central-memory subsets, higher cytokine output at both the start and the end of the challenge. In vivo, survival benefit in a disseminated DLBCL xenograft on both costim domains, and then a HER2-directed CAR against an orthotopic breast tumor, where the knockout improved not just peripheral expansion but infiltration into the tumor. Worth noting against the prior literature: one earlier group found that deleting PRDM1 alone triggered a compensatory NR4A3 program and that you needed the double knockout to get efficacy. Here single knockout was enough, in both a liquid and a solid model. The honest read is that the compensation is context-dependent — tumor, antigen density, CAR architecture — not that anyone was wrong.

Then the mechanism, which is where the paper earns its keep. Bulk sequencing on restimulated cells converged, across both costim domains, on mitochondrial, metabolic and antioxidant programs. The functional follow-up is good: knockout cells held lower mitochondrial superoxide at baseline and under antimycin A challenge, carried more mitochondrial mass by both staining and electron microscopy, and had higher spare respiratory capacity on Seahorse. The upstream node they land on is PGC one alpha, the coactivator that drives mitochondrial biogenesis, which goes up at the protein level when Blimp-1 is gone — consistent with older work showing Blimp-1 sits on the PGC one alpha promoter.

And then they do the experiment that most papers skip. They knocked PGC one alpha down with shRNA on top of the Blimp-1 knockout. In vivo, that completely abrogated the knockout's therapeutic benefit, while the same knockdown in control CAR T cells still produced a survival benefit over untreated. That's a clean epistasis result. The benefit of losing Blimp-1 runs through PGC one alpha specifically — it isn't a generic fitness gain that any metabolic insult would blunt.

Which sets up the translation. Bezafibrate is a pan-PPAR agonist, a decades-old lipid-lowering drug, and PPAR activation recruits PGC one alpha. They ran it two ways. Systemically, two and a half milligrams per kilogram every three days alongside the cells, which improved survival on both costim domains with no tumoricidal effect on its own. But PGC one alpha has oncogenic activity in some tumor types, so dosing it into a patient is a real risk, and they clearly knew that. So they ran the better version: thirty micromolar bezafibrate added after transduction and replenished through expansion, then the cells go in without the drug. That was sufficient to raise canonical PGC one alpha target genes and to improve survival in the lymphoma model and expansion and tumor control in the breast model, across donors with different baseline potency.

Three caveats keep this honest. These are all NSG xenografts, so there's no host immunity and none of the metabolic hostility a real tumor microenvironment imposes — the authors say so and flag humanized models as next. The survival curves run four to six mice per group, which is thin for a Kaplan-Meier claim. And bezafibrate is not a PGC one alpha agonist; it's a pan-PPAR agonist two steps upstream, so calling this a phenocopy of the knockout rests on target genes and tumor outcomes, not on a matched transcriptome. There's also no head-to-head of preconditioning against the knockout, so how much of the effect is actually recovered is unquantified.

Still, the idea is the right kind of cheap. Nearly every proposal to fix CAR T exhaustion adds an editing step, a regulatory conversation, and a manufacturing validation. This one is a media additive in the expansion phase, with decades of human safety behind the molecule, and a candidate release assay — Blimp-1 message in the product — sitting right next to it.
