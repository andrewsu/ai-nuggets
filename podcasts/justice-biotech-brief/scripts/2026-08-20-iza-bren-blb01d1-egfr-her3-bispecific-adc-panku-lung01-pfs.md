# Iza-bren (BL-B01D1) — EGFR×HER3 bispecific ADC becomes the first bispecific ADC to hit a Phase 3 primary endpoint in lung cancer, and it hit the easy one

Paper link: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06382116
Paper link: https://ea-cdn.eurolandir.com/press-releases-attachments./4167797/688506_20260818_XHCA.pdf

## Script

On Tuesday, Sichuan Baili Tianheng — Biokin, the Chengdu company behind the most talked-about antibody-drug conjugate in China — filed a voluntary disclosure with the Shanghai exchange saying that iza-bren, its E-G-F-R-by-HER-three bispecific A-D-C, met the progression-free survival primary endpoint at a prespecified interim analysis of a Phase 3 trial in non-small-cell lung cancer. The independent data monitoring committee made the call. On overall survival, the filing says only that a trend toward benefit was observed. The company frames this as the fourth Phase 3 this molecule has won, and as the first Phase 3 anywhere in which a bispecific A-D-C has met its primary endpoint in lung cancer. Bristol Myers Squibb, which paid eight hundred million dollars up front and committed up to eight-point-four billion for rights outside China, has a lot riding on that framing.

Start with why the molecule is worth your attention, because the design logic here is genuinely different from the bispecifics we've been talking about all month. Volrustomig, ivonescimab, pumitamig — in those, the second arm adds pharmacology. You're blocking a second pathway, or you're using one arm as an address label to make a toxic mechanism selective. Iza-bren isn't doing that. It's an anti-E-G-F-R IgG-one with anti-HER-three single-chain fragments fused on through glycine-serine linkers, carrying a camptothecin-derived topoisomerase-one payload on a cleavable tetrapeptide linker at a drug-to-antibody ratio of about eight. Neither arm is there to shut a pathway down. Both arms are there to get the warhead inside the cell. Engage two receptors on the same membrane and you get avidity and receptor crosslinking, which drives internalization and lysosomal trafficking harder than either arm alone would — so you can deliver a meaningful payload dose to a tumor where each individual antigen is only modestly expressed. The second arm is a delivery amplifier, not a second mechanism.

And the pairing is not arbitrary, which is the part I'd underline. E-G-F-R is abundant on these tumors but also on normal skin and gut epithelium, so E-G-F-R alone buys you uptake without much selectivity. HER-three is the interesting half: it gets upregulated as a resistance response after E-G-F-R tyrosine kinase inhibition. So in exactly the population this trial enrolled — patients who have progressed on a third-generation E-G-F-R inhibitor — the second antigen is enriched by the prior therapy. The drug is designed to exploit the resistance state rather than fight it.

Now the trial, and then the reason I'd hold my applause. Four hundred thirty-two patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous lung cancer carrying classical E-G-F-R mutations, all of whom had progressed on a third-generation inhibitor and — this matters — had received no systemic therapy other than E-G-F-R inhibitors. Randomized against pemetrexed plus cisplatin or carboplatin. Progression-free survival by blinded independent review was the sole primary endpoint. Overall survival is a secondary endpoint.

Here is why that distinction is the whole story. This exact trial has been run before, and it won and then lost. Daiichi Sankyo and Merck's patritumab deruxtecan — HER-three-D-X-d, a single-target HER-three A-D-C with a topoisomerase-one payload, in post-third-generation-inhibitor E-G-F-R-mutant lung cancer, against pemetrexed and platinum. Same slot, same comparator, same logic. In September 2024 they announced that it hit progression-free survival with statistical significance. At ASCO last year we got the numbers: median five-point-eight months versus five-point-four, hazard ratio zero-point-seven-seven. Statistically real, four-tenths of a month of median separation. And then overall survival came in at sixteen-point-zero months versus fifteen-point-nine, hazard ratio zero-point-nine-eight. Flat. Three days before that presentation, the companies voluntarily withdrew the U.S. application. So we already know, from a well-run global Phase 3, that beating platinum doublet on progression-free survival in this setting does not establish that you are helping anyone live longer. Biokin disclosed no hazard ratio, no medians, no event counts, no data cutoff, and no safety data. The number that would distinguish this readout from the last one is precisely the number that wasn't in the filing.

There is a real case for optimism, and it's about magnitude. At the World Conference on Lung Cancer last year, a Phase 2 subgroup of chemotherapy-naive patients who had failed a third-generation inhibitor — essentially this trial's population — showed a median progression-free survival of twelve-point-five months on iza-bren. That's not four-tenths of a month better than chemotherapy, that's roughly double what HER-three-D-X-d achieved. Discount it heavily: single-arm, Phase 2, Chinese population, and cross-trial comparison is the oldest way to fool yourself in oncology. But it's the right shape of prior. If the true effect size is anywhere near that, overall survival has room to follow rather than collapse. If it's a hazard ratio in the high seventies again, this readout means about as much as the last one did.

Two more things worth filing. First, the caveat nobody has addressed: dual E-G-F-R and HER-three targeting with a topoisomerase-one payload at a drug-to-antibody ratio of eight is not an obviously gentle combination, and the exclusion criteria tell you what the sponsors were worried about — no prior topoisomerase-one A-D-C, no history of interstitial lung disease requiring steroids, no active pneumonitis. Interstitial lung disease is the class problem for deruxtecan-type payloads, and a trial that discloses efficacy with no safety data is telling you about half of what you need. Second, and this is where it touches your V-E-G-F work directly: Biokin is starting a China Phase 3 called Panku-Lung-oh-five that combines iza-bren with a P-D-1-by-V-E-G-F bispecific antibody in first-line squamous lung cancer. Bispecific A-D-C plus bispecific checkpoint-plus-anti-angiogenic, in the front line. Between the two companies there are now roughly twenty Phase 3 or Phase 2/3 trials running on this one molecule, plus a global first-line study pairing it with osimertinib. The drug is already approved in China in nasopharyngeal and esophageal squamous carcinoma and under review for triple-negative breast cancer.

So the honest summary is that a bispecific A-D-C has now cleared a Phase 3 bar in lung cancer that no bispecific A-D-C had cleared before, which is a real platform milestone for the format — dual targeting for delivery works well enough to move a randomized endpoint. It is also, specifically, the bar that the last HER-three A-D-C cleared on its way to being withdrawn. Watch for the hazard ratio and the survival curves, and don't reprice the eight-point-four-billion-dollar bet until you see them.
