# Portfolio Watch — 2026-07-27

## Script

This is Portfolio Watch for Monday, July 27th, and I'll be straight with you up top: it's a quiet week for hard portfolio catalysts — no Blackbird company printed news this window. So today is a status read on the two fronts that are actually moving a thesis. The first is the non-dopaminergic schizophrenia market that our Lieber Institute and Third Rock program is walking into. The second is a fresh genetic-medicine Phase 3 that de-risks the RNA modality we keep betting on.

Start with schizophrenia, because that's where the picture is getting more legible. The competitor to keep your eye on is still Nxera's lead G-P-R fifty-two agonist — the only other Phase-2-ready G-P-R fifty-two program in the industry. Quick recap: Boehringer had an option on it, and back in December Boehringer walked away. Nxera has been shopping the program ever since, with a stated plan to out-license it to a big pharma or a specialty-neuroscience buyer this year. Here's the status as of today: no buyer has surfaced. Read that carefully. The direct, same-mechanism rival to our program still hasn't found a funded home — which is a modest reprieve on the timing pressure we flagged two weeks ago. Our Lieber NewCo is not, right now, racing a fully-resourced Phase 2 competitor. But it stays a live watch item, because if Otsuka, or Bristol Myers through Karuna, or Neurocrine, or AbbVie through Cerevel picks it up this quarter, the clock resets and we're suddenly 12-to-24 months behind a same-target rival that's already through Phase 1.

Now the harder truth, and it comes from the broader non-dopaminergic wave. Novelty does not automatically convert into a market. Cobenfy — the first non-dopaminergic antipsychotic, the muscarinic drug from Bristol Myers — has had a gradual launch, not a blockbuster ramp, and its study as an add-on to standard atypical antipsychotics missed. Meanwhile Neurocrine's M-four-selective agonist is moving through Phase 3. So the lesson for our program is direct: being a novel mechanism is not the moat. When our development candidate declaration comes later this year, the value case can't lean on first-in-class-ness. It has to be best-in-class G-P-R fifty-two chemistry that credibly reaches all three symptom domains — positive, negative, and cognitive — backed by a real biomarker and pharmacodynamic package. And to echo the point Martinowich made from inside the Lieber Institute a couple of weeks ago: pair that neuronal-engagement readout with a multicellular tissue-state readout, because the disease isn't purely neuronal. So the action stands for Hemaka and the Third Rock team — the differentiation conversation is the whole ballgame, and the market is telling us the bar is head-to-head-grade proof, not a new receptor.

The second thread is a genetic-medicine Phase 3 that landed on July 22nd. Arrowhead reported topline results from two studies — SHASTA-3 and SHASTA-4 — of plozasiran, brand name Redemplo, in severe hypertriglyceridemia. Both trials hit every endpoint. Triglycerides down as much as 81 percent, acute pancreatitis events down 78 percent overall and 100 percent in the highest-risk patients, clean safety, and dosing is a single subcutaneous shot once every three months. Arrowhead plans an FDA filing before year-end, with the full data at the European cardiology congress at the end of August. Now — this doesn't touch a Blackbird company directly, so why is it on our watch? Because plozasiran is a GalNAc-conjugated siRNA — the liver-directed RNAi playbook. It's another RNAi asset marching to approval, and it de-risks the modality two of our threads live in: Aletira's cell-selective genetic medicines, and the RNA-upregulation platform. More specifically, it's direct validation of the once-quarterly, liver-directed GalNAc-siRNA route that the h-L-M-R-one liver-long-noncoding-RNA sourcing thesis is predicated on — that's the Ruan lab target out of Johns Hopkins we ranked in Sunday's radar. When you're deciding whether to found a company around a knock-this-down liver target, watching yet another quarterly GalNAc-siRNA clear Phase 3 with a clean safety read is exactly the de-risking datapoint you want in front of you. Call it a tailwind, not a threat.

And a quick housekeeping note on aSKY, since it was the whole story on Sunday: no new catalyst since then. The map stands — Revolution Medicines' RAS drug daraxonrasib accepted for FDA review in pancreatic cancer, GSK's zidesamtinib approved in ROS-one lung cancer, the sibling A-L-K drug still under review. Nothing to act on today beyond the competitive-map refresh already on Eddie's list.

So that's the watch. No portfolio-company news, but two things worth the team's attention: the G-P-R fifty-two differentiation clock, where the market is quietly raising the bar even as our nearest rival stays unsold, and a genetic-medicine tailwind that keeps validating the RNAi lane we're sourcing into. Talk tomorrow.
