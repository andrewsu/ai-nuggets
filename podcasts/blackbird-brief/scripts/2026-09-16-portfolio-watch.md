QurCan Therapeutics, a Canadian company operating out of the BioLabs incubator in Toronto, signed an exclusive research and collaboration agreement with Lilly covering multiple programs in central and peripheral nervous system disease. QurCan gets an undisclosed upfront, a strategic equity investment from Lilly, research support, up to two hundred thirty-seven million dollars per program in milestones, and tiered royalties. Lilly leads research, clinical development and commercialization.

The load-bearing fact is what QurCan sells. Their platform is a polymer-lipid nanoparticle built for systemic, non-viral, repeat-dosable delivery of nucleic acid payloads — messenger R-N-A, D-N-A, antisense oligonucleotides, small interfering R-N-A. There is a central nervous system variant designed to cross the blood-brain barrier. The company's own framing is explicit about the point: delivering a genetic medicine directly into the brain or the cerebrospinal fluid is invasive, and systemic dosing would give you broad distribution across deep brain regions without that.

Now set it beside what Lilly already owns. In June of two thousand twenty-four, Lilly took an exclusive global license from QurAlis to a splice-switching antisense oligonucleotide that restores UNC13A function in A-L-S and frontotemporal dementia — forty-five million dollars upfront and up to five hundred seventy-seven million in milestones. QurAlis has been on NuGate's watch list since we opened the file.

So one company now holds a splice-switching oligonucleotide in exactly NuGate's indication and exactly NuGate's intellectual estate, and has just bought a systemic, repeat-dosable route into the brain to go with it. The rule we wrote on August thirty-first was that if systemically dosed central nervous system oligonucleotides arrive inside NuGate's development window, the comparator for an intrathecal antisense drug stops being no treatment and becomes a subcutaneous injection. Two weeks later, both halves of that sentence are sitting in one portfolio.

Two candid limits. QurCan is research-stage — no named molecule, no I-N-D, no clinical data — and the blood-brain-barrier claim is preclinical, which is the single largest graveyard in neuro delivery. What Lilly bought is an option, and it is priced like one. The mechanism is also undisclosed, so nobody outside Lilly can assess it. The honest read is that the route comparator is not here yet, but the buyer with the most to gain has now paid to build it. Jon, convert that watch from passive to a named calendar item.

The second read is Eddie's and Emily's, and it is about price. This is the fourth time in one week a bolt-on delivery-or-selectivity layer got valued in public. Which brings me to the harder NuGate item, because it landed the same day and it cuts against us.

Axoltis Pharma reported a Phase two in A-L-S where the primary endpoint was neurofilament light. Their drug is a synthetic twelve-amino-acid peptide derived from a brain protein called SCO-spondin, pitched as restoring blood-brain-barrier function and protecting neurons. Eighty-two patients in France, intravenous drug at five or ten milligrams per kilogram against placebo, three times weekly for four weeks, follow-up to ten months. The primary endpoint was change at week six in neurofilament light and in the albumin quotient, a measure of barrier integrity. Neither moved.

What the company led with instead is a post-hoc functional analysis. At week ten, monthly decline on the A-L-S functional rating scale was zero point seven seven points at the low dose, one point zero six at the high dose, and one point five seven on placebo — a fifty-one percent reduction versus placebo at the low dose. There was also a significant drop in plasma claudin-five at the high dose. Fuller data goes to the Society for Neuroscience meeting on November fifteenth.

Three things, Jon and Avi. Four weeks of dosing is almost certainly too short to bend a neurofilament curve, so this is weak evidence about the biomarker and strong evidence about trial design. But look at the shape of the claim. The dose that won on function is not the dose that won on the target-engagement marker — function at five milligrams per kilogram, claudin-five at ten. An inverse relationship across two readouts, found after the fact, in roughly twenty-seven patients per arm. That is the Angelman shape from September third in miniature, and a fifty-one percent post-hoc functional claim on those numbers gets discounted to zero in diligence.

The third point is the one that protects our own package. On September sixth we wrote the causal-versus-correlated rule off the pelacarsen failure: tofersen reached the market on neurofilament because SOD1 A-L-S has a monogenic causal chain. A barrier-repair peptide has no causal claim on neurofilament on a four-week clock. So this does not falsify neurofilament as NuGate's evidentiary spine. It demonstrates what happens when you make a biomarker your primary endpoint without owning a causal argument for it. Keep the spine, and write the causal paragraph before the biomarker plan.

Moving to the COPD theme, where there is a new differentiator on the board and a readout landing inside two weeks.

Connect Biopharma reported topline data on rademikibart, a monoclonal antibody against interleukin-four receptor alpha — the Dupixent receptor. One hundred sixty patients, adults and adolescents with asthma and type-two inflammation, eosinophils at or above three hundred cells per microlitre, all of them presenting with an acute exacerbation. A single six-hundred-milligram subcutaneous dose on top of standard of care.

The primary endpoint was treatment failure within twenty-eight days — death, hospital admission, an emergency visit, an unscheduled visit for worsening symptoms, or treatment intensification. Rademikibart cut the failure rate by sixty-six percent, at a p-value of zero point one five. Not significant. The company's explanation is that the overall failure rate came in lower than projected. On secondaries, lung function at week one improved by two hundred fifty millilitres against one hundred twenty on placebo — a hundred-and-thirty-millilitre difference that did clear significance. Emergency and unscheduled visits fell by half. Safety was clean.

And it just failed to register on the endpoint that defines the setting. This is the instrument rule from September ninth in a different disease.
