This is your Portfolio Watch for Friday, July thirty-first. And I'll be straight with the team: the direct-catalyst tape is quiet again. So today is a short one, built the honest way — anchored on the single near-term event that actually reprices a portfolio thesis, and then a tight status pass on the rest.

That one event is next Tuesday, August fifth, when Revolution Medicines reports its second-quarter results. On its own, another biotech's earnings call isn't news. But Revolution Medicines is the company setting the bar in the exact space aSKY has to define itself against — mutant-RAS oncology — so this call is the most useful thing on the calendar for us right now, and it's worth telling you what to listen for and why.

Here's the setup. Revolution Medicines runs the leading small-molecule RAS-on franchise. Their lead drug, daraxonrasib, is a multi-selective RAS-on inhibitor, and just over a week ago the FDA accepted its new drug application in previously-treated metastatic pancreatic cancer — into the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot, which can pull a review forward. That filing rests on a phase 3 pancreatic readout that was, frankly, unprecedented for the disease — median overall survival roughly doubled versus chemotherapy. Behind it they have a G-twelve-D-selective drug, zoldonrasib, showing better-than-fifty-percent response rates in second-line KRAS G-twelve-D non-small-cell lung cancer with a breakthrough designation, and a broader phase 3 program that reaches into lung cancer. So the whole mutant-RAS field is de-risking fast, and it's de-risking at the regulatory level, not just in a poster.

Why that matters for aSKY: aSKY's disclosed frame is a KRAS-directed program built on antibody engineering, through the AVS Bio partnership — a fundamentally different modality from Revolution Medicines' small molecules, and the specific mechanism still isn't public. A different modality is exactly how you differentiate in a crowded target space, but only if the story is sharp. And every de-risking datapoint out of Revolution Medicines raises the bar aSKY's eventual disclosure has to clear — it makes the undisclosed-mechanism gap a little more expensive each quarter. So the ask is simple: Eddie and the aSKY team should treat Tuesday's call as a competitive-map input. Listen for the pancreatic review timeline under the priority voucher, for the pace and combination plans in non-small-cell lung cancer, and for how aggressively they're moving into first line. That's the landscape aSKY is being measured against, and it's moving.
