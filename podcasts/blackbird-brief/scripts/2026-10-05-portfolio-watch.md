## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Monday, October fifth. The most important development for the portfolio is that the case against cell therapy in autoimmune disease is now being made in public by cell-therapy executives — and the mechanism they are blaming is the one our own open research item named a month ago.

Start there, because it bears directly on Winnow. The underlying events are not new: three patients died of immune effector cell-associated hemophagocytic syndrome in Novartis's autoimmune trials of rapcabtagene autoleucel, eight trials were halted, and we led with that on September first; Bristol Myers Squibb then paused its own autoimmune trials of zolacabtagene autoleucel over transient, reversible inflammatory events. What is new, in a piece that ran this morning, is the attribution.

Will Ho, chief executive of IN8bio, attributes both companies' problems to accelerated manufacturing protocols that over-stimulate T cells into producing excess cytokines, and explicitly distinguishes that from an inherent liability of the modality — the William Blair hypothesis from September second, now said out loud by somebody who manufactures cells for a living. And Sanjeev Luther, chief executive of Ernexa, states the risk calculus in one sentence: in oncology the risk-benefit ratio made sense because the patients face mortality, whereas autoimmune diseases are chronic and the people are not necessarily dying.

So here is the honest read, and it is narrower than we would like. Our open item asked whether rapid manufacturing was the suspect variable, because that decides whether the story is "cell therapy in autoimmunity" or "these two products." Two independent operators now say the second thing, and Kyverna's clean twelve-month safety — no high-grade cytokine release syndrome, no neurotoxicity, no hemophagocytic syndrome across twenty-six stiff person syndrome patients — came with the sponsor naming a validated conventional process as the reason. That cuts against the broadest version of our control-architecture argument. What survives is the stronger half anyway, and it is Luther's sentence, worth far more from a cell-therapy chief executive than from us. Jon and Eddie: take the quote, and stop litigating the empirical safety premise, because Kyverna is about to win that argument. They complete a rolling biologics license application in stiff person syndrome by year end, and the full dataset goes up at the joint multiple sclerosis meeting in Toronto, October twenty-first through twenty-third. If the first approved autoimmune cell therapy lands with a clean label, our selectivity claim has to be the antigen-specific cut, not a general safety claim — exactly where we landed on Merck's tulisokibart yesterday. Two items in two days pointing the same way is a signal.

Second item, and it goes to Aletira. AAVantgarde presented on Sunday at the European retina congress in Vienna. Their lead program is a dual-vector gene therapy for Usher syndrome type one-B, delivering the MYO7A gene split across two A-A-V-eight capsids by subretinal injection. Fifteen patients across three dose cohorts, enrollment completed in January. As of the August third data cut: no serious adverse events, no dose-limiting toxicities, nobody discontinued, and ocular inflammation limited and responsive to corticosteroids. Among the twelve patients in the low and mid-dose cohorts with at least six months of follow-up, seven gained at least one line of best-corrected visual acuity and four gained two lines or more, with supportive signals on low-luminance acuity, fixation stability and microperimetry. They also showed preclinical data on a Stargardt program that solves the same payload problem a different way — intein-mediated protein trans-splicing rather than splitting the DNA — reconstituting full-length ABCA4 in mouse, pig and primate, and in Stargardt pigs exceeding one hundred percent of endogenous ABCA4.

The read for us is a trade-off worth naming explicitly, because it will come up in a partner meeting. The field's answer to a payload bigger than one capsid is to split the gene across two vectors. That solves size and does nothing whatsoever for expression control. Our answer — SELEXON, cell-type-specific control — solves expression control and adds to payload. Those two constraints pull against each other, and yesterday's radar pick on inverted terminal repeat instability says the cassette is precisely where the risk lives. Avi and Hugh: when the dual-vector question arrives, the right answer is not "we don't need it." It is a number — the payload budget of a SELEXON-containing construct at the intended dose, against single-capsid capacity.

Two things come free. A competitor just printed above-endogenous expression as a success metric, which is exactly the failure mode native-promoter control is sold against — that makes our framing easier to sell. And limited, steroid-responsive inflammation across fifteen subretinal patients is another privileged-compartment data point. The cochlea is not the eye, but it is a great deal closer to the eye than to the liver.

Third, on the oncology surface-target theme. Genmab reported Saturday at the gynecologic cancer congress in Montreal on Rina-S — rinatabart sesutecan, a folate receptor alpha antibody-drug conjugate with a topoisomerase-one payload — in one hundred nine patients with platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. Confirmed response rate forty-five point nine percent, median progression-free survival nine and a half months, median duration of response twelve point one months. The population was hard: fifty-three percent had three or four prior lines, and a third had already failed mirvetuximab.

Two sentences in that release are the whole item. The first is that activity was seen regardless of folate receptor alpha expression level. The second is that post-mirvetuximab third. Set it against MIRASOL, where mirvetuximab required folate-receptor-alpha-high tumours — seventy-five percent of cells or more — and delivered forty-one point nine percent response with about five and a half months progression-free. Same antigen: a better linker and payload un-gated the biomarker, roughly doubled progression-free survival, and worked after the first drug against that target had failed.

Avi, this belongs in the antigen checklist as the counter-case, because we have been applying the density lesson too broadly. Density determines what a T-cell engager can do, because an engager has to build a synapse and needs enough surface target to do it. It determines much less for a conjugate, where internalization rate and payload potency are limiting. Same target, same indication, two modalities, opposite sensitivity. So the first question on a surface target is which modality we are screening it for — "is the antigen bright enough" is right for an engager and close to wrong for a conjugate.

Fourth, on fibrosis. CSL and Alentis announced an exclusive global collaboration this weekend on lixudebart, a monoclonal antibody against exposed claudin one — three hundred fifty-five million dollars up front, up to one point two billion in commercial milestones, CSL funding development, profits split fifty-five forty-five. Claudin one is a tight-junction protein whose exposed form drives inflammatory and fibrotic signalling in kidney, liver, lung and gut. A pan-organ antifibrotic target.

The structural lesson is the one we keep relearning: they did not take a pan-organ target into a pan-organ indication. The lead is a Phase 2 in ANCA-associated vasculitis with rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis — rare, fast, irreversible organ damage, and a setting where you can watch kidney function move in twenty-six patients. The breadth lives in the option value; focal segmental glomerulosclerosis and primary sclerosing cholangitis come after. That is the template we wrote down for VEXAS syndrome in September — a rare indication with a hard, fast endpoint is not a small market you apologize for, it is the pivotal-enabling wedge that gets a broad mechanism paid for. Hemaka and Anthony: for cardiac fibrosis the live question is not which indication is biggest, it is which one moves an endpoint inside a Phase 2 of under thirty patients. If we cannot name it, that is the gap in the package.

Fifth, briefly — yesterday's cold-chain item getting a second half. On Friday Alpha Teknova and Pluristyx put their PluriFreeze cryopreservation system on a Type Two drug master file, so developers can cross-reference it in their own applications rather than build that manufacturing package themselves. ARPA-H's room-temperature program commoditizes cold chain from above; this commoditizes the cryopreservation layer from below. The cheap action: on any cell-therapy program, ask whether the freeze medium carries a referenceable file. That is weeks of pre-I-N-D work for the price of an email.

And last, a sourcing item, and a candid one. Adecto Pharmaceuticals ceased operations on September thirtieth, reaching the trade press on Friday. Twelve years — founded in twenty-fourteen by three academic inventors, preclinical the entire time, developing a humanized monoclonal antibody against ADAM eight, a cell-surface metalloprotease the founders' own work identified as a driver of triple-negative breast cancer growth and metastasis. The company blamed a risk-averse funding environment. And the operative sentence is on their own website: the intellectual property is being transferred back to the inventors, who welcome discussions about asset acquisition, licensing, or collaborative development.

That is our business model's raw material appearing with a sign on it — a surface oncology target, a humanized antibody already built, and the IP about to sit with academic inventors who have just learned what this market thinks of their program. Eddie, that is a cheap call. The caveat goes in the same breath: twelve years without reaching the clinic usually means something, and the two candidates are "the data never got good enough" or "the money never showed up." Those have very different values, and one conversation plus one look at the package tells them apart.

The generalizable point is bigger than this asset: in a risk-averse market, reversion is a sourcing channel. Avi and Yixuan — stand up a standing watch on preclinical companies winding down with surface-target IP reverting to universities. Characterized assets, motivated inventors, and a license fee rather than a Series A as the comparable.

That is the watch. The theme is that two of today's six items are the market telling us what it will no longer pay for — a safety argument it has stopped believing, and a twelve-year preclinical program it stopped funding. Both are things we can act on this week. Back tomorrow.
