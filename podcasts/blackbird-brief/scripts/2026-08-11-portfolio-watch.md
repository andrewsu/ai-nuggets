## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Portfolio Watch for Tuesday, August eleventh. Same honest frame I've held all month: no Blackbird company had its own readout, financing, deal, or filing land, so the direct-catalyst tape is quiet for a sixteenth day. But the competitive tape is not quiet — and today's most important development lands on the same program that led yesterday's brief, the schizophrenia NewCo with the Lieber Institute and Third Rock. Yesterday it was a bar-raiser on tolerability and cognition. Today it's the clock. So that gets the lead, and I'll be candid: this is the single item on our watch list this week that has actually moved, as opposed to the standing markers I'll run through after.

Here's the item. On Friday, August seventh, Nxera Pharma — the London-headquartered neuroscience company formerly known as Sosei Heptares — reported its second-quarter results and held its earnings call. Buried in that print is the most concrete update we've had in months on the one thing that most directly reprices our G-P-R fifty-two program: the status of Nxera's own G-P-R fifty-two agonist, the compound coded N-X-E one-forty-nine. That molecule is the only other Phase-two-ready G-P-R fifty-two agonist in industry — the same target, the same mechanism, the direct competitor to what our NewCo is building toward a development candidate later this year.

Let me recap where that competitor has been, because the trajectory is the whole story. Boehringer Ingelheim had optioned N-X-E one-forty-nine and was expected to take up the license in late 2025. Instead, Boehringer walked away from the option in December, and all rights reverted to Nxera. Since then the program has been, in effect, on the shelf — Phase-two-ready, fully owned by Nxera, and quietly for sale. For eight months the honest read has been: it's unsold, so we're not yet racing a funded same-mechanism rival. That's the reprieve I've cited in this brief repeatedly.

Friday's call is where that changes. Management said on the record that they are in — their words — advanced discussions with several parties to partner the program, and they expect to execute at least one major out-license in the second half of this year. And critically, the CEO was explicit that Nxera will not run global Phase two studies itself — it is not, in his words, their intention to do so. They've taken the asset to its logical point of inflection and want a large, fully-funded pharma partner to carry it into Phase two and Phase three. So this is no longer a passive shopping process that's been dragging since December. It's an active, late-stage deal process with a stated close window measured in months.

Now the so-what for our NewCo, and this is exactly the calendar risk we wrote down. The concern was always: if a buyer picks up N-X-E one-forty-nine in the third quarter of 2026, our Lieber NewCo enters its development-candidate declaration — expected later this year — twelve to twenty-four months behind a direct, same-mechanism competitor that's already Phase-two-ready and now backed by a large pharma balance sheet. Friday's update moves that scenario from a hypothetical to a live, near-term probability. A funded pharma owner taking N-X-E one-forty-nine into a global Phase two is the thing that most cleanly caps the value of being early on this target — and Nxera just told the market they intend to make that happen before year-end.

Two things sharpen the read further. First, our own differentiation window is now the thing under pressure. The whole "best-in-class G-P-R fifty-two chemistry, first into a differentiated Phase two" framing only holds while nobody else is in Phase two. If Nxera signs this half, that framing has a shelf life — and it's short. Second, notice that the pressure is now bilateral. On the same call, Nxera's own management flagged that the muscarinic momentum — specifically MapLight's recent Phase-two readout, the exact dataset I walked yesterday — has strengthened the competitive picture in schizophrenia. So the field is consolidating from two directions at once: a same-mechanism buyer about to fund our direct rival, and a validated adjacent class, the muscarinics, raising the tolerability-and-cognition bar underneath the whole non-dopaminergic category. Our differentiation window is closing on both fronts simultaneously.

One more strategic wrinkle worth flagging for the team. Nxera has been positioning G-P-R fifty-two not just for schizophrenia but also for psychosis in Alzheimer's disease — a very large indication with no approved drug. If the eventual buyer takes N-X-E one-forty-nine broad into that lane, it does two things to us: it validates G-P-R fifty-two as a target worth a lot more than a single schizophrenia asset, which is good for the thesis — but it also means the buyer's incentive to move fast and own the mechanism goes up, which compresses our window further.

So the action for Hemaka and the Third Rock team, ahead of the development-candidate declaration, is the same one we flagged but now genuinely time-boxed. One: lock the best-in-class chemistry story and the first-differentiated-Phase-two framing now — that narrative is depreciating in real time and the runway is this half, not open-ended. Two: have the hard Third Rock conversation about whether to compress the development-candidate timing out of late 2026 to get ahead of a Nxera signing, versus holding for the cleaner molecule. And three: watch the deal tape — if and when Nxera announces a partner, and who it is, that resets our clock precisely, and the buyer's identity tells us which indications and which geographies the competition will actually contest.
