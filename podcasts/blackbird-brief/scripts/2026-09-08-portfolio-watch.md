## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Tuesday, September eighth. The most important development for the portfolio is not a single event. It is that the European Respiratory Society congress in Barcelona has spent four days measuring the exact ground our COPD program stands on, and the answer is that the upstream alarmin layer is now contested at four separate points on one pathway — and not one entrant in that race is selling a new target.

Start with where the field already was. AstraZeneca's tozorakimab is an antibody against interleukin thirty-three, the epithelial alarmin at the top of the inflammatory cascade in the airway, and it now holds three positive Phase three trials in COPD on the annualized rate of moderate-to-severe exacerbations. The population is the whole commercial argument: those trials enrolled irrespective of blood eosinophil count, irrespective of smoking status, across every stage of lung function severity, and the effect held in former smokers and in the overall population including current smokers. Every biologic that works in COPD today works inside an eosinophil-selected subgroup; this one claims not to need the selection. Now say the other half plainly. AstraZeneca has randomized more than five thousand four hundred patients on this pathway, holds three positive Phase threes, and has not put a single exacerbation rate ratio into public view.

This week, Mabwell in Shanghai presented Phase one-b/two-a data on an antibody against S-T-two — the receptor for interleukin thirty-three. Same axis, one step down the pathway. Eighty patients, randomized and placebo-controlled, dose escalation to nine hundred milligrams monthly, former smokers with moderate-to-severe disease, most with eosinophil counts at or below three hundred cells per microliter, so deliberately not the type-two-high population. Annualized moderate-to-severe exacerbations fell twenty-four percent at six hundred milligrams and eighty-one percent at nine hundred; severe exacerbations, forty-seven percent and one hundred percent. Phase two-b enrollment is complete and Phase three is guided for around year end. Be careful with those numbers, because the contrast is the useful part: eighty patients across five arms is about sixteen an arm, and a hundred-percent reduction in sixteen patients is a denominator artifact, not an effect size. One company with three positive Phase threes has disclosed nothing; another with sixteen patients an arm has disclosed everything, and it is the second set of numbers circulating this week.

Then this morning Upstream Bio presented the full Phase two dataset for verekitug, the only antagonist in development against the T-S-L-P receptor — T-S-L-P being the other epithelial alarmin, the one this brief flagged on the twenty-ninth of August as having consolidated. Four hundred and seventy-eight patients with severe asthma, enrolled regardless of eosinophils or any other type-two biomarker. One hundred milligrams every twelve weeks cut the annualized exacerbation rate fifty-six percent against placebo, with lung function, exhaled nitric oxide and symptom control all moving by week two. Phase three starts next quarter at quarterly dosing in broad populations with no biomarker restriction, and a separate Phase two of the same molecule is already running in COPD.

So count the seats: interleukin thirty-three the ligand, AstraZeneca; its receptor, Mabwell; T-S-L-P the ligand, AstraZeneca again with Tezspire and Generate Biomedicines with its designed antibody; its receptor, Upstream Bio. Four points, one pathway, everything upstream, all of it in or entering Phase three. Virginia, this is your frame from the twenty-ninth sharpened by a week of data. We said then that what protein design bought Generate in COPD was a property — a three-month half-life — and not a novel target. Every entrant this week is selling one of two things and neither is a target: a property, meaning quarterly dosing and at-home administration, or a population, meaning all-comers irrespective of eosinophils. If our differentiator is that we act upstream, upstream is now the crowded part of the pathway. It has to be a property none of those four holds, or a position downstream of where all four converge. The question stands unchanged: which property, and does it earn a line on the label.

One more Barcelona result reframes the category. Chloe Bloom's group at Imperial College London reported four parallel observational studies in United Kingdom medical records, twenty to twenty-two thousand patients each, comparing people started on a G-L-P-one receptor agonist against people started on a sulfonylurea: roughly forty percent fewer asthma attacks and twenty percent fewer COPD exacerbations. Bloom was appropriately careful — observational, confounded by who gets prescribed what, and in her own words it should not change treatment decisions on its own. But apply the comparator-drift rule we wrote for NuGate on the twenty-ninth. A cheap, marketed, non-biologic drug that much of the COPD population will be taking for metabolic reasons, cutting exacerbations twenty percent in an unselected population, is exactly the background therapy that turns a real effect into an unimpressive increment at readout.

Second item, and it is for Artemyx. Also at Barcelona, the COPERNICOS trial reported — randomized, double-blind, multicenter, four arms, in severe and very severe COPD, and the first prospective randomized test of an eosinophil-guided inhaled-corticosteroid strategy. The mechanic is the point: blood eosinophils measured every three months, and below zero point three billion cells per liter the steroid is paused for the next three months, at or above it continues. Patients under the threshold could have their steroid reduced without a significant increase in severe-exacerbation risk. Jørgen Vestbo named the barrier honestly — clinicians have to accept measuring a biomarker repeatedly over time, and spending more time with patients explaining why they may not need a drug they are already on.

That is the second monitoring precedent in five days. Yesterday's lead was camizestrant, where a recurring three-monthly blood draw in an asymptomatic patient got written into a companion-diagnostic label. This is the same structure with two differences that matter. It is non-oncology and chronic, which is Artemyx's actual territory. And the decision the test drives is stopping a drug rather than starting one, which tells you who pays for the evidence: nobody selling the inhaler funds COPERNICOS, an academic consortium ran it, and the beneficiary is the payer. Eddie, that widens yesterday's answer. When we ask which therapeutic decision our assay changes and whose Phase three proves it, de-escalation is a live third answer, and its sponsor is a payer or a health system rather than a pharma company. Different motion, different evidence requirements. Worth an hour before the liver-fibrosis validation plan is fixed.

Third item, and it lands on Clasp and Adventris. A manufacturing piece this morning takes the Merck and Moderna melanoma result — the personalized messenger-R-N-A cancer vaccine that nearly doubled Moderna's share price last month — and asks the operational question. Moderna sequences a patient's tumor and healthy cells, picks up to thirty-four patient-specific neoantigens, and formulates them, with turnaround now around six weeks. The framing that matters comes from a consultant on the messenger-R-N-A alliance board: the industry spent the pandemic learning to scale up, and the conversation now is scaling out and scaling down, because a company without in-house capability needs a contract manufacturer willing to invest in miniaturizing scale, and every one of them was built for large batches. Note the phrase — on Saturday we covered the cell-therapy version of exactly this, where a chief operating officer said you do not scale up an autologous product, you scale out. Same sentence, different modality, three days apart. For Clasp, this is the operational proof of what we wrote on the twentieth of August: the melanoma readout validates the biology of teaching T cells to see tumor-specific mutations and simultaneously prices the individualized route in public. An off-the-shelf T-cell engager is a vial, made once, and so is a shared-neoantigen peptide vaccine, which is Adventris. Hemaka, that is a partnering sentence, and it is stronger than it was three weeks ago because the industry is now saying the hard part out loud. The candid limit is that Moderna is executing, so the argument is about who else can.

Fourth and last, on the Parkinson's pre-spinout theme. A landscape piece this morning puts Jefferies' number on the disease at eight billion dollars by twenty thirty-five and calls it one of the largest untapped opportunities in neuroscience — against an alpha-synuclein thesis under real strain, with Biogen and Denali discontinuing their L-R-R-K-two inhibitor in mid-stage in May and Roche and Prothena's antibody missing its primary endpoint twice before entering a Phase three that does not read out until twenty twenty-nine. The response is diversification, and the money went to the inflammasome sensor N-L-R-P-three: Lilly agreed earlier this year to buy Ventyx for one point two billion dollars for that target, with Roche, NodThera, Insilico and Brenig in the same lane. So that is no longer white space, and the buyer who took it is the one we ranked first on the bidder set last week. But the read to act on is the constraint both the analyst and the Parkinson's Foundation's chief medical officer name, and it is not target selection — it is diagnostic latency. It is always easier to preserve function a patient still has than to regain function they have lost, and repeated failure of disease-modifying drugs traces to how late the disease is recognized. Notice what Denali did after its miss: continuing only in patients who carry the relevant gene variant. That is the third instance in two weeks of one rule, after Alumis burying a probably-real lupus effect by failing to select, and BioNTech selecting on circulating tumor D-N-A without being rescued by it. Anthony, Jon — for any Parkinson's program we look at, the value case has to say which patient it treats and how that patient gets found before it says anything about mechanism. In this indication the enrichment strategy is not a downstream detail; it is why the field has a graveyard.

Week ahead. Barcelona runs through tomorrow, so the tozorakimab full dataset could still surface, and the number to pull is the exacerbation rate ratio in the eosinophil-low stratum, because that is the claim the broad-population argument rests on. Ultragenyx has a gene-therapy decision in Sanfilippo type A on the nineteenth, the next agency temperature reading for Aletira. Sensorion's program day on its G-J-B-two gene therapy is the twenty-second, and the detail to listen for is the capsid and any expression-control element, neither of which has been disclosed. And Glasgow, the twenty-third through twenty-sixth, still carries the full engager safety dataset Jon has open.

Closing thought. Three of today's four items are the same shape: a field publishing its operational constraint rather than its biology. In COPD, four companies attacking one pathway and competing on dosing interval. In diagnostics, a trial whose product is a schedule of blood draws. In cancer vaccines, an industry conceding that the hard problem is making the thing. Where the science is settled enough to be boring, differentiation moves to logistics — a good market for an operator, and a bad one for a platform story.

That is the watch. Back tomorrow.
