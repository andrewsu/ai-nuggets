## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Tuesday, October thirteenth. The most important development for the portfolio is that the celiac readout we flagged yesterday came in positive, which makes three wins in six weeks on the same immune axis. That matters for Winnow less because of celiac than because of where the winner says it's going next.

First Tracks reported Cohort one of its Phase one-b study of A-N-B-zero-thirty-three on Monday morning. As a reminder, it's a subcutaneous antibody that blocks C-D-one-twenty-two, the receptor chain shared by I-L-fifteen and I-L-two. Thirty-two patients completed the study, seventeen on drug and fifteen on placebo. They got three loading doses over four weeks, then a fourteen-day gluten challenge of about two slices of bread a day, then a biopsy.

The histology is clean. On villus-to-crypt ratio, the standard measure of mucosal damage, placebo patients lost about two-thirds of a point and treated patients lost under a fifth of a point. That's a placebo-adjusted difference of about half a point, with a p-value under point zero one. Intraepithelial lymphocyte density rose by about nineteen cells on placebo and didn't move on drug, with a p-value under point zero zero one. The part nobody had shown before is that symptoms separated from the first day of the challenge. Forty-seven percent of placebo patients took rescue medication for gastrointestinal symptoms, against six percent on drug. There were no serious adverse events and no discontinuations on drug. The company says late-stage development starts in twenty twenty-seven, and Leerink called the data favorable against Teva's.

Hold one caveat. This was a two-week challenge, and argenx's anti-C-D-one-twenty-two ran an eight-week challenge in a hundred twenty-six patients. A stronger p-value from thirty-two patients on a shorter, more intense challenge isn't a head-to-head win. What it does show is that the provocation design works on this axis however you run it: one anti-I-L-fifteen and two anti-C-D-one-twenty-two programs, all positive since September second. Celiac is now a three-company indication, and Winnow shouldn't plan to enter it.

The more important line is the expansion plan. First Tracks is already running a Phase one-b in eosinophilic esophagitis, with data in the third quarter of twenty twenty-seven, and says Phase two in vitiligo and one unnamed indication start in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. Vitiligo is a tissue disease driven by C-D-eight T cells and sustained by I-L-fifteen. The analysts are already using the phrase "pipeline in a product." So a broad cytokine-receptor blocker is now walking into exactly the T-cell-driven tissue diseases where a selective engager would want to play.

Jon and Anthony, here's where I'd put Winnow's differentiation. C-D-one-twenty-two is also the I-L-two receptor beta chain that regulatory T cells depend on. A six-week study can't tell you what years of blocking that does to the Treg compartment. That's a hypothesis, not a finding. But it's the specific question a selective approach can ask of the broad one, and it's sharper than the generic safety argument we've already agreed to stop leaning on. Put two things on the diligence list: the Treg readouts in the First Tracks eosinophilic esophagitis and vitiligo protocols, and which of our candidate indications have a provocation model like the gluten challenge.

Second, the fibrosis theme. Two readouts in pulmonary fibrosis landed on Monday, and they point in opposite directions.

Bristol Myers Squibb's Phase three ALOFT-I-P-F trial of admilparant hit its primary endpoint. Admilparant is an oral L-P-A-one receptor antagonist, and we've tracked it since the liver-monitoring amendment surfaced on October third. The trial enrolled about twelve hundred fifty-five patients. The sixty-milligram dose significantly improved the change in forced vital capacity at week fifty-two. The hundred-twenty-milligram dose slowed decline but missed significance. Bristol gave no effect sizes. It described safety as favorable, with discontinuations similar to placebo, and the release says nothing about liver events. The Street split. Leerink said the lack of a dose response raises questions about the profile, and William Blair called it a likely blockbuster opportunity anyway. Contineum, which has a different L-P-A-one drug in Phase two, fell about thirty percent on Monday morning.

The same morning, Syndax's Phase two of axatilimab in I-P-F missed. Axatilimab is the anti-C-S-F-one-receptor antibody approved in graft-versus-host disease, and the rationale was to deplete pro-fibrotic macrophages. It enrolled a hundred forty-five patients for twenty-six weeks and showed no effect on the rate of lung-function decline. Syndax has dropped I-P-F.

Virginia, three things for the cardiac fibrosis work. First, on Monday the receptor antagonist acting on the epithelium and the fibroblast beat the strategy of depleting macrophages. That's one data point, but it's worth noting which side of the biology got paid. Second, this is the second flat dose-response in two days, after Cardurion. I wouldn't call admilparant's inverted, because the high dose still trended right. But where the top dose doesn't add anything, the full dataset gets judged on whether a mechanism is really saturated or whether toxicity is eating the margin. Ask for the liver table when Bristol presents. Third, and most relevant to our open item, it took twelve hundred patients and a year of follow-up to register a functional benefit on a fibrotic disease. That's the scale problem we flagged on October fifth. The pitch for our theme has to name an indication where the endpoint moves inside a small Phase two, and the C-S-L and Alentis rare-kidney template is still the model for that.

One shorter item, for NuGate. This morning Rona Therapeutics licensed an R-N-A-i candidate against A-L-K-seven, an adipose-tissue target for obesity, to Roche. The deal is worth up to a hundred million dollars upfront and in near-term milestones, plus up to one point zero six billion in later milestones. What Roche is buying is Rona's extrahepatic delivery platform, meaning it gets the oligonucleotide into fat rather than the liver. Separately, Endpoints reported that a new company, Undecimal Bio, launched with eighteen million dollars to take small interfering R-N-A beyond the liver, carved out of Eleven Therapeutics. The pattern is that money in the oligonucleotide space is flowing to delivery outside the liver, and partners are paying for a tissue-access story as much as for a target. NuGate's antisense goes to the central nervous system by intrathecal injection. That's the established route, but it isn't a platform story. Eddie, when NuGate pitches, expect a partner to ask whether there's anything proprietary about how the drug reaches motor neurons, and have an answer ready.

That's the watch. Freenome's next-generation colorectal screening data go up at the gastroenterology meeting tomorrow morning, so check the specificity definition for DELFI. Kyverna's full stiff person syndrome dataset follows at the multiple sclerosis congress in Toronto starting October twenty-first. Talk soon.
