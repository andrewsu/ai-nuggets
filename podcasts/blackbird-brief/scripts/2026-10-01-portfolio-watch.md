The primary endpoint is treatment failure within twenty-eight days — death, readmission, an emergency-department revisit, an unscheduled visit for worsening disease, or an intensification of drug therapy. Treatment failure fell eighty-one percent against placebo, p equals zero point zero one two two. New moderate-to-severe exacerbations fell eighty-five percent, p equals zero point zero three. Hospitalisations and emergency visits for new exacerbations fell a hundred percent, p equals zero point zero one three seven. Symptom total score improved at week two, rescue inhaler use fell from weeks two through seven, and safety was clean — fewer adverse events and fewer serious adverse events on drug than on placebo. The key secondary, post-bronchodilator F-E-V one at week one, missed. At week four the difference was seventy millilitres, p equals zero point one six.

Now set that against the asthma half, which we covered on September sixteenth. Same molecule, same eosinophil threshold, same single dose, a hundred and sixty patients instead of a hundred and fifty-nine. In asthma the primary — the identical treatment-failure endpoint — showed a sixty-six percent reduction and missed at p equals zero point one five, while F-E-V one at week one cleared at a hundred and thirty millilitres. In C-O-P-D the primary cleared and the lung function missed. These two trials are mirror images of each other, and what moved between them is not the mechanism. It is which arm fails. In asthma the control arm failed less often than the protocol assumed, which is why a sixty-six percent reduction could not reach significance. An acute C-O-P-D exacerbation is a higher-event setting, so a similar effect cleared comfortably at the same sample size.
