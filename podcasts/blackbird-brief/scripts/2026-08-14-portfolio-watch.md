Over the last several months the Hopkins group published three separate phase-one readouts of this vaccine, and the interesting thing is that they stake out three very different points on the disease timeline. Eighteen of the twenty, ninety percent, generated a significant mutant-K-RAS T-cell response. The T-cell clones persisted for up to two years. Cyst reduction or resolution showed up in about thirty-eight percent of the vaccinated group versus about seven percent in an unvaccinated comparison. And over a median follow-up of about sixteen and a half months, none of them developed pancreatic cancer. Now, the honest caveats: twenty people, single-arm, an immune-response endpoint, and cancer prevention is a long, expensive endpoint to actually prove — you don't get a prevention approval on a T-cell readout.
