# The Blackbird Brief — Portfolio Watch — 2026-10-09

## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Friday, October ninth. The most important development for the portfolio: F-D-A has spelled out why it is still holding a Phase Three schizophrenia program in the United States. The reason is not the drug's psychiatry. It is the drug's mechanism class, a sodium channel blocker, and a count of deaths whose exposure-adjusted rate actually favors the drug. That is a lesson for the G-P-R fifty-two NewCo, and a warning for our non-opioid pain theme.

Here is what happened. Newron Pharmaceuticals, the Milan company, is running two Phase Three trials of evenamide as an add-on for treatment-resistant schizophrenia. Evenamide modulates glutamate release by blocking voltage-gated sodium channels. In April, after a sudden death at a site outside the U-S, F-D-A stopped new enrollment at American sites in the second trial. Newron's safety board said to carry on, and the investigator called the death unrelated. Late last month F-D-A said the hold stays. Yesterday Newron disclosed the agency's written reasoning. F-D-A counted four deaths among patients on evenamide across every study, against one on placebo, and called that a potential safety signal. It also cited its concern that sodium channel blockers can cause arrhythmias and serious cardiovascular events. Investigators judged three of the four deaths unrelated. The fourth is "possibly related" only because two autopsies could not find a cause.

Now look at Newron's numbers. Raw incidence is zero point five six percent on drug against zero point two seven on placebo. Adjusted for time on treatment, it flips: one point three one deaths per hundred patient-years on drug, one point eight one on placebo. Run the arithmetic on those percentages and the program has roughly seven hundred patients on drug against fewer than four hundred on placebo. Newron says it has more than ten thousand E-C-Gs from about seven hundred treated people with no pattern of arrhythmia, and the drug slightly shortens Q-T-c rather than prolonging it. None of that has lifted the hold. The shares fell about eight and a half percent. The first Phase Three, roughly six hundred patients in twenty countries, still reads out in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven.

Hemaka, Avi, this goes in the G-P-R fifty-two pre-I-N-D book, for three reasons. First, the agency read a raw count of deaths in a small program and attached it to a mechanism prior, even though the exposure-adjusted rate favored the drug. Sudden death is not rare in schizophrenia, and a program that randomizes more patients to drug than placebo will produce a count imbalance by chance alone. So we prespecify the mortality analysis, independent death adjudication, and the comparison to the background rate before the first patient is dosed, not after the first death. Second, ask the question F-D-A asked here. What is the class prior for an orphan G-s-coupled receptor agonist? We should have a written cardiac and seizure answer in the dossier before anyone at the agency raises it. Third, for Eddie: the hold is U-S only, and Newron keeps enrolling in Europe, Asia and Latin America. That is a live example of why a global first-in-patient footprint is a hedge, not a cost. And if evenamide slips, the add-on slot in treatment-resistant schizophrenia stays open longer.

The second read is for the non-opioid pain theme, Anthony and Virginia. The F-D-A language is about sodium channel blockers as a class. That is the target family Navion and Vertex are selling into. Selectivity against the cardiac sodium channel has just moved from an efficacy talking point to a regulatory property. Any pain program we look at that touches a sodium channel needs that selectivity data and a cardiac package on slide one.

Staying with pain. Viatris has agreed to buy Pacira BioSciences for thirty-six dollars and fifty cents a share in cash, about one point six five billion dollars in equity value. Pacira sells Exparel, a liposomal bupivacaine for post-surgical pain, and Zilretta, an extended-release triamcinolone injection for knee osteoarthritis. Both are new formulations of old generic molecules. Over the past twelve months they brought in about seven hundred forty-six million dollars of revenue and a hundred seventy-seven million of adjusted E-B-I-T-D-A. So the price is a little over two times revenue and about nine times E-B-I-T-D-A. Generic Exparel arrives in early twenty thirty under patent settlements, starting in the high single digits of volume and ramping toward thirty percent within three years. Viatris says it will use its lifecycle skills to sustain sales after that. Leerink's Daniel Clark notes Exparel's growth has been held back by hospital formularies reluctant to add it to post-surgical protocols.

For our pain theme, read the buyer, not the price. The company paying for non-opioid pain today is a generics and established-brands house, buying hospital call points and anesthesiology access, and it valued the franchise on cash flow. A novel-mechanism program, like the M-R-G-P-R-X-one modulator work at Johns Hopkins, should not model a Vertex-style exit off this comp. It also tells us the formulary is the gate in post-surgical pain. A new non-opioid needs a pharmacoeconomic argument a hospital pharmacy committee will accept, not just a pain score. One more detail. Pacira's pipeline includes P-C-R-X two-oh-one, a locally injected gene therapy for knee osteoarthritis. A gene therapy now sits inside a commercial pain company, which adds to the gene-therapy column we opened on the pain competitive map last month.

Third, for Winnow, Jon and Anthony. Argenx put out two releases yesterday that read as one controlled experiment. The first: argenx stopped its Phase Three UNITY trial of Vyvgart Hytrulo in Sjögren's disease for futility. An interim look at more than five hundred of six hundred thirty patients found it could not hit a forty-eight-week systemic disease-activity endpoint. The readout had not been expected until the second half of twenty twenty-seven, and the stock fell about fourteen percent. William Blair blames the high placebo response that has dogged Sjögren's. The second: FB one-oh-two, the anti-C-D-one-twenty-two antibody argenx bought with Forte Biosciences this summer, met its Phase Two primary endpoint in celiac disease. A hundred twenty-six patients on a strict gluten-free diet got infusions or placebo while undergoing an eight-week gluten challenge. The drug protected the villus-to-crypt ratio of the small intestine, with a p-value of zero point zero one eight, and intraepithelial lymphocytes and symptoms moved the same way. It goes to Phase Three.

One company, one day: a six-hundred-patient chronic-endpoint trial died at interim, and a hundred-twenty-six-patient provocation trial won. That is the design read from Teva's anti-I-L-fifteen celiac result in September, now confirmed. C-D-one-twenty-two is the receptor chain I-L-fifteen signals through, so two antibodies on the same tissue-resident T-cell axis have now worked in the same challenge model. For Winnow there are two takeaways. Celiac is becoming a two-company indication. And the question we logged in September is now urgent: for each T-cell-mediated indication on Winnow's list, is there a provocation that turns a chronic endpoint into an eight-week one? A company that can prove mechanism in a hundred patients is cheaper to finance, and that shows up in the term sheet.

Last item, for the Georgiamune position, Eddie and Virginia. TRex Bio priced its I-P-O last night at fourteen dollars a share, the bottom of its fourteen-to-sixteen range, with the full eight point three million shares. That is about a hundred seventeen million dollars gross. It starts trading on Nasdaq today. TRex's lead is a T-N-F-R-two agonist that expands tissue regulatory T cells, aimed first at atopic dermatitis, with efficacy data in mid twenty twenty-seven. Lilly had said it intended to buy into the offering. The read: the window is open for a pre-data Treg company, but investors held the price at the low end. That is now a public mark for the Treg-activation class Georgiamune sits in. Watch how it trades over its first month.

One gene therapy note, for NuGate and Aletira. AviadoBio, the London spinout from King's College, updated its Phase One-Two trial of A-V-B one-oh-one in frontotemporal dementia caused by progranulin mutations. The vector is infused straight into the thalamus, so progranulin is made only in neurons. Seventeen patients are dosed across four cohorts. At twelve weeks, spinal-fluid progranulin was up thirty-six percent in the third cohort and ninety percent in the fourth. There have been no treatment-related serious adverse events, and nobody has needed immunosuppression. Serum neurofilament light at one year sat at or below baseline, against an expected rise of thirteen to fourteen percent a year. That is from the low-dose cohorts only, and there is no clinical efficacy yet. For NuGate, this is another program building its early case on neurofilament light against a known untreated slope, which is the split we drew last week. For Aletira, it is another example of local delivery doing the selectivity work: a small dose, one tissue, no immunosuppression. Separately, Atsena added pivotal-dose data at the ophthalmology meeting this week. Three of nine patients now see twenty-forty or better, which is driving vision, and they picked their lowest dose for Phase Three.

On the watch list. BioPharma Dive reports that bankers expect Caribou to draw reverse-merger interest. By Leerink's numbers it has a negative enterprise value of twenty to thirty million dollars, and Raymond James counts about two dozen biotech reverse mergers this year. That is live input for the shell inventory Emily and Eddie own. Newron's first Phase Three reads out in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven. Viatris will discuss Pacira on its November fifth earnings call, and its fast-acting meloxicam has an F-D-A decision date of December twenty-seventh.

That's the Portfolio Watch for Friday. Have a good weekend, Blackbird team.
