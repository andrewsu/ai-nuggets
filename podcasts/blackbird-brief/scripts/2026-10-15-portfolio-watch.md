## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Thursday, October fifteenth. The most important development for the portfolio is that a subcutaneous R-N-A-i drug just cut tau in the human central nervous system by nearly three quarters for three months, and that changes the question NuGate will get in every partner meeting from here on.

Arrowhead reported interim Phase one-two-a data yesterday morning for ARO-MAPT, a small interfering R-N-A that silences the gene for tau. It's given as a shot under the skin, and it is built to cross the blood-brain barrier on Arrowhead's TRiM delivery platform. In healthy volunteers, three weekly doses at two point one and six point four milligrams per kilogram cut cerebrospinal fluid total tau by an average of forty and sixty-nine percent. Two doses at twelve point eight milligrams per kilogram, two weeks apart, cut it by seventy-three percent on average, eighty-five in the best responder. All of that was measured at day one hundred five, which is ninety days after the last dose, so the company is talking about quarterly dosing or less. There were no serious adverse events and no discontinuations, and the release goes out of its way to say there was no persistent anemia, which is the liability people associate with transferrin-receptor brain shuttles. Arrowhead calls it the first clinical validation of a platform that carries s-i-R-N-A across the blood-brain barrier, and on the data in hand that's a fair description. Early Alzheimer's cohorts read out in twenty twenty-seven, and the company has opened regulatory discussions for MAPT-associated frontotemporal dementia and progressive supranuclear palsy. Stifel called the knockdown arguably better than anything else the brain-shuttle field has shown, while noting that tau still isn't proven as an Alzheimer's target.

Hemaka and Avi, this lands right on the question we opened on Tuesday. We said the field is paying for delivery platforms, not delivery routes, and that NuGate needs an answer to what's proprietary about reaching motor neurons. As of yesterday, a competing modality reaches the human brain from a subcutaneous injection, and the comparison a partner will draw is a quarterly lumbar puncture against a quarterly shot.

That said, it's a threat to half of the NuGate story, not all of it, and it matters which half. Cerebrospinal fluid tau mostly reports what neurons in the brain are making. It tells you nothing yet about lower motor neurons in the spinal cord, and the spinal cord is precisely where intrathecal delivery is strongest. Frontotemporal dementia is a cortical disease, and that's where a systemic shuttle with broad brain distribution should win. So the honest read is that the ALS leg of the NuGate pitch holds up better than the F-T-D leg, and the pitch should be weighted that way before somebody else draws the comparison for us. One more thing worth saying out loud: these are healthy volunteers and a biomarker. Lowering total tau in spinal fluid is not the same as clearing tangles or bending the curve of a disease, and the two big tau antibodies already taught the field that lesson expensively. What's validated here is the delivery, not the target.

Second item, and it's a bad day for one of the mechanisms sitting next to our schizophrenia program. Vanda reported that its Phase three study of V-Q-W seven-six-five, an alpha-seven nicotinic acetylcholine receptor partial agonist, missed its primary endpoint in social anxiety disorder. Five hundred forty-six adults, single oral dose against placebo, and the endpoint was distress during a standardized public-speaking stress test in the female population. The difference was four tenths of a point on a hundred-point scale, with a p-value of point eight-nine. The full population, male and female, was also flat. The drug was safe and well tolerated. The program is on hold.

Here's why this is on our watch, for Hemaka and for the neuropsych NewCo team. This molecule is the old Novartis compound A-Q-W zero-five-one, licensed to Vanda. Alpha-seven nicotinic agonism has been the great white whale of cognitive and negative symptoms in schizophrenia for twenty years, and this is another Phase three on that receptor that didn't confirm its Phase two. Note what happened at each step, because it's the shape that matters, not the receptor. A Phase two showed a numerical trend on a subjective, self-rated scale in a provoked population. The company built a Phase three around that trend, enriched for the subgroup where the trend lived, and the trend didn't come back. We now have three separate entries in this file saying variations of the same thing: a subjective rating scale in a provoked or challenged design will give you a promising number that does not replicate. Our GPR52 story is a cognitive and negative-symptom story. That means the instrument question — what are we measuring, and is it machine-measured or self-reported — is the single highest-value thing to settle before a first patient study, not after. And add alpha-seven to the list of cautionary class priors in the diligence committee package alongside the kynurenine aminotransferase case.

Third item is for Eddie and for the oncology surface targets theme. Verastem released the first real look at V-S seven-three-seven-five, its oral inhibitor of KRAS G12D, and the stock rose about eighteen percent. Response rates were forty-one percent in previously treated metastatic pancreatic cancer at the nine-hundred-milligram daily dose, thirty-three percent in colorectal and forty-three percent in non-small-cell lung cancer. The company priced a two hundred fifty million dollar offering the same morning and named a new chief executive, Michael Bailey, effective January first. It says it has alignment with the F-D-A on a first-line pancreatic Phase three starting before year-end.

Two things follow for us. The first is competitive. G12D is now a crowded, well-capitalized race — Revolution Medicines has an approved drug in the class and a G12D-specific molecule behind it, Merck paid four hundred million dollars upfront for a preclinical G12D inhibitor three weeks ago, and now a mid-cap has clinical responses in three tumor types and a funded path to three pivotal trials. For Adventris, whose mutant-KRAS vaccine sits in the same mutation space, this is neither a tailwind nor a threat in the simple sense. The vaccine plays the adjuvant and minimal-residual-disease board, where the question is preventing recurrence after surgery, not shrinking metastatic tumors. What a wave of small-molecule success does is validate the target and fund the diagnostic infrastructure that finds these patients — and it also means every oncology partner's KRAS budget is being spent somewhere else this quarter.

The second thing is a market observation worth filing. Verastem published data, raised a quarter of a billion dollars, and changed chief executives on the same morning. That is a company using a data day as a financing window and clearing out leadership risk while the window is open. The window is plainly still open for clinical data in a hot target class. It has not opened for platforms.

Which brings me to the last item, short, and it's a structure note. Lakefront Biotherapeutics, the company that used to be Galapagos, sold its preclinical C-A-R-T pipeline to a new Belgian company called Armed Cell Therapeutics. Eight million euros upfront, up to two hundred thirty million dollars in milestones, and a low-single-digit royalty. The buyer was formed by Paul Stoffels, who used to be chief scientific officer at Johnson and Johnson and then ran Galapagos, along with John Mellors and Peter Verhaeghe. The assets came originally from AboundBio, which Galapagos bought in twenty twenty-two.

Maisha and Emily, note the shape, because we have now seen it twice in two days. A preclinical program nobody inside wants to fund moves out to a purpose-built vehicle run by people who believe in it, with almost nothing paid up front and essentially all of the value in milestones and a royalty. Yesterday it was a seven-million-euro development milestone paid years after a small acquisition. Today it's an eight-million-euro upfront against two hundred thirty million in potential milestones — call it a three-and-a-half percent down payment on the headline. That ratio is the going rate for preclinical assets in a vehicle-formation deal right now, and it is a useful number to have in your head when we structure the next carve-out. It is also a reminder that the seller here is keeping a royalty, which is the part of these deals that survives if the buyer succeeds.

That's the watch. Freenome's updated colorectal screening data went to the gastroenterology plenary yesterday morning, and the market's reaction was a decline of about eleven percent on an improvement in precancerous-lesion detection from fourteen percent to eighteen — a reminder that for DELFI, what a blood test misses is read more harshly than what it finds. Kyverna's full stiff person syndrome dataset lands in Toronto on the twenty-first. Talk soon.
