There are three, and they map directly to what CAMP4 has now locked itself into. First, mechanism. CAMP4's approach works at the transcriptional door — an antisense oligo against a regulatory RNA upstream of the gene, releasing a brake so the cell makes more transcript. That is a specific mechanistic and intellectual-property lane. If our platform's mechanism is genuinely translation-level, that distinction is the whole story and we should be sharpening it now, not describing ourselves by analogy to them. Second, delivery. CAMP4 is intrathecal — into the spinal fluid, for central-nervous-system targets. Every haploinsufficiency that lives in the liver, in muscle, in the periphery is white space they are not dosing into. If our delivery reaches those tissues, that is not a nicer feature, it's a non-overlapping market. Third, the disease set itself. Twelve hundred disorders is a framing number, not a pipeline; they can prosecute a handful. The move is to plant our flag on the indications where turning protein up at the translation step has a real biological edge, and where they are demonstrably not — not to chase SYNGAP1 or the marquee names they've already claimed.
