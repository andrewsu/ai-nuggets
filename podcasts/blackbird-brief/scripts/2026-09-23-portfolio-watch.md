Otsuka and Ionis reported positive topline results from the Phase 3 FUSION trial of ulefnersen on Tuesday. Ulefnersen is an antisense oligonucleotide given intrathecally that binds F-U-S pre-messenger R-N-A and reduces production of F-U-S protein, including the mutant forms that kill motor neurons. F-U-S A-L-S is caused by pathogenic variants in the fused-in-sarcoma gene. It is about six-tenths of one percent of all A-L-S — but it is somewhere between forty-three and fifty-two percent of juvenile and pediatric A-L-S, and it often kills within one to two years of symptom onset. There has never been a therapy aimed at its genetic cause.

Here is the design, because the design is the item. The trial is a seamless Phase 1-through-3 study. Eighty-nine participants enrolled against a planned ceiling of ninety-five. Part one randomized two-to-one, drug against placebo, with the primary assessed at day five-oh-five. And the primary was not a single scale. It was a joint-rank analysis pooling three things: change in the A-L-S functional rating scale, time to rescue or discontinuation, and ventilation-assistance-free survival. Function and survival, ranked together. It returned a p-value of zero point zero zero zero five. Secondary endpoints also separated significantly, including serum neurofilament light and time to the earliest of death, permanent ventilation, rescue, or withdrawal for disease progression. Safety was favorable, most adverse events mild or moderate. Otsuka and Ionis are taking it to the F-D-A for an expedited pathway. Johns Hopkins was one of the twenty-five trial sites.

Three reads, and the first one closes a standing action. On September eighth, after Novartis lost its myotonic dystrophy Phase 3 to a video camera timing how fast a hand opens, we wrote a rule: before the biomarker plan is drafted, answer whether the clinical instrument we will be judged on can register the effect size we expect at the sample size we can afford. FUSION answers it, and it answers it in a specific way. The instrument that worked was not a better scale. It was a composite that pools function with survival so that a patient who dies and a patient who declines both land in the ranking. Set the three failures beside it: del-desiran lost at roughly a hundred fifty patients on a single-function endpoint. Novartis's TREM2 antibody lost at two hundred fifty-one patients on a function-and-survival composite — same instrument family — but the mechanism was microglial. Ulefnersen won at eighty-nine on the composite with a cell-intrinsic, genetically anchored target.
