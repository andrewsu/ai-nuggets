## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Portfolio Watch for Saturday, October third, and it covers two days, because yesterday's edition did not run. The biggest development for the portfolio is a price: Novartis paid five hundred and seventy-five million dollars upfront for a T-cell engager that the patient's own cells make, and it is pointed at autoimmune disease. Five items, and a short watch list at the end.

Start with the deal. On Friday Novartis signed a licence and option agreement with Abogen Biosciences of Suzhou. The centrepiece is A-B-O twenty-two oh three, an M-R-N-A in a lipid nanoparticle that encodes a CD19-by-CD3 T-cell engager. It is not a protein you manufacture and infuse. The patient's tissues translate the message and make the engager themselves. Five hundred and seventy-five million upfront, up to about seven point two billion in milestones if every option on every programme is exercised, plus royalties, and options on further assets from Abogen's R-N-A platform. Both companies' release says it has the potential to transform treatment "for patients with autoimmune diseases." Novartis declined to name indications. Fiona Marshall, who runs Novartis research, framed it as "durable immune reset."

Now look at the evidence behind the price. The only human data are from Abogen's April presentation, nine patients with relapsed or refractory B-cell lymphoma. Abogen reported no cytokine release syndrome, no dose-limiting toxicities, no neurotoxicity, and response rates that rose with dose, reaching a hundred percent at the top dose level. The registry also lists an Abogen study in refractory autoimmune disease, opened in December 2024 with a target of sixty-six patients. No autoimmune data has been disclosed. So the upfront payment buys a modality thesis, not a dataset.

Jon, Eddie, this connects to the control-architecture row we have been building since mid-September. The note from the twenty-ninth said what survives for Winnow is that an engager buys dose control and reversibility. Look at what Abogen is selling. It keeps the engager but gives up part of that control. Once the message is in, exposure is set by how fast the patient translates and clears it, not by an infusion pump you can switch off. Abogen's argument is that a slow, endogenous rise in exposure is exactly what avoids cytokine release. In other words, they are selling smoother exposure, not tighter control, and a pharma just paid half a billion upfront for it.

The same week, a Maryland company made the matching argument with cells. Cartesian Therapeutics, in Frederick, presented retreatment data on Descartes-08, its autologous anti-B-C-M-A CAR-T, at the neuromuscular meeting in Orlando on September twenty-ninth. Descartes-08 is M-R-N-A-engineered, so the CAR is not integrated and it fades. Five myasthenia gravis patients relapsed at least a year after their first course. They were retreated with the same six weekly outpatient infusions, with no lymphodepletion, a median of about sixteen and a half months later. At month twelve after retreatment they averaged a six point six point drop on the M-G activities-of-daily-living scale. All five had a clinically meaningful response and four of five held it to month twelve. No serious adverse events, no cytokine release, no neurotoxicity, no cytopenias. Their Phase 3, AURORA, reads out in the first quarter of 2027.

Two reads. First, redosability is no longer an engager-only claim. A transient-expression cell therapy just showed it in humans, and an in-vivo engager is being priced on it. The line we wrote on the twenty-ninth, that the defensible position is the antigen-specific cut, gets stronger this morning. Every product in this paragraph depletes a B-cell or plasma-cell compartment wholesale. Winnow's selectivity is the one claim neither of these deals touches, so lead with it, not with reversibility. Second, Eddie, this is a comp. Five hundred and seventy-five million upfront for a CD19 engager with nine lymphoma patients and no autoimmune data, from a pharma that just paused its own autoimmune CAR-T after three deaths, tells you what a large buyer will pay to own a safer immune-reset modality. That number belongs in the Winnow and Clasp financing decks. To be candid: five patients is a case series, the Abogen safety claims are company-reported off nine patients, and Novartis is under pressure after a bad September, which inflates the price.

Item two is ours. On Thursday the National Center to Accelerate Cures launched. It is housed at the Blackbird BioHub and funded by three million dollars in congressionally directed money secured by Senators Van Hollen and Alsobrooks. It is a partnership of three nonprofits: Blackbird Laboratories, the Critical Path Institute and the Arrowpoint Labs Foundation. Its job is to take new approach methodologies — human-cell chips, computational models, other non-animal tools — and build the regulatory-grade evidence packages F-D-A needs before it formally accepts a drug development tool. In year one it commits to supporting its first N-A-M regulatory submissions, publishing a shared terminology framework, and releasing an open data standard for N-A-M submissions.

You know the deal, so here is the operating read. Critical Path has taken more drug development tools through F-D-A qualification than anyone, and that relationship now sits inside our building. Two concrete asks. Hemaka, Yixuan: when NCAC picks its first high-priority areas, check whether any of them touches a portfolio programme's I-N-D-enabling package. A qualified human-relevant model in a programme's own indication is worth more to us than one in someone else's. Second, the F-D-A's direct final rule on N-A-Ms is open for comment until December seventh. We should file comments with NCAC's name on them, not just Blackbird's. The candid limit: three million dollars buys a secretariat and a first few qualification packages, not a validation lab. Whether this becomes durable infrastructure depends on getting industry money next year.

Item three is the C-O-P-D theme, and it lands two days after we called the eosinophil-low patient the last unowned position on the pathway. Pfizer presented full Phase 2 atopic dermatitis data on tilrekimig at the European dermatology congress on Thursday. Tilrekimig is a single trispecific antibody that binds I-L-four, I-L-thirteen and T-S-L-P, with a roughly thirty-seven-day half-life built for monthly dosing. The skin numbers: at week sixteen, fifty-eight, sixty-one and forty-eight percent of patients reached E-A-S-I seventy-five on the four hundred, two hundred and fifty milligram monthly doses, against nine percent on placebo. Pfizer says conjunctivitis ran lower than the rates reported for the I-L-four receptor drugs, which is a cross-trial comparison. Pfizer has Phase 3s dosing in atopic dermatitis, including head-to-head against Dupixent, and in asthma.

Here's the part for us. The release also confirms an ongoing Phase 2b/3 in C-O-P-D. On the registry it is eleven hundred and fifty-six patients on triple inhaled therapy with at least two moderate or severe exacerbations in the past year, and the key inclusion criteria posted have no eosinophil floor. Virginia, that changes the map. Our map put the alarmin layer and the type-2 layer in separate seats. Pfizer has put them in one molecule and is enrolling all comers. If the eosinophil-low patients respond, the residual we called unowned on Thursday gets claimed by a large pharma's combination, not by a single-mechanism entrant. If they don't, Pfizer's own subgroup becomes the cleanest proof yet that the residual needs a non-type-2 mechanism. Either way, that subgroup analysis is now the most important future data point for our C-O-P-D positioning. Put it on the calendar. To be candid: the full eligibility criteria may carry an eosinophil stratum the summary doesn't show, and there's no C-O-P-D efficacy data yet. RBC also flagged that it is unclear what T-S-L-P adds in skin.

Item four is for 1104health. On Wednesday A-R-P-A-H launched SURPASS, a five-year programme to build a "phaseless" trial design engine, a continuous inference engine meant to cut the size of conventional control groups, and an agentic operations layer to automate trial startup. Alongside it come three smaller projects. STACK uses A-I to speed site activation and to turn research-naive sites into sites that can run trials. COMMONS builds a national consent architecture. CINCH helps patients contribute their own real-world data. H-H-S framed all of this explicitly as competition with China and Australia on trial speed and cost.

Eddie, Maisha — STACK is 1104health's market thesis written as a federal programme: the community site that has never run a trial is the bottleneck, and the government is now paying to convert it. That cuts two ways. It validates the problem, and the agency is a potential non-dilutive customer or performer for exactly what 1104health does in community oncology. It also means a funded, A-I-first competitor for site conversion could emerge from the performer list. Read the solicitation for whether a clinician-workflow marketplace fits as a performer. If it does, apply. If it doesn't, the performer announcements are the competitive scan.

Item five is a sourcing item. The Maryland Stem Cell Research Commission announced four point eight million dollars across fifteen investigators in its first fiscal 2027 cycle. Three names to flag. Saima Riazuddin at U-M-B received a Launch award. She is co-P-I with Zubair Ahmed on the C-I-B-two and C-I-B-three hearing gene therapy we logged in July as a direct Aletira adjacency. Hee Cheol Cho at Johns Hopkins received a Validation award. His recent fund-backed work is electrically autonomous organoids for heart-rhythm disorders, which touches the cardiac theme. Taeyoung Hwang at the Lieber Institute also received a Launch award. Other awardees include Mary Kay Lobo and Christopher Ward at U-M-B, Elias Zambidis and Ludovic Zimmerlin at Hopkins, and SereNeuro Therapeutics, with a commercialization award for an osteoarthritis-pain cell therapy, which touches non-opioid pain. Esther, the release does not give project titles. Pull them before anyone assumes Riazuddin's award is the hearing work. Validation awards go to faculty with I-P that needs more validation, so check Cho's Validation project against J-H-T-V's filings first. The commission says the next cycle's request for applications comes in late October or early November. That is a cheap co-funding channel for any early project we are shepherding at either school.

The watch list, quickly. On Wednesday, at the Movement Disorders congress in Seoul, Arvinas presents late-breaking oculomotor and biomarker data from the Phase 1 of A-R-V one-oh-two. It is an oral, brain-penetrant PROTAC that degrades L-R-R-K-two. It is the first look at whether degrading the kinase, rather than inhibiting it, moves anything measurable in Parkinson's patients, and it bears on our Parkinson's theme. On fibrosis, Bristol Myers amended its Phase 3 of admilparant, an L-P-A-one antagonist, to monitor liver injury after a small number of events and one death. It is unclear whether that patient received drug, and outside monitors keep recommending the trial continue. The readout is due by year end. Any cardiac-fibrosis programme touching lysophosphatidic acid signalling should note the class's liver history before it gets a question about it.

That's the watch. Have a good weekend.
