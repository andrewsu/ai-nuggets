## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Monday, September fourteenth. The most important development for us happened Saturday, and it is an approval: the F-D-A cleared a drug whose label requires the patient to already be taking somebody else's genetic medicine. That is a market shape we should be designing for.

Here is what happened. On September thirteenth the F-D-A approved Scholar Rock's ISEMBYLD — apitegromab — for spinal muscular atrophy in adults and children two and older. Apitegromab is a fully human antibody that binds promyostatin and latent myostatin and stops myostatin from being activated. It does nothing to the genetic defect. It treats the muscle.

And read the indication carefully, because the indication is the whole item. It is approved for patients who are currently receiving an S-M-N two-targeted treatment. Not "may be taking." Currently receiving. Nusinersen or risdiplam. The label mandates the incumbent.

The pivotal was SAPPHIRE — global, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, a hundred eighty-eight patients aged two to twenty-one with 5q S-M-A across nine countries, all of them on background S-M-N therapy. The primary endpoint was change on the Hammersmith expanded motor scale at one year, and the drug delivered two point two points. Thirty-four percent of treated patients gained three points or more against thirteen and a half percent on placebo, an odds ratio of three point eight.

Two candid notes before the read. Both of those p-values are reported as nominal, meaning they were not alpha-controlled inside a testing hierarchy, and the agency approved anyway. And fractures occurred in nine percent of treated patients against two percent on placebo, which is a real signal in a pediatric population you are trying to make stronger.

Now the read, and it is for Geoff and for Matt. Genetic medicine in spinal muscular atrophy has been available since twenty sixteen. A decade later, the residual functional deficit in treated patients was large enough, durable enough, and measurable enough to support an entire second approved product with its own pivotal trial. The gene therapy did not close the gap. It moved the gap somewhere a different drug could be sold into.

Aletira should assume the same is true of congenital hearing loss, because we already have the evidence. Regeneron's otoferlin therapy, approved in April, reported eighty percent of evaluable patients with improved hearing. Improved. Not normal. There is a residual there, and nobody has named it, priced it, or built an endpoint for it — and the residual in hearing is not audiogram threshold, it is speech perception in noise and auditory processing, which is exactly the layer that a threshold-based approval never has to demonstrate.

So there are two questions, and they run in opposite directions. The opportunity question is whether a G-J-B two program should be positioning for a second product on top of its own first one. The discipline question is harder and more urgent: Scholar Rock had the Hammersmith scale — a validated, granular functional instrument sensitive enough to register a two-point increment on top of a genetic medicine in a hundred eighty-eight patients. What is the equivalent instrument in hearing? If the answer is "we would have to develop one," that belongs in the pre-I-N-D ask, next to the external-control question we flagged on the fourth when uniQure filed in Huntington's. You cannot sell into a residual you cannot measure.

Second item, and it is the most directly useful thing I have read for the oncology surface targets theme in a month.

On Saturday at the World Conference on Lung Cancer in Seoul, Whitehawk Therapeutics presented two real-world analyses of P-T-K seven — protein tyrosine kinase seven — in pretreated, E-G-F-R wild-type non-small cell lung cancer. Whitehawk has a next-generation antibody-drug conjugate against that target in Phase one. The analyses were run with Tempus A-I, on real-world tumor samples, not in a trial.

What they asked is the interesting part. Three questions, none of which is "does our drug work."

First, does the target survive treatment? P-T-K seven expression stayed largely stable after chemotherapy, immunotherapy, chemo-immunotherapy and tyrosine kinase inhibitors — and, explicitly, with less treatment-associated variation than M-E-T, P-D-L one and integrin beta six, three targets in late-stage antibody-drug conjugate development. That is a comparative claim against named competitors, generated without running a comparative trial.

Second, does it depend on genotype? Expression held steady across tumors with and without K-RAS G twelve C, A-L-K and M-E-T alterations.

Third — and this is the one nobody asks early enough — is the target prognostic? It is not. P-T-K seven expression was not independently associated with overall or progression-free survival; outcomes tracked performance status and prior therapy. Which means it behaves as a target-engagement marker rather than a prognostic confounder, so a response in a high-expressing patient is attributable to the drug and not to the patient having had a better disease all along.

Avi, Eddie — I want that turned into a checklist. Our oncology surface targets theme is a bet on antigens, and those three questions are the ones that decide whether an antigen is buildable, and all three are answerable from a clinico-genomic database before anybody makes a conjugate. Does expression survive standard of care. Is it independent of the driver mutations. Is it prognostic. A surface target that fails the first question gives you a drug that stops working in exactly the second-line population you designed it for.

The candid limit, and we should say it because Whitehawk did not: P-T-K seven-low tumors were enriched for KEAP-one and S-T-K eleven alterations. Those are the immunotherapy-refractory, poor-prognosis subsets — so the target is dimmest precisely where the unmet need is greatest. That is a real constraint on the commercial case, and it is sitting in their own data.

The other half of this item is the cheap part. A Phase one company bought competitive differentiation against three later-stage rivals from a database partnership. No patients, no sites, no protocol. For a Blackbird program going into a corp-dev conversation with preclinical data, that is a real and affordable way to put a target's durability on the record before anyone asks.

Third item. The cancer vaccine field published a scorecard yesterday, and it sorts by setting rather than by platform — which matters for a company in our building.

The rundown covers five challengers to Merck and Moderna. Roche and BioNTech's autogene cevumeran, a personalized messenger-R-N-A neoantigen therapy, showed seven of eight responders alive four to six years after surgery in Phase one pancreatic cancer against a thirteen percent five-year baseline — and had its Phase two colorectal study halted for futility in August. BioNTech's two off-the-shelf constructs are mid-stage in head and neck and lung. OSE's Tedopi is an off-the-shelf T-cell epitope vaccine with Phase three data due late twenty twenty-seven. Agenus is running a dual-antibody combination. And Elicio's ELI-zero-zero-two, a K-RAS peptide vaccine, missed its Phase two primary endpoint in June in K-RAS-driven pancreatic cancer, then found benefit in completely resected tumors on biomarker analysis and is advancing into Phase three in that subset only.

Now line those up by setting instead of by modality. Every positive signal in that list is in resected or early disease. Every clean failure is in established or metastatic disease. Colorectal autogene failed; pancreatic, post-surgery, did not. Elicio failed overall and survived in the resected subset. Merck and Moderna's Phase three win was adjuvant melanoma.

That is the exact board Adventris is playing. Their mutant-K-RAS vaccine has three Phase one readouts, and the differentiated one is interception — twenty people with hereditary pancreatic cancer risk and a radiographic lesion but no cancer, vaccine alone with no checkpoint inhibitor, ninety percent T-cell response, cyst reduction in about thirty-eight percent against seven percent unvaccinated. Eddie, the useful thing to carry into that conversation is that the field's own failure pattern now argues for their setting. The vaccine modality is not failing. It is failing in metastatic disease, which is where most of the capital went.

And we should note what is underneath all of it, because it touches a second thing of ours. BioSpace also reported yesterday that messenger R-N-A has recovered its footing — the roughly five hundred million dollars of BARDA contracts cancelled in August twenty twenty-five, then the F-D-A approving Moderna's flu vaccine on the one-year anniversary, then the Merck-Moderna Phase three melanoma win in eleven hundred thirty-seven patients. The quote worth having is from Jeff Coller at Johns Hopkins, who called it a reinvigoration of the space. Adventris's delivery arm is a trans-amplifying R-N-A vector, and the Mao lab lipid nanoparticle work is on our sourcing list. Both were harder to fund eighteen months ago than they are today.

Fourth item, and it is happening this afternoon about thirty-five miles from us.

The F-D-A holds a public hearing today, twelve-thirty to four-thirty Eastern, at White Oak in Silver Spring, on the potential future therapeutic use of psychedelic drugs. It implements the April executive order on accelerating treatments for serious mental illness, it follows the agency finalizing its psychedelic clinical trial guidance in July, and the written comment docket stays open until October fifth. The agenda is provider training, patient safety protocols, access, data collection standards, and manufacturing and distribution infrastructure. Compass's psilocybin program is expected to reach a decision by early twenty twenty-seven, and Definium reports topline Phase three data in generalized anxiety disorder this morning — two hundred forty-five patients, an oral L-S-D tablet at one hundred or fifty micrograms against placebo, twelve-week change on the Hamilton anxiety scale.

Eight days ago we looked at psilocybin preventing chemotherapy-induced pain hypersensitivity through peripheral neurons and concluded that all the psychedelic capital has gone to psychiatry and nobody is bidding on the peripheral receptor. Today's hearing sharpens that, and in our favor. What the agency is about to institutionalize is the expensive part — supervised and supportive settings, trained providers, monitored administration. That is a cost structure bolted permanently onto every psychiatric psychedelic. A peripherally acting, non-hallucinogenic asset dosed once before chemotherapy carries none of it, and the gap between those two cost structures is about to be written into federal guidance.

For the non-opioid pain theme and the Gould lab work at U-M-B, that is the argument, and there is a comment docket open for three more weeks to make it in.

Last, short. Novartis finished last week down thirteen percent, at a two hundred twelve billion dollar market capitalization, after three assets came apart in five days. We covered the events. What is new is the price. Vontobel took a three billion dollar peak sales line for the myotonic dystrophy asset out of the model entirely and cut its target by three Swiss francs. Guggenheim cut its risk-adjusted twenty thirty-three forecast for the same drug forty-five percent, from one point seven six billion to nine hundred sixty million. Novartis paid twelve billion for that company in February.

Emily — that is a usable number. One Phase three miss took roughly half the modeled value off an asset that a large-cap had already paid twelve billion for seven months earlier. When we build a value case around a program's probability of success, that is the magnitude of the downside branch, and it is worth having a real figure rather than a haircut.

Quiet where it matters. Nothing on Clasp, where the monotherapy readout is still guided to this year. Nothing on G-P-R fifty-two or the Nxera out-licensing process, with under four months left in the stated year. Nothing new on NuGate, Winnow, Artemyx, Biolinco or 1104health. That is the watch. Have a good Monday.
