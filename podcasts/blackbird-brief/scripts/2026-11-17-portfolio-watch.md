## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Tuesday, November seventeenth. Three items, and the first one is the second half of yesterday's argument — a different group, a different vehicle, and the same conclusion about where selectivity has to live.

Start with Aletira.

Yesterday the news was Seawolf, a hundred million dollars and a Sanofi deal behind lipid nanoparticles carrying D-N-A, betting that the field solves gene delivery by abandoning the capsid. Today, in Nature Biotechnology, a group at Nanjing University's Drum Tower Hospital working with collaborators at Yale published the opposite bet, executed well. They took the A-A-V six capsid and evolved it directly in primary human T cells, three rounds of selection in two independent donors, and pulled out a family of variants sharing a three-residue motif in one of the surface loops. The lead variant transduces human T cells and natural killer cells at doses roughly a hundred to a thousand times lower than parental A-A-V six. A genome-scale CRISPR screen told them why: the evolved capsid has acquired the ability to engage C-D seven, a surface receptor expressed on T cells, natural killer cells and their progenitors and almost nowhere else.

The functional numbers are good. Knocking a chimeric antigen receptor into the T-cell receptor locus, they got over seventy percent knock-in at a multiplicity of a thousand, where the parent capsid gave ten to eighteen percent under identical conditions. That held for large cargo — a bispecific receptor cassette of about two point six kilobases, and a T-cell receptor cassette of about three. In mice they injected the capsid systemically and made C-A-R T cells inside the animal, and those animals controlled leukemia and pancreatic tumors where the parental capsid did essentially nothing. A patent has been filed, with the senior author and the five co-first authors as inventors.

Now, Matt, Avi — the sentence that matters for us is in the discussion, and the authors wrote it themselves rather than making a reviewer drag it out of them. The capsid did not stop using the ordinary A-A-V receptor. It kept that pathway and added C-D seven on top. Their own words are that this is a layered entry mechanism, with the canonical receptor providing a body-wide baseline everywhere and C-D seven conferring a lineage-restricted enhancement in T and natural killer cells. And then the concession: they interpret the profile as favorable lineage-restricted detargeting relative to A-A-V six, rather than absolute organ-level specificity. Biodistribution bears that out — less liver and lung than the parent, no significant difference in heart, brain or skeletal muscle.

So read the two days together. Yesterday: a vehicle with no tropism at all, where any cell-type selectivity has to come from somewhere other than the particle. Today: state-of-the-art capsid engineering, a directed-evolution campaign against a genuinely lineage-restricted receptor, published in Nature Biotechnology — and what it buys is enrichment, not restriction. There is still a floor of expression everywhere in the body, because the old entry route never went away. That is the argument for SELEXON stated by someone with no interest in making it. Targeting the vehicle shifts the distribution. It does not set anything to zero. A cassette that gates expression after entry is the complement to capsid engineering, not a competitor to it, and it is the only thing in either system that can actually turn the floor off.

Which reinforces yesterday's action rather than changing it. Prove the cassette outside an A-A-V genome before the Series A, and when you write the positioning, write it as "works with your capsid, including your best one," not "instead of." The honest framing is that the better the capsid gets, the more the residual off-target expression is the thing standing between a program and its therapeutic index — and that residual is exactly what we sell.

Two secondary notes. First, Avi, the paper names autoimmunity and cardiac fibrosis as the next applications, and specifically selectively eliminating activated fibroblasts. Those are two of our standing project themes, and this is a delivery chassis pointed at both. Second, a discipline point on the headline. The in vivo C-A-R T experiment required human T cells to be transferred into the mouse first, and then the capsid injected. That is not a patient receiving one injection and making their own C-A-R T cells; it is a humanized model with four or five animals per group. When we see this capsid cited as in vivo C-A-R T — and we will, because the field is hungry for it — remember what the experiment actually was.

Item two is NuGate, and it is the day's biggest competitive event.

QurAlis announced yesterday that the F-D-A cleared its investigational new drug application for Q-R-L two-oh-one, which opens the path to a pivotal U.S. trial called CONQUR in the first half of twenty twenty-seven, with the design to be disclosed early next year. Q-R-L two-oh-one is a first-in-class antisense oligonucleotide that restores stathmin-2. Stathmin-2 is the gene most strongly regulated by T-D-P forty-three in humans and the one most consistently lost across ALS expression studies — when T-D-P forty-three leaves the nucleus, the stathmin-2 message mis-splices and the full-length protein disappears. That is the same cryptic-splicing biology we have been tracking all month.

They released the final dose-range-finding data from the ANQUR study alongside the clearance. At day two hundred fifty-three the ten-milligram group showed a fifty percent reduction in the rate of functional decline on the ALS functional rating scale versus placebo, at p less than point oh five. Cerebrospinal fluid phosphorylated neurofilament heavy fell forty-six percent from baseline in the three-milligram group at day one ninety-seven. Plasma neurofilament light fell eighteen percent from baseline at three milligrams and thirty-six percent from baseline at ten milligrams at day four twenty-one — landing at roughly one point four to one point seven times healthy levels, which the company points out is comparable to what genetically targeted ALS drugs achieve in familial subpopulations. No serious adverse events in that phase. Merit Cudkowicz, who runs the Healey Center, sits on their clinical advisory board and provided the supportive quote.

Hemaka, Anthony, Jon — three things.

The first is that the chain is intact, and that is the part to internalize. Back in October we set out the standard for an antisense program: target engagement leads to knockdown, knockdown leads to neurofilament movement, and each link needs its own evidence. QurAlis has now shown all three links in sporadic patients, in a randomized setting, with a functional signal on top. That is the comparator package, and the bar it sets for NuGate's pre-IND work is now concrete rather than hypothetical.

The second is the dose story, and I want us candid about it because it cuts both ways. The functional result is at ten milligrams. The cerebrospinal fluid biomarker result is at three. The plasma biomarker is significant at both but the versus-placebo comparison at three milligrams is p equals point oh seven, which is not significant. And the open-label extension is running on three. This is a company release with roughly seventeen patients per arm reporting one significant functional endpoint and a scatter of biomarker endpoints across two doses and several timepoints. I would not build a competitive model on the fifty percent number. I would build it on the fact that the F-D-A looked at the whole package and cleared a pivotal.

The third is the one for the pre-IND package, and it is the trap we flagged two weeks ago. ALS and frontotemporal dementia and a third of Alzheimer's all carry T-D-P forty-three pathology, and the QurAlis release says exactly that. The temptation is to write a label ambition that spans them. Remember the hepatitis B precedent from October: the agency did not grant a broad label, it granted a label shaped exactly like the population where the mechanism dominates, gated on an authorized assay. Watch what population CONQUR actually enrolls when the design publishes early next year, because that is the agency telling us, for free, how narrow it thinks a cryptic-splicing claim has to be.

Last item, and it is for Winnow. It lands one day after we put complement on the map, from the other direction.

Yesterday we added a third answer to the depletion question — Commit Biologics in Copenhagen, recruiting complement to kill B cells instead of recruiting a T cell. Yesterday as well, the F-D-A approved gefurulimab, branded Klygefa, from AstraZeneca's Alexion. It is a complement drug pointed the other way: a dual-binding nanobody that blocks C5 in the terminal cascade, with a second arm that binds serum albumin to extend its half-life. The European Commission approved it the same day. The indication is generalized myasthenia gravis in adults positive for acetylcholine receptor antibodies — about eighty-five percent of the disease, roughly eighty-one thousand people in the United States out of about ninety-eight and a half thousand.

The pivotal was PREVAIL: two hundred sixty patients, one-to-one against placebo, a hundred thirteen sites in twenty countries, twenty-six weeks. The daily-living score improved four point two points against two point six on placebo, a treatment difference of one point six. The quantitative score improved four point five against two point four. Forty-six percent of treated patients hit a five-point or greater improvement on that score, against twenty-five percent. Separation on daily living was visible by week one. And here is the commercially load-bearing detail: it is a weight-based, once-weekly, self-administered subcutaneous autoinjector. Alexion's own two C5 drugs, Soliris and Ultomiris, are infusions.

Virginia, Jon — two reads.

The first is a correction to how we filed yesterday's item. Complement in autoimmunity is not a frontier, it is a mature franchise with an approved standard of care, and it arrives with a boxed warning, a risk-management program, and mandatory meningococcal vaccination before dosing. When Commit Biologics pitches depletion without cytokine release, the regulatory conversation they walk into is not blank — it is this one, and it comes with an established infection liability for interfering with terminal complement at all. That is a real differentiation problem for them and it is useful to us, because it means answer three is encumbered in a way that answer two is not.

The second read is the sharper one and it is about the bar, not the mechanism. Myasthenia gravis is precisely where the lead autoimmune cell therapy is running its registrational trial — Kyverna's KYSA-6, seven of seven patients with clinically meaningful improvement at twenty-four weeks, eighty-six percent off immunosuppressants. As of yesterday, the alternative that prescriber sees is a weekly autoinjector the patient gives themselves at home, approved, with a one-point-six-point effect size and separation inside a week. A cell therapy does not beat that on convenience and it never will. It beats it on the thing a weekly injection cannot do, which is stop. Drug-free remission, durable, off all therapy. That is the same conclusion yesterday's lupus item produced from completely different data, and two independent routes to the same answer in two days is worth treating as settled. Build the first clinical claim on depth and time off drug, in patients who have failed the convenient options — not on a scale where a nanobody with an autoinjector is already good enough.

That is the watch. A beautifully engineered capsid proves, in its own discussion section, that vehicle targeting buys enrichment and not restriction, which is the argument for a selectivity cassette made by a disinterested party. QurAlis has an I-N-D, a pivotal and a complete biomarker chain in sporadic ALS, and the thing to watch is which population their trial enrolls. And complement is now an approved, self-administered, boxed-warning franchise in myasthenia gravis — which tells us what a cell therapy has to be worth. Back tomorrow.

**LEAD (Aletira / SELEXON; secondary Winnow + cardiac-fibrosis theme) — a directed-evolution AAV capsid that engages CD7 still can't switch off the body-wide floor, and the authors say so themselves.**
Hong, Chen, Liu, Cao, Li et al. (Institute of Modern Biology / Dept. of Hematology, Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital, Nanjing University; with Yale School of Medicine Genetics + Laboratory Medicine; senior author **Lupeng Ye**), *Nature Biotechnology*, published 2026-11-16, **open access, full text read.** doi:10.1038/s41587-026-03344-5 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03344-5
- **AAV.APR31 (a.k.a. HONG31)**: AAV6 capsid evolved directly in primary human T cells (3 rounds, 2 donors); family of variants carrying an **APR motif at residues 455–457 in VR-IV**. Transduces primary human T and NK cells at **~100–1,000-fold lower MOI than AAV6** (10²–10³ vs 10⁵–10⁶ vg/cell) and outperforms the human T-cell-tropic capsid **AAV-hT7**.
- **Mechanism:** genome-scale CRISPR screen identified **CD7** as a lineage-specific entry enhancer alongside the canonical **AAVR** pathway; structural modeling puts the APR motif at a novel CD7-binding interface; CD7 overexpression in CD7-negative NALM-6 B cells raised transduction.
- **Function:** **>70% knock-in** of CD22-, MSLN- and GD2-CAR into *TRAC* at MOI 1,000 vs **10–18% for AAV6**; held for a **~2.6-kb bispecific CD19.20-CAR** and a **~3-kb NY-ESO-1 TCR**; also CAR-NK and transposon (MAJESTIC) delivery. In humanized NOG mice, systemic scAAV-CAG-EGFP gave **45.0% EGFP+ human CD3+ splenic T cells vs 18.2% for AAV6** at 48 h (n=5/group). Systemic CD22-CAR in NALM-6-GL leukemia and MSLN-CAR in AsPC-1 pancreatic xenografts controlled tumor and extended survival; **AAV6 did essentially nothing** (n=4–5/group).
- **Competing interests: a patent has been filed**, inventors L.Y., L.H., Y.L., G.C., Y.C., X.L.
- **⇒ READ (Matt Tremblay + Avi Khanna) — THE DISCUSSION CONTAINS OUR PITCH, WRITTEN BY A DISINTERESTED PARTY.** Authors' own framing: a **"layered-entry mechanism"** — retained AAVR gives a **CD7-independent baseline throughout the body**, on which CD7 confers lineage-restricted enhancement; they explicitly call the result **"favorable lineage-restricted detargeting relative to AAV6 rather than absolute organ-level specificity."** Biodistribution: reduced liver and lung vs AAV6, **no significant difference in heart, brain or skeletal muscle.** **⇒ Capsid engineering shifts the distribution; it does not set anything to zero. A post-entry expression gate is the only thing in either architecture that can. Pairs with the 11-16 Seawolf lead: a DNA-LNP has no tropism at all, and the best capsid in the literature still leaks — same conclusion from both ends.**
- **⇒ POSITIONING (Avi Khanna): write SELEXON as "works with your capsid, including your best one," not "instead of." The better the capsid, the more the residual off-target expression is the binding constraint on therapeutic index — and that residual is the product.**
- **⇒ THEME NOTE: the paper names autoimmunity and *cardiac fibrosis* (selective elimination of activated fibroblasts) as next applications — two standing Blackbird project themes with a delivery chassis now pointed at them.**
- **⇒ DISCIPLINE NOTE: the "in vivo CAR-T" headline is a humanized model — human T cells adoptively transferred FIRST, then capsid injected, n=4–5 mice. Not a patient making their own CAR-T from one injection. Expect the citation to drift.**
