# The Blackbird Brief — Portfolio Watch — 2026-10-08

## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Thursday, October eighth. The most important development for the portfolio: an orphan G-protein-coupled receptor expressed in the same striatal neurons as G-P-R fifty-two just won a Phase Three. The program is in Parkinson's, not schizophrenia, and it pushes the receptor in the opposite direction, but it is the closest thing to a precedent the G-P-R fifty-two NewCo has had.

The facts first. Cerevance, the Takeda spinout in Boston, reported yesterday at the Movement Disorders congress in Seoul that its Phase Three trial, called ARISE, met its primary endpoint. The drug is solengepras, a once-daily oral inverse agonist of G-P-R six. Three hundred forty-one patients with Parkinson's who still had motor fluctuations on levodopa were randomized to placebo, seventy-five milligrams or a hundred fifty milligrams for twelve weeks. They started with about five and a half hours a day of "off" time. At the high dose, off time fell by about an hour and a half, against just under an hour on placebo. That is a placebo-adjusted benefit of zero point six one hours, with a p-value of zero point zero three five. "On" time without troublesome dyskinesia, the key secondary, improved by zero point six hours and only just cleared significance. Daily-living motor scores and daytime sleepiness also improved. There were no serious adverse events at the high dose, and discontinuations for side effects were the same as placebo. Cerevance gave no efficacy numbers for the low dose. It will now meet F-D-A about a new drug application.

Why this matters to us is the biology. G-P-R six and G-P-R fifty-two are both orphan receptors, both couple to G-s and raise cyclic AMP, and both sit almost entirely in the striatal neurons that carry the dopamine D-two receptor, which form the brain's indirect motor pathway. In those cells, D-two signalling lowers cyclic AMP. Solengepras turns G-P-R six's constant background signalling down, so it mimics more dopamine at D-two, and that is what Parkinson's needs. A G-P-R fifty-two agonist does the reverse. It pushes cyclic AMP up in the same cells, mimicking D-two blockade, and that is the reason it is expected to act as an antipsychotic. So the two programs are mirror images in the same neurons.

Three reads for the G-P-R fifty-two NewCo. First, the evidentiary one. Two weeks ago, when tavapadon was approved, we noted that G-P-R fifty-two has no approved drug class to borrow confirmatory evidence from: orphan receptor, mechanism not well understood, nothing on the market. If solengepras is approved, there will be an approved drug on an orphan G-s receptor in exactly this cell type, with human safety data on adjusting cyclic AMP there through a drug taken every day. It does not count as a class, because the direction and the indication are both different, but Hemaka should have it in the pre-I-N-D briefing book as the nearest mechanistic precedent.

Second, the risk it makes concrete. Solengepras shows that changing cyclic AMP tone in these neurons changes movement in people. Turn that around: an agonist that raises it is pushing toward the motor side effects of D-two blockade. The preclinical G-P-R fifty-two literature says the class avoids catalepsy, and that may hold. But that argument now has a human counterexample from the other direction, so Avi should make sure the development plan includes a prespecified motor-safety readout, ideally an extrapyramidal symptom scale from the first patient study, rather than leaving it as an assumption.

Third, the platform and the bidder. Cerevance says solengepras was validated on its own platform, which sequences nuclei from specific cell types in donated human brain to find targets restricted to the cells that matter. That is the same thesis as the Lieber target-generation work built on the postmortem brain collection, and a cell-type-restricted target now has a pivotal win to point to. Cerevance has stayed private since twenty-sixteen and paid for the end of this trial with a twenty-million-dollar Series C. Eddie, two notes. The paper that first proposed G-P-R fifty-two as a psychiatric target came out of Takeda's own C-N-S unit in twenty-fourteen, and Takeda now has a Phase Three read on its sister receptor through a company it helped create, so Takeda belongs on the bidder list. And Cerevance itself, if this gets approved, becomes a natural buyer for anything else that works in this cell type.

The candid caveat. Six-tenths of an hour is a normal size for an adjunct in Parkinson's, not a large one. The reason to prescribe will be tolerability and the non-motor benefits rather than the size of the effect. And the same drug failed on its own last year: in the Phase Two called ASCEND, in early untreated patients, it missed its primary endpoint because the physician-rated motor exam did not separate from placebo. The fair reading is that turning down G-P-R six helps when dopamine treatment is already on board, and is weak without it. That is a useful warning for any G-P-R fifty-two plan that assumes monotherapy. None of it changes the reads above, which depend on the mechanism working in people, not on the size of the market.

Two quick Parkinson's notes from the same congress. Lundbeck, on our G-P-R fifty-two bidder list, started a hundred-fifty-patient randomized Phase Two of an oral dopamine-agonist prodrug, so its own Parkinson's bet is still on the dopamine side. And Arvinas showed that its brain-penetrant LRRK2 degrader produced dose-dependent improvement in an objective eye-movement measure over twenty-eight days, with falls in spinal-fluid LRRK2 tracking the change. That fits our rule for this theme, a measurable defect, though the release gives no figure for how much LRRK2 fell.

Second item, on Aletira and on how we write licences. Ultragenyx signed an agreement on Tuesday to sell a rare pediatric disease priority review voucher for two hundred ten million dollars in cash. That is above every twenty-twenty-six sale we have on file, where prices ran from about one hundred eighty to two hundred million. The voucher came with the August approval of Genglycos, its liver-directed AAV gene therapy for glycogen storage disease type one-a. The detail that matters is in the eight-K. Under a twenty-eighteen patent licence with N-I-H, twenty percent of the gross proceeds goes to N-I-H, which is forty-two million dollars to the licensor without it running a single trial.

Two reads. For Emily and Eddie, this is the residuals principle we wrote down in September when the Incyte voucher was awarded, now with a number on it: develop to proof, license to an operator, keep a share of the one-time windfalls. A twenty percent voucher share is apparently a term a government licensor can get. So any Blackbird grant agreement or licence that could end in a pediatric rare-disease approval should say in writing who owns the voucher and what share comes back. If Johns Hopkins or U-M-B is negotiating that term and we are not, the residual goes to someone else. For Aletira, the arithmetic is unchanged. The program cannot reach approval before the voucher program ends in September twenty-twenty-nine on its own, so a voucher only comes into play through a partner already in the clinic. What changes is the price. In September, analysts said Ultragenyx's gene therapies would be valued more on their vouchers than on their sales. That view now has a sale price behind it, and it belongs in any Aletira partnering negotiation.

Third, a financing signal for Winnow and for the cash-shell question. Caribou Biosciences, Jennifer Doudna's CRISPR company, is discontinuing its entire clinical pipeline, halting its trials and making what it calls a substantial cut to its staff. It had a lymphoma CAR-T with an F-D-A-agreed Phase Three design, a regenerative medicine designation, an eighty-two percent response rate and no graft-versus-host disease, and it could not raise the money for the pivotal study. Leerink's Daina Graybosch put it bluntly: investors were unwilling to fund the trial. Back in February another allogeneic CAR-T chief executive called the sector a nuclear winter, and the money is going to autologous and in vivo approaches instead.

For Winnow, the read is good news with a caveat. The capital markets are now deciding between cell therapy approaches on modality before they look at data, and an off-the-shelf protein engager sits on the side that is getting funded. The caveat is that the sector being punished is the one that made the "off-the-shelf" argument first. Jon should make sure Winnow's version of that argument is about dose control and reversibility, not convenience alone. For Emily and Eddie, Caribou had a hundred fourteen million dollars at the end of June and is now reviewing strategic alternatives. That is exactly the cash-carrying Nasdaq shell with a dissolved pipeline that we listed in September as a third exit route, and it is worth tracking where Caribou ends up.

Fourth, a short one on the infections theme, with a Hopkins spinout behind it. Pearl Diagnostics in Baltimore, built on Johns Hopkins technology and founded by the transplant infectious-disease physician Kieren Marr, published the clinical studies behind the F-D-A clearance of its urine test for invasive aspergillosis in Clinical Infectious Diseases this week. The assay detects Aspergillus antigens carried on fungal extracellular vesicles in urine. In a case-control study it picked up forty-six of fifty patients with invasive aspergillosis, which is ninety-two percent sensitivity, with specificity of about eighty-eight percent. The more useful figure is that nineteen of twenty-four patients whose blood galactomannan test was negative still tested positive at least once on urine. Full data come at IDWeek on October twenty-second.

The read for Yixuan and Eddie: this is a cleared, Baltimore-based, Hopkins-derived diagnostic for the immunocompromised patients an anti-infective program would enroll, and a noninvasive test that catches patients the standard blood test misses is what makes such a trial enrollable. Pearl is not a portfolio company. Treat it as a local company to know and a possible stratification partner. Candidly, case-control numbers flatter sensitivity, and a specificity in the high eighties will produce false positives in a low-prevalence screening setting, so the prospective IDWeek data decide how far to take this.

That is the watch. One date: GRAIL's Edinburgh posters run through today with ESMO from October twenty-third, with the F-D-A decision on Galleri still pending, which is the DELFI read-through to watch.
