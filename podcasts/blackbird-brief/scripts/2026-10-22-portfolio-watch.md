## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Thursday, October twenty-second. The most important development for the portfolio is that the company selling the instruments on both sides of our chronic-infections thesis is being taken private, and its American headquarters is forty miles from Baltimore.

QIAGEN agreed yesterday to an all-cash takeover by EQT Private Equity at forty-six euros and fifty cents a share — a forty percent premium to where the stock sat before takeover reports surfaced in July, valuing the company at about twelve point one billion dollars including roughly a billion dollars of net debt. Both boards recommend it unanimously. Closing is expected in the middle of twenty twenty-seven.

So what for Blackbird. Three weeks ago we logged QIAGEN as the fourth instrument on the chronic-infections map, when the FDA cleared its gram-negative bloodstream panel to sit alongside the gram-positive one — thirty-three pathogen targets and twenty-eight resistance markers across the pair, about an hour from a positive blood culture. The read then was that the instrumentation around our theme is filling in from both ends and leaving the middle open: the tolerance phenotype in the Chatterjee lab work at U-M-B has no gene to report, so it comes back susceptible on the panel and the infection survives the course anyway. We wrote down an action — have the conversation with QIAGEN before they are the competitor rather than the collaborator. That action now has a clock on it.

Here is the candid version. EQT has real life-science operating history, and the agreement carries commitments on footprint and research spending. But a sponsor financing a twelve-billion-dollar buyout prices every product line against cash generation, and an exploratory partnership with a pre-company academic program is the kind of thing that gets slower during an eight-month regulatory close, not faster. Eddie, Yixuan — the window is now, while the current commercial team still owns its own roadmap, and the ask should be small and concrete: can a panel of theirs be made to read something other than susceptibility. One local note. Our own reference has QIAGEN's United States headquarters in Germantown, Maryland. The release says nothing about the American footprint either way and I will not infer anything from silence, but a Maryland site of that size changing hands is something Matt should know before someone asks him at a regional event.

The second item is a modality approval that NuGate should put in its deck this week. On Tuesday the FDA approved GSK's bepirovirsen, branded Hibsago, for chronic hepatitis B. It is the first antisense oligonucleotide ever approved in that disease, it came through Priority Review with Breakthrough Therapy designation, and GSK licensed it from Ionis back in twenty nineteen.

The numbers are unusual enough to say out loud. Pooled across the two B-Well Phase Three trials, nineteen percent of treated patients achieved functional cure against zero on placebo, rising to twenty-six percent in those starting with surface antigen at or below a thousand international units per milliliter — again against zero. Functional cure means viral DNA and surface antigen undetectable for at least twenty-four weeks after stopping all treatment, and standard of care achieves under one percent in a year. It is a six-month course, self-administered after the first dose, with a boxed warning for on-treatment hepatic flares and thrombocytopenia.

Two things for NuGate. The modality read first: an antisense oligonucleotide just produced a durable, off-treatment, binary outcome in a chronic disease and the agency approved it on that basis. That is the best precedent the class has had outside neurology, and it is what to cite with a generalist who still thinks antisense means a permanent infusion schedule. The second is the part Hemaka should actually use. The label is gated on a test. It covers patients without cirrhosis, on nucleoside therapy for at least six months, with suppressed viral DNA and surface antigen at or below three thousand units, as determined by an FDA-authorized assay. The drug did not get a broad label; it got one shaped exactly like the population where the mechanism works. That is the same bargain NuGate will be offered, and it argues against the pitch line that the asset treats all forms of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and frontotemporal dementia. Pick the population where the cryptic-splicing mechanism is the dominant driver and own it. The boxed warning is the other half of the trade — a one-course, off-treatment outcome gets paid for in on-treatment tolerability.

A footnote. The same morning, GSK confirmed it has terminated the Phase One study of a PAPD5 and PAPD7 inhibitor it had been running in hepatitis B since twenty twenty-two, and would not say whether the asset is dead. With a TLR8 agonist already scrapped, the only significant follow-up left is a Phase Two short interfering RNA. A lead approval is when the follow-ons get cut, not when they get funded.

Third item, and it moves the bar on one of our live sourcing leads. At the joint ACTRIMS–ECTRIMS meeting in Toronto, which runs through tomorrow, Roche presented late-breaking Phase Three data on fenebrutinib, its oral BTK inhibitor. In relapsing MS over ninety-six weeks and primary progressive MS over a hundred and twenty weeks, it reduced the volume of slowly expanding lesions by up to thirty percent, and in relapsing MS it cut the accumulation of paramagnetic rim lesions by sixty-two percent, both against an active comparator. Roche calls this the first prospective demonstration of a therapeutic effect on chronic active lesions in a Phase Three trial. The supporting package is strong — fifty-five percent reduction in annualized relapse rate, twenty-three percent less annual brain volume loss, sixteen percent on twelve-week confirmed disability progression. Roche filed at the end of September and the FDA has accepted it.

So what for Blackbird, and this is for Avi and Eddie. In September we wrote down that a second BTK inhibitor hitting disability progression turned the Kornberg liver-X-receptor remyelination program at Johns Hopkins into an add-on program, and that the open question was no longer whether LXR activation restores oligodendrocyte maturation under interferon-gamma — the paper answered that in mouse and human cells — but what the add-on readout is. This data set narrows that question. Chronic active lesions, the smoldering rim pathology, were the compartment a repair agent was supposed to own by default, and a pill about to be approved now has prospective Phase Three evidence of moving them. That does not kill the thesis. It means the readout can no longer be a lesion measure, because the drug underneath it is already moving lesion measures. It has to be myelin content, or a function measure that myelin explains. Calabresi runs these trials and knows which of those is financeable. One candid note from the paper's own disclosures: Kornberg consults for Genentech, which is Roche, and for TG Therapeutics. Not a conflict for us, but he has already heard this argument from the other side.

Fourth, a competitive benchmark for Winnow. Roivant and its subsidiary Priovant reported yesterday that brepocitinib hit in both Phase Three trials in non-infectious uveitis, CLARITY-1 and CLARITY-2, three hundred and seventy patients between them. Everyone got an eight-week steroid course tapered to zero, and the primary endpoint was time to treatment failure from week six to week forty-eight. Median time to failure exceeded forty-eight weeks on drug against about twelve weeks on placebo, with hazard ratios of zero point two seven and zero point two two. Pooled, sixty-two percent achieved steroid-free quiescence in both eyes against twenty percent. Three percent discontinued for adverse events. They file in the first half of twenty twenty-seven.

Here is the read. Brepocitinib is already approved as Lisraya in dermatomyositis, and it is the comparator column we built for Winnow's target product profile, with the boxed warning as the row. That column just got much harder. A once-daily oral TYK2 and JAK1 inhibitor — about as unselective an immune intervention as you can write down — produced a hazard ratio of zero point two two in a T-cell-mediated eye disease with no new safety signals over forty-eight weeks. Winnow cannot win that comparison on efficacy at week forty-eight and should stop trying. The two rows it can win are the ones we already identified: depth, meaning what the antigen-specific engager does not delete and the assay that proves it, and durability, meaning one course and drug-free remission rather than a daily pill forever. Jon, Hemaka — this is the argument for putting an off-treatment durability arm in the development plan now rather than leaving it to a future study. A forty-eight-week head-to-head against a drug this effective is a trial Winnow loses; an off-treatment comparison is one it could win.

Last item, and it is about our own exit path. J.P. Morgan published a report Tuesday counting twenty-seven reverse mergers in the first nine months of this year, worth seven and a half billion dollars — more deals than in any full year since twenty twenty-one, against twelve in all of last year. The financing structure changed more than the count did. Nineteen of the twenty-seven came with a private investment in public equity, against three of twelve last year. Paragon Labs, the Fairmount-backed builder, accounts for three of them, taking Korsana, Mentari and Velaris public and raising three hundred and eighty, two hundred, and three hundred and fifty million dollars. Meanwhile venture funding slipped from nine point nine billion in the second quarter to six point seven billion in the third.

So what for Blackbird. We have been tracking the Paragon pattern as a spinout-structure comparator and watching Caribou Biosciences as a live shell candidate. This report says the pattern is not a curiosity, it is the dominant route, and the reason is the PIPE. A reverse merger is no longer a tired company finding a listing; it is a financing event with a listing attached, and crossover investors are underwriting the new company on day one. For a studio that incubates assets to a defined package, that is a different endgame from Series B into Series C into an eventual offering, and it rewards a clean single-asset entity with an uncomplicated cap table — which is what our spinout structure already produces. Emily, Maisha — worth an hour on whether our standard carve-out documents would survive PIPE diligence without rework. The counterweight is in the same report: venture funding fell by a third quarter over quarter. The back door is crowded because the front door is narrower.
