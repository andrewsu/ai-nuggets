Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Saturday, September twenty-sixth, and the most important development for the portfolio this week is not a readout. The public comment record closed on the F-D-A's rewrite of what counts as substantial evidence of effectiveness, and the filings are now readable. Three of our programs are being designed right now against a standard that is actively being redrafted, and one sentence in the draft puts a price on how well you understand your own mechanism.

Four items. The evidence standard leads. Then two myeloma readouts from the same stage in Glasgow on the same afternoon, a G-P-C-R portfolio coming home from a pharma partner with no money attached, and a jury telling Eli Lilly what it costs to lose interest in someone else's asset.

The F-D-A published a revised draft guidance in June titled Demonstrating Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness for Human Drug and Biological Products. Comments closed Monday. Argenx, U-C-B, Ultragenyx, PhRMA and BIO all filed, the trade press wrote it up yesterday, and the docket is public, so I read the filings rather than the coverage.

Start with what the draft offers, because it is two doors, not one. The first door is an ordinary adequate and well-controlled trial plus what the agency calls a source of strong confirmatory evidence. The draft says that generally means data from related controlled trials — a related disease or condition, or a related product — or controlled trials of other approved drugs in the same pharmacological class. What makes an indication close enough is spelled out: similarity in the pathophysiology of the two diseases, similarity in the drug's mechanism of action across them, and similarity in the endpoints. The second door is a single highly persuasive trial, where the early-phase work you already did to justify running it becomes the confirmatory evidence. The draft lists what that trial has to look like: a broad, representative, U-S-relevant population; a clinically meaningful primary endpoint, with irreversible morbidity or mortality given as the example; supportive results on distinct prespecified secondary endpoints; consistency across important subgroups; and minimal missing data.

Then there is the sentence I want in front of Hemaka and Yixuan. The draft says the appropriate significance level will depend on the pre-trial probability that the drug is effective — and that where that prior is low, because the disease pathophysiology and the drug mechanism are not well understood, testing at the conventional one-sided zero point zero two five may not adequately limit false positive conclusions.

Read that as pricing. Mechanistic confidence is no longer a story you tell in a deck; in this draft it sets your alpha, and therefore your sample size. For Aletira it cuts our way — a monogenic loss-of-function hearing disorder is about as high a prior as biology offers. For the G-P-R fifty-two program it cuts the other way twice: the receptor is an orphan, the mechanism is precisely what is not well understood, and the first door is shut to us because there is no approved G-P-R fifty-two agonist class to borrow confirmatory evidence from. Whatever the neuropsychiatric NewCo's plan says today about the number of trials, it should say it with that clause attached.

Now the filings. Ultragenyx has six rare-disease approvals since twenty seventeen across small molecules, biologics and gene therapy, which makes it the closest public analogue to an Aletira, and it made one ask. The draft recognises historical controls external to a trial, but says nothing about an internal baseline control — within-patient comparison, or a run-in period. Ultragenyx wants that named, and it wrote down the consequence of not naming it: without the clarification, companies will concentrate on diseases that either have comprehensive natural history data or are common enough to randomise, when the science already exists to treat populations far smaller than that.

That is our pre-I-N-D ask, stated by someone with more standing than we have. Auditory brainstem response thresholds are machine-measured, per-ear, with an untreated ear available as its own control. If Ultragenyx wins this clarification, that design gets easier to defend. If it does not, we need an external natural-history cohort agreed early — because the draft says plainly that the one-trial-plus-confirmatory-evidence approach should be discussed with the agency before the single trial starts. Not at end of Phase 2. Before.

PhRMA's filing is the operational one. It supports the framework, then asks the agency to define its terms: strong confirmatory evidence, early-phase confirmatory evidence, closely related indications, same pharmacological class. Note which terms those are. They are exactly the two doors a platform company walks through when it takes a second indication, and industry is now on the record that nobody knows how wide they are. That is a real planning risk for SELEXON and for anything we license with a multi-indication story.

The counterweight, because it will shape the final version: the Program on Regulation, Therapeutics and Law at Brigham and Harvard filed against letting mechanistic evidence confirm anything unless it is strongly linked to clinical outcomes, and for post-marketing studies that begin before approval and carry consequences for non-completion. Their headline number is that thirty-seven percent of the Phase 3 trials supporting initial F-D-A cancer approvals between twenty eighteen and twenty twenty-five delivered low clinical benefit on a validated scale. Expect the final guidance to land between PhRMA and that.

The action is Anthony's and Jon's. Our open item asks which of our candidate endpoints are machine-measured with a steep natural slope, and which are rater-scored scales needing a control arm we may not be able to afford. This draft is the specification for answering it — write the list against the highly-persuasive-trial checklist rather than against intuition.

Second item. Two myeloma readouts landed in Glasgow yesterday afternoon, and together they are the clearest public statement yet of the trade Winnow is making.

Since the roughly thirty-month cut, one patient developed acute myeloid leukaemia, and there were two deaths, one from progression and one from breast cancer. The pairing is the point. One infusion buys a genuine tail: half a small cohort treatment-free at five years, which no engager has shown. What it costs is control. Carvykti carries a boxed warning that includes secondary blood cancers, and this cohort has now produced one. R-B-C's read is that the product suits younger, higher-risk patients because the efficacy is balanced against toxicity older and frailer patients should not take. For Winnow that is the argument rather than a complication. In relapsed myeloma the patient is dying, so a small probability of a second cancer is a trade a physician will make. In an autoimmune indication the patient is not dying, and the same boxed warning is disqualifying. The durability case for cell therapy in autoimmunity is strong and got stronger with Kyverna's twelve-month data on Wednesday. What Glasgow adds, in the same room on the same day, is the price tag, in the one disease with five years of follow-up to read it off.

Two reads, both for Eddie and Avi. The first is the divested-asset screen, open since the fifth of September, which now has a fourth data point with a different shape. When the G-P-R fifty-two NewCo or a SELEXON licence gets structured, the question is not what the headline number is. Which brings me to the closer. On Wednesday a federal jury in the Northern District of California found that Eli Lilly breached the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing in its licence agreement with Nektar Therapeutics over rezpeg, an interleukin-two pathway drug aimed at regulatory T cells in autoimmune disease. Put that beside the Addex item and you have both ends of the same risk on one week's tape. Addex's partner walked away and Addex got the asset back, free, with nothing in the bank. For Emily and Eddie: on every out-licence and every BioVentures position where the value depends on somebody else's continued interest, the diligence question is what happens on the day that interest ends, and whether the answer is written down or has to be argued to a jury.

**ITEM 1 (LEAD) — THE EVIDENTIARY STANDARD OUR THREE PRE-IND PROGRAMMES ARE BEING DESIGNED AGAINST IS BEING REWRITTEN, THE COMMENT RECORD CLOSED 2026-09-22 AND IS PUBLIC, AND ONE CLAUSE PRICES MECHANISTIC CONFIDENCE IN ALPHA. Touches ALETIRA, the GPR52 NEUROPSYCH NEWCO, NUGATE + the VEXAS/indication-selection thread.** Guidance published 2026-06-24/25; comments closed 2026-09-22; trade coverage 2026-09-25.
  - **The document: FDA revised draft guidance *Demonstrating Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness for Human Drug and Biological Products* (CDER + CBER + OCE, June 2026, Revision 1), Docket No. FDA-2019-D-4964.** Consolidates the 1998 guidance, the 2019 draft and the 2023 one-trial-plus-confirmatory-evidence draft. **Read in full from the primary PDF, not from coverage.**
  - **DOOR 1 — one adequate and well-controlled trial + a source of STRONG confirmatory evidence.** FDA "generally expects that such strong confirmatory evidence would come from related adequate and well-controlled trial data, such as trial data in related diseases or conditions or for related products," **or from controlled trials of other approved drugs in the same pharmacological class.** Closeness test is explicit: **degree of similarity in disease pathophysiology, in the drug's mechanism of action across the two diseases, and in the efficacy endpoints.**
  - **DOOR 2 — a HIGHLY PERSUASIVE trial + early-phase confirmatory evidence.** FDA's checklist: generalizable/US-relevant design across multiple sites with a standard-of-care control; **clinically meaningful primary endpoint (irreversible morbidity or mortality given as the example)**; sufficient power; results persuasive on magnitude + CI + p-value; **supportive on distinct prespecified secondary endpoints**; **largely supportive across important subgroups**; comprehensive follow-up, minimal missing data, robustness to assumption violations.
  - **THE CLAUSE (Hemaka Rajapakse + Yixuan Qiu).** Verbatim: *"The appropriate significance level will depend on the prior (i.e., pretrial) probability of the drug being effective; in cases where the prior probability of effectiveness is low (e.g., because the disease pathophysiology and drug mechanism are not well understood), a single trial with hypothesis testing at the common one-sided 0.025 significance level may not adequately limit the probability of false positive effectiveness conclusions."* ⇒ **Mechanistic confidence now sets alpha, and therefore n. Aletira (monogenic loss-of-function) sits at a high prior. GPR52 sits at a low one AND cannot use Door 1, because there is no approved GPR52-agonist class to borrow from.** Fold into the standing GPR52 DC-package items (signalling bias, biomarker instrument, PD readout).

Before the pivotal starts.** Pairs with the Expedited IND Pilot item (09-15) and the standing Aletira pre-IND ask.
  - **ULTRAGENYX (six rare-disease approvals since 2017 across small molecules, biologics and gene therapy — our closest public analogue).** Single core ask: the draft recognises **external/historical** controls but **never names an INTERNAL BASELINE CONTROL** — within-patient comparison or a run-in period.   - **argenx** — evidentiary value should be judged **in the context of the disease, treatment and scientific question, not against a single methodological benchmark**; wants confirmatory evidence from **RWE, expanded-access data, related products, shared mechanisms of action and clinically validated targets** spelled out; wants worked examples for Bayesian methods, global programmes, and bridging across formulations/routes. **BIO** — same direction, adds a request for consistency **across review divisions** and for Phase 2 proof-of-concept/dose-ranging to be usable as confirmatory evidence.
  - **COUNTERWEIGHT — PORTAL (Brigham + Harvard Medical School; Tibau Martorell, Kesselheim).** Neither one nor two pivotal trials should be the default; **small effects on survival or irreversible morbidity should not be presumed clinically meaningful from the endpoint alone**; mechanistic evidence should confirm **only when strongly linked to meaningful clinical outcomes**; when approval rests on one trial, FDA's public review should explain in a standardized table how totality compensates for missing replication; postmarketing studies should **start before approval when uncertainty is substantial and carry consequences for non-completion.** Their number: **37% of Phase 3 trials supporting initial FDA cancer approvals 2018–2025 provided low clinical benefit** on a validated framework. **Expect the final to land between PhRMA and PORTAL.**
