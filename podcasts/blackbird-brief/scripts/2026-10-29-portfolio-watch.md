## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Thursday, October twenty-ninth. The most important development for the portfolio is that somebody raised six hundred million dollars to do a structural version of what Blackbird does — one entity per asset, one shared development organization underneath — and the limited partners are our peer group, not venture funds.

Formation Bio announced the Drug Development Accelerator Fund yesterday in New York. Six hundred million dollars in capital commitments. The stated ambition is to acquire ten to fifteen drug programs over the next three to five years and carry each one through a major clinical milestone. Formation in-licenses or acquires Phase One and Phase Two assets, targets clinical proof of concept within two to four years of acquisition, and expects to partner programs with pharmaceutical companies for commercialization after meaningful clinical progress.

Three details are the item. First, the structure. Each funded program is housed in its own standalone corporate entity, so the economics of each asset stay financially distinct, while the development work is done by one established organization sitting underneath all of them. Formation's own stated reason is that this directs capital toward clinical development rather than toward, in their words, separate redundant operating infrastructure inside multiple companies. That is a sentence from the Blackbird BioHub pitch deck, written by somebody else and funded at nine figures.

Second, the limited partners. Formation names them as leading research foundations, institutional endowments, hospitals, and philanthropic organizations. Not crossover funds, not traditional life-science venture. That is the Bisciotti Family Foundation's category. A pool of philanthropic and endowment capital has just agreed that the right unit of investment is the individual drug program rather than the company around it — and it did so through a general partner that is itself an operating drug developer, with Formation committing its own capital alongside.

Third, the liquidity logic, which chief executive Benjamine Liu said out loud. The fund gives the parent, quote, paths to liquidity without selling the parent company, and positions it to lower its cost of capital over time. Translate that into our situation. Blackbird's nonprofit grants de-risk academic I-P and BioVentures writes the early check when a program spins out. The structural question this raises is whether a program-level vehicle sits between those two — a pool that can take a de-risked Hopkins or U-M-B asset into its own entity, draw on the BioHub bench rather than standing up seven finance functions, and reach proof of concept without each program having to be a fundable company first.

The candid limits. This is an announcement, not a track record. The fund has committed capital and a target, and the number of programs is an ambition. Formation is an A-I-native developer and some of the efficiency claim rests on technology that has not been shown to work at scale in public. And the asset class is not new — Royalty-style and development-stage asset vehicles have existed for twenty years, and most of the ones that failed failed on selection, not on structure. What is new is the L-P base and the fact that the general partner runs the trials.

The read for Eddie and for Matt. Two things to take from this. The slower one is that if endowments and foundations are now underwriting asset-level development vehicles, there is a conversation to be had with the same category of capital that does not involve asking anyone to back a venture fund. The faster one is a positioning note. When somebody asks why Blackbird keeps programs inside a shared bench instead of spinning each one out on day one, the answer now has a third-party comparable with a number attached to it.

Second item. There is a new blood test paper in A-L-S, and it is a clean argument against the thing NuGate has been building its evidentiary case around.

Emory researchers published in Nature Communications a ten-protein plasma signature that separates A-L-S patients from healthy controls with ninety-eight point three percent balanced accuracy and an area under the curve of point nine nine two, in an independent validation cohort. The discovery set was six hundred and thirty-two A-L-S patients and a hundred and sixty controls; validation was a hundred and forty-four patients and thirty-seven controls. Over seven hundred and fifty A-L-S cases in total. The measurement platform is Alamar Biosciences' technology, which reads low-abundance proteins — they ran more than three hundred neurological and inflammatory markers per sample.

Here is the number that matters to us. Neurofilament light alone, in the same validation cohort, gave eighty-nine point nine percent balanced accuracy and an area under the curve of point nine one six. The ten-protein panel beat it, and the paper states explicitly that performance was not dependent on neurofilament light alone and was driven by the proteomic signature rather than by clinical variables. The panel keeps both neurofilament markers, light and heavy chain, adds phospho-tau one eighty-one and V-E-G-F-A, and then brings in six proteins not previously reported as A-L-S blood biomarkers. One of those, folate receptor alpha, earns its place specifically by correctly classifying people who do not have A-L-S but whose neurofilament is elevated. It reduces false positives.

Two reads, and they point in different directions.

For NuGate, this is a caution with a constructive edge, and it is addressed to Hemaka and the design team. Our standing position since early September has been to make neurofilament the evidentiary spine of the package — the Qalsody precedent, the n-of-one work, a small trial with one prespecified functional primary and neurofilament carrying the biological story. That position is not wrong, and nothing here touches the use of neurofilament as a within-patient pharmacodynamic readout, which is what we actually need it for. But a peer-reviewed cohort of this size has now put a number on the ceiling of neurofilament as a classifier, and named the specific failure mode: elevated neurofilament in people who do not have the disease. If a regulator or a partner asks what our enrichment and eligibility strategy is, the honest answer can no longer be neurofilament by itself. Ask the question now, before first-in-patient, rather than in a review division.

For Artemyx, this is supportive and it is a free argument. The whole Artemyx thesis is longitudinal multi-analyte monitoring in chronic disease rather than a single marker. This is a well-powered demonstration, in a disease with a famously good single biomarker, that the multi-marker panel beats the single marker — and that the gain comes specifically from specificity, from the proteins that tell you who does not have the disease. Avi, that is a slide, and it is somebody else's data.

The limits, stated plainly. This is case-versus-healthy-control classification, which is the easy version of the problem. The hard version is A-L-S versus the conditions that mimic it, and the paper does not claim to have solved it — the authors call the mimics group a diagnostic challenge and position the work as supporting development of a screening tool. Early-view article, not yet the final version of record. No competing interests declared by the authors, which is worth noting given the platform company issued the press release.

Third, two things on the calendar rather than in the rearview, both bearing on Winnow.

Kymera reports Phase One healthy-volunteer results this morning, single and multiple ascending dose, for K-T five seventy-nine. That is an oral degrader of I-R-F five, a transcription factor the company describes as a master regulator of immunity, and the named indications are lupus, Sjögren's, rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel disease. We cannot read the data before this episode goes out, so here is the bar to hold it against rather than a verdict. For an oral small molecule in autoimmunity, a healthy-volunteer package has to show target engagement — degradation depth and duration at a dose a person can take daily — with a clean tolerability profile, because the entire argument for an oral agent in these indications is that patients will take it for years. Jon, the competitive point for Winnow is not whether this molecule works. It is that the convenience end of the autoimmune market is filling up with oral mechanisms that require no infusion, no conditioning and no monitoring, which pushes a T-cell engager's claim further toward depth and durability of remission and away from anything resembling maintenance therapy.

And Biogen reported its third quarter yesterday, with one date in it we should all write down. Topline data from both Phase Three studies of litifilimab — that is the anti-B-D-C-A-two antibody — in systemic lupus are expected in the first half of December. This year. Six weeks out. Felzartamab in antibody-mediated rejection reads out in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. We logged litifilimab in September as the mechanism holding the cutaneous lupus space; now the systemic lupus pivotal pair lands before the end of the year, and whichever way it goes it will reprice the whole non-depleting approach to lupus that Winnow's depth argument is measured against. The secondary note for Eddie: Biogen took a two hundred and eighty-four million dollar acquired research charge in the quarter, almost entirely for the RayThera acquisition — three preclinical anti-inflammatory small molecules bought for up to a billion dollars, with the lead now in Phase One. A large-cap neurology company paying nine figures up front for preclinical immunology assets is the buyer behavior we keep saying we want to see.

One housekeeping item that has now been on this list for four consecutive runs. The F-D-A's expedited I-N-D pilot application window closes tomorrow night, eleven fifty-nine Eastern. Sponsor and institution apply as a pair, with the sponsor submitting. It still needs a go or no-go and an institution. After tomorrow it is moot.

Quiet today on Aletira's own program, on the GPR52 NewCo, on Clasp, Biolinco, D-E-L-F-I, Georgiamune and 1104health, and nothing dated from Johns Hopkins, U-M-B or the Lieber Institute inside the window. Have a good Thursday.
