## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Sunday, September twentieth, and the most important development for the portfolio is that the gene therapy we led with on Friday got a price — three million nine hundred fifty thousand dollars — and Wall Street is valuing the approval on the transferable voucher rather than on the drug.

Five items. Aletira, NuGate, the grant instrument itself, the comparator column, and a short note on the exit map.

Start with the price, because Friday we only had half the story.

On Friday we led with the approval of Fayuvi, Ultragenyx's A-A-V gene therapy for Sanfilippo syndrome type A — the first-ever approved treatment for that disease, granted standard full approval off seventeen treated patients against a twenty-seven-patient external natural-history cohort. That was the regulatory half. The commercial half landed the same day. The price is three point nine five million dollars per dose, and it is the second gene therapy Ultragenyx has brought to market since August.

Here is the line that should change a number in our model. William Blair's Sami Corwin wrote that analysts do not project either of those two approvals to become a blockbuster — and named the sellable drug-review vouchers Ultragenyx obtained alongside both as the thing that should significantly bolster the balance sheet. Read that slowly. A first-ever therapy for a fatal pediatric neurodegenerative disease, priced just under four million dollars, is being valued by the street on the transferable voucher, not on product revenue. The company still warned of coming cost cuts after its Angelman drug failed. The stock still went up double digits on the approval.

Three things follow for Aletira, and Geoff and Emily should take all three.

First, we now have a real anchor for the price line instead of a guess. Just under four million dollars is what a single-dose, ultra-rare, pediatric A-A-V gene therapy prices at in the United States in September twenty twenty-six. Put that number in the model and stop arguing about it.

Second, and more consequential — if the voucher is the asset, then whether a congenital hearing-loss program qualifies for one is a regulatory-strategy question with real money attached, and it is not a question anyone at Blackbird has answered in writing. It belongs in the pre-I-N-D file next to the two items we already have open there: the validated functional scale, and the diagnosed-versus-prevalence ascertainment problem. And note how those three interact. If the voucher is where the value is, the voucher is a one-time event, which puts even more weight on the launch volume — which is governed by how many children carry a confirmed molecular diagnosis, not by the two hundred thousand prevalence figure in the deck.

Third, on the same day, most-favored-nation pricing moved another notch. President Trump announced that all fifty states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico have signed into a Medicaid model under which participating manufacturers rebate down to the most-favored-nation price on expensive branded drugs. The Council of Economic Advisers puts the savings at twenty-seven point six billion to the states and thirty-six point six billion federally over a decade; critics point out the underlying agreements are not public, so nobody outside can verify them. Be honest about the direct relevance: Medicaid is not the primary channel for a four-million-dollar one-time pediatric therapy. But the standing action from September first was to revisit the US price assumption in every program value case, on the argument that the acquirer of a Blackbird asset has already pre-committed the launch price of a drug that does not exist yet. That roster went from a voluntary manufacturer list to every state in the union in three weeks. The direction of travel is not ambiguous.

Second item, and it lands on NuGate.

Skyhawk Therapeutics reported final results Tuesday from a mid-stage study of a once-daily pill for Huntington's disease. The mechanism is R-N-A splicing modulation — a small molecule that gets the splicing machinery to skip over defective sequence. At fifteen months, treated patients improved by an average of nought point nine four points on the composite scale used to assess function, cognition and movement in Huntington's, against a comparison group drawn from a natural-history database that declined by nought point six five. The separation is one point five nine points, and the literature estimate for clinically meaningful on that scale is one point two. Patients on drug were ahead at every checkpoint, and the difference reached statistical significance from the nine-month mark onward. No serious adverse events reported. At the higher dose, mutant huntingtin protein down consistently by more than sixty percent, and the messenger R-N-A for a D-N-A-repair protein that worsens the underlying repeat expansion down twenty-five percent.

Three reads, and the first is the uncomfortable one.

The modality comparator is moving. For NuGate the reference case has been an intrathecal antisense drug against a standard of care that has not moved in a decade. In July, Roche scrapped two Ionis-partnered antisense programs in Huntington's. Three months later, the readout in that same indication is a tablet. This is the same lesson as the Lilly and QurCan systemic-delivery deal we logged two weeks ago, arriving from a completely different direction: by the time NuGate reads out, the comparator may not be "nothing." It may be "a pill."

Second read: this is the third external-control datapoint this month, and the three of them now describe a pattern. Ultragenyx ran seventeen against twenty-seven and cleared full approval. uniQure is at the agency with an external-control Huntington's gene therapy package. Skyhawk used a natural-history database comparator in Phase 2 — and then went and ran a randomized Phase 3 anyway. What external controls reliably buy you is a Phase 3 and a conversation. What converted to an approval was a natural-history cohort somebody had built years before they needed it. That standing action does not change; it just keeps getting confirmed.

Third, the candid limit, because the number is seductive. One point five nine points is a difference against an external comparator, not a randomized one. The one-point-two threshold is an estimate from the literature, not something agreed with a regulator. The study is small and open-label. It is a genuinely good result and it is not a controlled one, and the Phase 3 program — one trial fully enrolled at a hundred forty-four patients, a second worldwide study heading for about six hundred — is what will settle it.

Third item, and it is about our own instrument rather than any one program.

The OpenAI Foundation — the nonprofit that governs the for-profit OpenAI — gave forty million dollars to the U-N-C Lineberger Comprehensive Cancer Center to generate data for better personalized cancer vaccines. The work is led by immunologist Benjamin Vincent and computational biologist Alex Rubinsteyn: hundreds of de-identified tumor and immune samples from three biobanks, used to train models that pick better tumor antigens, plus head-to-head comparison of vaccine formulations in triple-negative breast cancer trials. It is the launch grant of the foundation's second science program, which is called Public Data for Health.

Read the terms, not the headline. Forty million dollars is forty percent of Blackbird's founding grant, written in one check, into one academic cancer center, by an organization with no biotech operation. And the deliverable is explicitly an open-access dataset that researchers anywhere can build on. That is our instrument — a large nondilutive grant into academic translational work — pointed at the exact opposite output. We fund to create a defensible position. They fund to destroy the scarcity.

Two consequences, and Eddie and Maisha should own them.

One: there is now a class of very large, very fast, non-dilutive funder competing for the attention of precisely the principal investigators we fund, and it asks for nothing in return. On every axis a P-I cares about except the one that matters to a future company, those terms beat ours.

Two, and this is the actionable half. If a program we are evaluating has already taken data-generation money carrying an open-publication or open-data obligation, then part of the composition-of-matter position we said last week was the only differentiator worth claiming may already be spent before we get there. Add prior non-dilutive funding and its publication and data-sharing obligations to the diligence intake checklist. It is a five-minute question and it is now load-bearing.

A secondary note for Adventris, the K-RAS-vaccine company in our building: the money went specifically at antigen selection, which is the hardest problem in personalized cancer vaccines, and the output will be public. That is a tailwind for anyone whose moat is not antigen selection, and a headwind for anyone whose moat is.

Fourth item, thirty seconds on the comparator column.

On Friday China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, with nine other government bodies, released the pharmaceutical five-year plan for twenty twenty-six through twenty thirty. The targets: annual revenue at major drugmakers above three point five trillion yuan, roughly five hundred twenty billion dollars; more than twenty percent annual growth in the innovative-drug sector; at least twenty-five percent of the world's first-in-class drugs originating in China; at least five Chinese-developed products above a billion dollars in global sales; fifty companies each at about one and a half billion in revenue; research spending above ten percent of revenue. Named frontier priorities include gene editing, cell therapy and A-I-designed drugs. The plan itself cites Chinese out-licensing up thirty-six percent year on year, cumulative value past a hundred and twenty billion dollars.

Every rung we have logged this month — DualityBio at forty-five million upfront, Simcere at seventy-five, HUTCHMED at a hundred and ten, Harbour's asset into Solstice, and GSK buying a preclinical trispecific from Chimagen for up to seven hundred fifty million — is now described by the seller's own government as the intended output of industrial policy, with a volume target attached to it. The conclusion does not change: the only thing worth claiming in that column is an unencumbered composition-of-matter position. What changes is the tempo we should assume. And the three named frontier priorities are three things our portfolio is in.

Fifth and last, short, on the exit map.

Two reverse mergers were announced on the same Thursday with four hundred and five million dollars of new money behind them. Marea Therapeutics went into Lisata Therapeutics with two hundred twenty-five million committed by Forbion, venBio and Third Rock, carrying an antibody licensed from Novartis into Phase 2b in severe hypertriglyceridemia. North Immunology went into Aethlon Medical with a hundred and eighty million placement from Bain Capital, Janus Henderson and Deep Track, on a dual-acting antibody aimed at Dupixent's limitations.

Two details worth carrying. First, both shells were distressed — Lisata had cut seventy-two percent of its workforce and was suing a failed acquirer, and Aethlon's legacy business was carved out into a contingent value right. The mechanism is now routine; counterparty selection is the whole game, which is exactly what we flagged on September second. Second, and this one is for Emily: North Immunology is going public on an asset that does not start Phase 1a until the first quarter of next year. The entry bar for this door is a syndicate, not data. That is a materially different statement from the one we made on Friday about the I-P-O window, and both are true at once.

Quiet this weekend: nothing on Clasp, Winnow, Artemyx, Biolinco, 1104health or Georgiamune; no G-P-R fifty-two movement; no fresh otology readouts; nothing on gout, cardiac fibrosis or VEXAS. And a reminder that the Operation TrialBlazer application deadline is October thirtieth, and we still have not decided which program and which institution. Radar follows this morning. Have a good Sunday.
