# Portfolio Watch — 2026-07-26

## Script

This is Portfolio Watch for Sunday, July 26th, and I'll be straight with you: no Blackbird company printed news this window. The signal is the ground moving underneath one of them — the precision lung-cancer and RAS landscape hardened around aSKY Therapeutics in a single day this week, with two separate regulatory milestones.

Start with the one that names our competitor directly. On July 22nd, the FDA approved GSK's zidesamtinib — brand name Jideytro — for previously-treated, ROS-one-positive non-small-cell lung cancer. It's a selective inhibitor of the ROS-one driver, and it cleared almost two months ahead of its FDA decision date. Two things matter here. First, this is GSK's first-ever lung-cancer approval, and it came fast — the early payoff from their roughly ten-point-six-billion-dollar acquisition of Nuvalent. Zidesamtinib and its sibling neladalkib — the A-L-K inhibitor still under FDA review with a November target — are exactly the GSK-Nuvalent axis we flagged in aSKY's competitive watch. Second, the read-through for us: precision-oncology dealmaking in lung cancer is paying off on a compressed timeline, which validates the category aSKY plays in — but it also keeps raising the bar. Every additional selective agent that reaches approval means aSKY's still-undisclosed KRAS-frame mechanism needs a clearer, defensible lane. The one caveat is that these are different drivers — ROS-one and A-L-K, not KRAS — so it's landscape validation more than a head-to-head threat.

The more direct competitive datapoint landed the same day. Revolution Medicines said the FDA accepted its new drug application for daraxonrasib — their oral, multi-selective RAS inhibitor, the so-called RAS-on approach — in previously-treated metastatic pancreatic cancer, and it was pulled into the FDA's national priority-voucher pilot. Pancreatic is the lead indication, but daraxonrasib's Phase 3 program runs into lung cancer too. This is the one aSKY's team should sit with. Revolution is the lead RAS-on player, and a KRAS-frame lung-cancer program has to define what it does that daraxonrasib and the covalent-KRAS crowd don't. RAS-mutant oncology is de-risking fast at the regulatory level — that's a tailwind for the science and a pressure test for differentiation at the same time.

So the action item this window isn't a fire drill — it's diligence hygiene. Eddie and the aSKY team should refresh the competitive map now that the KRAS and RAS field has two more validating data points and a clearer sense of who's reaching the FDA first. The undisclosed-mechanism problem gets more expensive the longer the field matures around it.

One quick landscape note on the other side of the portfolio, and I'll flag up front it's from just outside our window, late last week. In the clinical-trial-enrollment space where eleven-oh-four Health operates, Massive Bio — one of the AI patient-matching players — expanded its cancer-trial prescreening onto a large clinical-site network. The trend to watch is consolidation plus AI matching hardening into the incumbents. It doesn't change eleven-oh-four Health's clinician-workflow-and-subsidy thesis, but it's the competitive weather.

That's the watch. No portfolio-company news, but a real hardening of the KRAS and RAS lung-cancer landscape around aSKY — worth a competitive-map refresh this week. Talk tomorrow.
