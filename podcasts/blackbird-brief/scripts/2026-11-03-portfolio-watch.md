## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Tuesday, November third. Yesterday the venture numbers for the third quarter landed, and they answer a question we have been arguing from anecdote for two months: after three sets of deaths in autoimmune cell therapy trials, the capital did not leave. It rotated. Where it went is the part that matters for Winnow.

Here are the figures, from PitchBook by way of BioSpace. Cell and gene therapy took in two point two billion dollars of venture money in the third quarter across fifty-one deals. Small molecules took three point two billion. Biologics took one point five billion — so cell and gene therapy beat biologics, which has happened only twice since twenty twenty-two, and it did so in the same three months in which Novartis paused its C-A-R-T trials after three deaths, Bristol Myers disclosed a death in a multiple sclerosis trial, and Artiva shut its program down entirely.

Now look at what the money actually bought, because the headline number hides the rotation. The single largest event in the quarter was Sail Biomedicines and Johnson and Johnson — up to nine hundred and twenty-five million dollars, of which seven hundred and eighty-five million was upfront, and four hundred and sixty-five million of that upfront was an equity purchase. The deal closed in August at a two point eight billion dollar valuation, and Johnson and Johnson took an exclusive option to buy Sail outright for two point five eight billion. Sail is doing in-vivo C-A-R-T for immune-mediated conditions. Meanwhile Caribou Biosciences — Jennifer Doudna's company, allogeneic C-A-R-T — suspended research and development entirely, with the chief executive naming the fundraising environment for allogeneic cell therapy as the reason.

Three reads, and the first is for Jon and Anthony. We have been making the category argument since October twenty-eighth: that a T-cell engager does not require lymphodepleting conditioning, and that fludarabine neurotoxicity is a toxicity class our route structurally does not carry. That argument is now visible in capital flows rather than in our deck. The money moved out of the modality that needs apheresis, manufacture and conditioning, and into the one that does not. We were right about the direction. The problem is who else noticed.

Second read, and it is the uncomfortable one. The route the capital rotated toward is in-vivo C-A-R-T, not engagers. This is the fourth in-vivo data point since late September and it is now the one with the largest cheque behind it. On October nineteenth we agreed to retire the manufacturing half of our control-architecture row before a partner retired it for us. This quarter is the evidence that the retirement was correct and probably late. What survives is the control half — interruptibility, titratability, redosability — and we should be stating it in those three words rather than in a paragraph about manufacturing.

Third read, for Eddie, and it goes straight into the comparator ladder. The structure Johnson and Johnson used is worth more to us than the number. They bought an option: four hundred and sixty-five million dollars of equity now, development milestones behind it, and a fixed price of two point five eight billion to take the whole company later. Put that next to the Dren Bio comparable we logged on October thirtieth — six hundred million dollars cash upfront, outright, for an antibody route to deep depletion before any autoimmune data existed. Same destination, two completely different instruments, and the difference is whether the buyer wanted the asset or the platform. Winnow should know which of those two it is selling before anyone asks.

Second item, and it is the most consequential regulatory event of the day for Aletira. The F-D-A has accepted uniQure's Huntington's disease gene therapy for priority review with a decision date of May first, and the agency has said it is tentatively planning to discuss the application at an advisory committee.

You know the history. Three-year data from a Phase One-Two trial showed seventy-five percent slowing of disease. The agency reversed its own guidance last November and said that data would not support a filing. Leadership changed, guidance reversed again this spring, the company filed on September second, and then four-year data came in weaker than the three-year data and the stock fell as much as thirty-eight percent. Despite all of that, the application is in, with priority review, on an accelerated approval path, and both Stifel and William Blair read the acceptance as incrementally positive specifically for the accelerated-approval question.

Here is the part I want Anthony and the pre-I-N-D team to carry. The confirmatory trial is the load-bearing element, and uniQure disclosed its shape. Roughly two hundred patients, randomized to the gene therapy or to standard of care. Primary endpoint is total functional capacity — a functional scale, not a biomarker — measured over thirty-six months. Screening is anticipated to begin by the end of this year, and the company says the study will be well underway by the time of a potential approval. That sentence is the price of the accelerated approval, and it was paid before the approval was granted.

So update our standing ask. Since October sixteenth it has read: an external control with a version lock, a pre-specified missing-data rule, and a validated functional scale. Add a fourth clause. The confirmatory study must be designed, agreed with the division, and enrolling at the time you ask for the accelerated path — not sketched for later. A regulator who has reversed itself twice on the same product still moved forward when the applicant brought a randomized confirmatory trial with a start date. That is the behavior being rewarded, and it is cheaper to commit to it at pre-I-N-D than to retrofit it under review.

And a candid note for Hemaka on the NuGate side, because it cuts the other way. The four-year data were weaker than the three-year data on the same patients in the same trial. Any argument we build on a natural-history or external comparison has to survive our own longer follow-up, and the honest planning assumption is that the effect you show at thirty-six months is the largest one you will ever show.

Third item, short, and it is an instrument problem. Tiziana Life Sciences reported topline from its Phase Two-a trial of intranasal foralumab, an anti-C-D-three antibody, in non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis — a population with no approved treatment. Nineteen patients on the fifty-microgram dose met both primary endpoints, safety and a statistically significant reduction in microglial activation on P-E-T imaging at twelve weeks versus placebo. Eleven patients on the hundred-microgram dose missed the efficacy endpoint. The company released no numbers behind either result. The stock fell seventeen percent, to sixty-seven cents.

Two things to take from this. The first is the instrument rule again, and I count this as the fifth instance this autumn. An eleven-patient arm did not demonstrate the absence of an effect; it demonstrated that eleven patients cannot demonstrate anything. But the market does not read it that way, and neither will a partner, so the asymmetry is real: a split dose result in two tiny arms is strictly worse than not having run the second arm. When Avi and Yixuan are sizing our own early cohorts, the question is not only whether the instrument can register the effect — it is whether every arm you fund can, because an arm too small to speak will still be quoted against you.

The second is more specific and it is about biomarker primaries. A P-E-T imaging readout as a co-primary at twelve weeks is exactly the design shape a Blackbird neuro program would want: fast, mechanistic, far cheaper than a disability endpoint. Here it bought the speed and then could not explain its own result, and the company did not publish the numbers to let anyone else try. A biomarker primary converts a two-year question into a twelve-week one, and the cost is that when the doses disagree you have no clinical anchor to adjudicate between them. Price that trade deliberately rather than inheriting it.

Fourth and last, for Eddie, and it is the second duress-pricing data point in two days. Karyopharm Therapeutics filed for Chapter Eleven yesterday and named the U-K company Ayrmid as lead bidder in a court-supervised sale, with a transaction expected to close in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven. Karyopharm secured commitments for up to fifty-five million dollars in debtor-in-possession financing from its existing secured lenders. This is not a shell. It sells an approved multiple myeloma drug, and it filed a supplemental application in August asking for accelerated approval in myelofibrosis.

Rodman and Renshaw downgraded it the same morning with a sentence worth memorizing. Their estimates and price target, they wrote, were built on an operating-company framework that assumed the equity would capture the value of the commercial and pipeline assets — and that framework no longer fits. They had previously carried the company at nearly nine hundred and ninety million dollars of enterprise value. Lenders and note holders recover first, and what is left for shareholders, they said, could be minimal.

Yesterday we logged Alligator selling a Phase Two antibody with positive pancreatic cancer data for seven hundred and fifty thousand dollars of certain money. Today is the same lesson from the other end of the pipeline. A company with a marketed product and a pending accelerated approval filing may return nothing to its equity, because the capital structure, not the asset, decided the outcome. The operator point for every Blackbird spinout is that secured debt in a small biotech converts a pipeline decision into a creditor decision, and it does so at the exact moment you most want to control the timing. Worth noting on the other side of the ledger, and it is genuinely unusual: there are no layoffs attached to the filing, and Ayrmid has indicated it intends to retain the majority of the organization. The team survived the capital structure. The shareholders may not.

That is the watch for today. The short version: the money did not leave cell therapy, it rotated to the route that skips the conditioning regimen; a running confirmatory trial is what buys an accelerated path; and a company can have an approved drug and still return nothing to its owners. Talk tomorrow.
