## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Sunday, November first, and the lead is the resolution of the question we left open on Friday: Gilead's clinical hold is on a dual-targeting lymphoma car-T, the trigger is neurotoxicity, and it is oncology, not immunology.

Here is what we now know. On Thursday's third-quarter call, Gilead disclosed a partial clinical hold on two studies of a car-T called KITE-seven-five-three in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. It is an anti-C-D-nineteen, anti-C-D-twenty dual-targeting product, and the two paused studies are the Phase Two second-line trial and the Phase Three third-line-plus relapsed-refractory trial, both called PALISADES. The chief medical officer, Dietmar Berger, said they paused to investigate safety differences they had noticed between Phase One and Phase Two. Cindy Perettie, who runs Kite, was more specific: no deaths, no grade five events, and the issue is grade three and grade four I-CANS — the neurotoxicity syndrome — in a small number of patients. And the sequence matters: Gilead paused voluntarily first, and then aligned with the agency on a partial hold.

For context, the Phase One data last year showed seventy-one percent complete responses with safety described as comparable to their myeloma product, and Johnson and Johnson dropped out of the competing dual-targeting race in May, which left this program with an unusually open field. Separately on that call, Gilead's cell therapy sales fell eighteen percent to three hundred fifty-five million dollars, with Yescarta down from three hundred forty-nine million to two hundred forty-nine.

Three reads. Jon, Anthony — the first one is the one we were waiting on, and the answer closes it rather than opening it. This is oncology. It does not join the autoimmune cell-therapy safety run we have been tracking since the B-M-S multiple sclerosis death on the twenty-seventh and the Artiva discontinuation on the twenty-eighth. Say that plainly in the Winnow deck rather than letting it get swept in. What it does do is add to the toxicity-category argument, which is a different and better argument. I-CANS is what happens when a self-amplifying cell population does its job somewhere you did not want it done, and the defining feature is that once the cells are in, you cannot dial it back. That is the third instance this month of a cell product discovering a toxicity it has no mechanism to stop. Winnow's claim is interruptibility. Keep arguing the category, not comparative gentleness.

The second read is general and it is the expensive one. Phase One did not predict Phase Two. Same product, same disease, different safety profile. That is the single most costly surprise in cell therapy, and it is almost never mysterious after the fact — something changed between the phases, and it is usually the patient population, the line of therapy, the dose, or the manufacturing process. Anthony, for every cell-therapy asset that comes across the desk, the diligence question is not what Phase One showed. It is what will be different in Phase Two, and which of those differences touches safety. Write that into the template.

And a third, smaller one, which is about behavior rather than biology. Gilead stopped on its own and then went to the agency, rather than waiting to be told. That is what we would want from a portfolio company, and it is worth saying so out loud, because the instinct under financing pressure runs the other way.

Second item, and it puts a number on a deal structure we have been describing all week. Bristol Myers Squibb has walked away from a Phase One neurodegeneration program it licensed from Prothena. The asset was called P-R-X-oh-one-nine, renamed B-M-S-nine-eight-six-four-nine-five, an antibody; the I-N-D cleared in December twenty twenty-three; B-M-S paid eighty million dollars in May twenty twenty-four for exclusive global rights; Prothena ran the Phase One in healthy adults this year. B-M-S reviewed the data and decided not to advance. The fifty-five million dollar milestone that advancing would have triggered does not get paid. They disclosed no data and gave no reason beyond the decision itself. The broader Prothena neuroscience partnership continues through a Phase Two Alzheimer's antibody.

Now put it next to September, when B-M-S dropped a C-D-thirty-three-targeting degrader-antibody conjugate it had bought from Orum for a hundred million dollars, also after a Phase One look. That is two negative exercises in six weeks, both on assets acquired for eight figures before any human data existed.

Eddie, Matt Lawler — this is exactly the transaction we have been calling the realistic first deal for a Blackbird spinout: the large-cap does not buy the company, it buys an option on an I-N-D package. These two data points tell you what that option is actually worth from the seller's side. The upfront is real and the seller keeps it. The milestone is a lottery ticket. And the exercise decision gets made on data the buyer is under no obligation to show the seller, the public, or the next buyer. Prothena is left with eighty million dollars, a dead program, and no published Phase One.

Two practical consequences. First, if a spinout's financing plan assumes the exercise happens, the plan is wrong — structure the upfront so it funds the next value-creating step standing alone. Second, and Hemaka and Emily should own this one: negotiate a data-return provision at signing. If the partner walks, the full Phase One dataset comes back in a form you can hand to the next buyer. That costs almost nothing to ask for at signing and is close to impossible to get afterward. And note which asset B-M-S kept — the one in Phase Two with a named indication. Stage beat relationship.

Third item is for Avi and the NuGate team, and it is the commercial half of a story we have only been telling scientifically. Alnylam reported its third quarter on Thursday. The transthyretin franchise did one point one five billion dollars, up fifty-nine percent year over year. The stock fell by a quarter in a single session to a hundred seventy-four dollars, is down fifty-six percent year to date, and the market cap is twenty-three point three billion. B-M-O called it a trifecta of uncertainties. The chief executive, Yvonne Greenstreet, said on the call, quote, we are not satisfied with the pace of our future demand growth, and we need to do better.

None of the three uncertainties are about whether the drug works. Amvuttra was approved in polyneuropathy in twenty twenty-two and in cardiomyopathy in March twenty twenty-five, and the second launch has been rough — guidance already cut once this year, and BridgeBio and Pfizer taking share. The third pressure is a reimbursement rule: the three-forty-B pricing update does not take effect until January twenty twenty-seven, but the chief financial officer said health systems reassessing their economics and their site-of-care choices could create fourth-quarter ordering volatility right now.

Avi, we have used Alnylam twice this month as a scientific comparator — most recently on the twenty-seventh, on the point that a central-nervous-system oligonucleotide can post a world-class target-engagement number and still be worth nothing. This is the other half of that lesson. Here is an R-N-A medicine with a positive cardiovascular outcomes trial, fifty-nine percent growth and a billion-dollar quarter, and it lost a quarter of its market value in a day on launch pace, site of care, and a pricing rule. For a rare-neurology oligonucleotide those are the same three variables, and not one of them appears anywhere in a Series A deck. When we build the NuGate case, the commercial model deserves as much scrutiny as the knockdown data, and right now it gets a fraction.

Eddie, one connection before I close. On Friday we covered Sanofi saying explicitly that it is hunting later-stage and commercial assets. Two days later, Oppenheimer named Sanofi as the most likely acquirer of a twenty-three billion dollar commercial R-N-A company that has fallen fifty-six percent this year. The large-cap appetite we described is now shopping in a market where the prices have just moved, and that cuts both ways for us — it raises the bar for what counts as a differentiated story, and it lowers the price of the comparables a partner will quote back at us.

That is the watch.
