## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Wednesday, November eighteenth. Three items. The first one closes a three-day argument about delivery, and it closes it from the one direction we hadn't tested yet.

Start with Aletira and SELEXON.

A company called Marrow Therapeutics launched yesterday out of Watertown, Massachusetts, with a hundred and seventy-three million dollars across a Series A and a Series B announced together. Fifty-three million in the A, co-led by Scion Life Sciences, which founded the company, and venBio. A hundred and twenty million in the B, led by OrbiMed, with R-T-W, Surveyor Capital, Opaleye, KKR, Columbia Threadneedle and several others alongside. The lead program is in I-N-D-enabling studies. Nothing has been in a human being.

What they are building is an antibody that binds the transferrin receptor — the receptor cells use to take up iron — with a linker and a payload hung off it. They call it a transferrin antibody bioconjugate. The lead molecule, M-A-R one-oh-one, carries a small interfering R-N-A that knocks down B-C-L eleven-A, which is the switch that silences fetal hemoglobin. Knock it down in the right cells and fetal hemoglobin comes back, which treats both sickle cell disease and beta thalassemia with one molecule. That is exactly the mechanism behind Casgevy, except Casgevy requires taking a patient's stem cells out, editing them, giving the patient chemotherapy to make room, and putting them back, at a price in the millions and only at specialized centers. Marrow's version is an injection. It can be redosed. It can be titrated.

Now here is why this is the third day of the same conversation and not a new one.

Monday was Seawolf — a hundred million dollars behind lipid nanoparticles carrying D-N-A, a vehicle with essentially no tropism at all, where any cell-type selectivity has to come from somewhere other than the particle. Tuesday was the Nature Biotechnology capsid, an A-A-V six evolved in human T cells until it picked up the ability to engage C-D seven, and whose own authors wrote in their discussion that what they bought was lineage-restricted enhancement on top of a body-wide baseline, not organ-level specificity. Today is the third vehicle, and it is the one with the best-validated receptor in the business.

And look at how Marrow describes its own selectivity. Bone marrow has to make blood continuously, so it needs enormous amounts of iron, so marrow cells express the transferrin receptor — their words — at markedly higher levels than most other human cells. Markedly higher. Not exclusively. The transferrin receptor is on essentially every dividing cell in the body, which is why the same receptor is the shuttle everyone uses to cross the blood-brain barrier. Marrow's claim to selectivity is a quantitative gradient on a ubiquitous receptor.

Matt, Avi — this is the cleanest version of the point we have been circling all week, because here the floor has a name and a clinical consequence. The press release says the team understands precisely how to engage the receptor while avoiding off-target effects, and then it names the one it means: the anemia risk that limits other transferrin-receptor-targeted approaches. Think about what that sentence is admitting. If you bind the receptor that red cell precursors use to take up iron, and you bind it hard enough or broadly enough, you cause anemia — in patients whose problem is already anemia. The off-target expression floor is not an abstraction in this program. It is the toxicity. And the only lever they have against it is binding affinity and dose, which is to say they are trying to solve a selectivity problem with a dial rather than a switch.

Three vehicles, three days, three teams who are each among the best in the world at what they do, and the structural answer is identical every time. Targeting the vehicle moves the distribution. It does not set anything to zero. A cassette that gates expression after the payload is already inside the cell is the only thing in any of these architectures that can. Nothing about this week changes the SELEXON action — prove the cassette outside an A-A-V genome before the Series A — but it widens the positioning considerably. The pitch is not "works with your capsid." It is "works with whatever you are using to get in," and that now includes conjugates.

Two more reads on this one.

Emily, Hemaka — the financing structure is the part I would study. Here is a platform company with no clinical data raising a hundred and seventy-three million, with a hundred and twenty of it from a crossover-heavy syndicate, at the I-N-D-enabling stage. We have been saying since October that the capital window is open for clinical data and closed for platforms. Marrow got platform money anyway, and the way it did that was by borrowing validation on both axes at once. The target is borrowed — B-C-L eleven-A as the fetal hemoglobin switch is Stuart Orkin's work, and Orkin is on their scientific advisory board, and the mechanism has an approved product behind it. The delivery technology is borrowed — the release says explicitly that the platform was optimized using lessons learned from validated first-generation transferrin-receptor platforms targeting other tissues, and they hired accordingly. Their chief scientific officer and their chief operating officer both came from Dyne. Art Levin, the founding chief scientific officer of Avidity, joined the board. So the story an investor is asked to underwrite is: known target, known receptor, known chemistry, new tissue. That is a template, and it is one SELEXON can partly run — the gene is validated, the vector class is validated, and what is new is the cassette.

And the candid counterweight, because we covered it two weeks ago. Avidity's del-desiran is a transferrin-receptor antibody-oligonucleotide conjugate, the same modality class, and it missed its Phase 3 primary endpoint in myotonic dystrophy in September, which cost Novartis a one-point-nine-billion-dollar impairment on a twelve-billion-dollar acquisition. Dyne's conjugates in the same disease and in Duchenne are on accelerated-approval paths and have Breakthrough designation, so the modality is not dead by any means. But the honest summary is that transferrin-receptor conjugates have one Phase 3 miss, zero approvals, and a hundred and seventy-three million dollars of fresh money betting the problem was the tissue and not the chemistry. Worth knowing which of those two it was before anyone cites this round as proof that conjugate delivery is solved.

Item two is for Adventris, and it is the strongest single data point the cancer vaccine thesis has gotten.

Sellas Life Sciences reported topline results yesterday from REGAL, a randomized, open-label Phase 3 in acute myeloid leukemia. Their drug, galinpepimut-S, is an off-the-shelf peptide vaccine — four peptides derived from W-T one, licensed from Memorial Sloan Kettering out of David Scheinberg's lab, designed to raise both C-D four and C-D eight T-cell responses across multiple H-L-A types. A hundred and twenty-six patients, randomized one to one, all of them in second or later complete remission after salvage therapy and ineligible for an allogeneic transplant. The vaccine was given as maintenance monotherapy. The control arm was investigator's choice of best available treatment, which could include a hypomethylating agent, venetoclax, low-dose cytarabine — or observation.

The primary endpoint was overall survival, analyzed after a prespecified eighty deaths. Median survival was twenty-four months on the vaccine against fourteen point seven months on best available treatment. Hazard ratio of point five nine — a forty-one percent reduction in the risk of death — at a two-sided p equals point oh three five. Treatment-related adverse events were mostly grade one and two, mostly injection-site reactions. The company is going to the F-D-A to discuss an accelerated filing and is preparing a B-L-A for the first half of next year. The stock went up more than a hundred and fifty percent, and then the company announced a hundred and fifty million dollar offering and the stock gave back about ten percent after hours.

Eddie, Virginia — the read is the sort, and the sort just got a randomized Phase 3.

Back in September we assembled a scorecard across five cancer-vaccine challengers and the pattern was unambiguous: every positive signal in the field was in resected or early disease, and every clean failure was in established or metastatic disease. Colorectal autogene cevumeran failed; post-surgery pancreatic did not. Elicio missed overall and survived only in the completely resected subset. Merck and Moderna won in adjuvant melanoma. The conclusion we drew was that the modality is not failing — it is failing in metastatic disease, which is where most of the capital went. That conclusion was assembled out of other people's subgroup analyses. It is now a randomized, registrational overall-survival win in the lowest-tumor-burden setting anybody runs: patients in remission, with residual disease you cannot see, where the vaccine's only job is to keep it from coming back. The sort holds, and it holds at the highest level of evidence the field has produced.

That matters for Adventris specifically in two ways. The first is that their differentiated Phase 1 is interception — twenty hereditary-pancreatic-cancer-predisposed individuals with a radiographic lesion and no cancer, vaccine alone, no checkpoint inhibitor. That is the same board Sellas just won on, one notch earlier. The second is more interesting on the science. W-T one is a self-antigen that tumors overexpress. Central tolerance works against you; the T cells that would recognize it best have mostly been deleted. Adventris vaccinates against mutant KRAS, which is a genuine neoantigen — a sequence that does not exist in healthy tissue. If a four-peptide self-antigen vaccine can move overall survival by nine months in remission maintenance, the specificity argument runs in Adventris's favor, not against it. Carry that into the next partner conversation.

Now be candid about what this result is not. It is open-label, with a control arm that could be observation, which means both the patients and the investigators knew, and post-protocol treatment decisions were made by people who knew. P equals point oh three five on a hundred and twenty-six patients is a result that clears the bar and does not clear it by much. The secondary endpoints have not been evaluated yet — the company says that analysis is still running, which is an unusual thing to say on the day you announce a positive Phase 3. And the hundred and fifty million dollar raise on the same news is the tell that the company read this as a financing window, which is a judgment about the data as much as about the market. Treat this as category validation for the setting. Do not treat the nine-month number as a benchmark anyone has to beat yet.

Last item, and it is a follow-up on a date we put on the calendar four days ago.

On Friday I flagged that the Senate health committee was scheduled to vote on Heidi Overton as F-D-A commissioner today, and that the commissioner's voucher question stayed open until it did. The vote is not happening. The White House pulled the nomination on Tuesday evening, hours before the committee was due to meet, with Republicans holding a one-seat majority on that committee and at least two of them publicly undecided. Opposition came from both directions — Democrats, some Republicans, and parts of the Health Secretary's own movement. Bill Cassidy, the outgoing chair and a physician, disputes the account that she would have lost, but he had said on television on Sunday that he still had questions and could not predict the outcome. Roger Marshall, the Kansas senator who lost his seat this month, has been floated as a replacement.

Hemaka, Anthony, Jon — here is the operative fact, and it is not about any individual. The F-D-A has had no confirmed commissioner since Marty Makary left in May. Kyle Diamantas, a food lawyer, has been acting commissioner for six months. With the nomination pulled, any successor has to be confirmed in a narrow window before the Senate changes hands in January, and after that the confirmation math is completely different. The realistic planning assumption is that the agency runs under an acting commissioner through most of next year.

Cassidy put the trade-off better than I can. He framed it as a choice between a series of short-term administrators who will probably have more influence from the Secretary, and a permanent commissioner who might not be anyone's ideal candidate but would have the standing to establish some independence. As of Tuesday evening we are in the first world.

Three consequences for us. The first is that every commissioner-level initiative we have been designing against should be assumed to slip. The revised substantial-evidence guidance — the one with the clause saying that the acceptable significance level depends on the pretrial probability that the drug works, which is the clause that sets alpha and therefore sample size for the neuropsych program — closed comments in September and the final is pending. The Rare Disease Framework, where we intend to file the untreated-ear internal-control ask for Aletira, had its public meeting three weeks ago and was floated as possibly proceeding by regulation. Regulation requires a commissioner's signature. Plan the Aletira pre-I-N-D against the draft as written and against the review division, not against a final that may not arrive before the program needs an answer.

The second is a genuine positive and I want to give it its due. Advisory committees came back under the acting commissioner. Twenty-seven percent of federal advisory boards had been eliminated since January of twenty twenty-five; Diamantas restarted adcomm meetings after taking over in May, and the biologics center director has been public about wanting more of them. For a first-in-class modality in a rare indication, an advisory committee is usually a friend rather than a hazard — it is the venue where natural-history and internal-control arguments actually get heard. That machinery working is worth more to Aletira than who holds the title.

The third is simply that the commissioner's voucher question is now answered by default. No awards since May, no confirmed commissioner to make them, and no vote today. Take it off the list of things that might accelerate a timeline and leave it on the list of things to check again in the spring.

That is the watch. A hundred and seventy-three million dollars went into a conjugate whose selectivity is a gradient on a receptor every cell expresses, and whose named failure mode is the very disease it treats — which is the third independent proof this week that targeting the vehicle buys enrichment and not restriction. An off-the-shelf peptide vaccine won a randomized Phase 3 on overall survival in remission maintenance, which is the setting our own field scorecard said was the winnable one, and Adventris sits one notch earlier on the same board with a better antigen. And the F-D-A will be run by an acting commissioner into next year, so design the regulatory asks against the draft and the division, not against a final and a signature. Back tomorrow.
