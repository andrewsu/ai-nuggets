## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Sunday, August twenty-third. The day's most important development for the portfolio: Novartis's venture arm just funded the one company in the world whose Phase 2 readout, due before the end of this year, tests the exact claim the Lieber Institute program is being built to make. And separately, I owe you a correction on Capricor that changes the lesson we have been drawing from it.

Start with Kynexis. On Wednesday, Kynexis — a Netherlands company in Naarden — closed a forty-million-euro extension to its Series A, taking the round to ninety-seven million euros, about a hundred and ten million dollars. The extension was led by a new investor, the Novartis Venture Fund, with the existing syndicate of Forbion, Ysios and Sunstone following on, and Novartis's Marianne Uteng takes a board seat. The money is for three named things: closing out the Phase 2 proof-of-concept, preparing for registrational development in cognitive impairment associated with schizophrenia, and moving into other cognitive disorders including Alzheimer's. Enrollment in that Phase 2 is complete and topline is guided to the end of this year.

The asset is KYN-5356, a purpose-built inhibitor of KAT-two, the enzyme that makes kynurenic acid in the brain. Kynurenic acid is an endogenous antagonist at both the alpha-seven nicotinic and N-M-D-A receptors, and the thesis is that too much of it upstream is part of why cognition is impaired in schizophrenia. You know where that biology came from: Robert Schwarcz at the University of Maryland School of Medicine essentially built it, and he is a scientific co-founder of Kynexis. We covered the human proof of mechanism in July — the Buchanan and Schwarcz tryptophan challenge study at the Maryland Psychiatric Research Center, where N-acetylcysteine, which inhibits that enzyme, blunted the kynurenic acid rise and preserved cognitive performance that degraded on placebo.

Three reads. The first is the competitive clock on the G-P-R fifty-two program, and it just got shorter. The reason a G-P-R fifty-two agonist justifies a new company is reaching all three symptom domains, and the domain carrying the value is cognition. On the tenth I told you MapLight had planted a flag on independent cognition with a muscarinic — effect size around point five in the baseline-impaired subgroup. That was a subgroup finding inside a trial powered for psychosis. Kynexis is different in kind: a trial designed around cognition as the thing being measured, in a company built entirely on that claim, now with a large pharma's venture arm on the board and stated preparation for registrational work. So by the time our development candidate is declared later this year, there will be a dedicated, purpose-built, non-dopaminergic cognition readout in the public record. If it is positive, first-in-class in cognitive impairment in schizophrenia is gone and our differentiation has to be breadth across all three domains plus tolerability, not novelty. If it is negative, that is not free either — a clean miss on a well-designed cognition trial makes every investor harder on anyone claiming a cognitive benefit from any mechanism. Hemaka, that is a conversation with Third Rock before the candidate is declared, not after the readout.

The second read is for Eddie and for UM Ventures. This is the second time in a month I have pointed at a mechanism whose foundational biology is Maryland's and whose franchise is somewhere else. The molecule Kynexis is running came out of Mitsubishi Tanabe's library, not out of Baltimore. U-M-B has the world's leading kynurenine pathway scientist, the trialist, the N-I-H program-project grant and now the human proof-of-mechanism data — and the company capturing that value sits in Naarden with Forbion and Novartis on the cap table. The transferable lesson for the Luetkens cathepsin-B lead, on that same UM Ventures and Blackbird co-investment surface: world-class biology without composition of matter is a citation, not a company. Answer the chemical matter question before the diligence, not during it.

The third read is smaller but worth carrying: Novartis was not on our buyer list for the Nxera asset. We have been assuming Otsuka, Bristol Myers via Karuna, Neurocrine and AbbVie via Cerevel. A Novartis venture check into schizophrenia cognition this week says they are at least looking. Add them. On Nxera itself, still no named buyer and no G-P-R fifty-two news of any kind in the window. And one adjacent note: Molecular Psychiatry published a review Wednesday on COMT inhibition for cognition, co-authored by Daniel Weinberger at the Lieber Institute with a first author now at Boehringer Ingelheim — the company that walked away from the G-P-R fifty-two option in December. It is a review, not data. I flag it only because the cognition lane around us is filling from every direction, and our claim will be argued against rather than accepted.

Second item, and this is the correction. Yesterday was Capricor's action date for deramiocel in Duchenne. As of this morning there is no announcement from the company and nothing from the agency. But I need to sharpen something I have told you repeatedly, most recently yesterday, because the version I gave you leads to the wrong lesson for Aletira.

I have been describing HOPE-3 as a trial that hit its primary endpoint on upper limb function, with the advisory committee voting against on a cardiac secondary. That is the company's framing, and The Lancet did publish a fifty-four percent slowing of upper limb decline. But the agency's own briefing document from July twenty-seventh says the study did not meet its pre-specified primary and secondary efficacy endpoints, and that F-D-A considers the analyses based on the post-study statistical analysis plan versions to be post hoc and exploratory. The final analysis plan, version three, is dated November twenty-fourth of last year — one day before unblinding — and per the securities complaint now filed in the Southern District of California, it was never submitted to or agreed with the agency before the resubmission. STAT wrote on Thursday that the therapy is heading for a rejection.

So the lesson is not the one I drew. I have been calling this the live test of whether F-D-A rewards a sponsor for narrowing to the endpoint it can prove. It is not that test. It is a case about analysis-plan provenance. Narrowing is fine. What is not fine is choosing the endpoint after you have seen the shape of the data and asking for credit for it. The operative sentence for every program here: the agency will let you make a small claim, but only the claim you wrote down in advance, in a plan it has seen. Hugh and Avi — when the SELEXON expression-control package goes into a pre-I-N-D, the endpoint and the analysis are the parts to agree with the division early and in writing. Cheap now, the whole company later.

And note the contrast with the approval we covered on Thursday. Ultragenyx's Genglycos cleared F-D-A four days early on a cornstarch-reduction surrogate, in a forty-six-patient Phase 3, with two years of confirmatory data agreed up front. Narrow claim, pre-agreed surrogate, obligation accepted. Same regulator, same month, opposite outcome — and the difference is not how big the claim was, it is whether the plan existed before the data did.

Third, a diligence item for Avi, and a nice accident of timing. On Wednesday Bristol Myers Squibb announced a collaboration with Chai Discovery to use its folding and design models for antibody discovery across the portfolio; Chai already has Lilly, Pfizer and Novartis. The same day, Nature Biotechnology published a blinded, prospective benchmark — five hundred and eleven machine-designed or predicted antibodies from twenty-nine organisations, three tasks, real experimental affinity and developability readouts. Optimising an antibody you already have was modelled effectively. Predicting which clone in a cluster is the high-affinity one was, for every model but one, worse than picking at random. Designing outside the library was highly variable, with many submissions failing to beat a standard selection campaign — and the groups that did well on one task did not transfer to the others. So the rule when someone pitches us machine-learning antibody discovery, or when we look again at aSKY's engineering partner: ask which of those three jobs the model is doing. One is demonstrated. Two are not, whatever a pharma partnership implies.

Fourth, the calendar item. The Inspector General's request for information on safe harbours for trial participant remuneration closes tomorrow at five p.m. Eastern. I re-pulled the docket this morning: still eight comments, most recent still August fourteenth, Massive Bio on it, 1104health not. Rose Wang's team has one working day. And keep the scope correction from the thirteenth in mind — this docket is about paying patients, not about paying physicians for coordination, which is 1104health's actual thesis and runs on a slower track.

The rest is short. Nothing from Clasp, Adventris or aSKY. Nothing from CAMP4 since the quarter on the thirteenth; the analyst day is still September twenty-eighth and first dosing still guided to the fourth quarter. Nothing from Skylark or Sensorion. Zero mentions of Blackbird anywhere in the window. One market note for Eddie, beside the reverse-merger arithmetic: Scribe Therapeutics priced a hundred-and-fifty-million-dollar offering at twenty-one dollars fifty with its lead asset only in Phase 1 and interim data not until the first half of twenty twenty-seven — and the stock is up forty-three percent since. Leerink's read is conservative pricing plus a near-term value-inflecting readout. A cheap price and a dated catalyst is what buys an early-stage company a listing right now; the template is not the stage of the asset.

To close. Novartis money and a year-end readout mean the cognition claim under the Lieber program gets tested by somebody else before our candidate is declared, and the Maryland-biology, foreign-franchise pattern has now repeated. Capricor is not a story about whether the agency rewards narrowing; it is a story about writing the analysis down before you look, and Ultragenyx is what the same regulator does when you do. And there is one working day left on that docket. I'll see you tomorrow.
