## Script

Good morning, Blackbird team. This is the Blackbird Brief Portfolio Watch for Sunday, October fourth. The most important development for us this week is that the validation layer around new approach methodologies just got federally capitalized — three days after we put a national center for exactly that work inside our own building — and the money went to the referee, not to the evidence package.

Start there. A-T-C-C — the Manassas, Virginia biological resource center — this week announced eight point three million dollars across three new federal contracts. Four point two million from the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases to build a human lung-on-a-chip for influenza countermeasure testing. Two point four million, also from that institute, for a human brain and blood-brain-barrier chip to screen antivirals against neuroinvasive viruses. And one point seven million from the F-D-A's Oncology Center of Excellence to build standardized, cryopreserved organoid models of pediatric brain tumors — and the stated purpose of that third contract is the agency's own effort to modernize drug evaluation using human-relevant alternatives to animal testing.

Read the third one twice. That is the F-D-A contracting directly, with its own money, for qualified non-animal models to use in regulatory review. For the National Center to Accelerate Cures, which launched at the Blackbird BioHub on Thursday with three million dollars of congressionally directed funding, that is the demand signal you want to see. It says the buyer is real and the buyer is the regulator.

But there is a second half, and it is the one Eddie should spend time on. In the same week, the Advanced Research Projects Agency for Health named A-T-C-C the independent verification and validation partner for its BioStabilization Systems program — up to eighty-seven million dollars, four contract awards announced September twenty-fifth, aimed at producing, storing and shipping cell-based medicines at room temperature instead of minus one hundred ninety-six degrees. Three performer teams are competing: one led by DesiCorp with the University of Louisville and Ossium Health, one led by Draper with Likarda and MiNK Therapeutics, and one led by U-C Davis with Mayo Clinic and Case Western. A-T-C-C does not compete. A-T-C-C supplies the government-selected working cell banks every team has to use, and then renders the verdict on whether the preserved product is viable and functional.

That is a structurally better position than being a performer, and it is adjacent to the one we just helped stand up. The center in our building owns the regulatory-grade evidence package. A-T-C-C owns the reference materials and the scorecard. Those can be complementary — a center that assembles evidence packages needs characterized reference materials, and the organization that supplies them to the government is an obvious partner. They can also collide, because whoever holds the reference standard sets the acceptance criteria, and acceptance criteria are where the value in a qualification business actually sits. Eddie, that is a first-call conversation this week, and the question is one sentence: partner or referee.

One more read on the same award, for the therapeutics side. If room-temperature stabilization of cell products works on anything like the four-year timeline A-R-P-A-H is funding, then cold chain stops being a durable differentiator for anybody. Do not let a cell-therapy thesis in our pipeline rest on logistics. It will be commoditized by a program that is already paying three teams to do it.

Second item, and it bears directly on Winnow. Merck released the detailed phase two-b numbers for tulisokibart, its anti-T-L-one-A antibody, in moderate-to-severe hidradenitis suppurativa. We flagged the topline on October first with no figures attached. Here are the figures. One hundred forty-nine patients randomized across high, medium and low dose plus placebo. Seventy-two percent of patients on the high dose hit a fifty percent reduction in abscesses and inflammatory nodules; sixty-four percent on the medium dose. Placebo-adjusted, that is a thirty-seven point advantage at the high dose and twenty-nine at the medium. For scale, adalimumab in its registrational trial put thirty-one percent of patients over the same bar. Dosing is once every four weeks, against every two weeks for bimekizumab. And on safety, adverse events were modestly higher than placebo, serious events were infrequent and evenly distributed, and there were no serious or opportunistic infections reported.

That last clause is the one that matters to us, and it cuts against our story. Our pitch for a selective engager is partly an efficacy pitch and substantially a safety pitch — spare the T cells you want to keep, and you spare the patient the infection risk that comes with broad immunosuppression. A broad biologic that posts best-in-class efficacy in a hard dermatology indication with a clean infection table weakens the second half of that argument in exactly the populations where it was most persuasive. It does not weaken the first half. Hemaka and Virginia — the differentiation we can still defend is durability off treatment and the antigen-specific cut, not generic safety. Make sure the deck says that.

Third, on NuGate. Mayo Clinic published, and then this week publicized, the one-year results of a single-patient antisense trial in a rare genetic form of A-L-S. The patient carries an R-fifteen-L variant in C-H-C-H-D-ten, a mitochondrial gene that causes A-L-S and frontotemporal dementia through a toxic gain of function, with T-D-P-forty-three pathology downstream. The n-Lorem Foundation — Stanley Crooke's nonprofit — screened more than three hundred twenty antisense oligonucleotides against the target and built one for him. Six intrathecal doses over a year, starting at fifty milligrams and rising to seventy-five.

The numbers are the interesting part, because they are a clean test of the instrument question we have been circling on this program. Plasma neurofilament light started at twenty-seven point four picograms per millilitre, above the upper limit of normal, fell to thirteen point four at six months, and sat at seventeen point two at twelve. Forced vital capacity went from forty-eight percent of predicted to sixty percent in the first month and then held in the mid-fifties. The functional rating scale had fallen from forty-seven to thirty-three over the three years before treatment; it held flat at thirty-three for nine months, then went up to thirty-five and thirty-six. Two separate head injuries during the year moved the biomarker and the score, which the authors report rather than smooth over.

Here is the read for us. At n equals one, with no control arm, the evidence that persuades is the machine-measured pair: a biomarker with a steep untreated slope and a spirometer. The self-reported functional scale is the weakest leg of the argument even when it moves in the right direction. That is the same split we applied to the Encoded Angelman design last month, and it keeps coming out the same way. Anthony and Jon — for the NuGate package, that argues for weighting neurofilament and a machine-measured respiratory endpoint in the registrational conversation and treating the functional scale as supportive rather than primary.

Two things to track. The same oligonucleotide has been extended to eight more carriers through the SILENCE A-L-S program, run by n-Lorem with Columbia and funded by the N-I-H, and the investigator's read is that people who start with milder disease respond better — the same lesson tofersen taught. And two-year data go up at the Northeast A-L-S Consortium meeting next month. Put it on the calendar.

Fourth, a safety item on the fibrosis theme. Bristol Myers Squibb amended the protocol of its phase three A-L-O-F-T program for admilparant, an L-P-A-one receptor antagonist in pulmonary fibrosis, after multiple liver injury events including one death from multi-organ failure following acute liver injury. Two details matter more than the headline. The amendment was made in May and became public this week through an analyst note, not a company disclosure. And the study is still blinded, so nobody — including Bristol Myers — knows whether the patient who died was on drug. The independent monitoring committee has reviewed the unblinded data and recommended the trial continue, and a second committee has been set up specifically for liver events. The phase three readout in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis is still guided to the fourth quarter. The rival, Contineum's P-I-P-E seven ninety-one, sold off on the news and recovered; the sell-side read is that this is molecule-and-dose specific rather than mechanism-wide, but nobody can prove that until the data are unblinded.

For our cardiac fibrosis work the lesson is not about this molecule. Anti-fibrotics are chronic drugs given to people who are not dying this year, which is the setting where a hepatic signal is least forgivable and most likely to end a program. Build the liver monitoring plan into the pre-I-N-D package as a designed feature, not as a response to a finding.

Fifth, briefly, for 1104health. The F-D-A has established an Office of Innovation and Clinical Trial Modernization inside the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research, announced Friday, with dedicated divisions for artificial intelligence and for real-world evidence analytics. Combined with the A-R-P-A-H trial programs we covered earlier this week, that is now two separate parts of the government building permanent capacity around trial execution and real-world data in the same fortnight. For a company whose product is trial access inside community oncology workflow, the relevant question is not the policy — it is whether any of this creates a funded counterparty. Esther, worth a scan of who staffs that office.

And last, a sourcing note. The University of Maryland School of Pharmacy laid out a five-year strategic plan built around what the dean, Sarah Michel, calls pharmapreneurship, and it includes launching an accelerator. That school runs a roughly thirty-eight-million-dollar grants and contracts portfolio spanning basic, translational, clinical and regulatory science. We already have an active lead out of that building — the cross-institution docking platform that pairs the School of Pharmacy with Hopkins pharmacology. An accelerator is a front door, and the best time to be standing in it is before it opens. Yixuan and Eddie — get us in the room while the design is still being written, because what an accelerator decides about equity, field of use and who takes the first meeting is decided early and then never revisited.

That is the watch. The theme of the week is that the infrastructure around our programs is being funded faster than the programs themselves — a validator, a modernization office, an accelerator, all in five days. Infrastructure is where position is cheap and early. Sourcing Radar is next.
